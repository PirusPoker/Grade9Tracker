## What it is

**I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud** by William Wordsworth describes a walk on which the speaker suddenly sees a huge spread of daffodils beside a lake, dancing in the wind. At the time he simply enjoys them. Only later, lying at home, does he realise what they gave him: the memory flashes back and fills him with joy. So the poem is about **two experiences**: seeing nature, and remembering it. The second matters more.

**Stanza by stanza**

| Stanza | What happens | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wandering alone, he sees a "crowd" of daffodils by the lake, under trees, moving in the breeze | "lonely as a cloud": detached and drifting; the flowers are "A host, of golden daffodils" |
| 2 | The daffodils stretch endlessly along the bay, like stars | "Continuous as the stars that shine"; "Ten thousand saw I at a glance" |
| 3 | The waves dance too, but the flowers outdo them; he gazes, not yet understanding | "I gazed—and gazed—but little thought / What wealth the show to me had brought" |
| 4 | Now, at home, the memory returns and his heart dances | "They flash upon that inward eye / Which is the bliss of solitude" |

**Voice and speaker.** A first-person speaker, usually read as Wordsworth himself, calls himself "A poet" in stanza 3. He begins isolated and aimless ("wandered", "lonely") and ends content in solitude: the same aloneness, transformed by nature.

**Key language**
- **"lonely as a cloud"**: a simile that makes him part of nature but separate, floating "on high" above life. Clouds drift without purpose; he is waiting to be moved.
- **Personification of the daffodils**: a "crowd", a "host", "dancing", "Tossing their heads". They become joyful company, the opposite of loneliness. "host" can also suggest an army or a multitude of angels, hinting that nature is something spiritual.
- **"golden"** and **"wealth"**: the language of riches. The daffodils' value is not money but the joy they store up for later.
- **Cosmic simile and hyperbole**: "Continuous as the stars", "never-ending line", "Ten thousand". The scene seems infinite; nature overwhelms the senses.
- **Sound and movement**: "Fluttering and dancing", "sprightly", "glee", "jocund". The lexis of happiness and movement makes the whole landscape celebrate.
- **"I gazed—and gazed—"**: repetition and dashes slow the line to show absorption, and "but little thought" admits he did not yet understand.
- **"that inward eye / Which is the bliss of solitude"**: the imagination, or memory, which can "see" the flowers again. Loneliness becomes "bliss".
- **The final line**: "And dances with the daffodils" makes the speaker join the dance he only watched before, so memory completes the experience.

**Form and structure**
- **Four six-line stanzas rhyming ABABCC** in regular **iambic tetrameter** (four beats a line). The steady, song-like rhythm suits a lyric of calm joy.
- **Each stanza ends in a rhyming couplet**, which rounds it off neatly, like a dance step completed.
- **The shift of tense** in stanza 4: past ("wandered", "saw") to present ("when on my couch I lie", "fills", "dances"). This is the poem's turn, from event to memory, and it shows the experience is still alive.
- **Movement from outside to inside**: from the lake to the couch, from the eye to the "inward eye". The poem stages its own idea that nature's real power is felt later, in the mind.

**Context, woven into meaning**
- **Wordsworth (1770–1850)** was a leading **Romantic** poet. With Coleridge he published *Lyrical Ballads* (1798), which argued for poetry in plain language about ordinary experience and nature.
- **The real walk**: in April 1802 Wordsworth and his sister **Dorothy** saw daffodils by Ullswater in the Lake District; Dorothy described them in her journal. The poem was written a couple of years later, from memory, which is exactly what it is about. Notice that Dorothy is left out: the speaker is "lonely".
- **"Emotion recollected in tranquillity"**: Wordsworth's own phrase for how poetry begins. Strong feeling, remembered later in calm, becomes poetry. Stanza 4 is a picture of that process.
- **Nature as renewal**: for the Romantics nature restored the spirit and taught moral and spiritual truths, a reaction against the growing industrial towns and the rationalism of the Enlightenment.

**Themes**: nature's beauty and power; memory and imagination; solitude and company; joy; the poet's mind.

**Best pairings for the comparison question**
- **I Shall Return** (McKay): memory of a beloved landscape; Wordsworth can summon the scene at will, McKay longs for a place he is exiled from.
- **Blackberry Picking** (Heaney): nature and memory; Heaney's memory of nature ends in disappointment, Wordsworth's in lasting joy.
- **The Schoolboy** (Blake): Romantic joy in nature, freedom outdoors.
- **Drummer Hodge** (Hardy): a landscape, and the stars, seen very differently.

::: terms
- **Lyric**: a short poem expressing personal feeling, often musical in rhythm.
- **Romanticism**: a movement valuing nature, feeling, imagination and the individual, reacting against industry and pure reason.
- **Personification**: giving human qualities to something non-human, like the dancing daffodils.
- **Simile**: a comparison using "like" or "as", such as "lonely as a cloud".
- **Hyperbole**: deliberate exaggeration for effect, like "Ten thousand saw I at a glance".
- **Iambic tetrameter**: a line of four unstressed-stressed beats, giving a steady, song-like rhythm.
- **Rhyming couplet**: two consecutive rhyming lines, closing each stanza here.
- **Inward eye**: Wordsworth's image for memory and imagination, which can see things again in the mind.
:::

1. **Overview first**: a poem about seeing nature and, more importantly, remembering it.

3. **Analyse personification, the similes and the language of wealth**, and what they show about nature's value.
4. **Use form**: regular ABABCC stanzas, tetrameter, the couplets, and the change to present tense in stanza 4.
5. **Weave in context**: Romanticism, the walk with Dorothy, "emotion recollected in tranquillity", nature as renewal.

::: example The opening simile
Explain how the first line presents the speaker. (3)

"I wandered lonely as a cloud" uses a simile that links the speaker to nature from the start (1). A cloud floats high above the land, so he is detached from the world below and drifting without direction ("wandered") (1). The loneliness sets up a contrast with the daffodils' "crowd", which will become his company and change his mood (1).
:::

::: example The language of wealth
Explain how Wordsworth uses the words "golden" and "wealth". (4)

The daffodils are "golden", a colour linked with sunlight and riches (1). In stanza 3 he says he "little thought / What wealth the show to me had brought", so the flowers are a treasure he did not know he had received (1). The wealth is emotional and spiritual, a store of joy he can draw on later, not money (1). This reflects the Romantic belief that nature's value lies in what it does for the mind, in contrast with a world increasingly ruled by industry and profit (1).
:::

::: example The final stanza
Explain why the last stanza is the most important. (4)

It moves into the present tense, "when on my couch I lie", showing the experience continues long after the walk (1). The daffodils "flash upon that inward eye": memory and imagination recreate the scene suddenly and vividly (1). "the bliss of solitude" turns the opening loneliness into happiness, so the poem's journey is complete (1). This matches Wordsworth's idea of poetry as emotion recollected in tranquillity: the true value of nature is felt later, in calm reflection (1).
:::

::: example A comparative topic sentence
Write a topic sentence comparing this poem with I Shall Return on memory of a place. (3)

"Both speakers treasure the memory of a natural landscape (1), but Wordsworth can call his back whenever he likes, 'on my couch', whereas McKay's speaker is cut off from his homeland and can only promise to return (1); for the Romantic Englishman memory is a comfort, while for the Jamaican exile writing in America it is bound up with 'long, long years of pain' (1)."
:::
