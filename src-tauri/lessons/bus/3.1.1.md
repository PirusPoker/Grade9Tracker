## What it is

A **business** is any organisation that provides **goods** (physical products — a bike, a sandwich) or **services** (things done for you — a haircut, insurance) to customers, usually to make a **profit**. People start businesses to make money, to be their own boss, to turn a hobby or skill into a living, because they have spotted a gap in the market, or because they lost a job.

Businesses exist because people have **needs** (essentials for survival — food, water, shelter, warmth) and **wants** (everything else — a phone, a holiday). Wants are unlimited; the resources to satisfy them are not.

**The four factors of production** — the resources every business uses:

| Factor | Meaning | In a café |
|---|---|---|
| **Land** | natural resources, including the site | the plot the café stands on; the coffee beans |
| **Labour** | the people and their effort | baristas, the cleaner |
| **Capital** | equipment, machinery and buildings used in production (**not** money) | the espresso machine, the ovens, the building |
| **Enterprise** | the entrepreneur who takes the risk and brings the other three together | the owner who set it up |

Because resources are limited, every choice has an **opportunity cost**: the next best alternative given up. Spending 5,000 on a new coffee machine means not spending it on advertising — the advertising is the opportunity cost.

**Sectors of industry:**

- **Primary** — extracting raw materials: farming, fishing, mining, forestry, oil.
- **Secondary** — manufacturing and construction: turning raw materials into products — a bakery, a car plant, a builder.
- **Tertiary** — services: retail, banking, hairdressing, transport, hotels, education.

Most UK businesses and jobs are tertiary; a business can span sectors (a farm shop is primary and tertiary).

**An entrepreneur** is a person who takes the **risk** of starting and running a business. What they do: spot an opportunity, organise resources, make decisions, take the financial risk. What they are like: **risk-taker, determined, creative, confident, hard-working, organised, good communicator, self-motivated**. What they want out of it — their **objectives**: financial (profit, wealth, a secure income) and non-financial (independence, satisfaction, a challenge, helping others, flexible hours).

**The dynamic nature of business** — the business environment never stays still: **customer tastes** change (plant-based food), **technology** changes (streaming replaced DVDs), **competitors** appear, **the economy** rises and falls, **laws** change. A business that does not adapt loses customers to one that does — so businesses must keep innovating and reviewing what they offer.

Key terms to learn:

- **Business**: an organisation that provides goods or services, usually to make a profit.
- **Enterprise**: the willingness to take a risk to start a business; also the factor of production.
- **Entrepreneur**: a person who takes the risk of setting up and running a business.
- **Opportunity cost**: the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
- **Dynamic business environment**: the way the conditions a business operates in constantly change.

::: method
1. **AQA command words set the length.** *State/identify* (1 mark each) — a word or phrase. *Define* (2) — a full definition. *Explain* (2–3) — point plus a "this means / because" development. *Analyse* (6) — a chain of at least three linked steps applied to the business in the case. *Evaluate / Recommend / To what extent* (9) — both sides, a decision, and a justified "it depends on…".
2. **Apply to the case.** Almost every question names a business. Use its details — the product, its size, its customers. A generic answer caps out at half marks.
3. **Goods vs services, needs vs wants**: identify which the business sells; a service is intangible and cannot be stored.
4. **Capital means equipment.** If a question says "the owner invested 20,000", that is money used to *buy* capital.
5. **Sectors**: ask what the business *does* — extracts, makes, or serves. Say why.
6. **Entrepreneur questions**: link a characteristic to what it lets the entrepreneur do — "determination means she keeps going after a poor first year, which a new business often has".
7. **Dynamic environment**: name the change, then the consequence for the business, then what it must do.
:::

::: example State and define
(a) State one need and one want. (2) (b) Define "opportunity cost". (2)

(a) Need: water (1); want: a games console (1). (b) The next best alternative (1) that is given up when a decision is made (1).
:::

::: example Sectors
Amir runs a company that catches fish, smokes them in its own smokehouse and sells them from its website. Identify the sector of each activity. (3)

Catching fish — primary, extracting a natural resource (1). Smoking — secondary, processing a raw material into a product (1). Selling online — tertiary, providing a retail service (1).
:::

::: example Explain — entrepreneur characteristics
Explain one characteristic an entrepreneur needs. (3)

Willingness to take risks (1). An entrepreneur invests their own money and often gives up a secure job with no guarantee the business will succeed (1); without this willingness, they would never start the business in the first place, because most new businesses take time to become profitable (1).
:::

::: example Analyse — the dynamic environment
Priya's DVD rental shop has seen sales fall for three years as streaming services have grown. Analyse the impact of this change on her business. (6)

Streaming is a change in technology and customer tastes: customers can watch films instantly at home, so demand for renting physical DVDs falls (2). Lower sales mean lower revenue while her fixed costs — rent, staff wages — stay the same, so her profit falls and could turn into a loss, threatening the survival of the shop (2). To respond she must adapt, for example by moving into a niche streaming cannot serve — rare films, retro games, a café — or by closing the shop and moving online; if she does nothing the business will eventually fail, showing why businesses must keep reviewing what they offer (2).
:::

::: q
State two reasons why someone might start a business. (2)
---
To make a profit; to be their own boss (or to follow an interest, spot a gap in the market, after redundancy).
:::

::: q
Define "goods" and "services", giving an example of each. (2)
---
Goods are physical products, e.g. a bicycle (1); services are intangible activities done for customers, e.g. a taxi ride (1).
:::

::: q
Identify the four factors of production. (4)
---
Land, labour, capital, enterprise.
:::

::: q
Explain why money is not classed as capital. (2)
---
Capital means the equipment, machinery and buildings used to produce goods (1); money is only used to buy those items and produces nothing itself (1).
:::

::: q
A bakery buys a new oven for 8,000 instead of redecorating the shop. State the opportunity cost and explain why it matters. (3)
---
The opportunity cost is the redecoration (1). Resources are limited, so the money can only be used once (1); the bakery must judge whether the oven brings more benefit than the alternative it gave up (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one difference between the secondary and tertiary sectors. (2)
---
The secondary sector makes physical products from raw materials, e.g. a furniture factory (1); the tertiary sector provides services, e.g. a furniture shop (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one non-financial objective an entrepreneur might have. (3)
---
Independence (1): being their own boss means they make the decisions and set their own hours (1), which many people value more than a higher but supervised salary (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one way the business environment is dynamic and how a business might respond. (3)
---
Customer tastes change, e.g. rising demand for vegan food (1); a restaurant that ignores this loses customers to competitors (1), so it should add vegan dishes to its menu (1).
:::

::: q
Analyse why determination is important to an entrepreneur starting a new business. (6)
---
Most new businesses make little or no profit at first, because they have few customers and high start-up costs (2). A determined entrepreneur keeps working through this period, adjusting the product and finding customers rather than giving up, so the business survives long enough to become established (2). Determination also helps with setbacks such as a bank refusing a loan or a supplier letting them down — they look for alternatives instead of closing, which is why it is often the difference between a business that survives its first year and one that does not (2).
:::

- **Apply to the business in the question**: name its product, customers or situation in every developed point.
- Sectors are about **what the business does**, not what it sells: a bakery is secondary, a shop selling bread is tertiary.

:::

The difference between the price of a product and the cost of the materials used to make it (1); e.g. a café sells a coffee for 3 that cost 40p in beans and milk (1).

Willingness to take risks — investing money and time with no guarantee of success (2); creativity, determination or organisational skills to spot opportunities and keep going through setbacks (2).
:::

::: test

::: test
Explain why a company might add value by branding its products. (2)
---
A brand makes the product seem more desirable or reliable (1); customers will pay a higher price for the same basic product (1).

::: test
Analyse how a poor economic climate can affect the number of new businesses started. (3)
---
Lower consumer spending reduces expected sales, so fewer people take the risk (1); banks lend less readily (1); but redundancies may push some people into starting businesses, and cheap premises may become available (1).
