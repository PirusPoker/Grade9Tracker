## What it is

**Marketing** is identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer needs — profitably. Everything in marketing starts with the customer: a business that does not know what its customers want is guessing with its money.

**Customer needs** are what customers require from a product: the right **price**, **quality**, **choice** and **convenience** — and increasingly speed of delivery, after-sales service and ethical standards. Different customers weigh these differently: a student wants a cheap laptop that works; a designer wants power and a great screen.

**Why identifying and satisfying customer needs matters:**

- **Selling more** — a product that meets a real need sells; customers return and recommend it, so revenue grows.
- **Getting the marketing mix right** — knowing the customer decides the product's features, the price they will pay, where they shop and what promotion reaches them (the 4Ps, 3.5.4).
- **Avoiding costly mistakes** — developing, making and promoting a product nobody wants wastes the money spent; understanding customers first prevents it.
- **Staying competitive** — rivals are trying to satisfy the same customers; the business that understands them best wins them, and tastes change, so it must keep listening.
- Also: charging the right price, spotting gaps in the market, building loyalty and a reputation.

**How a business finds out what customers need**: **market research** (3.5.3) — surveys, questionnaires, interviews, focus groups, observing behaviour; **sales data** — what sells, when, where, to whom; **customer feedback** — complaints, reviews, social media comments, loyalty-card data; **talking to customers** — a shop owner listens every day; **watching competitors** — what they offer and what customers say about them; **market trends** and reports.

Key terms to learn:

- **Marketing**: the process of identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer needs profitably.
- **Customer needs**: the requirements customers have when buying a product, such as price, quality, choice and convenience.
- **Market research**: collecting and analysing information about customers, competitors and the market.
- **Marketing mix**: the combination of product, price, promotion and place a business uses to sell.

::: method
1. **Name the need and apply it**: "convenience — commuters want coffee in under two minutes, so the café needs an app for pre-ordering".
2. **Why it matters** — the chain: know the need → right product/price/place/promotion → sales → avoid wasted spending → competitive. Three links for an analyse.
3. **How to find out**: name a method and say what it would reveal for this business.
4. **Link to failure**: a good evaluation point is that many product failures come from assuming rather than asking.
5. Keep it short — this topic is small; marks come from application.
:::

::: example Explain — a customer need
Explain one customer need that a business selling children's shoes must satisfy. (2)

Quality (1): parents need shoes that fit well and last, because children's feet are still growing and cheap shoes can harm them (1).
:::

::: example Explain — why it matters
Explain why understanding customer needs helps a business avoid costly mistakes. (3)

Developing and launching a product costs money for design, stock and promotion (1). If the business does not know what customers want, it may make a product that does not sell (1), losing all that spending; research first means money goes only into products customers have shown they need (1).
:::

::: example Analyse — competitiveness
Analyse how identifying customer needs helps a gym stay competitive. (6)

By asking members what they want — classes at certain times, more equipment, childcare — the gym can offer exactly those things, so members are satisfied and less likely to switch to a rival gym (2). Knowing that, say, most members are students on tight budgets lets it set the right price and promote through social media rather than newspapers, so its marketing mix fits and marketing money is not wasted (2). Needs change — home workouts and apps grew rapidly — so a gym that keeps listening can add online classes before rivals do; one that assumes it knows its members loses them to whoever meets the new need first (2).
:::

::: q
State two customer needs. (2)
---
Price; quality (or choice, convenience).
:::

::: q
Define "marketing". (2)
---
The process of identifying and anticipating what customers need (1) and satisfying those needs profitably (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one way a clothing retailer could find out what its customers want. (2)
---
Analyse sales data (1) to see which sizes, colours and styles sell fastest and which are left unsold (1).
:::

::: q
Explain how understanding customer needs helps a business set its price. (2)
---
Knowing how much customers value the product and can afford (1) lets the business set a price they will pay without leaving money on the table (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why customer needs might change over time. (2)
---
Incomes, fashion and technology change (1) — for example, customers now expect to order online and get fast delivery (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one benefit of listening to customer complaints. (3)
---
Complaints show where the product or service fails to meet needs (1); fixing the problem stops other customers leaving for the same reason (1) and can turn the complainer into a loyal customer (1).
:::

::: q
Analyse why a business that ignores customer needs is likely to fail. (6)
---
Products designed without asking customers may lack the features, price or convenience buyers want, so sales are low and the money spent on development, stock and promotion is wasted (2). Competitors who do research offer what customers want, so customers switch, and the business loses market share and reputation (2). Falling sales with fixed costs unchanged mean losses; without profit the business cannot invest in fixing its products, and unless it starts listening — through research, feedback and sales data — the decline continues until it closes (2).
:::

::: watch
- The chain is **know the customer → right marketing mix → sales, no wasted spending → competitive**. Learn it.
- Name a **specific need** (price, quality, choice, convenience) and **apply it** to the product.
- "How does the business find out?" — give a **method and what it reveals**.
- A short topic worth **easy marks** — do not skip it in revision.
:::
