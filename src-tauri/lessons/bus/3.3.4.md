## What it is

**Customer service** is everything a business does to look after its customers before, during and after a sale. AQA is the only major board to examine it as a topic — easy marks if you know it.

**The sales process** — the stages of turning an interested person into a satisfied customer:

1. **Customer interest** — attracting attention: a shop window, an advert, a website, social media.
2. **Speed and efficiency of service** — greeting quickly, short queues, fast website, answering enquiries promptly.
3. **Customer engagement** — talking to the customer, finding out what they need, listening; product knowledge; making them feel valued.
4. **Post-sales service** — after the purchase: **user training** (showing how to use a product — a new phone, software, a fitted kitchen), **help lines** and online support, **servicing** (maintenance, repairs, warranties), handling returns and complaints.
5. **Customer feedback** — asking how it went; reviews; using feedback to improve.

**Benefits of good customer service:**

- **Customer satisfaction** → customers return: **loyalty and repeat purchase**, which is far cheaper than finding new customers.
- **Higher spend** — satisfied customers buy more and buy add-ons; trust makes them accept higher prices.
- **Recommendation** — word of mouth and positive reviews bring new customers at no cost.
- **Competitive advantage** — service is hard for rivals to copy, unlike price.
- **Fewer complaints and returns**, so lower costs.
- **Higher revenue and profit** overall, and a stronger brand.

**Damage done by poor customer service:**

- **Lost customers** — most people who receive poor service simply do not come back.
- **Bad word of mouth** — an unhappy customer tells many others; online reviews and social media multiply this: a single viral complaint can reach millions.
- **Lost revenue** now and in future; **lower profit**.
- **Damaged reputation and brand**; harder to charge premium prices.
- **Costs** of handling complaints, refunds and compensation; possible legal action under consumer law.
- **Staff morale** falls when dealing with angry customers.

**How technology has changed customer service:**

- **Websites** — 24-hour information, FAQs, order tracking, online booking; customers expect instant answers.
- **E-commerce** — buying at any time from anywhere; easy comparison, so service must match the best; returns handled by post.
- **Social media** — customers ask questions and complain publicly and expect a fast reply; businesses can respond in minutes, build a community, and show they care — or be seen ignoring problems; reviews on Google, Trustpilot and TripAdvisor shape buying decisions.
- Also: live chat and chatbots, email support, apps with loyalty schemes, automated order updates, personalised recommendations from customer data. Faster and cheaper for the business, but less personal, and any failure is visible to everyone.

Key terms to learn:

- **Customer service**: the help and support a business gives customers before, during and after a purchase.
- **Customer engagement**: building a relationship with customers by communicating with them and understanding their needs.
- **Post-sales service**: support provided after a purchase, such as training, help lines and servicing.
- **Customer loyalty**: customers choosing to return to the same business repeatedly.
- **Word of mouth**: customers telling other people about their experience of a business.

::: method
1. **Name the stage of the sales process** the question is about and apply it: "post-sales service — the kitchen fitter returns to fix a sticking drawer".
2. **Benefits chain**: good service → satisfaction → loyalty and recommendation → higher revenue at low marketing cost → profit. Make three links for an "analyse".
3. **Poor service chain**: dissatisfaction → customer lost → tells others (amplified online) → falling sales and reputation → cost of complaints. Quantify the amplification where you can ("one review seen by thousands").
4. **Technology**: give the specific tool (social media, live chat, tracking) and both sides — faster and cheaper, but public and impersonal.
5. **Evaluate**: service matters most where products are similar and switching is easy, and for high-value or complex products; weigh the cost of staff training and support against retained customers.
:::

::: example State and explain
Identify one part of the sales process and explain how it could help a mobile-phone shop increase sales. (3)

Post-sales service, such as user training (1). Showing a customer how to set up and use their new phone before they leave (1) makes them confident with the product, so they are more likely to return for their next upgrade and recommend the shop (1).
:::

::: example Explain — damage from poor service
Explain one way poor customer service could damage a hotel. (3)

Bad word of mouth (1). A guest who is treated rudely at reception posts a one-star review on TripAdvisor that thousands of potential guests read before booking (1), so future bookings fall even though those readers never experienced the hotel themselves (1).
:::

::: example Analyse — benefits of good service
Analyse how excellent customer service could benefit an independent bicycle shop competing with online retailers. (6)

The shop cannot match online prices, but staff who fit the bike, explain the gears and offer a free first service give customers something a website cannot — so customers pay a little more for confidence and help (2). Satisfied customers return for repairs, accessories and their next bike, giving the shop repeat revenue at no marketing cost, and they recommend it to friends and in local cycling groups, bringing new customers (2). Over time the shop builds a loyal customer base and a reputation that shields it from price competition; the cost is staff time and training, but a lost sale online is far cheaper than a lost customer for the shop, so the investment is worthwhile (2).
:::

::: example Evaluate — social media
A restaurant chain is deciding whether to handle customer complaints through social media. Evaluate whether it should. (9)

For: customers already post about bad experiences online, so replying quickly and publicly shows care and can turn a complaint into loyalty; responses are visible to everyone, advertising good service; it is faster and cheaper than call centres, and problems are spotted early across all branches (3). Against: every complaint and every reply is public — a clumsy or slow response goes viral; staff must monitor accounts constantly, including evenings and weekends; some customers post unfair or false complaints that are hard to answer publicly; personal details and refunds still need private handling (3). Judgement: it should, because ignoring social media is no longer an option — customers will complain there anyway — but with trained staff, clear response times, and a process to move each complaint to private messages quickly; the benefit depends on responding consistently well, so the chain must invest in the people to do it (3).
:::

State two stages of the sales process. (2)
---
Customer engagement; post-sales service (or customer interest, speed of service, feedback).
:::

Give two examples of post-sales service. (2)
---
A help line; servicing or repairs under warranty (or user training, handling returns).
:::

::: q
Explain one benefit to a business of customer loyalty. (3)
---
Repeat purchases (1): loyal customers keep returning without the business paying to attract them (1), so revenue rises while marketing costs per sale fall (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why customer engagement is important in selling a complex product such as a computer. (2)
---
Staff who ask about the customer's needs can recommend the right model (1), so the customer buys with confidence and is less likely to return it (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one cost to a business of poor customer service. (2)
---
Handling complaints and refunds takes staff time and money (1), reducing profit even before lost sales are counted (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one way a website has improved customer service. (2)
---
Order tracking lets customers see where their delivery is at any time (1), reducing anxiety and calls to the business (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one drawback of using chatbots for customer service. (2)
---
They cannot handle unusual problems and frustrate customers who want a person (1), so complex issues may go unresolved and customers leave (1).
:::

::: q
Analyse how poor customer service could affect a mobile-phone network's profit. (6)
---
Customers who wait on hold for an hour or whose faults go unfixed switch to a rival network at the end of their contract, so revenue is lost and the network must spend on advertising and discounts to replace them (2). Unhappy customers post complaints on social media and review sites, deterring potential customers and forcing the network to compete on price rather than reputation, cutting margins (2). Complaints also cost money to handle and may lead to regulator fines or compensation; the network could invest in better support, which costs more in the short term but retains customers whose monthly payments are worth far more than the cost of serving them well (2).
:::

::: watch
- This topic is **AQA-only** — know the five stages of the sales process and the post-sales examples (**training, help lines, servicing**).
- Benefits chain: **satisfaction → loyalty → higher spend and recommendation → profit**. Costs chain: **lost customer → word of mouth (amplified online) → lost revenue**.
- Technology answers need the **specific tool** and **both sides** — faster and cheaper, but public and less personal.
- Retaining a customer is **cheaper than winning a new one** — a strong point in any evaluation.
- Service is a **competitive advantage** that price-cutting rivals cannot easily copy.
:::
