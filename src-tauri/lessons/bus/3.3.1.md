## What it is

**Production** is turning inputs — materials, labour, machinery — into goods or services. The **production process** a business chooses affects its costs, quality, flexibility and the skills it needs. AQA examines two methods, plus lean production and just-in-time.

**Job production** — making **one product at a time**, each to the customer's individual requirements: a wedding dress, a made-to-measure kitchen, a bridge, a portrait, a bespoke website.

*Advantages*: high quality, each product exactly what the customer wants, so a **high price** can be charged; work is varied and skilled, so **motivating**; flexible. *Disadvantages*: **slow** and **labour-intensive**, so **high unit costs**; needs highly skilled (expensive) workers; hard to gain economies of scale; long waiting times for customers.

**Flow production** — a **continuous** process where the product moves along a **production line**, each stage adding something, producing **identical items in huge numbers**: cars, canned drinks, crisps, newspapers, smartphones.

*Advantages*: very **low unit costs** through economies of scale and automation; fast; consistent quality; unskilled or semi-skilled labour; suits mass markets. *Disadvantages*: **huge set-up cost** for machinery; **inflexible** — hard to change the product; a breakdown at one stage stops the whole line; repetitive work is boring, lowering motivation; large stocks of materials and finished goods may be needed; only worthwhile with high, steady demand.

*Choosing*: job for unique, high-value, low-volume products where customers pay for individuality; flow for standardised products with large, steady demand. (Batch production is *not* on this specification — do not waste space on it.)

**Lean production** — an approach that aims to **cut waste** of every kind while keeping quality: wasted time, wasted materials, wasted movement, over-production, excess stock, defects and unused worker skills. Techniques include just-in-time, continuous improvement (*kaizen* — small improvements suggested by workers), cell production, and total quality management (3.3.3). Benefits: lower costs, higher quality, faster response to customers, less space needed. Drawbacks: relies on reliable suppliers and workers; little slack when things go wrong.

**Just-in-time (JIT)** — the key lean technique for stock: materials arrive **exactly when needed** for production, and finished goods are made to order, so the business holds **little or no stock**.

*How it makes production more efficient*: no money tied up in stock, so **cash flow** improves; no warehouse space, storage or insurance costs; less waste from stock going out of date, being damaged or becoming obsolete; problems show up immediately because there is no buffer, forcing them to be fixed. *Drawbacks*: **depends totally on suppliers** — one late delivery stops production; no buffer for a sudden rise in demand, so sales may be lost; frequent small deliveries cost more and lose bulk discounts; needs excellent IT and planning.

Key terms to learn:

- **Job production**: producing one-off items, each made to the customer's individual requirements.
- **Flow production**: continuous production of identical items along a production line.
- **Lean production**: an approach to production that minimises waste of resources, time and effort while maintaining quality.
- **Just-in-time (JIT)**: a system where materials are delivered only when needed for production, so little stock is held.
- **Unit cost**: the cost of producing one item.

::: method
1. **Identify the method from the product**: unique and made to order → job; identical in huge quantities → flow. State *why* it suits the business.
2. **Advantages and disadvantages in terms of cost, quality, flexibility, motivation** — the four themes. Apply each to the case.
3. **Recommend** a method (6–9 marks): match the product, the volume of demand, the customers' willingness to pay, and the finance available for machinery. Conclude with a condition.
4. **Lean and JIT**: explain the *mechanism* — "holding less stock frees cash and cuts storage costs" — then the *dependence on suppliers* as the standard drawback.
5. **Link production to other topics**: flow needs finance (3.6) and gives economies of scale (3.1.7); JIT needs reliable suppliers (3.3.2); lean supports quality (3.3.3).
:::

::: example Identify and justify
A business makes bespoke oak dining tables to customers' own measurements and designs. Identify the production method and explain why it suits the business. (3)

Job production (1). Each table is different, so it cannot be made on a standard production line (1); customers pay a high price for a unique, hand-crafted product, which covers the higher labour cost (1).
:::

::: example Explain — flow production
Explain one advantage of flow production for a soft-drinks manufacturer. (3)

Low unit costs (1). Automated lines fill thousands of cans an hour, spreading the cost of machinery over a huge output (1), so each can costs very little to produce and the business can compete on price in a mass market (1).
:::

::: example Analyse — just-in-time
Analyse the effects of introducing just-in-time on a car manufacturer. (6)

Parts arrive at the line only as they are needed, so the manufacturer no longer holds warehouses of components; the money previously tied up in stock is freed, improving cash flow, and storage, insurance and handling costs fall (2). Quality problems become visible at once because there is no stock to hide them, encouraging suppliers and workers to fix faults quickly, and less stock means less waste from parts becoming obsolete when models change (2). The risk is dependence on suppliers: a strike, a traffic accident or a late shipment stops the whole line within hours, costing far more than the stock saved, so JIT works only with reliable suppliers close to the factory and excellent IT systems to coordinate deliveries (2).
:::

::: example Recommend
Nadia's bakery makes 200 hand-decorated celebration cakes a month and has been offered a contract to supply 20,000 standard cupcakes a week to a supermarket. Recommend whether she should switch to flow production. Justify your answer. (9)

For flow: 20,000 identical cupcakes a week cannot be made by hand at a sensible cost; a production line would give low unit costs, consistent quality and the speed the contract needs, and the supermarket contract provides the steady demand that makes the machinery worthwhile (3). Against: the machinery is a large investment she would need to borrow for; flow is inflexible, so if the supermarket ends the contract she is left with expensive equipment and no market; her reputation and margins come from bespoke celebration cakes, which need job production and skilled decorators who may be demotivated by line work (3). Recommendation: take the contract and run flow production for the cupcakes as a separate line, keeping job production for celebration cakes — provided the contract is long enough to repay the machinery and she can finance it; if the supermarket will only commit short-term, she should decline or subcontract the cupcakes rather than risk the core business (3).
:::

::: q
State one product suited to job production and one suited to flow production. (2)
---
Job: a wedding dress (or a custom kitchen, a bridge). Flow: cans of cola (or cars, crisps).
:::

::: q
Explain one disadvantage of job production. (3)
---
High unit cost (1): each product is made individually by skilled workers, taking many hours (1), so the labour cost per item is high and the business must charge a high price (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one disadvantage of flow production. (3)
---
Inflexibility (1): the line is set up to make one standard product (1), so changing the design or responding to a shift in customer tastes is slow and expensive (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why flow production is unsuitable for a business with small, variable demand. (2)
---
The expensive machinery is only worth buying if it runs continuously (1); with low, uneven demand it would sit idle and the unit cost would be high (1).
:::

::: q
Define "lean production". (2)
---
An approach to production that aims to cut all forms of waste — time, materials, stock, effort (1) — while maintaining or improving quality (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one way lean production reduces costs. (2)
---
Producing only what is ordered avoids over-production (1), so no money or space is wasted on goods that may never sell (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one drawback of just-in-time for a supermarket. (3)
---
No buffer stock (1): if a delivery is late or demand suddenly rises, shelves are empty (1), so customers cannot buy and may go to a rival, losing sales (1).
:::

::: q
Explain how job production can motivate workers. (2)
---
Each product is different and requires skill (1), so work is varied and workers take pride in a finished item they can identify as their own (1).
:::

::: q
Analyse why a business making luxury handbags might choose job production over flow production. (6)
---
Luxury customers pay for craftsmanship and individuality — hand-stitching, choice of leather, personalisation — which only job production can deliver; a production line would produce identical bags that undermine the exclusive image (2). The high price customers pay covers the higher labour cost per bag, so the low unit cost of flow production is not needed, and demand is too small and varied to keep a line running (2). Job production also motivates skilled craftspeople whose reputation is part of the brand; the trade-off is slow output and long waiting lists, but for a luxury brand scarcity itself adds to desirability — so job production fits both the product and the marketing (2).
:::

::: watch
- AQA examines **job and flow only** — batch production earns nothing.
- Job: **high quality, high unit cost, skilled, flexible.** Flow: **low unit cost, high set-up cost, inflexible, repetitive.**
- Match the method to **volume of demand and willingness to pay** — that is the justification examiners want.
- JIT's benefit is **cash flow and storage costs**; its drawback is **dependence on suppliers**. Both every time.
- Lean is about **waste** — name the kinds of waste it cuts.
:::
