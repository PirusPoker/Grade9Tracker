## What it is

**Motivation** is the desire to work hard and do a job well. A motivated workforce gives a business:

- **Higher productivity** — more output per worker, so lower unit costs.
- **Better quality and customer service** — staff who care make fewer mistakes and treat customers well.
- **Lower staff turnover (higher retention)** — motivated people stay, saving recruitment and training costs and keeping skills and experience.
- **Lower absenteeism**, fewer disputes, more ideas and flexibility, a better reputation as an employer.

AQA states that **motivational theories are not examined** — no Maslow, Herzberg or Taylor. The marks are for the **methods** and their effects.

**Financial methods** — motivating with money:

| Method | What it is | Motivates because | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Salary** | a fixed annual amount paid monthly regardless of hours or output | security and status; suits managers and professionals | no direct link to effort — a poor performer is paid the same |
| **Wage** | paid per hour (**time rate**) or per unit made (**piece rate**) | overtime pays more; piece rates reward speed | time rates reward hours not effort; piece rates can harm quality and safety |
| **Commission** | a percentage of the value of sales made | directly rewards selling; suits sales staff | income insecure; encourages pushy selling and rivalry between staff |
| **Profit sharing** | a share of the business's profit paid to employees | staff feel part of the business and want it to succeed | individual effort has little effect on total profit; nothing paid in a bad year |
| Also: **bonuses** for hitting targets, **performance-related pay**, **share ownership**, **fringe benefits** with cash value | | | |

Money motivates up to a point, but pay rises are quickly taken for granted, and financial methods raise costs.

**Non-financial methods** — motivating without extra pay:

- **Management style** — an **autocratic** manager gives orders with no consultation (fast, clear, but demotivating for skilled staff); a **democratic** manager involves staff in decisions (motivating, better ideas, but slower); a **laissez-faire** manager leaves staff to it (suits experts; risky otherwise). Praise, respect and listening cost nothing and motivate strongly.
- **Training** — shows the business values the employee, builds confidence and opens promotion; see 3.4.4.
- **More responsibility** — **delegation**, **job enrichment** (more challenging tasks), **job enlargement** (more variety), **job rotation** (moving between tasks), **empowerment** (freedom to make decisions): staff feel trusted and grow.
- **Fringe benefits (perks)** — company car, private healthcare, gym membership, staff discounts, pension contributions, free meals, extra holiday. Valued because they are tangible and can be cheaper for the business than the equivalent pay rise.
- Also: **flexible working**, promotion opportunities, team working, a pleasant environment, recognition (employee of the month), clear communication.

**Which method?** It depends on the job (sales → commission; assembly → piece rate or bonus; professional → salary and responsibility), the employee (young workers may want training and progression; parents may want flexibility), the business's finances (non-financial methods are cheaper), and its objectives (quality → avoid piece rates).

Key terms to learn:

- **Motivation**: the willingness of employees to work hard and do their job well.
- **Salary**: a fixed amount paid to an employee each year, usually in monthly instalments.
- **Wage**: payment based on hours worked (time rate) or units produced (piece rate).
- **Commission**: a payment to sales staff based on the value of what they sell.
- **Profit sharing**: paying employees a share of the business's profits.
- **Fringe benefits**: non-cash rewards given to employees, such as a company car or private healthcare.
- **Staff retention**: keeping employees in the business rather than losing them to other employers.

::: method
1. **State the benefit of motivation and chain it**: motivated staff → higher productivity → lower unit cost → lower prices or higher profit; or → better service → loyal customers.
2. **Financial method questions**: name it, explain *how* it links pay to effort, then a drawback (cost, quality, insecurity).
3. **Non-financial methods**: explain *why* they motivate — being trusted, valued, developed — and note they are cheaper.
4. **Recommend a method** for the business and job in the case, with a reason and a limitation. Consider a combination.
5. **Do not write theory.** No Maslow pyramids. If you mention "needs", tie them to a method.
:::

::: example Explain — benefit of motivation
Explain one benefit to a call centre of having a motivated workforce. (3)

Lower staff turnover (1). Motivated employees are less likely to leave for other jobs (1), so the call centre avoids the high cost of constantly recruiting and training new staff and keeps experienced people who serve customers better (1).
:::

::: example Explain — commission
Explain one drawback to a car dealership of paying sales staff mainly by commission. (3)

Pushy selling (1). Staff paid on sales may pressure customers into cars or extras they do not want (1), leading to complaints, returns and a reputation that drives future customers away (1).
:::

::: example Analyse — non-financial methods
A small accountancy firm cannot afford pay rises but is losing staff to larger firms. Analyse how non-financial methods could help it retain employees. (6)

Giving staff more responsibility — running their own client accounts and making decisions without checking with a partner — makes them feel trusted and developed, which larger firms with rigid hierarchies often cannot offer (2). Paying for professional training and offering flexible or home working shows the firm invests in them and respects their lives, and these cost far less than matching a big firm's salaries (2). A democratic management style, where staff are consulted on how the firm is run and praised for good work, builds loyalty; these methods will not stop everyone leaving for much higher pay, but they make the firm attractive to those who value development and flexibility over money (2).
:::

::: example Recommend — a method
A furniture factory wants to raise output on its assembly line without lowering quality. Recommend whether it should introduce piece-rate pay or a team bonus for hitting quality-checked output targets. Justify your answer. (9)

Piece rates reward each worker directly for every unit made, so output rises quickly and the link between effort and pay is clear; but workers may rush, cutting corners on finishing, and quality could fall — the opposite of the objective — while faster workers resent slower colleagues (3). A team bonus paid when the line hits an output target that has passed quality inspection rewards speed and quality together, encourages workers to help each other and share techniques, and costs the business only when it gains; but individuals can coast on the team's effort, and if targets are missed the bonus demotivates (3). Recommendation: the team bonus, because the factory's objective includes quality and assembly is team work; targets should be realistic and reviewed, and the scheme combined with training and recognition; piece rates would suit only if each worker made complete items alone and quality could be checked per piece (3).
:::

::: q
State two financial methods of motivation. (2)
---
Commission; profit sharing (or salary, wages, bonuses).
:::

::: q
State two non-financial methods of motivation. (2)
---
Training; more responsibility (or management style, fringe benefits).
:::

::: q
Explain one advantage to an employee of a salary rather than an hourly wage. (2)
---
A guaranteed fixed income each month (1), making it easier to budget and giving security (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one drawback of piece-rate pay for a business. (2)
---
Workers may rush to make more units (1), so quality falls and faulty products must be scrapped or reworked (1).
:::

::: q
Explain how profit sharing might motivate employees. (3)
---
Staff receive a share of the profit (1), so they have a direct interest in the business succeeding (1) and work harder and waste less, since it affects their own pay (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a democratic management style might motivate staff. (3)
---
Employees are consulted on decisions (1), so they feel valued and that their ideas matter (1), making them more committed to carrying out plans they helped shape (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one advantage of fringe benefits over a pay rise for the business. (2)
---
Perks such as staff discounts or gym membership can cost the business less than the equivalent salary increase (1) while still being valued by staff (1).
:::

::: q
Analyse how giving employees more responsibility could affect a retail business. (6)
---
Letting shop staff decide displays, handle refunds and resolve complaints without a manager makes them feel trusted and skilled, raising motivation and job satisfaction (2). Motivated staff serve customers better and solve problems faster, so customer satisfaction and sales rise, and fewer staff leave, cutting recruitment costs (2). The risks are that untrained staff make costly mistakes or inconsistent decisions, so the business must train them and set limits; some employees may not want extra responsibility without extra pay, so the method works best alongside recognition or a modest bonus (2).
:::

::: watch
- **No theories** — Maslow and Herzberg earn nothing on AQA. Write about **methods and effects**.
- Benefits of motivation: **productivity, quality, retention** — chain to cost, sales and profit.
- Every financial method has a **drawback** — cost, quality, insecurity, rivalry. Give it.
- Non-financial methods are **cheaper** and often stronger for skilled staff — a key evaluation line.
- Recommend a method that fits **the job, the employee and the business's finances**, usually a combination.
:::
