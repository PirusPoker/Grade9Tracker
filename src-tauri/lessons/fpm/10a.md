## What it is

**Radians** are the natural unit for angles: one radian is the angle at the centre of a circle that cuts off an arc equal in length to the radius. A full turn is $2\pi$ radians, so

$$180° = \pi \text{ radians}, \qquad 1° = \frac{\pi}{180}, \qquad 1 \text{ rad} = \frac{180°}{\pi} \approx 57.3°$$

With the angle $\theta$ in radians, two formulae become beautifully simple:

$$\text{arc length } s = r\theta, \qquad \text{sector area } A = \tfrac12 r^2\theta$$

(Those only work in radians. In degrees you would need $\frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ and $\frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2$.) A **segment** is the bit of a sector cut off by the chord: sector minus triangle, where the triangle's area is $\frac12 r^2 \sin\theta$.

The three ratios are defined for **any** angle, not just the acute ones in a triangle, through the unit circle: for an angle $\theta$ measured anticlockwise from the positive $x$-axis, $\cos\theta$ is the $x$-coordinate and $\sin\theta$ the $y$-coordinate of the point on the circle, and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. That gives the graphs: $\sin$ and $\cos$ are waves between $-1$ and $1$ repeating every $360°$ ($2\pi$); $\tan$ repeats every $180°$ ($\pi$) with vertical asymptotes at $90°$, $270°$, …

**Exact values** you must know without a calculator:

| $\theta$ | $0$ | $30° = \frac\pi6$ | $45° = \frac\pi4$ | $60° = \frac\pi3$ | $90° = \frac\pi2$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin\theta$ | $0$ | $\frac12$ | $\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $\cos\theta$ | $1$ | $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$ | $\frac12$ | $0$ |
| $\tan\theta$ | $0$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt3}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt3$ | — |

(Some books write $\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$ as $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt3}$ as $\frac{\sqrt3}{3}$ — same numbers.)

::: terms
- **Radian**: the angle at the centre of a circle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius; π radians = 180°.
- **Arc length (radians)**: l = rθ, with θ in radians.
- **Sector area (radians)**: A = ½r²θ.
- **Segment area**: ½r²(θ − sin θ).
- **CAST diagram**: shows which ratios are positive in each quadrant — All (0–90°), Sin (90–180°), Tan (180–270°), Cos (270–360°).
- **Periodic**: sin and cos repeat every 360° (2π); tan repeats every 180° (π).
:::

::: method
1. **Convert:** degrees → radians, multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$; radians → degrees, multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$. Leave radians as multiples of $\pi$ where you can: $150° = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.
2. **Arc, sector, segment:** check the angle is in radians, then $s = r\theta$, $A = \frac12 r^2\theta$, segment $= \frac12 r^2\theta - \frac12 r^2\sin\theta = \frac12 r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$. Perimeter of a sector $= 2r + r\theta$; of a segment $=$ arc $+$ chord, chord $= 2r\sin\frac\theta2$.
3. **Ratios of any angle — the CAST rule.** Draw the axes. In the four quadrants going anticlockwise from the bottom-right: **A**ll positive (first quadrant, $0$–$90°$), **S**in positive (second, $90$–$180°$), **T**an positive (third, $180$–$270°$), **C**os positive (fourth, $270$–$360°$). To find a ratio of any angle: find the acute angle it makes with the $x$-axis (the "reference angle"), take the ratio of that from the table, then apply the sign from the quadrant.
4. **Reference angle:** second quadrant $180° - \theta$; third $\theta - 180°$; fourth $360° - \theta$. Negative angles go clockwise; angles above $360°$ wrap round (subtract $360°$).
5. **Sketching the graphs:** $y = \sin x$ starts at $0$, peaks at $90°$; $y = \cos x$ starts at $1$, zero at $90°$; $y = \tan x$ passes through the origin and shoots off at $90°$. Mark the key points and asymptotes.
:::

::: example Converting
Convert $135°$ to radians, and $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$ to degrees.

$135° = 135 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.

$\dfrac{7\pi}{4} = \dfrac{7}{4} \times 180° = 315°$.
:::

::: example Arc length and sector area
A sector has radius $8$ cm and angle $1.2$ radians. Find its arc length, perimeter and area.

$s = r\theta = 8 \times 1.2 = 9.6$ cm. Perimeter $= 9.6 + 8 + 8 = 25.6$ cm.

$A = \dfrac12 r^2\theta = \dfrac12 \times 64 \times 1.2 = 38.4$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example Finding the angle
A sector of a circle of radius $5$ cm has area $20$ cm$^2$. Find the angle in radians and the arc length.

$20 = \dfrac12 \times 25 \times \theta \Rightarrow \theta = 1.6$ rad. Arc $= 5 \times 1.6 = 8$ cm.
:::

::: example A segment
A chord subtends an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ at the centre of a circle of radius $6$ cm. Find the area of the minor segment, exactly.

Sector: $\dfrac12 \times 36 \times \dfrac\pi3 = 6\pi$. Triangle: $\dfrac12 \times 36 \times \sin\dfrac\pi3 = 18 \times \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = 9\sqrt3$.

Segment $= 6\pi - 9\sqrt3$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example Exact values of larger angles
Find the exact values of $\sin 150°$, $\cos 225°$, $\tan 300°$ and $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$.

$150°$: second quadrant, reference $30°$, sin positive: $\sin 150° = \dfrac12$.

$225°$: third quadrant, reference $45°$, cos negative: $\cos 225° = -\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$.

$300°$: fourth quadrant, reference $60°$, tan negative: $\tan 300° = -\sqrt3$.

$\dfrac{5\pi}{3} = 300°$, cos positive in the fourth quadrant: $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{3} = \dfrac12$.
:::

::: example Negative angles and beyond a full turn
Find $\sin(-30°)$, $\cos 420°$ and $\tan\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$.

$-30°$ is $30°$ clockwise, into the fourth quadrant: $\sin(-30°) = -\dfrac12$.

$420° = 360° + 60°$, so $\cos 420° = \cos 60° = \dfrac12$.

$\dfrac{7\pi}{6} = 210°$: third quadrant, reference $30°$, tan positive: $\tan\dfrac{7\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$.
:::

::: q
Convert $240°$ and $22.5°$ to radians (exact), and $\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$ and $2$ radians to degrees.
---
$240° = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$; $22.5° = \dfrac{\pi}{8}$; $\dfrac{5\pi}{12} = 75°$; $2$ rad $= \dfrac{360}{\pi} \approx 114.6°$.
:::

::: q
A sector has radius $10$ cm and angle $0.8$ rad. Find its arc length and area.
---
Arc $8$ cm; area $\dfrac12 \times 100 \times 0.8 = 40$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A sector of angle $2.5$ radians has arc length $15$ cm. Find the radius and the area of the sector.
---
$r = \dfrac{15}{2.5} = 6$ cm; area $= \dfrac12 \times 36 \times 2.5 = 45$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A sector of a circle of radius $12$ cm has perimeter $40$ cm. Find the angle of the sector in radians and its area.
---
$2r + r\theta = 40 \Rightarrow 24 + 12\theta = 40 \Rightarrow \theta = \dfrac43$. Area $= \dfrac12 \times 144 \times \dfrac43 = 96$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A chord of a circle of radius $9$ cm subtends an angle of $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ at the centre. Find the exact area of the minor segment.
---
$\dfrac12 \times 81\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - \sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) = \dfrac{81}{2}\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) = 27\pi - \dfrac{81\sqrt3}{4}$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
Find the perimeter of the minor segment cut off by a chord subtending $1.5$ radians at the centre of a circle of radius $4$ cm.
---
Arc $= 4 \times 1.5 = 6$; chord $= 2 \times 4 \times \sin 0.75 = 8 \times 0.6816 = 5.45$. Perimeter $\approx 11.5$ cm.
:::

::: q
Write down the exact values of $\sin 120°$, $\cos 135°$, $\tan 210°$ and $\sin 330°$.
---
$\sin 120° = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$; $\cos 135° = -\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$; $\tan 210° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$; $\sin 330° = -\dfrac12$.
:::

::: q
Find the exact values of $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, $\sin\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, $\tan\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\cos\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$.
---
$\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = 135°$: $-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$. $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} = 240°$: $-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$. $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 150°$: $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$. $\dfrac{11\pi}{6} = 330°$: $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.
:::

::: q
Find $\sin(-135°)$, $\cos(-60°)$ and $\tan 405°$.
---
$-135°$ is in the third quadrant (reference $45°$): $\sin(-135°) = -\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$. $\cos(-60°) = \cos 60° = \dfrac12$ (cos is even). $\tan 405° = \tan 45° = 1$.
:::

::: q
Given that $\sin\theta = \dfrac35$ and $\theta$ is obtuse, find the exact values of $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.
---
Obtuse: second quadrant, cos and tan negative. From a 3-4-5 triangle, $\cos\theta = -\dfrac45$, $\tan\theta = -\dfrac34$.
:::

::: q
Sketch $y = \cos x$ for $0 \le x \le 360°$ and state the values of $x$ in this range for which $\cos x = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.
---
Starts at $(0, 1)$, through $(90°, 0)$, down to $(180°, -1)$, back through $(270°, 0)$ to $(360°, 1)$.

$\cos x = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$: reference angle $30°$, cos negative in quadrants 2 and 3: $x = 150°$ and $x = 210°$.
:::

::: q
Sketch $y = \tan x$ for $-180° \le x \le 180°$, marking the asymptotes, and solve $\tan x = 1$ in this range.
---
Passes through $(-180°, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, $(180°, 0)$; asymptotes at $x = \pm 90°$. $\tan x = 1$ at $x = 45°$ and $x = -135°$.
:::

::: watch
- **Calculator mode.** For anything in radians, switch to RAD. $\sin 1.2$ in degree mode is the single most common wrong answer in the trig questions.
- $s = r\theta$ and $A = \frac12 r^2\theta$ are **radians only**. An angle given in degrees must be converted first.
- Exact values mean surds: $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, not $0.866$.
- CAST: get the **sign** from the quadrant and the **size** from the reference angle. Two separate steps — do them in that order.
- Segment $=$ sector $-$ triangle, and the triangle uses $\frac12 r^2\sin\theta$ ($\sin$, not $\theta$).
- $\cos$ is even ($\cos(-x) = \cos x$) but $\sin$ and $\tan$ are odd ($\sin(-x) = -\sin x$). If a negative angle appears, place it on the diagram rather than trusting memory.
:::
