## What it is

Calculus applied to things that change with time.

**Kinematics** — a particle moving in a straight line. Its displacement $s$ (position from a fixed origin), velocity $v$ and acceleration $a$ are linked by differentiating and integrating with respect to time:

$$v = \frac{ds}{dt}, \qquad a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}, \qquad s = \int v\,dt, \qquad v = \int a\,dt$$

Differentiate to go down the list ($s \to v \to a$); integrate to go up ($a \to v \to s$), using the starting conditions to find each constant. Velocity can be negative (moving backwards); speed is its size. The particle is momentarily **at rest** when $v = 0$; it **changes direction** when $v$ changes sign.

**Rates of change** — anything written "the rate at which … is changing" is a derivative with respect to time. If a balloon's radius grows at 2 cm/s, that is $\dfrac{dr}{dt} = 2$. When you want a rate you cannot differentiate directly, the **chain rule** connects it to one you can:

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dr} \times \frac{dr}{dt}$$

You know $V$ in terms of $r$ (a formula), so you can find $\dfrac{dV}{dr}$; you are told $\dfrac{dr}{dt}$; multiply.

**Small changes:** for a small change $\delta x$, the change in $y$ is approximately $\delta y \approx \dfrac{dy}{dx}\,\delta x$. That is the same chain-rule idea with the "$dt$" removed.

::: terms
- **Displacement (s)**: position relative to a fixed point.
- **Velocity**: v = ds/dt.
- **Acceleration**: a = dv/dt = d²s/dt².
- **Reversing the process**: s = ∫v dt and v = ∫a dt, using initial conditions to find the constants.
- **At rest**: the particle's velocity is zero.
- **Connected rates of change**: dy/dt = dy/dx × dx/dt — linking the rates of two related quantities.
- **Small changes**: δy ≈ (dy/dx) × δx.
:::

::: method
1. **Kinematics, given $s$ or $v$ as a function of $t$:** differentiate to get what you need. "When is it at rest?" → solve $v = 0$. "Maximum velocity?" → solve $a = 0$. "Initial" means $t = 0$.
2. **Kinematics, given $a$ or $v$ and asked for $v$ or $s$:** integrate, add $c$, and use the given condition ("initially at the origin", "starts from rest", "when $t = 2$, $v = 5$") to find $c$.
3. **Distance travelled** in a time interval: if $v$ does not change sign, it is $\int v\,dt$ (which equals the change in $s$). If $v$ changes sign, split the integral at the moment $v = 0$ and add the sizes — the same as area below the axis.
4. **Connected rates:** write down the rate you are given and the rate you want, as derivatives. Find the formula linking the two variables and differentiate it. Write the chain rule connecting the three, substitute the values **at the instant in question**, and solve for the unknown rate.
5. **Units and signs:** a decreasing quantity has a negative rate. State units in the answer.
6. **Small changes:** $\delta y \approx \dfrac{dy}{dx}\,\delta x$; percentage change in $y$ is $\dfrac{\delta y}{y} \times 100\%$.
:::

::: example Displacement given
A particle moves so that $s = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$ metres after $t$ seconds. Find when it is at rest, and its acceleration at those times.

$v = \dfrac{ds}{dt} = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3(t - 1)(t - 3) = 0 \Rightarrow t = 1$ or $t = 3$.

$a = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = 6t - 12$. At $t = 1$: $-6$ m/s$^2$. At $t = 3$: $6$ m/s$^2$.
:::

::: example Acceleration given, integrate twice
A particle starts from rest at the origin with acceleration $a = 6t - 4$ m/s$^2$. Find $v$ and $s$ in terms of $t$, and the displacement when $t = 3$.

$v = \displaystyle\int (6t - 4)\,dt = 3t^2 - 4t + c$. Starts from rest: $v = 0$ at $t = 0$, so $c = 0$. $v = 3t^2 - 4t$.

$s = \displaystyle\int (3t^2 - 4t)\,dt = t^3 - 2t^2 + k$. At the origin when $t = 0$: $k = 0$. $s = t^3 - 2t^2$.

At $t = 3$: $s = 27 - 18 = 9$ m.
:::

::: example Distance when the direction changes
A particle has velocity $v = 4 - 2t$ m/s. Find the distance travelled in the first 4 seconds.

$v = 0$ at $t = 2$; positive before, negative after — it turns round at $t = 2$.

$0 \to 2$: $\displaystyle\int_0^2 (4 - 2t)\,dt = [4t - t^2]_0^2 = 8 - 4 = 4$ m.

$2 \to 4$: $\displaystyle\int_2^4 (4 - 2t)\,dt = (16 - 16) - (8 - 4) = -4$: $4$ m back the other way.

Distance travelled: $4 + 4 = 8$ m. (The displacement after 4 s is $0$ — it is back where it started.)
:::

::: example Connected rates: a balloon
A spherical balloon is inflated so that its radius increases at $0.5$ cm/s. Find the rate at which the volume is increasing when the radius is $6$ cm.

Given $\dfrac{dr}{dt} = 0.5$; want $\dfrac{dV}{dt}$.

$V = \dfrac43\pi r^3$, so $\dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2$.

$\dfrac{dV}{dt} = \dfrac{dV}{dr} \times \dfrac{dr}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \times 0.5 = 2\pi r^2$. At $r = 6$: $72\pi \approx 226$ cm$^3$/s.
:::

::: example Connected rates, the other way round
Water is poured into a conical container at $20$ cm$^3$/s. The container is such that the volume of water when the depth is $h$ cm is $V = \dfrac{\pi h^3}{12}$. Find the rate at which the depth is rising when $h = 4$.

Given $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = 20$; want $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$.

$\dfrac{dV}{dh} = \dfrac{\pi h^2}{4}$. Chain rule: $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = \dfrac{dV}{dh} \times \dfrac{dh}{dt}$, so $20 = \dfrac{\pi h^2}{4} \times \dfrac{dh}{dt}$.

At $h = 4$: $20 = 4\pi\,\dfrac{dh}{dt}$, so $\dfrac{dh}{dt} = \dfrac{5}{\pi} \approx 1.59$ cm/s.
:::

::: example Small changes
The radius of a circle increases from $10$ cm to $10.1$ cm. Estimate the increase in area.

$A = \pi r^2$, $\dfrac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi r = 20\pi$ at $r = 10$.

$\delta A \approx \dfrac{dA}{dr}\,\delta r = 20\pi \times 0.1 = 2\pi \approx 6.28$ cm$^2$.

(Exact: $\pi(10.1^2 - 10^2) = 2.01\pi$ — the approximation is very close.)
:::

::: q
A particle has displacement $s = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t$ m at time $t$ s. Find its velocity and acceleration when $t = 2$.
---
$v = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 = 24 - 36 + 12 = 0$ m/s. $a = 12t - 18 = 6$ m/s$^2$.
:::

::: q
A particle moves with $s = t^3 - 12t + 5$. Find the times at which it is at rest and its displacement at each.
---
$v = 3t^2 - 12 = 0 \Rightarrow t = 2$ (taking $t \ge 0$). $s = 8 - 24 + 5 = -11$ m.
:::

::: q
A particle has velocity $v = 3t^2 - 4t + 1$ m/s and is at $s = 2$ m when $t = 0$. Find $s$ when $t = 3$.
---
$s = t^3 - 2t^2 + t + c$, $c = 2$. $s(3) = 27 - 18 + 3 + 2 = 14$ m.
:::

::: q
A particle starts from rest and has acceleration $a = 2 - 6t$ m/s$^2$. Find its velocity at $t = 2$ and the time at which it is next at rest.
---
$v = 2t - 3t^2 + c$, $c = 0$. $v(2) = 4 - 12 = -8$ m/s. At rest when $2t - 3t^2 = t(2 - 3t) = 0$: $t = \dfrac23$ s.
:::

::: q
A particle moves with velocity $v = t^2 - 6t + 8$ m/s. Find the distance travelled between $t = 0$ and $t = 4$.
---
$v = (t - 2)(t - 4)$: positive on $0 < t < 2$, negative on $2 < t < 4$.

$\displaystyle\int_0^2 = \left[\dfrac{t^3}{3} - 3t^2 + 8t\right]_0^2 = \dfrac83 - 12 + 16 = \dfrac{20}{3}$. $\displaystyle\int_2^4 = \left(\dfrac{64}{3} - 48 + 32\right) - \dfrac{20}{3} = \dfrac{16}{3} - \dfrac{20}{3} = -\dfrac43$.

Distance $= \dfrac{20}{3} + \dfrac43 = 8$ m.
:::

::: q
A particle's velocity is $v = 4\sin 2t$. Find its acceleration when $t = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, and its displacement from the start when $t = \dfrac\pi4$ if $s = 0$ at $t = 0$.
---
$a = 8\cos 2t = 8\cos\dfrac\pi3 = 4$.

$s = -2\cos 2t + c$; $0 = -2 + c \Rightarrow c = 2$. $s\!\left(\dfrac\pi4\right) = -2\cos\dfrac\pi2 + 2 = 2$.
:::

::: q
The side of a cube is increasing at $0.2$ cm/s. Find the rate of increase of its volume when the side is $5$ cm.
---
$V = x^3$, $\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 3x^2 = 75$. $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = 75 \times 0.2 = 15$ cm$^3$/s.
:::

::: q
The area of a circle is increasing at $12$ cm$^2$/s. Find the rate at which the radius is increasing when the radius is $3$ cm.
---
$\dfrac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi r = 6\pi$. $12 = 6\pi\,\dfrac{dr}{dt} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dr}{dt} = \dfrac{2}{\pi} \approx 0.637$ cm/s.
:::

::: q
A spherical snowball melts so that its volume decreases at $8\pi$ cm$^3$/s. Find the rate at which the radius is decreasing when $r = 2$ cm.
---
$\dfrac{dV}{dt} = -8\pi$; $\dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 = 16\pi$. $-8\pi = 16\pi\,\dfrac{dr}{dt} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dr}{dt} = -\dfrac12$: decreasing at $0.5$ cm/s.
:::

::: q
The volume of water in a tank when the depth is $h$ m is $V = 4h^2 + 3h$. Water flows in at $2$ m$^3$/min. Find how fast the depth is rising when $h = 1.5$ m.
---
$\dfrac{dV}{dh} = 8h + 3 = 15$. $2 = 15\,\dfrac{dh}{dt} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dh}{dt} = \dfrac{2}{15} \approx 0.133$ m/min.
:::

::: q
$y = x^3$. Use $\delta y \approx \dfrac{dy}{dx}\,\delta x$ to estimate the change in $y$ when $x$ increases from $2$ to $2.02$.
---
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 = 12$; $\delta y \approx 12 \times 0.02 = 0.24$.
:::

::: q
The radius of a sphere is measured as $5$ cm with a possible error of $0.05$ cm. Estimate the resulting possible error in the volume, and the percentage error.
---
$\dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 = 100\pi$; $\delta V \approx 100\pi \times 0.05 = 5\pi \approx 15.7$ cm$^3$.

$V = \dfrac{500\pi}{3}$; percentage error $\dfrac{5\pi}{500\pi/3} \times 100 = 3\%$.
:::

::: watch
- **"At rest" means $v = 0$**, not $s = 0$. "Returns to the start" means $s = 0$. "Maximum velocity" means $a = 0$.
- "Starts from rest" gives $v = 0$ at $t = 0$; "starts at the origin" gives $s = 0$ at $t = 0$. Use each to find its own constant.
- Distance and displacement differ when the particle turns round. Check whether $v$ changes sign in the interval; if it does, split.
- For connected rates, write **all three** derivatives with their letters before touching numbers, then substitute the values at the instant asked. Substituting $r = 6$ into $V$ before differentiating gives $\dfrac{dV}{dr} = 0$, which is nonsense.
- A quantity that is decreasing has a **negative** rate. Put the sign in, then describe it in words ("decreasing at …").
- Units: m/s for velocity, m/s$^2$ for acceleration, cm$^3$/s for a volume rate. An answer without units is incomplete.
:::

::: test
A particle has velocity $v = t^2 - 5t + 6$. Find the times at which it is at rest and the distance it travels between them. (3)
---
$(t - 2)(t - 3) = 0$: $t = 2$ and $t = 3$ (1); $\int_2^3 v\,dt = -\dfrac{1}{6}$ (1); distance $\dfrac{1}{6}$ m (1).

::: test
The radius of a circle is increasing at 0.5 cm/s. Find the rate of increase of its area when the radius is 4 cm. (2)
---
$\dfrac{dA}{dt} = \dfrac{dA}{dr}\dfrac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi r \times 0.5$ (1) $= 4\pi \approx 12.6$ cm$^2$/s (1).
:::

::: test
The volume of a cube is increasing at 6 cm$^3$/s. Find the rate of increase of its edge length when the edge is 2 cm. (3)
---
$V = x^3$, $\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 3x^2 = 12$ (1); $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = \dfrac{dV}{dt} \div \dfrac{dV}{dx}$ (1) $= 0.5$ cm/s (1).
:::

::: test
A spherical balloon loses air so that its volume decreases at 8 cm$^3$/s. Find the rate at which the radius is decreasing when $r = 2$ cm. (3)
---
$\dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 = 16\pi$ (1); $\dfrac{dr}{dt} = \dfrac{-8}{16\pi}$ (1) $= -\dfrac{1}{2\pi} \approx -0.159$ cm/s (1).
:::

::: test
$y = x^2 + 1$. Use small changes to estimate the change in $y$ when $x$ increases from 3 to 3.02. (2)

$\delta y \approx \dfrac{dy}{dx}\delta x = 2x\,\delta x = 6 \times 0.02$ (1) $= 0.12$ (1).
:::

::: test
$y = 2x^3$. Use small changes to estimate the percentage change in $y$ when $x$ increases by 1%. (2)
---
$\delta y \approx 6x^2\,\delta x$, so $\dfrac{\delta y}{y} \approx \dfrac{6x^2\,\delta x}{2x^3} = 3\dfrac{\delta x}{x}$ (1). About 3% (1).
:::

$v = 3t^2$ (1). $s = t^3$ (1). At $t = 2$, $s = 8$ m (1).
:::

::: test
A particle moving in a straight line has velocity $v = 3t^2 - 12$ m/s. Find when it is at rest (for $t > 0$), and the total distance it travels from $t = 0$ to $t = 3$. (4)
---
$t = 2$ s (1). $s = t^3 - 12t$ (1). $s(2) = -16$ and $s(3) = -9$ (1). Distance $= 16 + 7 = 23$ m (1).
:::

::: test
The volume of a sphere increases at 20 cm$^3$/s. Find the rate of increase of its surface area when the radius is 5 cm. (5)
---
$\dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 = 100\pi$ (1). $\dfrac{dr}{dt} = \dfrac{20}{100\pi} = \dfrac{1}{5\pi}$ (1). $\dfrac{dS}{dr} = 8\pi r = 40\pi$ (1). $\dfrac{dS}{dt} = 40\pi \times \dfrac{1}{5\pi}$ (1) $= 8$ cm$^2$/s (1).
:::

::: test
Water is poured at 4 cm$^3$/s into an inverted cone (vertex at the bottom) of radius 6 cm and height 12 cm. (a) Show that when the depth is $h$ cm the volume of water is $V = \dfrac{\pi h^3}{12}$. (b) Find the rate at which the depth is increasing when $h = 4$. (6)
---
(a) By similar triangles $r = \frac{h}{2}$ (1). $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^2 h = \frac{\pi h^3}{12}$ (1). (b) $\dfrac{dV}{dh} = \dfrac{\pi h^2}{4} = 4\pi$ at $h = 4$ (2). $\dfrac{dh}{dt} = \dfrac{4}{4\pi} = \dfrac{1}{\pi}$ (1) $\approx 0.318$ cm/s (1).
:::
