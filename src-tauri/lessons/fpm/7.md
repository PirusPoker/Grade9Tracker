## What it is

A **vector** has size and direction — a displacement, not a position. In this course everything is in a plane, written either as a column $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ or with the unit vectors $\mathbf i$ (one step right) and $\mathbf j$ (one step up): $3\mathbf i - 2\mathbf j$. The two notations mean the same thing.

The **magnitude** (length) of $a\mathbf i + b\mathbf j$ is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ — Pythagoras. A **unit vector** has length 1: to get one in the direction of $\mathbf v$, divide $\mathbf v$ by its own magnitude.

A **position vector** is the vector from the origin $O$ to a point: $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$. Then the vector from $A$ to $B$ is $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a$ — *end minus start*. Nearly every vector question is that one line, used repeatedly.

The geometry comes from one fact: **two vectors are parallel exactly when one is a multiple of the other.** So $\overrightarrow{AB} = k\,\overrightarrow{CD}$ proves $AB \parallel CD$; and if $\overrightarrow{AB} = k\,\overrightarrow{AC}$ then $A$, $B$, $C$ are **collinear** (on one line), because the two vectors are parallel *and* share the point $A$.

And one more: if $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ are not parallel, and $\lambda_1 \mathbf a + \mu_1 \mathbf b = \lambda_2 \mathbf a + \mu_2 \mathbf b$, then $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2$ and $\mu_1 = \mu_2$. You can **compare coefficients** of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ — that is how the "find $\lambda$ and $\mu$" questions are done.

::: terms
- **Vector**: a quantity with magnitude and direction.
- **i and j**: unit vectors in the x- and y-directions.
- **Magnitude**: the length of a vector: |ai + bj| = √(a² + b²).
- **Unit vector**: a vector of magnitude 1; the unit vector in the direction of a is a ÷ |a|.
- **Position vector**: the vector from the origin O to a point.
- **Vector from A to B**: AB = b − a.
- **Parallel vectors**: one is a scalar multiple of the other.
- **Collinear points**: points on one straight line — AB and BC are parallel and share point B.
- **Resolving**: splitting a vector into perpendicular components, e.g. a force F at angle θ has components F cos θ and F sin θ.
:::

::: method
1. **Add and subtract component by component**; multiply by a scalar by multiplying each component. $2(3\mathbf i - \mathbf j) - (\mathbf i + 4\mathbf j) = 5\mathbf i - 6\mathbf j$.
2. **Magnitude:** $\lvert a\mathbf i + b\mathbf j \rvert = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$. Leave it as a surd unless asked for decimals.
3. **Unit vector** in the direction of $\mathbf v$: $\hat{\mathbf v} = \dfrac{\mathbf v}{\lvert \mathbf v \rvert}$. A vector of length 10 in that direction is $10\hat{\mathbf v}$.
4. **Vector between points:** $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a$. Position vector of a point on $AB$: $\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf a + t\,\overrightarrow{AB}$ for some $t$; the midpoint is $\frac12(\mathbf a + \mathbf b)$; the point dividing $AB$ in the ratio $m : n$ is $\dfrac{n\mathbf a + m\mathbf b}{m + n}$.
5. **Route-finding in a figure:** to write an unknown vector, walk from its start to its end along vectors you know, adding as you go (going backwards along an arrow is minus). Write the route down before the algebra.
6. **To prove parallel:** show one vector is a scalar multiple of the other. **To prove collinear:** show $\overrightarrow{AB} = k\overrightarrow{BC}$ (or $\overrightarrow{AC}$) and say they share a point. **To find a ratio:** the multiple $k$ *is* the ratio of lengths.
7. **Two routes to the same point:** write the position vector of the point two ways (via two different lines), equate them, and compare the coefficients of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ to find the unknowns.
:::

::: example Components, magnitude, unit vector
$\mathbf p = 4\mathbf i - 3\mathbf j$ and $\mathbf q = -2\mathbf i + 5\mathbf j$. Find $2\mathbf p + \mathbf q$, $\lvert \mathbf p \rvert$, and a unit vector in the direction of $\mathbf p$.

$2\mathbf p + \mathbf q = 8\mathbf i - 6\mathbf j - 2\mathbf i + 5\mathbf j = 6\mathbf i - \mathbf j$.

$\lvert \mathbf p \rvert = \sqrt{16 + 9} = 5$.

Unit vector: $\dfrac{1}{5}(4\mathbf i - 3\mathbf j) = \dfrac45\mathbf i - \dfrac35\mathbf j$.
:::

::: example Position vectors and a point on a line
$A$ and $B$ have position vectors $\mathbf a = \mathbf i + 2\mathbf j$ and $\mathbf b = 7\mathbf i - \mathbf j$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$, its magnitude, and the position vector of the point $P$ that divides $AB$ in the ratio $1 : 2$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a = 6\mathbf i - 3\mathbf j$. $\lvert \overrightarrow{AB} \rvert = \sqrt{36 + 9} = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt5$.

$P$ is one third of the way from $A$ to $B$: $\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf a + \dfrac13\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf i + 2\mathbf j + 2\mathbf i - \mathbf j = 3\mathbf i + \mathbf j$.

(Or by the formula with $m : n = 1 : 2$: $\dfrac{2\mathbf a + \mathbf b}{3} = \dfrac{2\mathbf i + 4\mathbf j + 7\mathbf i - \mathbf j}{3} = 3\mathbf i + \mathbf j$.)
:::

::: example Proving collinearity
Points $A$, $B$, $C$ have position vectors $2\mathbf i + \mathbf j$, $5\mathbf i + 3\mathbf j$ and $11\mathbf i + 7\mathbf j$. Show that they are collinear and find the ratio $AB : BC$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = 3\mathbf i + 2\mathbf j$. $\overrightarrow{BC} = 6\mathbf i + 4\mathbf j = 2\overrightarrow{AB}$.

So $AB$ and $BC$ are parallel; they share the point $B$; hence $A$, $B$, $C$ are collinear, with $AB : BC = 1 : 2$.
:::

::: example Vectors in a figure
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf c$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $N$ is the point on $OB$ with $ON : NB = 2 : 1$. Find $\overrightarrow{OM}$ and $\overrightarrow{ON}$, and show that $C$, $N$ and $M$ are collinear.

$\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf a + \mathbf c$. $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf c$, so $\overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf a + \dfrac12\mathbf c$.

$\overrightarrow{ON} = \dfrac23(\mathbf a + \mathbf c)$.

$\overrightarrow{CN} = \overrightarrow{ON} - \mathbf c = \dfrac23\mathbf a - \dfrac13\mathbf c = \dfrac13(2\mathbf a - \mathbf c)$.

$\overrightarrow{CM} = \overrightarrow{OM} - \mathbf c = \mathbf a - \dfrac12\mathbf c = \dfrac12(2\mathbf a - \mathbf c)$.

$\overrightarrow{CM} = \dfrac32\overrightarrow{CN}$: parallel and sharing $C$, so $C$, $N$, $M$ are collinear.
:::

::: example Comparing coefficients
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. $P$ is the midpoint of $OA$ and $Q$ is on $AB$ with $AQ : QB = 1 : 3$. Lines $OQ$ and $BP$ meet at $X$. Find $\overrightarrow{OX}$ in terms of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$.

$\overrightarrow{OQ} = \mathbf a + \dfrac14(\mathbf b - \mathbf a) = \dfrac34\mathbf a + \dfrac14\mathbf b$. $X$ is on $OQ$: $\overrightarrow{OX} = \lambda\left(\dfrac34\mathbf a + \dfrac14\mathbf b\right)$.

$\overrightarrow{OP} = \dfrac12\mathbf a$; $\overrightarrow{BP} = \dfrac12\mathbf a - \mathbf b$. $X$ is on $BP$: $\overrightarrow{OX} = \mathbf b + \mu\left(\dfrac12\mathbf a - \mathbf b\right) = \dfrac{\mu}{2}\mathbf a + (1 - \mu)\mathbf b$.

Compare coefficients: $\mathbf a$: $\dfrac{3\lambda}{4} = \dfrac{\mu}{2}$; $\mathbf b$: $\dfrac{\lambda}{4} = 1 - \mu$.

From the first, $\mu = \dfrac{3\lambda}{2}$; substitute: $\dfrac{\lambda}{4} = 1 - \dfrac{3\lambda}{2} \Rightarrow \lambda + 6\lambda = 4 \Rightarrow \lambda = \dfrac47$.

$\overrightarrow{OX} = \dfrac47\left(\dfrac34\mathbf a + \dfrac14\mathbf b\right) = \dfrac37\mathbf a + \dfrac17\mathbf b$.
:::

::: q
$\mathbf u = 2\mathbf i + 5\mathbf j$, $\mathbf v = -3\mathbf i + \mathbf j$. Find $3\mathbf u - 2\mathbf v$ and $\lvert 3\mathbf u - 2\mathbf v \rvert$.
---
$6\mathbf i + 15\mathbf j + 6\mathbf i - 2\mathbf j = 12\mathbf i + 13\mathbf j$. Magnitude $\sqrt{144 + 169} = \sqrt{313}$.
:::

::: q
Find a unit vector in the direction of $5\mathbf i - 12\mathbf j$, and a vector of magnitude 26 in the same direction.
---
$\lvert 5\mathbf i - 12\mathbf j \rvert = 13$. Unit vector $\dfrac{1}{13}(5\mathbf i - 12\mathbf j)$. Magnitude 26: $10\mathbf i - 24\mathbf j$.
:::

::: q
$A = (3, -1)$ and $B = (-1, 5)$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\lvert \overrightarrow{AB} \rvert$ and the position vector of the midpoint of $AB$.
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = -4\mathbf i + 6\mathbf j$; $\lvert \overrightarrow{AB} \rvert = \sqrt{16 + 36} = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13}$; midpoint $\mathbf i + 2\mathbf j$.
:::

::: q
$\mathbf a = 4\mathbf i + \mathbf j$ and $\mathbf b = -2\mathbf i + 7\mathbf j$ are the position vectors of $A$ and $B$. Find the position vector of the point $P$ on $AB$ such that $AP : PB = 2 : 1$.
---
$\overrightarrow{OP} = \dfrac{1 \cdot \mathbf a + 2\mathbf b}{3} = \dfrac{4\mathbf i + \mathbf j - 4\mathbf i + 14\mathbf j}{3} = 5\mathbf j$.
:::

::: q
Show that the points with position vectors $\mathbf i - \mathbf j$, $4\mathbf i + \mathbf j$ and $10\mathbf i + 5\mathbf j$ are collinear.
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = 3\mathbf i + 2\mathbf j$, $\overrightarrow{AC} = 9\mathbf i + 6\mathbf j = 3\overrightarrow{AB}$. Parallel and both through $A$, so collinear.
:::

::: q
The vectors $(k + 1)\mathbf i + 4\mathbf j$ and $3\mathbf i + 2\mathbf j$ are parallel. Find $k$.
---
$(k + 1)\mathbf i + 4\mathbf j = \lambda(3\mathbf i + 2\mathbf j)$: $4 = 2\lambda \Rightarrow \lambda = 2$, so $k + 1 = 6$, $k = 5$.
:::

::: q
$OABC$ is a parallelogram, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf c$. $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$. Find $\overrightarrow{OD}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}$ in terms of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf c$.
---
$\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf a + \mathbf c$; $\overrightarrow{OD} = \mathbf c + \dfrac12\mathbf a$. $\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{OD} - \mathbf a = \mathbf c - \dfrac12\mathbf a$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $G$ is the point on $OM$ with $OG : GM = 2 : 1$. Find $\overrightarrow{OG}$, and show that $\overrightarrow{AG} = \dfrac13(\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a)$.
---
$\overrightarrow{OM} = \dfrac12(\mathbf a + \mathbf b)$; $\overrightarrow{OG} = \dfrac23 \cdot \dfrac12(\mathbf a + \mathbf b) = \dfrac13(\mathbf a + \mathbf b)$.

$\overrightarrow{AG} = \overrightarrow{OG} - \mathbf a = \dfrac13\mathbf a + \dfrac13\mathbf b - \mathbf a = \dfrac13\mathbf b - \dfrac23\mathbf a = \dfrac13(\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a)$. ✓
:::

::: q
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. $P$ is on $OA$ with $OP : PA = 1 : 2$, and $Q$ is the midpoint of $OB$. $AQ$ and $BP$ meet at $X$. Find $\overrightarrow{OX}$.
---
$\overrightarrow{AQ} = \dfrac12\mathbf b - \mathbf a$, so $\overrightarrow{OX} = \mathbf a + \lambda\left(\dfrac12\mathbf b - \mathbf a\right) = (1 - \lambda)\mathbf a + \dfrac\lambda2\mathbf b$.

$\overrightarrow{BP} = \dfrac13\mathbf a - \mathbf b$, so $\overrightarrow{OX} = \mathbf b + \mu\left(\dfrac13\mathbf a - \mathbf b\right) = \dfrac\mu3\mathbf a + (1 - \mu)\mathbf b$.

$\mathbf a$: $1 - \lambda = \dfrac\mu3$; $\mathbf b$: $\dfrac\lambda2 = 1 - \mu$, so $\mu = 1 - \dfrac\lambda2$.

$1 - \lambda = \dfrac13 - \dfrac\lambda6 \Rightarrow 6 - 6\lambda = 2 - \lambda \Rightarrow \lambda = \dfrac45$.

$\overrightarrow{OX} = \dfrac15\mathbf a + \dfrac25\mathbf b$.
:::

::: q
Given $\mathbf a = 2\mathbf i - \mathbf j$ and $\mathbf b = \mathbf i + 3\mathbf j$, find $\lambda$ and $\mu$ such that $\lambda\mathbf a + \mu\mathbf b = 7\mathbf i$.
---
$\mathbf i$: $2\lambda + \mu = 7$; $\mathbf j$: $-\lambda + 3\mu = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda = 3\mu$. So $7\mu = 7$, $\mu = 1$, $\lambda = 3$.
:::

::: q
A particle moves from $A(1, 2)$ to $B(9, 8)$. Find the distance travelled and the unit vector in the direction of motion.
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = 8\mathbf i + 6\mathbf j$; distance $\sqrt{64 + 36} = 10$; unit vector $\dfrac45\mathbf i + \dfrac35\mathbf j$.
:::

::: watch
- **$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a$**: end minus start. Writing $\mathbf a - \mathbf b$ gives the vector pointing the wrong way, and every ratio after it comes out wrong.
- A ratio $m : n$ along $AB$ puts the point $\dfrac{m}{m + n}$ of the way from $A$. For $AP : PB = 1 : 2$ that is one **third**, not one half.
- To prove collinear you need **two** things: parallel (a scalar multiple) **and** a shared point. Write both.
- In "find $\lambda$ and $\mu$" questions, write the position vector of the meeting point along **both** lines, then compare the $\mathbf a$ coefficients and the $\mathbf b$ coefficients separately. Two equations, two unknowns.
- Column and $\mathbf i$, $\mathbf j$ forms are interchangeable, but give the answer in the form the question used.
- Magnitude is a **length** — it needs the square root. $\lvert 3\mathbf i + 4\mathbf j \rvert = 5$, not $25$.
:::

::: test
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $\overrightarrow{OM}$ and $\overrightarrow{CM}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c}$. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{c}$, so $\overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf{a} + \dfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{c}$ (2); $\overrightarrow{CM} = \overrightarrow{OM} - \overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{a} - \dfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{c}$ (1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $P$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $2 : 1$ and $Q$ is the midpoint of $OB$. Find $\overrightarrow{PQ}$. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{a} + \dfrac{2}{3}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \dfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{a} + \dfrac{2}{3}\mathbf{b}$ (1); $\overrightarrow{OQ} = \dfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{b}$ (1); $\overrightarrow{PQ} = -\dfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{a} - \dfrac{1}{6}\mathbf{b}$ (1).
:::

::: test
$\overrightarrow{OA} = 2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = 8\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}$. The point $X$ lies on $AB$ with $AX : XB = 1 : 2$. Find $\overrightarrow{OX}$. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = 6\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}$ (1); $\overrightarrow{AX} = 2\mathbf{i} - \dfrac{2}{3}\mathbf{j}$ (1); $\overrightarrow{OX} = 4\mathbf{i} + \dfrac{7}{3}\mathbf{j}$ (1).
:::

$\dfrac{1}{13}(5\mathbf{i} + 12\mathbf{j})$.
:::

::: test
A force of 10 N acts at 30° above the direction of $\mathbf{i}$. Write it in the form $a\mathbf{i} + b\mathbf{j}$, giving exact values. (2)
---
$10\cos 30°\,\mathbf{i} + 10\sin 30°\,\mathbf{j}$ (1) $= 5\sqrt{3}\,\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$ (1).

::: test
A particle starts at the point with position vector $\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j}$ and moves with constant velocity $(2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j})$ m/s. Find its position vector after $t$ seconds, and the time at which it lies on the line $y = x$. (3)
---
$\mathbf{r} = (1 + 2t)\mathbf{i} + (-1 + 3t)\mathbf{j}$ (1). $1 + 2t = -1 + 3t$ (1). $t = 2$ s (1).
:::

::: test
$ABCD$ is any quadrilateral, and $P$, $Q$, $R$, $S$ are the midpoints of $AB$, $BC$, $CD$ and $DA$. Using position vectors $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{c}$, $\mathbf{d}$, prove that $PQRS$ is a parallelogram. (5)
---
$\mathbf{p} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$, $\mathbf{q} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c})$, $\mathbf{r} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{c} + \mathbf{d})$, $\mathbf{s} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{d} + \mathbf{a})$ (1). $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a})$ (1). $\overrightarrow{SR} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a})$ (1). $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{SR}$, so $PQ$ and $SR$ are parallel and equal in length (1). A quadrilateral with one pair of sides equal and parallel is a parallelogram (1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $C$ is on $OA$ with $\overrightarrow{OC} = \frac{2}{3}\mathbf{a}$, and $D$ is the midpoint of $AB$. The lines $OD$ and $BC$ meet at $E$. Find the ratios $OE : ED$ and $BE : EC$. (8)
---
$\overrightarrow{OD} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$ (1). $\overrightarrow{OE} = \lambda \cdot \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$ (1). $\overrightarrow{OE} = \mathbf{b} + \mu\left(\frac{2}{3}\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\right)$ (1). Comparing $\mathbf{a}$: $\frac{\lambda}{2} = \frac{2\mu}{3}$; comparing $\mathbf{b}$: $\frac{\lambda}{2} = 1 - \mu$ (1). $\mu = \frac{3}{5}$ (1), $\lambda = \frac{4}{5}$ (1). $OE : ED = 4 : 1$ (1). $BE : EC = 3 : 2$ (1).
:::
