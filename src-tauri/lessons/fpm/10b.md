## What it is

Right-angled triangles are SOH CAH TOA and Pythagoras. For **any** triangle — label the angles $A$, $B$, $C$ and the sides opposite them $a$, $b$, $c$ — three formulae do everything:

**Sine rule:** $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$ — for a side; flip it upside down for an angle.

**Cosine rule:** $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ — for a side when you know the other two sides and the angle between them; rearranged, $\cos A = \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$, for an angle when you know all three sides. (This one is on the formula sheet; the others are not.)

**Area:** $\text{Area} = \tfrac12 ab\sin C$ — two sides and the angle between them.

**Three dimensions** adds nothing new in formulae, only in seeing. A cuboid, a pyramid, a wedge: the question is always about a right-angled triangle hiding inside the solid, or the angle a line makes with a face, or the angle between two faces. The skill is drawing the right triangle flat on the page.

- The **angle between a line and a plane** is the angle between the line and its **projection** (shadow) on the plane — drop a perpendicular from the top of the line to the plane, and the angle is at the bottom end of the line.
- The **angle between two planes** is measured along the line where they meet, using two lines, one in each plane, both perpendicular to that common edge.

::: terms
- **Sine rule**: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C.
- **Cosine rule**: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A.
- **Area of a triangle**: ½ab sin C.
- **Ambiguous case**: using the sine rule to find an angle may give two possible triangles, because sin θ = sin(180° − θ).
- **Angle between a line and a plane**: the angle between the line and its projection on the plane.
- **Angle between two planes**: the angle between two lines, one in each plane, both perpendicular to their line of intersection.
:::

::: method
1. **Label the triangle** with $A$, $B$, $C$ and $a$, $b$, $c$ (side $a$ opposite angle $A$). Write down what you know and what you want.
2. **Choose the rule.** Know a pair (a side and the angle opposite it)? **Sine rule.** Know two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides? **Cosine rule.** Area? $\frac12 ab\sin C$.
3. **Sine rule for an angle — check for the ambiguous case.** $\sin$ gives two possible angles, $\theta$ and $180° - \theta$. If the question says the angle is obtuse, or the second value still leaves a valid triangle (angle sum under $180°$), you must give it or explain why it is rejected.
4. **Bearings and word problems:** draw the diagram, put every angle you can on it (angles on a straight line, alternate angles from parallel north lines), then it is a plain triangle.
5. **3D:** identify the triangle you need and **redraw it on its own**, flat, with its right angle if it has one. Often you need two triangles: one to find a length (usually with Pythagoras on the base), the second to find the angle.
6. **Angle between line and plane:** the line, its projection onto the plane, and the perpendicular height form a right-angled triangle; the angle is opposite the height, so $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{projection}}$.
7. **Angle between two planes:** find the common edge; find the midpoint or foot of the perpendicular on that edge; draw the two perpendiculars (one in each plane) meeting there; the angle between them is the angle you want.
:::

::: example Sine rule for a side
In triangle $ABC$, $A = 40°$, $B = 75°$ and $a = 8$ cm. Find $b$.

$\dfrac{b}{\sin 75°} = \dfrac{8}{\sin 40°} \Rightarrow b = \dfrac{8\sin 75°}{\sin 40°} = 12.0$ cm (3 s.f.).
:::

::: example Cosine rule for a side, then the area
In triangle $PQR$, $PQ = 7$ cm, $PR = 9$ cm and angle $P = 55°$. Find $QR$ and the area of the triangle.

$QR^2 = 7^2 + 9^2 - 2 \times 7 \times 9 \times \cos 55° = 49 + 81 - 126\cos 55° = 130 - 72.27 = 57.73$, so $QR = 7.60$ cm.

Area $= \dfrac12 \times 7 \times 9 \times \sin 55° = 25.8$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example Cosine rule for an angle
A triangle has sides $5$, $7$ and $10$. Find the largest angle.

The largest angle is opposite the longest side: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{5^2 + 7^2 - 10^2}{2 \times 5 \times 7} = \dfrac{25 + 49 - 100}{70} = -\dfrac{26}{70}$.

$\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{26}{70}\right) = 111.8°$. (Negative cosine — obtuse — as expected for the largest angle of a triangle where $10^2 > 5^2 + 7^2$.)
:::

::: example The ambiguous case
In triangle $ABC$, $a = 6$, $b = 9$ and $A = 35°$. Find the two possible values of $B$.

$\dfrac{\sin B}{9} = \dfrac{\sin 35°}{6} \Rightarrow \sin B = \dfrac{9\sin 35°}{6} = 0.8604$.

$B = 59.4°$ or $B = 180° - 59.4° = 120.6°$. Both are possible: $35° + 120.6° < 180°$, so the obtuse one still leaves room for angle $C$.
:::

::: example Angle between a line and a plane
$ABCDEFGH$ is a cuboid with $AB = 8$, $BC = 6$ and $CG = 5$ (the vertical edges are $AE$, $BF$, $CG$, $DH$). Find the angle the diagonal $AG$ makes with the base $ABCD$.

The projection of $AG$ onto the base is $AC$. In the base, $AC = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$.

Triangle $ACG$ is right-angled at $C$: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{CG}{AC} = \dfrac{5}{10}$, so $\theta = 26.6°$.
:::

::: example Angle between two planes
A square-based pyramid $VABCD$ has base side $10$ cm and vertex $V$ vertically above the centre $O$ of the base, with $VO = 12$ cm. Find the angle between the face $VBC$ and the base.

The common edge is $BC$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$; $OM \perp BC$ (in the base) and $VM \perp BC$ (in the face — the face is isosceles). So the angle is $\angle VMO$.

$OM = 5$ (half the base side). In triangle $VOM$, right-angled at $O$: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{VO}{OM} = \dfrac{12}{5}$, so $\theta = 67.4°$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $B = 62°$, $C = 48°$ and $b = 11$ cm. Find $c$.
---
$c = \dfrac{11\sin 48°}{\sin 62°} = 9.26$ cm.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $a = 12$, $c = 8$ and $C = 38°$. Find angle $A$, given that it is acute.
---
$\sin A = \dfrac{12\sin 38°}{8} = 0.9235$, $A = 67.4°$.
:::

::: q
Find the third side of a triangle with sides $6$ cm and $10$ cm and included angle $120°$.
---
$x^2 = 36 + 100 - 120\cos 120° = 136 + 60 = 196$, $x = 14$ cm.
:::

::: q
A triangle has sides $8$, $11$ and $15$. Find its smallest angle.
---
Opposite the shortest side: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{11^2 + 15^2 - 8^2}{2 \times 11 \times 15} = \dfrac{282}{330}$, $\theta = 31.3°$.
:::

::: q
Find the area of a triangle with sides $9$ cm and $13$ cm and included angle $70°$.
---
$\dfrac12 \times 9 \times 13 \times \sin 70° = 55.0$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A triangle has area $30$ cm$^2$, and two of its sides are $8$ cm and $10$ cm. Find the two possible values of the angle between them.
---
$\dfrac12 \times 80 \sin\theta = 30 \Rightarrow \sin\theta = 0.75 \Rightarrow \theta = 48.6°$ or $131.4°$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $a = 5$, $b = 7$, $A = 40°$. Show that there are two possible triangles and find both values of $C$.
---
$\sin B = \dfrac{7\sin 40°}{5} = 0.900$, $B = 64.2°$ or $115.8°$. Both leave a positive $C$: $C = 180 - 40 - 64.2 = 75.8°$ or $C = 180 - 40 - 115.8 = 24.2°$.
:::

::: q
A ship sails $10$ km on a bearing of $050°$, then $8$ km on a bearing of $120°$. How far is it from its start?
---
The angle between the two legs, inside the triangle, is $180° - (120° - 50°) = 110°$.

$d^2 = 100 + 64 - 160\cos 110° = 164 + 54.7 = 218.7$, $d = 14.8$ km.
:::

::: q
A cuboid has base $12$ by $5$ and height $6$. Find the length of the longest diagonal and the angle it makes with the base.
---
Base diagonal $\sqrt{144 + 25} = 13$; space diagonal $\sqrt{169 + 36} = \sqrt{205} = 14.3$.

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{6}{13}$, $\theta = 24.8°$.
:::

::: q
A square-based pyramid has base side $8$ cm and slant edges of length $9$ cm. Find its height, and the angle a slant edge makes with the base.
---
Half the base diagonal: $\dfrac{8\sqrt2}{2} = 4\sqrt2 = 5.657$. Height $= \sqrt{81 - 32} = \sqrt{49} = 7$ cm.

$\cos\theta = \dfrac{4\sqrt2}{9}$ (or $\tan\theta = \dfrac{7}{4\sqrt2}$): $\theta = 51.1°$.
:::

::: q
In the pyramid of the previous question, find the angle between a triangular face and the base.
---
Foot of the perpendicular from the apex to a base edge is the edge's midpoint, $4$ cm from the centre. $\tan\theta = \dfrac74$, $\theta = 60.3°$.
:::

::: q
A wedge $ABCDEF$ has a horizontal rectangular base $ABCD$ with $AB = 10$ and $BC = 6$; the rectangular face $CDEF$ is vertical with $DE = CF = 4$; the sloping face is $ABFE$. Find the angle between the sloping face $ABFE$ and the base.
---
The common edge is $AB$. $BC$ is perpendicular to $AB$ in the base; $BF$ is perpendicular to $AB$ in the sloping face. So the angle is $\angle CBF$ in the vertical right-angled triangle $BCF$: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{CF}{BC} = \dfrac46$, $\theta = 33.7°$.
:::

::: watch
- **Side opposite angle.** $a$ is opposite $A$. If you pair a side with the wrong angle the sine rule gives a confident wrong answer.
- Sine rule for an angle: **check the second solution** $180° - \theta$. If the question says "acute", reject it and say so; if not, both may be valid.
- Cosine rule: the angle in the formula is the one **between** the two sides you multiply. $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ — the $A$ goes with the $a$ on the left.
- $\cos 120°$ is **negative**, so "$- 2bc\cos A$" becomes a **plus**. Don't lose the sign.
- Keep full calculator accuracy until the end; round only the final answer (3 s.f. unless told otherwise, angles to 1 d.p.).
- 3D: **redraw the triangle flat**, label its right angle, then it is GCSE trig. Angle with a plane goes to the **projection**; angle between planes is measured **perpendicular to the common edge** — never along a slant edge.
:::

The face's slant height meets the base at the midpoint of an edge, 3 cm from the centre (1); $\tan\theta = \dfrac{8}{3}$ (1); $\theta = 69.4^\circ$ (1).

---
The angle between the two legs is $90^\circ$ (1); $d^2 = 25 + 49$ (1); $d = 8.60$ km (1).
