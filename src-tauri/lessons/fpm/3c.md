## What it is

An inequality in two variables, like $2x + y \le 8$, is a **region** of the plane — everything on one side of the line $2x + y = 8$. Several inequalities together mark out a shape (the **feasible region**), and questions ask you to draw it, name a point inside it, or find the point in it that makes some quantity — cost, profit, time — as large or as small as possible. That last part is **linear programming**, and in Further Maths it is kept simple: a few straight lines, one objective, read the best vertex.

The whole topic is drawing lines carefully and shading the right side.

::: terms
- **Region**: the set of points satisfying a set of inequalities in x and y.
- **Boundary line**: drawn solid for ≤ or ≥, broken for < or >.
- **Feasible region**: the region satisfying all the constraints in a problem.
- **Objective function**: the quantity to be maximised or minimised, such as profit P = 3x + 5y.
- **Optimal point**: the maximum or minimum of the objective function is found at a vertex of the feasible region.
:::

::: method
1. **Draw each boundary line.** Rewrite the inequality as an equation, find where it crosses the axes (put $x = 0$, then $y = 0$), and draw the line through those two points. Use a **solid** line for $\le$ or $\ge$, a **dashed** line for $<$ or $>$.
2. **Decide which side.** Test a point not on the line — the origin $(0, 0)$ if the line does not pass through it. If the test point satisfies the inequality, the region is on that side; if not, it is the other side.
3. **Shade consistently.** Exam papers usually say "shade the region that satisfies all the inequalities" or "shade the unwanted regions" — read which. Label the wanted region $R$.
4. **Objective function:** to maximise (or minimise) something like $P = 3x + 2y$ over the region, evaluate $P$ at each **vertex** (corner) of the feasible region. The best value is always at a vertex. Find the vertices by solving pairs of boundary equations.
5. **Integer answers:** if the question involves whole objects (tables, cars, people), the best point must have integer coordinates. Check the integer points nearest the best vertex if it is not itself an integer point.
:::

::: example Drawing a region
Show, on a diagram, the region defined by $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$, $x + y \le 6$ and $y \le 2x$.

$x \ge 0$ and $y \ge 0$: the first quadrant.

$x + y = 6$ crosses the axes at $(6, 0)$ and $(0, 6)$. Test $(0, 0)$: $0 \le 6$ is true, so the origin side (below the line) is wanted.

$y = 2x$ passes through $(0, 0)$ and $(3, 6)$. Test $(1, 0)$: $0 \le 2$ true, so the side containing $(1, 0)$ — below the line — is wanted.

The region is the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(6, 0)$ and where $x + y = 6$ meets $y = 2x$: $3x = 6$, $x = 2$, $y = 4$, so $(2, 4)$.
:::

::: example Writing inequalities from a diagram
A region is bounded by the $x$-axis, the line $x = 4$, and the line through $(0, 2)$ and $(4, 6)$, and lies below that line. Write down the three inequalities.

The line through $(0, 2)$ and $(4, 6)$ has gradient $\dfrac{6 - 2}{4 - 0} = 1$, so it is $y = x + 2$.

Region: $y \ge 0$, $x \le 4$, $y \le x + 2$.
:::

::: example A linear programming problem
A workshop makes chairs ($x$) and tables ($y$). Each chair takes 2 hours of cutting and 1 hour of finishing; each table takes 1 hour of cutting and 3 hours of finishing. There are 16 hours of cutting and 18 hours of finishing available. Profit is £10 per chair and £15 per table. How many of each should be made to maximise profit?

Constraints: $2x + y \le 16$ (cutting), $x + 3y \le 18$ (finishing), $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$.

Vertices of the region: $(0, 0)$; $(8, 0)$ where $2x + y = 16$ meets the $x$-axis; $(0, 6)$ where $x + 3y = 18$ meets the $y$-axis; and the intersection of the two lines: from the first $y = 16 - 2x$, so $x + 3(16 - 2x) = 18$, $x + 48 - 6x = 18$, $5x = 30$, $x = 6$, $y = 4$, giving $(6, 4)$.

$P = 10x + 15y$: at $(0,0)$: $0$; at $(8, 0)$: $80$; at $(0, 6)$: $90$; at $(6, 4)$: $60 + 60 = 120$.

Maximum profit £120 from 6 chairs and 4 tables.
:::

::: example Minimising
Minimise $C = 4x + 3y$ subject to $x + y \ge 5$, $2x + y \ge 8$, $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$.

The region is above both lines. Vertices: $(0, 8)$ (on $2x + y = 8$, the $y$-axis), $(5, 0)$ (on $x + y = 5$, the $x$-axis), and the intersection: subtracting, $x = 3$, $y = 2$, so $(3, 2)$.

$C$: at $(0, 8)$: $24$; at $(5, 0)$: $20$; at $(3, 2)$: $12 + 6 = 18$.

Minimum $C = 18$ at $(3, 2)$.
:::

::: q
Draw the region satisfying $x \ge 1$, $y \ge 0$ and $x + 2y \le 8$, and state its vertices.
---
$x + 2y = 8$ meets $y = 0$ at $(8, 0)$ and $x = 1$ at $y = \frac72$, i.e. $(1, 3.5)$. The third vertex is $(1, 0)$.

Vertices: $(1, 0)$, $(8, 0)$, $(1, 3.5)$. The region is below $x + 2y = 8$ (test $(2, 0)$: $2 \le 8$ ✓).
:::

::: q
Write down the three inequalities that define the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(4, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$.
---
$x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$, and the line through $(4, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$: $\dfrac{x}{4} + \dfrac{y}{3} = 1$, i.e. $3x + 4y = 12$. The origin is inside, so $3x + 4y \le 12$.
:::

::: q
The region $R$ is defined by $y \le 2x + 1$, $y \ge x - 1$ and $x \le 3$. Find the coordinates of its vertices.
---
$y = 2x + 1$ meets $y = x - 1$: $2x + 1 = x - 1$, $x = -2$, $y = -3$: $(-2, -3)$.

$x = 3$ meets $y = 2x + 1$ at $(3, 7)$ and $y = x - 1$ at $(3, 2)$.

Vertices: $(-2, -3)$, $(3, 7)$, $(3, 2)$.
:::

::: q
Which of the points $(2, 3)$, $(4, 1)$ and $(1, 5)$ lie in the region $x + y \le 6$, $y \ge 2$, $y \le 3x$?
---
$(2, 3)$: $5 \le 6$ ✓, $3 \ge 2$ ✓, $3 \le 6$ ✓ — inside.

$(4, 1)$: $1 \ge 2$ ✗ — outside.

$(1, 5)$: $6 \le 6$ ✓, $5 \ge 2$ ✓, $5 \le 3$ ✗ — outside.

Only $(2, 3)$.
:::

::: q
Maximise $P = 2x + 5y$ subject to $x + y \le 7$, $x + 2y \le 10$, $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$.
---
Vertices: $(0, 0)$, $(7, 0)$, $(0, 5)$, and the intersection: subtracting, $y = 3$, $x = 4$: $(4, 3)$.

$P$: $0$, $14$, $25$, $8 + 15 = 23$. Maximum $P = 25$ at $(0, 5)$.
:::

::: q
A farmer plants $x$ hectares of wheat and $y$ hectares of barley on at most 20 hectares. Wheat needs 3 days' labour per hectare and barley 2 days, with 48 days available. Profit is £300 per hectare of wheat and £250 per hectare of barley. Find the planting that maximises profit, and the profit.
---
$x + y \le 20$, $3x + 2y \le 48$, $x, y \ge 0$.

Vertices: $(0, 0)$, $(16, 0)$, $(0, 20)$, and the intersection: $y = 20 - x$, so $3x + 40 - 2x = 48$, $x = 8$, $y = 12$: $(8, 12)$.

$P = 300x + 250y$: $0$; $4800$; $5000$; $2400 + 3000 = 5400$.

8 ha wheat, 12 ha barley, profit £5400.
:::

::: q
Minimise $C = 5x + 4y$ subject to $x + y \ge 6$, $x + 2y \ge 8$, $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$.
---
Vertices of the (unbounded, upper) region: $(0, 6)$, $(8, 0)$ and the intersection: subtracting, $y = 2$, $x = 4$: $(4, 2)$.

$C$: $24$; $40$; $20 + 8 = 28$. Minimum $C = 24$ at $(0, 6)$.
:::

::: q
A region is bounded by $y \ge 0$, $y \le 4$, $y \le 2x$ and $x + y \le 8$. Find the vertex at which $x + 3y$ is greatest.
---
Vertices: $(0, 0)$; $(2, 4)$ where $y = 2x$ meets $y = 4$; $(4, 4)$ where $y = 4$ meets $x + y = 8$; $(8, 0)$.

$x + 3y$: $0$, $14$, $16$, $8$. Greatest at $(4, 4)$.
:::

::: watch
- **Solid line for $\le$ and $\ge$, dashed for $<$ and $>$.** It is a mark on its own.
- Test a point. Guessing the side from the direction of the inequality sign goes wrong the moment there is a negative coefficient.
- Read whether to shade the **wanted** or the **unwanted** region — papers do both. Label the region $R$ either way.
- The best value of a linear objective is **always at a vertex**. Find every vertex, evaluate at each, and show the working — the evaluations are the method marks.
- Vertices come from solving two boundary **equations** simultaneously. Write the pair down; do not read them off a rough sketch.
- If the answer must be whole numbers (chairs, hectares in whole units), say so and check the integer points near the vertex.
:::

$(1, 1)$ (1); $(1, 5)$ (1); $(3, 3)$ (1).
:::

Minimise $C = 2x + 3y$ subject to $x + y \ge 4$, $2x + y \ge 6$, $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$. (4)
---
Vertices of the feasible region: $(0, 6)$, $(2, 2)$, $(4, 0)$ (2); $C = 18, 10, 8$ (1); minimum $8$ at $(4, 0)$ (1).

::: test
Does the point $(2, 4)$ lie in the region $y \le 3x - 1$, $x + y \le 7$? Show your check. (1)
---
$4 \le 5$ and $6 \le 7$ — both hold, so yes.

::: test
A bakery makes $x$ loaves and $y$ cakes a day. Flour limits them to $2x + 3y \le 60$ and oven time to $x + y \le 25$. Profit is £3 per loaf and £4 per cake. Find the numbers that maximise the profit, and the profit. (4)
---
Feasible vertices $(0, 20)$, $(15, 10)$, $(25, 0)$ (2); profits £80, £85, £75 (1); 15 loaves and 10 cakes, £85 (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why the maximum of a linear objective function over a bounded feasible region always occurs at a vertex. (1)
---
Lines of constant value of the objective are parallel straight lines; sliding one across a convex polygon, the last point of contact is a corner (or a whole edge, which includes its corners).
:::

::: test
Maximise $P = 5x + 3y$ subject to $3x + 2y \le 12$, where $x$ and $y$ are non-negative integers. (3)
---
Integer points near the boundary: $(4, 0)$ gives 20, $(2, 3)$ gives 19, $(3, 1)$ gives 18, $(0, 6)$ gives 18 (2); maximum $20$ at $(4, 0)$ (1).
:::
