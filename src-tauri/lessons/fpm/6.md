## What it is

The binomial series expands $(1 + x)^n$ without multiplying out brackets:

$$(1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!}x^3 + \dots$$

When $n$ is a **positive integer**, the series stops after $n + 1$ terms (the coefficients are the row of Pascal's triangle, $\binom{n}{r}$), and it is true for every $x$.

When $n$ is a **fraction or negative**, the series never stops, and it is only true — "valid" — when $\lvert x \rvert < 1$. That is what lets you expand $\sqrt{1 + x}$ or $\dfrac{1}{(1 + x)^2}$ as a polynomial, and use the first few terms as an approximation.

For $(a + bx)^n$ you take out $a$: $(a + bx)^n = a^n\left(1 + \dfrac{b}{a}x\right)^n$, expand the bracket, and the validity becomes $\left\lvert \dfrac{b}{a}x \right\rvert < 1$.

::: method
1. **Write the formula with your $n$** substituted in, keeping the fractions unsimplified until the end: $n$, $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$, $\dfrac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}$.
2. **Replace $x$** by whatever is actually in the bracket — if it is $(1 - 2x)^n$, every $x$ becomes $(-2x)$, **in brackets**, then simplify the powers: $(-2x)^2 = 4x^2$, $(-2x)^3 = -8x^3$.
3. **Positive integer $n$:** you can use $\binom{n}{r}$ or Pascal's triangle instead; the two are the same thing. "Ascending powers of $x$" means start with the constant; "the coefficient of $x^3$" means the number in front, with its sign.
4. **Fractional or negative $n$:** give the number of terms asked for ("up to and including $x^3$"), then **state the validity**: $\lvert x \rvert < 1$ for $(1 + x)^n$; for $(1 + kx)^n$ it is $\lvert kx \rvert < 1$, i.e. $\lvert x \rvert < \dfrac{1}{\lvert k \rvert}$.
5. **$(a + bx)^n$:** factor out $a^n$ first. Do not try to expand $(2 + x)^{1/2}$ directly.
6. **Approximations:** choose the $x$ that makes $(1 + x)^n$ equal to the number you want — e.g. $\sqrt{1.02}$ is $(1 + x)^{1/2}$ with $x = 0.02$ — substitute into your expansion, and say how many decimal places you trust.
7. **Products:** for $(1 + 2x)(1 - x)^{-2}$, expand the awkward factor, then multiply through and collect only the powers you need.
:::

::: example Positive integer $n$
Expand $(1 + 2x)^5$ in ascending powers of $x$ up to and including the term in $x^3$.

$n = 5$: coefficients $1, 5, 10, 10, \dots$

$(1 + 2x)^5 = 1 + 5(2x) + 10(2x)^2 + 10(2x)^3 + \dots = 1 + 10x + 40x^2 + 80x^3 + \dots$
:::

::: example Taking out a factor
Find the first three terms of $(2 - x)^6$ in ascending powers of $x$.

$(2 - x)^6 = 2^6\left(1 - \dfrac{x}{2}\right)^6 = 64\left(1 + 6\left(-\dfrac x2\right) + 15\left(-\dfrac x2\right)^2 + \dots\right)$

$= 64\left(1 - 3x + \dfrac{15}{4}x^2 + \dots\right) = 64 - 192x + 240x^2 + \dots$
:::

::: example Fractional $n$, with validity
Expand $(1 + x)^{1/2}$ up to the term in $x^3$ and state the values of $x$ for which it is valid.

$n = \frac12$: $\quad 1 + \frac12 x + \dfrac{\frac12 \cdot (-\frac12)}{2}x^2 + \dfrac{\frac12 \cdot (-\frac12) \cdot (-\frac32)}{6}x^3 + \dots$

$= 1 + \dfrac12 x - \dfrac18 x^2 + \dfrac{1}{16}x^3 + \dots$, valid for $\lvert x \rvert < 1$.
:::

::: example Negative $n$ and a multiple of $x$
Expand $(1 - 3x)^{-2}$ up to and including $x^3$, and state the range of validity.

$n = -2$: $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2} = \dfrac{(-2)(-3)}{2} = 3$, $\quad \dfrac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6} = \dfrac{(-2)(-3)(-4)}{6} = -4$.

$(1 - 3x)^{-2} = 1 + (-2)(-3x) + 3(-3x)^2 + (-4)(-3x)^3 + \dots = 1 + 6x + 27x^2 + 108x^3 + \dots$

Valid for $\lvert 3x \rvert < 1$, i.e. $\lvert x \rvert < \dfrac13$.
:::

::: example Using the series to approximate
Use the expansion of $(1 + x)^{1/2}$ to estimate $\sqrt{1.04}$ to 4 decimal places.

$x = 0.04$: $1 + 0.02 - \dfrac{0.0016}{8} + \dfrac{0.000064}{16} = 1 + 0.02 - 0.0002 + 0.000004 = 1.019804$.

$\sqrt{1.04} \approx 1.0198$.
:::

::: example A product of expansions
Find the coefficient of $x^2$ in $(1 + 2x)(1 + x)^{-3}$.

$(1 + x)^{-3} = 1 - 3x + \dfrac{(-3)(-4)}{2}x^2 + \dots = 1 - 3x + 6x^2 + \dots$

$(1 + 2x)(1 - 3x + 6x^2 + \dots)$: the $x^2$ terms are $1 \times 6x^2$ and $2x \times (-3x) = -6x^2$. Coefficient: $6 - 6 = 0$.
:::

::: q
Expand $(1 + 3x)^4$ fully.
---
$1 + 4(3x) + 6(3x)^2 + 4(3x)^3 + (3x)^4 = 1 + 12x + 54x^2 + 108x^3 + 81x^4$.
:::

::: q
Find the coefficient of $x^3$ in the expansion of $(1 - 2x)^7$.
---
$\binom73(-2x)^3 = 35 \times (-8)x^3 = -280x^3$. Coefficient $-280$.
:::

::: q
Find the first three terms in the expansion of $(3 + 2x)^5$ in ascending powers of $x$.
---
$3^5\left(1 + \dfrac{2x}{3}\right)^5 = 243\left(1 + 5 \cdot \dfrac{2x}{3} + 10 \cdot \dfrac{4x^2}{9} + \dots\right) = 243 + 810x + 1080x^2 + \dots$
:::

::: q
Expand $(1 + x)^{-1}$ up to and including $x^3$ and state when the expansion is valid.
---
$1 - x + x^2 - x^3 + \dots$ (the coefficients $\frac{(-1)(-2)}{2} = 1$, $\frac{(-1)(-2)(-3)}{6} = -1$). Valid for $\lvert x \rvert < 1$.
:::

::: q
Expand $(1 + 4x)^{1/2}$ up to and including $x^2$ and state the validity.
---
$1 + \frac12(4x) + \dfrac{\frac12(-\frac12)}{2}(4x)^2 = 1 + 2x - \dfrac18 \cdot 16x^2 = 1 + 2x - 2x^2 + \dots$

Valid for $\lvert 4x \rvert < 1$, i.e. $\lvert x \rvert < \dfrac14$.
:::

::: q
Expand $(1 - x)^{-3}$ up to and including $x^3$.
---
$n = -3$: coefficients $-3$, $\frac{(-3)(-4)}{2} = 6$, $\frac{(-3)(-4)(-5)}{6} = -10$.

$1 + (-3)(-x) + 6(-x)^2 + (-10)(-x)^3 = 1 + 3x + 6x^2 + 10x^3 + \dots$
:::

::: q
Expand $(4 + x)^{1/2}$ up to the term in $x^2$, and state the range of $x$ for which it is valid.
---
$(4 + x)^{1/2} = 2\left(1 + \dfrac x4\right)^{1/2} = 2\left(1 + \dfrac x8 - \dfrac18 \cdot \dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dots\right) = 2 + \dfrac x4 - \dfrac{x^2}{64} + \dots$

Valid for $\left\lvert \dfrac x4 \right\rvert < 1$, i.e. $\lvert x \rvert < 4$.
:::

::: q
Use the expansion of $(1 - 2x)^{1/3}$ up to $x^2$ to estimate $\sqrt[3]{0.98}$ to 5 decimal places.
---
$(1 - 2x)^{1/3} = 1 + \frac13(-2x) + \dfrac{\frac13(-\frac23)}{2}(-2x)^2 = 1 - \dfrac{2x}{3} - \dfrac{4x^2}{9} + \dots$

$1 - 2x = 0.98 \Rightarrow x = 0.01$: $1 - 0.006667 - 0.0000444 = 0.99329$ (5 d.p.).
:::

::: q
Find the coefficient of $x^2$ in the expansion of $(1 - x)(1 + 2x)^{-2}$.
---
$(1 + 2x)^{-2} = 1 - 4x + \dfrac{(-2)(-3)}{2}(2x)^2 + \dots = 1 - 4x + 12x^2 + \dots$

$x^2$ terms of the product: $1 \times 12x^2 + (-x)(-4x) = 16x^2$. Coefficient $16$.
:::

::: q
In the expansion of $(1 + kx)^6$ the coefficient of $x^2$ is $60$. Find the possible values of $k$.
---
$\binom62 k^2 = 15k^2 = 60 \Rightarrow k^2 = 4 \Rightarrow k = \pm 2$.
:::

::: q
Given that $(1 + ax)^n = 1 + 12x + 54x^2 + \dots$, find $a$ and $n$.
---
$na = 12$ and $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}a^2 = 54$. From the first, $a = \frac{12}{n}$; substitute: $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2} \cdot \dfrac{144}{n^2} = 54 \Rightarrow \dfrac{72(n-1)}{n} = 54 \Rightarrow 72n - 72 = 54n \Rightarrow n = 4$, $a = 3$.
:::

::: watch
- **Brackets round the whole $x$-term.** $(-2x)^2 = 4x^2$; write $-2x^2$ and the rest is wrong.
- **State the validity** whenever $n$ is not a positive integer — and adjust it for the multiple of $x$: $(1 - 3x)^{-2}$ is valid for $\lvert x \rvert < \frac13$, not $< 1$.
- $(2 + x)^n$ is **not** $2^n + x^n$ and is not $(1 + x)^n$ with a 2 in front. Take $2^n$ out and expand $\left(1 + \frac x2\right)^n$.
- Keep the coefficients as exact fractions; don't decimalise $\frac{1}{16}$.
- "The coefficient of $x^3$" is a number (with its sign) — not the whole term $-280x^3$.
- When $n$ is a negative integer, $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$ has two negatives multiplied: $(-2)(-3)/2 = +3$. Track the signs one at a time.
:::
