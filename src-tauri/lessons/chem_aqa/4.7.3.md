## What it is

**Separate science only:** the whole of AQA 4.7.3 (synthetic and naturally occurring polymers) is **(chemistry only)**. It is in GCSE Chemistry 8462 but **not** in Combined Science: Trilogy 8464. **Condensation polymerisation** and **amino acids** are **(Higher tier)** only; addition polymerisation and DNA and other natural polymers are for both tiers.

A **polymer** is a very large molecule made when many small molecules, called **monomers**, join together in a long chain. The joining reaction is **polymerisation**. There are two types at GCSE:

| | Addition polymerisation | Condensation polymerisation (Higher tier) |
|---|---|---|
| Monomers | alkenes: contain a **C=C** | have **two functional groups** each |
| Number of types of monomer | usually one | usually **two different** monomers (or one, like an amino acid, with two different groups) |
| Other product | **none**: the polymer is the only product | a **small molecule**, usually **water**, for each link made |
| Repeating unit | same atoms as the monomer | has fewer atoms than the monomers put together |
| Examples | poly(ethene), poly(propene) | polyesters, polypeptides and proteins |

### Addition polymerisation

**Alkenes** can be used to make polymers such as **poly(ethene)** and **poly(propene)** by **addition polymerisation**. Many monomer molecules join: the **C=C double bond in each monomer opens** and the carbons link to the neighbouring monomers, forming a long chain of carbon atoms joined by **single** bonds.

n CH₂=CH₂ → –(CH₂–CH₂)ₙ–

**Because no other molecule is formed, the repeating unit has exactly the same atoms as the monomer.** Poly(ethene)'s repeating unit is C₂H₄, just like ethene; the only change is that the double bond has become a single bond with two "spare" bonds that link to the next units.

**Drawing the polymer from a given monomer (the skill AQA tests):**

1. Draw the monomer's displayed formula with the two carbons of the C=C in a row.
2. Change the **C=C to C–C**.
3. Keep every other atom or group **in the same place** (H, CH₃, Cl, or whatever is attached).
4. Draw a bond **out of each side** of the two carbons, passing **through** a bracket.
5. Write **n** outside the bottom right bracket, and **n** in front of the monomer in the equation.

**Going backwards** (repeating unit → monomer): take the two carbons in the chain inside the brackets, put a **double bond** between them, remove the trailing bonds, keep the side atoms. The monomer is named by removing "poly" and the brackets: poly(chloroethene) comes from **chloroethene**, CH₂=CHCl.

**Recognising** addition polymers and monomers: a monomer for addition polymerisation **must contain a C=C**. An addition polymer has a **backbone of carbon atoms only**, with no O or N in the chain. The name is **poly(monomer name)**.

| Monomer | Polymer | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ethene, CH₂=CH₂ | poly(ethene) | plastic bags, bottles |
| propene, CH₂=CHCH₃ | poly(propene) | crates, ropes |
| chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl | poly(chloroethene), PVC | window frames, pipes |
| tetrafluoroethene, CF₂=CF₂ | poly(tetrafluoroethene), PTFE | non-stick coatings |

Only ethene and propene are named in the spec; the others are useful practice because AQA often gives an unfamiliar monomer and asks you to draw the polymer.

### Condensation polymerisation (Higher tier)

Condensation polymerisation involves **monomers with two functional groups**. When these monomers react, they join together and **lose a small molecule, usually water**, at each link. So these are called **condensation reactions**, and there are **two products**: the polymer and water.

The simplest condensation polymers are made from **two different monomers**, each with **two of the same functional group**. AQA's example is a **polyester**:

- **ethanediol**, HO–CH₂–CH₂–OH (a **diol**: two –OH groups)
- **hexanedioic acid**, HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH (a **dicarboxylic acid**: two –COOH groups)

An –OH on the diol reacts with a –COOH on the acid to make an **ester link (–COO–)** and a molecule of water. Because each monomer has a group at **both ends**, the chain can keep growing in both directions. Using boxes for the carbon chains:

n HO–□–OH + n HOOC–■–COOH → –(O–□–O–OC–■–CO)ₙ– + 2n H₂O

For the AQA example the repeating unit is –O–CH₂CH₂–O–CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–. Each repeating unit contains **two** ester links, so **2n** water molecules are made per n of each monomer (strictly 2n − 1 for one finished chain, but AQA accepts 2n).

**Explaining the basic principles** means referring to the **functional groups** in the monomers (–OH and –COOH), saying they react to form an **ester link**, that **water is eliminated** at each link, and relating the **repeating unit** to the two monomers (each monomer loses an H or an OH).

### Amino acids (Higher tier)

**Amino acids** have **two different functional groups** in one molecule: an **amine group, –NH₂**, and a **carboxylic acid group, –COOH**. The –NH₂ of one amino acid reacts with the –COOH of the next, losing water. So amino acids react by **condensation polymerisation** to produce **polypeptides**.

Glycine is H₂NCH₂COOH. Polymerising glycine:

n H₂NCH₂COOH → –(HN–CH₂–CO)ₙ– + n H₂O

Here only **one** kind of monomer is needed, because each molecule carries both kinds of group. **Different amino acids** can be combined in the same chain to produce **proteins**; the order of amino acids decides which protein forms.

### DNA and other naturally occurring polymers

**DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid)** is a large molecule essential for life. It encodes the **genetic instructions** for the development and functioning of living organisms and viruses. Most DNA molecules are **two polymer chains**, made from **four different monomers called nucleotides**, twisted together in a **double helix**.

Other naturally occurring polymers important for life:

| Natural polymer | Type of monomer |
|---|---|
| **DNA** | **nucleotides** (four different ones) |
| **Proteins** | **amino acids** |
| **Starch** | **sugars** (glucose) |
| **Cellulose** | **sugars** (glucose) |

Starch is the energy store in plants; cellulose makes plant cell walls; proteins include enzymes and structural molecules such as keratin. Like polyesters, these are all made by condensation reactions that release water, which is why digestion (the reverse) needs water.

::: terms
- **Monomer**: a small molecule that can join with many others like it to form a polymer.
- **Polymer**: a very large molecule made from many monomers joined together in a chain.
- **Addition polymerisation**: polymerisation of alkene monomers in which the C=C opens and no other product is formed.
- **Repeating unit**: the section of a polymer chain that repeats, drawn in brackets with n outside.
- **Condensation polymerisation**: polymerisation of monomers with two functional groups in which a small molecule, usually water, is lost at each link.
- **Polyester**: a condensation polymer made from a diol and a dicarboxylic acid, joined by ester links.
- **Amino acid**: a molecule with an amine group (–NH₂) and a carboxylic acid group (–COOH) that polymerises to form polypeptides.
- **Nucleotide**: one of the four different monomers from which the two polymer chains of DNA are made.
:::

::: method
**Spot the type first.** Monomer with C=C → addition, single product, repeating unit has the same atoms as the monomer. Monomers with two functional groups (–OH, –COOH, –NH₂) → condensation (Higher tier), water also formed.

**Drawing questions (2–3 marks).**
- Addition: the double bond becomes **single**; **side groups stay on the same carbons**; **bonds through both brackets**; **n** outside. Missing the extending bonds or leaving the C=C costs a mark each.
- Monomer from a repeating unit: two carbons, put the **double bond** back, remove the extending bonds.
- Condensation (Higher tier): you can use **boxes** for the carbon chains. Show the **ester link** (–O–CO–) or amide link (–NH–CO–), and write **water** as the other product.

**Command words.**
- **Compare** addition and condensation: give the difference in **monomers** (C=C vs two functional groups), **number of products** (one vs polymer + water) and **repeating unit** (same atoms as monomer vs not).
- **Explain** condensation polymerisation (Higher tier): name the functional groups, say they react to form a link, say water (a small molecule) is lost, say each monomer has **two** groups so the chain can continue.
- **Name** monomers of natural polymers: DNA → **nucleotides**, proteins → **amino acids**, starch and cellulose → **sugars** (glucose).

**Counting atoms.** For an addition polymer, the formula of the repeating unit equals the formula of the monomer. For a condensation polymer, the repeating unit = monomers added together **minus water** (for each link in the unit). Use this to check your drawing.
:::

::: example Drawing an addition polymer
Draw the repeating unit of the polymer formed from chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl, and name the polymer. (3)

Two carbons joined by a **single** bond (1); the first carbon carries H and H, the second carries H and **Cl**, and bonds extend out through square brackets on both sides (1); **n** written outside the brackets. Name: **poly(chloroethene)** (PVC) (1).
:::

::: example Finding the monomer
A repeating unit is two carbons in brackets joined by a single bond: one carbon has two F atoms, the other has F and CF₃. Give the displayed formula of the monomer. (2)

The same two carbons with a **double bond** between them (1), with F and F on one carbon and F and CF₃ on the other, and no extending bonds (1). (It is hexafluoropropene, CF₂=CFCF₃.)
:::

::: example Polyester (Higher tier)
Ethanediol, HOCH₂CH₂OH, reacts with hexanedioic acid, HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH, to form a polyester. Explain how the polymer forms and name the other product. (4)

Each monomer has **two functional groups**: ethanediol has two –OH groups and hexanedioic acid has two –COOH groups (1). An –OH reacts with a –COOH to form an **ester link** (1), losing a small molecule of **water** each time (1). Because there is a group at each end of each monomer, the reaction repeats to make a long chain: **condensation polymerisation** (1).
:::

::: example Polypeptide formula (Higher tier)
Glycine is H₂NCH₂COOH (C₂H₅NO₂). What is the formula of the repeating unit of polyglycine? (2)

Each link loses H₂O, so repeating unit = C₂H₅NO₂ − H₂O (1) = **C₂H₃NO**, that is –NH–CH₂–CO– (1).
:::

::: example Comparing the two types
Give two differences between addition and condensation polymerisation. (2)

Addition monomers contain a **C=C**; condensation monomers have **two functional groups** (1). Addition gives **only the polymer**; condensation also gives a **small molecule such as water** (1). (Also: addition repeating unit has the same atoms as the monomer.)
:::

::: q
What is a polymer? (1)
---
A very large molecule made from many small molecules (monomers) joined together.
:::

Name the polymer made from propene and the type of polymerisation. (2)
---
**Poly(propene)** (1); **addition** polymerisation (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why the repeating unit of an addition polymer has the same atoms as its monomer. (1)
---
No other molecule (product) is formed in the reaction; all the atoms of the monomers end up in the polymer.
:::

::: q
Describe how the displayed formula of the repeating unit of poly(ethene) differs from that of ethene. (2)

The C=C double bond has become a C–C **single** bond (1); there are bonds extending out from each carbon through brackets, with n outside (1).
:::

::: q
How can you tell from its formula that a molecule could be the monomer for an addition polymer? (1)
---
It contains a carbon–carbon **double bond (C=C)** / it is an alkene.
:::

::: q
(Higher tier) Name the two functional groups in an amino acid and the type of polymer amino acids form. (3)
---
Amine group, –NH₂ (1); carboxylic acid group, –COOH (1); **polypeptides** / proteins (1).
:::

::: q
(Higher tier) Name the small molecule usually lost in condensation polymerisation and the type of link in a polyester. (2)
---
**Water** (1); **ester** link (1).
:::

::: q
Name the monomers of DNA, proteins and starch. (3)
---
DNA: **nucleotides** (1). Proteins: **amino acids** (1). Starch: **sugars** / glucose (1).
:::

::: q
Describe the structure of most DNA molecules. (2)
---
Two polymer chains (1) made of four different nucleotides, twisted into a **double helix** (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Leaving the double bond in the polymer**, or forgetting the bonds that extend through the brackets. The repeating unit has a C–C single bond and two open-ended bonds.
- **Moving the side group.** In poly(propene) the CH₃ is a side branch on one carbon of the chain, not part of the backbone.
- **Putting n inside the brackets** or leaving it out. It goes outside, bottom right, and in front of the monomer.
- **(Higher tier) Forgetting the water** in condensation polymerisation, or saying addition polymerisation makes water.
- **(Higher tier) "One functional group".** Condensation monomers need **two** functional groups each, otherwise the chain cannot continue.
- **Monomer mix-ups.** DNA is made from **nucleotides**, not amino acids; starch and cellulose are made from **sugars**.
:::

Tetrafluoroethene, CF₂=CF₂, is used to make PTFE. (a) Name the polymer. (b) Describe the displayed formula of its repeating unit. (3)
---
(a) **Poly(tetrafluoroethene)** (1). (b) Two carbons joined by a single bond, each carbon carrying **two F atoms** (1); bonds extending through square brackets on each side with **n** outside (1).

::: test
A poly(ethene) molecule has a relative formula mass of 28 000. Calculate the number of ethene monomers that joined to make it. (Relative atomic masses: C = 12, H = 1.) (2)
---
$M_r$ of ethene, C₂H₄ $= 2 \times 12 + 4 \times 1 = 28$ (1). Number of monomers $= 28\,000 \div 28 = $ **1000** (1).
:::

::: test
Name the natural polymer described in each case: (a) made from glucose and found in plant cell walls; (b) carries genetic instructions and is a double helix; (c) made from amino acids and includes enzymes. (3)
---
(a) **Cellulose** (1). (b) **DNA** (1). (c) **Proteins** (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) Glycine, H₂NCH₂COOH, polymerises. (a) Name the type of polymerisation. (b) Write the repeating unit. (c) Explain why only one type of monomer is needed here, but two are needed to make a polyester. (4)
---
(a) **Condensation** polymerisation (1). (b) –(NH–CH₂–CO)– with n (1). (c) Glycine has **two different** functional groups (–NH₂ and –COOH) that can react with each other (1); a diol has only –OH groups and a dicarboxylic acid only –COOH groups, so each needs the other type to react with (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) Ethanediol, C₂H₆O₂, and hexanedioic acid, C₆H₁₀O₄, form a polyester. Each repeating unit contains one of each monomer and two ester links. Calculate the relative formula mass of the repeating unit. (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16.) (3)
---
Monomers: $M_r$(C₂H₆O₂) $= 62$ and $M_r$(C₆H₁₀O₄) $= 146$, total 208 (1). Two links, so 2 H₂O are lost: $2 \times 18 = 36$ (1). Repeating unit $= 208 - 36 = $ **172** (C₈H₁₂O₄) (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) A student says "Poly(ethene) and polyester are made the same way because both are polymers." Evaluate this statement. (4)
---
The statement is wrong (or only partly right) (1 for judgement backed by points). Both are polymers made by joining many monomers (1). Poly(ethene) forms by **addition**: ethene monomers have C=C, the double bonds open, and there is **no other product** (1). Polyester forms by **condensation**: two monomers each with two functional groups (diol and dicarboxylic acid) react, forming ester links and **losing water** (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) Compare addition polymerisation and condensation polymerisation. Refer to the monomers, the reactions and the products, using poly(propene) and a polyester as examples. (6)
---
Levels of response. **Level 3 (5–6):** a detailed comparison covering monomers, how they join and the products for both types, with correct examples and linked differences. **Level 2 (3–4):** a description of both types with some comparison; may lack detail on functional groups or products. **Level 1 (1–2):** simple statements about one type or isolated facts.

Indicative content. Addition: monomer propene, an alkene with a C=C; one type of monomer; the double bond opens and monomers join into a chain of carbon atoms; no other product; repeating unit has the same atoms as the monomer, e.g. –(CH₂–CH(CH₃))ₙ–; backbone of carbon only. Condensation: monomers with two functional groups each, e.g. ethanediol (two –OH) and hexanedioic acid (two –COOH); two different monomers (or one with two different groups, such as an amino acid); –OH and –COOH react to form ester links; a small molecule, water, is lost at every link; two products, polymer and water; repeating unit has fewer atoms than the two monomers combined; backbone contains O atoms in the ester links. Similarities: both make very long chains from many monomers.
:::
