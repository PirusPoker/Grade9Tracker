## What it is

Some reactions can go **both ways**. This topic (AQA 4.6.2) is in both GCSE Chemistry 8462 and Combined Science: Trilogy 8464 (numbered 5.6.2 there). Everything about **changing conditions at equilibrium and Le Chatelier's Principle is (Higher tier)**.

### Reversible reactions

In some reactions the **products can react to produce the original reactants**. These are **reversible reactions**, written with the symbol ⇌:

$$\text{A} + \text{B} \rightleftharpoons \text{C} + \text{D}$$

The reaction left to right is the **forward reaction**; right to left is the **reverse (backward) reaction**. The **direction** of a reversible reaction can be changed by **changing the conditions**.

**Example: ammonium chloride.** Heating white solid ammonium chloride decomposes it into two gases; when the gases cool (for example, at the cooler top of a test tube) they react to re-form the white solid:

$$\text{ammonium chloride} \underset{\text{cool}}{\overset{\text{heat}}{\rightleftharpoons}} \text{ammonia} + \text{hydrogen chloride} \qquad \text{NH}_4\text{Cl(s)} \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_3\text{(g)} + \text{HCl(g)}$$

### Energy changes and reversible reactions

If a reversible reaction is **exothermic in one direction**, it is **endothermic in the opposite direction**. The **same amount of energy** is transferred in each case.

**Example: hydrated copper sulfate.** Heating **blue** hydrated copper sulfate drives off water and leaves **white** anhydrous copper sulfate (endothermic: energy must be supplied). Adding water to the white powder turns it blue again and the mixture gets **hot** (exothermic).

$$\underset{\text{(blue)}}{\text{hydrated copper sulfate}} \rightleftharpoons \underset{\text{(white)}}{\text{anhydrous copper sulfate}} + \text{water}$$

$$\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CuSO}_4 + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

If the forward reaction takes in 78 kJ, the reverse reaction gives out exactly 78 kJ. (The white-to-blue colour change is also a test for water.)

| Direction | Hydrated copper sulfate example | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| forward (heat the blue solid) | blue → white + water | endothermic: energy taken in |
| reverse (add water to white solid) | white + water → blue | exothermic: the same amount of energy released |

### Equilibrium

When a reversible reaction takes place in apparatus that **prevents the escape of reactants and products** (a **closed system**), it reaches **equilibrium**:

- **equilibrium is reached when the forward and reverse reactions occur at exactly the same rate**;
- the reactions have **not stopped**: both are still happening, which is why it is called **dynamic** equilibrium;
- the **amounts (concentrations)** of reactants and products stay **constant**, though they are usually **not equal** to each other.

How it happens: at the start only reactants are present, so the forward rate is high and the reverse rate is zero. As reactants are used up, the forward rate falls; as products build up, the reverse rate rises. When the two rates become equal, nothing seems to change any more.

In an **open** system (e.g. an open beaker where a gas can escape), equilibrium cannot be reached because a product is lost.

### Changing the conditions (Higher tier)

The **relative amounts** of all the reactants and products at equilibrium depend on the **conditions** of the reaction. If a system is at equilibrium and a change is made to any of the conditions, then **the system responds to counteract the change**. This is **Le Chatelier's Principle**, and it lets you make qualitative predictions.

"The **position of equilibrium shifts to the right**" means more products form; "shifts to the left" means more reactants form.

#### Concentration (Higher tier)

If the concentration of one of the reactants or products is changed, the system is no longer at equilibrium and the **concentrations of all the substances will change** until equilibrium is reached again.

- **Increase the concentration of a reactant** → more products are formed until equilibrium is reached again (shifts right, using up the added reactant).
- **Decrease the concentration of a product** (e.g. remove it) → more reactants react until equilibrium is reached again (shifts right, replacing the product).
- The reverse also works: adding product or removing reactant shifts the position to the left.

**Example: chromate and dichromate.** $2\text{CrO}_4^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$, where chromate is **yellow** and dichromate is **orange**. Adding acid (more H⁺, a reactant) shifts the position to the right: the solution turns **orange**. Adding alkali removes H⁺ (OH⁻ reacts with it), so the position shifts left: the solution turns **yellow**.

#### Temperature (Higher tier)

| Temperature change | Forward reaction **endothermic** | Forward reaction **exothermic** |
|---|---|---|
| **increase** | relative amount of **products increases** | relative amount of **products decreases** |
| **decrease** | relative amount of **products decreases** | relative amount of **products increases** |

The reasoning: raising the temperature favours the **endothermic** direction, because that direction takes in energy and counteracts the rise. Lowering the temperature favours the **exothermic** direction.

**Example: nitrogen dioxide.** $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}_2\text{(g)}$. N₂O₄ is colourless; NO₂ is brown. The forward reaction is endothermic. Heating a sealed tube makes the gas **darker brown** (more NO₂); cooling it in ice makes it **paler**.

#### Pressure (Higher tier)

For **gaseous** reactions at equilibrium, count the number of **molecules** (from the balancing numbers of the gases) on each side:

- an **increase in pressure** shifts the position towards the side with the **smaller number of molecules**;
- a **decrease in pressure** shifts the position towards the side with the **larger number of molecules**.

Fewer molecules take up less space, which counteracts the increased pressure.

| Reaction | Molecules (left : right) | Increase pressure gives |
|---|---|---|
| $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ | 4 : 2 | more ammonia |
| $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3$ | 3 : 2 | more sulfur trioxide |
| $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}_2$ | 1 : 2 | more N₂O₄ (paler) |
| $\text{H}_2 + \text{I}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$ | 2 : 2 | **no change** in position |

If both sides have the **same number** of gas molecules, changing the pressure has **no effect** on the position of equilibrium.

#### Catalysts

A **catalyst** speeds up the forward and reverse reactions **equally**, so equilibrium is reached **faster**, but it does **not** change the position of equilibrium or the amount of product at equilibrium.

### Interpreting data (Higher tier)

AQA questions often give a table or graph of **percentage yield** of product against temperature or pressure and ask you to predict or explain. Illustrative data for $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ (forward reaction exothermic):

| Pressure (atm) | % ammonia at 350 °C | % ammonia at 450 °C | % ammonia at 550 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 37 | 16 | 7 |
| 200 | 52 | 27 | 13 |
| 300 | 60 | 35 | 18 |

- Along a row: **higher temperature → lower yield**, because the forward reaction is exothermic, so raising temperature favours the reverse (endothermic) reaction.
- Down a column: **higher pressure → higher yield**, because the right-hand side has fewer molecules (2 compared with 4).

Industrial conditions are a compromise between yield, rate and cost; how this applies to the Haber process is covered in 4.10.4 (chemistry only).

::: terms
- **Reversible reaction**: a reaction in which the products can react to re-form the original reactants, shown by the ⇌ symbol.
- **Forward reaction**: the reaction from the left-hand side (reactants) to the right-hand side (products) of the equation.
- **Reverse reaction**: the reaction from the right-hand side back to the left-hand side, re-forming the reactants.
- **Closed system**: apparatus that stops any reactants or products from escaping, so equilibrium can be reached.
- **Dynamic equilibrium**: the state where the forward and reverse reactions occur at exactly the same rate, so amounts stay constant.
- **Position of equilibrium**: the relative amounts of reactants and products present in a mixture at equilibrium.
- **Le Chatelier's Principle**: if a change is made to a system at equilibrium, the system responds to counteract the change.
- **Anhydrous**: without water; anhydrous copper sulfate is white and turns blue when water is added.
:::

::: method
**Command words.**
- **Describe** what happens at equilibrium: "the forward and reverse reactions happen at the **same rate**, so the amounts of reactants and products stay **constant**". Never say the reaction has stopped.
- **Explain** an energy point: "the forward reaction is endothermic, so the reverse reaction is exothermic and transfers the **same amount** of energy".
- **Predict and explain (Higher tier)** the effect of a change. Use a three-step answer:
  1. **What changes**: "the temperature is increased".
  2. **Which direction is favoured, and why** (Le Chatelier): "the system counteracts the change by favouring the endothermic direction, which is the forward reaction".
  3. **The result**: "the position of equilibrium shifts to the right, so the amount of product increases" (and any observation, such as a colour change).
- For **pressure**, always quote the **number of molecules** on each side from the balanced equation ("4 molecules on the left, 2 on the right").
- **Use data**: describe the trend with numbers, then explain it with Le Chatelier. Deduce whether a forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic from how yield changes with temperature.

**Calculations and data.** The maths here is reading tables and graphs, counting molecules from balancing numbers (only count **gases**), and using "same energy in both directions" (if forward is −92 kJ, reverse is +92 kJ).

**6-mark extended response (Higher tier).** Typical: "Explain how changing the temperature and the pressure affects the yield of product." Level 3 needs **both** factors covered, each with a **linked** explanation (change → direction favoured → reason → effect on yield), and correct use of any data. Mentioning that a catalyst does not affect yield, only how quickly equilibrium is reached, is a good extra linking point.

**There is no required practical in this subsection**, but questions use the copper sulfate and ammonium chloride experiments: know the colours (blue hydrated, white anhydrous; white solid NH₄Cl re-forming at the cool end of the tube).
:::

::: example Energy in a reversible reaction
Heating blue hydrated copper sulfate gives white anhydrous copper sulfate and water. (a) What would you observe when water is added to the white solid? (b) The forward reaction takes in 78 kJ per mole. State the energy change for the reverse reaction. (3)

(a) The white solid turns **blue** (1) and gets **hot** (1).
(b) **78 kJ per mole released** (exothermic), the same amount in the opposite direction (1).
:::

::: example What equilibrium means
Explain what is meant by dynamic equilibrium. (2)

The forward and reverse reactions are still happening (1) but at **exactly the same rate**, so the amounts of reactants and products do not change (1).
:::

::: example Pressure (Higher tier)
2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). Predict and explain the effect of increasing the pressure on the amount of sulfur trioxide at equilibrium. (3)

There are 3 molecules of gas on the left and 2 on the right (1). Increasing pressure shifts the position towards the side with **fewer molecules** to counteract the change (1), so the position moves right and the amount of **SO₃ increases** (1).
:::

::: example Temperature from data (Higher tier)
For a reaction X ⇌ Y, the percentage of Y at equilibrium is 62% at 200 °C and 35% at 400 °C. Deduce whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic. Explain your answer. (3)

Increasing the temperature **decreases** the yield of Y (1). Increasing temperature favours the endothermic direction, so the reverse reaction must be endothermic (1). Therefore the forward reaction is **exothermic** (1).
:::

::: example Concentration (Higher tier)
2CrO₄²⁻ (yellow) + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange) + H₂O. Predict and explain the colour change when dilute acid is added to a yellow solution of chromate ions. (3)

Adding acid increases the concentration of H⁺, a reactant (1). The system counteracts this by using up H⁺, so the position shifts to the right / more products form (1). The solution turns from yellow to **orange** (1).
:::

::: q
What does the symbol ⇌ show in a chemical equation? (1)
---
The reaction is **reversible** (it can go in both directions).
:::

::: q
Describe what happens when ammonium chloride is heated in a test tube. (2)
---
It decomposes into ammonia and hydrogen chloride gases (1); at the cooler top of the tube the gases react to re-form white solid ammonium chloride (1).
:::

::: q
The forward reaction of a reversible reaction is exothermic and transfers 50 kJ. What can you say about the reverse reaction? (2)
---
It is **endothermic** (1) and takes in **50 kJ**, the same amount of energy (1).
:::

::: q
Why must a reversible reaction take place in a closed system to reach equilibrium? (1)
---
So that no reactants or products (e.g. gases) can **escape**; otherwise the reverse (or forward) reaction could not keep happening.
:::

::: q
State two features of a reaction at equilibrium. (2)
---
Forward and reverse reactions happen at the **same rate** (1); amounts/concentrations of reactants and products stay **constant** (1). (Also accept: both reactions still taking place.)
:::

::: q
(Higher tier) State Le Chatelier's Principle. (1)
---
If a change is made to the conditions of a system at equilibrium, the system **responds to counteract** (oppose) the change.
:::

::: q
(Higher tier) H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). Explain why changing the pressure has no effect on the position of equilibrium. (2)
---
There are **2 molecules** of gas on each side (1), so neither direction reduces or increases the number of molecules; there is nothing to counteract the pressure change (1).
:::

::: q
(Higher tier) N₂O₄ (colourless) ⇌ 2NO₂ (brown); the forward reaction is endothermic. Predict what you would see if a sealed tube of the mixture is put in ice. (2)
---
The gas becomes **paler** (less brown) (1): lowering the temperature favours the exothermic (reverse) reaction, so more N₂O₄ forms (1).
:::

::: q
(Higher tier) Does a catalyst change the yield of product at equilibrium? Explain. (2)
---
**No** (1): it speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster but its position is unchanged (1).
:::

::: watch
- **"The reaction has stopped" at equilibrium.** It is **dynamic**: both reactions continue at equal rates.
- **"Equal amounts" at equilibrium.** The **rates** are equal; the **amounts** are constant but usually not equal.
- **(Higher tier) Not saying which direction is favoured, and why.** "Yield goes up" alone scores little. Say: "increasing temperature favours the endothermic direction, the forward reaction, so the position shifts right and the yield increases".
- **(Higher tier) Counting atoms instead of molecules.** For pressure, count the balancing numbers of gas molecules (N₂ + 3H₂ is **4** molecules, not 8 atoms).
- **(Higher tier) Saying a catalyst increases yield.** It only gets to equilibrium faster.
- **Copper sulfate colours the wrong way round.** Hydrated is **blue**; anhydrous is **white**.
:::

::: test
Which statement is true for a reaction at dynamic equilibrium in a closed system? A: the forward reaction has stopped; B: the amounts of reactants and products are always equal; C: the forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate; D: the reverse reaction is faster. (1)
---
**C**.
:::

::: test
Hydrated cobalt chloride is pink; anhydrous cobalt chloride is blue. Heating the pink solid turns it blue. (a) Write a word equation for this reversible reaction. (b) Is the reverse reaction exothermic or endothermic? Explain. (3)
---
(a) hydrated cobalt chloride ⇌ anhydrous cobalt chloride + water (1). (b) **Exothermic** (1): the forward reaction needs heating so it is endothermic, and a reversible reaction is exothermic in the opposite direction, transferring the same amount of energy (1).
:::

::: test
Describe how a reversible reaction in a closed system reaches equilibrium, starting with only reactants. (3)
---
At the start the forward rate is high and the reverse rate is zero because there are no products (1). As reactants are used up the forward rate decreases, and as products form the reverse rate increases (1). Equilibrium is reached when the two rates become equal, and the amounts then stay constant (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). The forward reaction transfers 92 kJ to the surroundings. (a) Give the energy change for the reverse reaction. (b) Predict and explain the effect on the yield of ammonia of increasing the temperature. (4)
---
(a) **92 kJ taken in** (endothermic) (1). (b) Increasing temperature favours the endothermic direction to counteract the rise (1), which is the **reverse** reaction (1), so the position shifts left and the yield of ammonia **decreases** (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g). Explain how the yield of methanol changes if (a) the pressure is increased, (b) the methanol is removed as it forms. (4)
---
(a) 3 molecules on the left, 1 on the right (1); increasing pressure shifts the position to the side with fewer molecules, so the yield of methanol **increases** (1). (b) Removing a product lowers its concentration (1); the system counteracts this by forming more product, so more CO and H₂ react and more methanol is made (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) Percentage of product Z at equilibrium in a gas reaction: 100 atm, 300 °C: 45%; 100 atm, 500 °C: 70%; 200 atm, 300 °C: 33%; 200 atm, 500 °C: 58%. Use the data to deduce (a) whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic, and (b) which side of the equation has more molecules. Explain each answer. (4)
---
(a) At the same pressure, higher temperature gives more Z (45% → 70%) (1), so the forward reaction is **endothermic**, because raising temperature favours the endothermic direction (1). (b) At the same temperature, higher pressure gives less Z (45% → 33%) (1), so the position shifts left; increased pressure favours the side with fewer molecules, so the **products side has more molecules** (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) A student says: "Adding a catalyst and increasing the concentration of a reactant both increase the amount of product in the equilibrium mixture." Evaluate this statement. (3)
---
Increasing the concentration of a reactant does increase the amount of product: the system counteracts by using up the added reactant, so the position shifts right (1). A catalyst does **not**: it increases the rate of the forward and reverse reactions equally (1), so equilibrium is reached faster but the position, and the amount of product, are unchanged. The statement is only half correct (1).
:::

::: test
(Higher tier) Sulfur trioxide is made by the reaction 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g); the forward reaction is exothermic. Explain how changing the temperature and the pressure would affect the yield of sulfur trioxide, and why a catalyst is used. (6)
---
Levels of response. **Level 3 (5–6):** clear, linked explanations of the effects of both temperature and pressure, using Le Chatelier's Principle and the equation, plus the role of the catalyst. **Level 2 (3–4):** correct effects of both with some explanation, or one fully explained. **Level 1 (1–2):** simple correct statements, e.g. "higher pressure gives more SO₃", without explanation.

Indicative content. Temperature: the forward reaction is exothermic; lowering the temperature favours the exothermic direction, so the position shifts right and the yield of SO₃ increases; raising the temperature favours the endothermic reverse reaction and decreases the yield; but a low temperature makes the rate slow. Pressure: 3 molecules of gas on the left, 2 on the right; increasing pressure shifts the position to the side with fewer molecules, so the yield of SO₃ increases; decreasing pressure lowers the yield. Catalyst: increases the rate of both forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster; it does not change the yield; it allows a reasonable rate at a lower temperature (which keeps the yield higher).
:::
