## What it is

This topic is the foundation of the whole Chemistry course: what atoms are, how they combine, how our model of the atom was built up from evidence, and how electrons are arranged. It is examined directly on Paper 1 and used everywhere on Paper 2. Everything here is common to GCSE Chemistry 8462 and Combined Science: Trilogy 8464.

### Atoms, elements and compounds

- **Atom** — the smallest part of an element that can exist. All substances are made of atoms.
- **Element** — a substance made of only **one type of atom** (all with the same number of protons). Each element has a **chemical symbol**: O for oxygen, Na for sodium, Fe for iron. There are about **100** elements, all shown in the periodic table.
- **Compound** — **two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions**, e.g. water H₂O (always 2 H to 1 O), carbon dioxide CO₂. Compounds are formed from elements **by chemical reactions** and can only be separated back into elements **by chemical reactions**.
- A **chemical reaction** always forms **one or more new substances** and often involves a **detectable energy change** (temperature change, light, sound).

You are given a periodic table in the exam, but you must be able to use the **names and symbols of the first 20 elements**, the elements of **Groups 1 and 7**, and other elements in the spec; **name compounds from formulae** (NaCl is sodium chlor**ide**; CuSO₄ is copper sulf**ate**; KNO₃ is potassium nitr**ate**), and write **word equations** and **balanced symbol equations**.

**Naming rules:** metal first, non-metal second. Two elements only → ending **-ide** (MgO magnesium oxide, CaCl₂ calcium chloride). With oxygen in a group → **-ate** (sulfate SO₄²⁻, nitrate NO₃⁻, carbonate CO₃²⁻).

**Balancing:** you may only change the **big numbers in front** (coefficients), never the small subscripts, because changing a subscript changes the substance. Count each type of atom on both sides.

Worked: magnesium burns in oxygen.
Word equation: magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide
Unbalanced: Mg + O₂ → MgO (2 O on the left, 1 on the right)
Put 2 in front of MgO: Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (now 2 Mg on the right)
Put 2 in front of Mg: **2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO** ✓

### Half equations and ionic equations (Higher tier)

- A **half equation** shows electrons being lost or gained by one species: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (electron lost); Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ (electrons gained). Charges must balance as well as atoms.
- An **ionic equation** shows only the particles that change, leaving out **spectator ions**. For any acid + alkali: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l). For chlorine displacing bromide: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂.

### Mixtures and separation

A **mixture** is two or more elements or compounds **not chemically combined**. Each substance keeps its **own chemical properties**, and the proportions can vary (air, sea water, crude oil). Mixtures are separated by **physical processes** — no chemical reaction, no new substance.

| Technique | Use it to separate | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Filtration** | an insoluble solid from a liquid | solid stays on the filter paper (**residue**); liquid passes through (**filtrate**). Sand from water. |
| **Crystallisation** | a soluble solid from its solution | heat to evaporate some solvent until crystals start to form, leave to cool, filter off and dry the crystals. Copper sulfate from solution. |
| **Simple distillation** | a solvent from a solution | heat; solvent boils, vapour is cooled in a **condenser** and collected as the distillate. Pure water from salt water. |
| **Fractional distillation** | liquids with **different boiling points** that are mixed (miscible) | a **fractionating column** is hotter at the bottom, cooler at the top; the liquid with the lowest boiling point reaches the top and condenses first. Ethanol from water; crude oil. |
| **Chromatography** | soluble substances, e.g. the dyes in ink | substances move different distances up paper with a solvent, depending on solubility. |

To **suggest** a technique, look at the given data: insoluble vs soluble, which substance you want to keep, and boiling points. Rock salt (salt + sand) needs **dissolving → filtration → crystallisation**.

### Developing the model of the atom

New experimental evidence can lead to a scientific model being changed or replaced. This is the classic example.

| Stage | Model | Evidence / reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Atoms are **tiny solid spheres that cannot be divided** | before the electron was discovered |
| 2 | **Plum pudding model** — a **ball of positive charge** with **negative electrons embedded** in it | discovery of the **electron** (atoms contain smaller, negative particles) |
| 3 | **Nuclear model** — mass concentrated in a tiny, positively charged **nucleus** at the centre; electrons outside | **alpha particle scattering experiment** |
| 4 | **Bohr model** — electrons **orbit the nucleus at specific distances** (energy levels) | Bohr's theoretical calculations **agreed with experimental observations** |
| 5 | **Protons** — the nucleus's positive charge is a whole number of smaller particles, each with the same positive charge | later experiments |
| 6 | **Neutrons** in the nucleus | **James Chadwick's** experimental work, about 20 years after the nucleus was accepted |

**Alpha scattering** — positive alpha particles were fired at very thin gold foil:
- **Most passed straight through** → most of the atom is **empty space**.
- **Some were deflected** → the centre is **positively charged** (repels the positive alpha particles).
- **A very few bounced back** → the mass and charge are concentrated in a **tiny, dense nucleus**.

The plum pudding model predicted the alpha particles would all pass through with little deflection, because the positive charge and mass were spread out. The results did not fit, so the model was replaced. Details of Bohr's and Chadwick's experiments are **not** required.

**Plum pudding vs nuclear model** — plum pudding: positive charge spread throughout, mass spread out, no nucleus, no empty space. Nuclear: positive charge and almost all the mass in a tiny central nucleus, electrons in shells around it, mostly empty space.

### Subatomic particles

| Particle | Relative charge | Relative mass | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | +1 | 1 | nucleus |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 | nucleus |
| Electron | −1 | very small (about 1/1840) | shells (energy levels) |

- In an atom, **number of electrons = number of protons**, so atoms have **no overall charge**.
- **Atomic number** = number of protons. All atoms of an element have the same number of protons; different elements have different numbers.
- **Mass number** = protons + neutrons.
- **Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.**

Atoms are written as $^{23}_{11}\text{Na}$: mass number 23 on top, atomic number 11 at the bottom. So sodium-23 has 11 protons, 11 electrons and 23 − 11 = **12 neutrons**.

**Ions** have gained or lost electrons; protons and neutrons do not change. $^{27}_{13}\text{Al}^{3+}$: 13 protons, 14 neutrons, 13 − 3 = **10 electrons**. $^{32}_{16}\text{S}^{2-}$: 16 protons, 16 neutrons, 16 + 2 = **18 electrons**.

### Size and mass of atoms

- An atom has a radius of about **0.1 nm** = $1 \times 10^{-10}$ m.
- The nucleus has a radius **less than 1/10 000** of the atom's: about $1 \times 10^{-14}$ m.
- **Almost all the mass is in the nucleus.**
- Prefixes: nano = $10^{-9}$, micro = $10^{-6}$, milli = $10^{-3}$, centi = $10^{-2}$.

Scale: if a nucleus were a 1 cm marble, the atom would be about 10 000 cm = **100 m** across — a football pitch with a marble in the centre circle. A human hair is about $1 \times 10^{-4}$ m wide, so roughly $\dfrac{1 \times 10^{-4}}{2 \times 10^{-10}} = 500\,000$ atoms fit across it.

### Isotopes and relative atomic mass

**Isotopes** are atoms of the **same element** (same number of protons) with **different numbers of neutrons** (different mass numbers). Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 both have 17 protons; they have 18 and 20 neutrons. Isotopes have the same chemical properties because they have the same electron arrangement.

**Relative atomic mass ($A_r$)** is the **average** mass of an element's atoms, taking account of the **abundance** of each isotope:

$$A_r = \frac{\sum (\text{mass number} \times \text{abundance})}{\text{total abundance (100)}}$$

Worked: chlorine is 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37.
$A_r = \dfrac{(35 \times 75) + (37 \times 25)}{100} = \dfrac{2625 + 925}{100} = 35.5$

Sense check: the answer must lie between the isotope masses and closer to the more abundant one.

### Electronic structure

Electrons occupy the **lowest available energy levels** (innermost shells) first. For the first 20 elements: first shell holds **2**, second holds **8**, third holds **8** (then the fourth starts at potassium).

| Element | Z | Structure | Element | Z | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | 1 | 1 | Na | 11 | 2,8,1 |
| He | 2 | 2 | Mg | 12 | 2,8,2 |
| Li | 3 | 2,1 | Al | 13 | 2,8,3 |
| C | 6 | 2,4 | Si | 14 | 2,8,4 |
| N | 7 | 2,5 | P | 15 | 2,8,5 |
| O | 8 | 2,6 | S | 16 | 2,8,6 |
| F | 9 | 2,7 | Cl | 17 | 2,8,7 |
| Ne | 10 | 2,8 | Ar | 18 | 2,8,8 |
| | | | K | 19 | 2,8,8,1 |
| | | | Ca | 20 | 2,8,8,2 |

You must show these as **numbers** (sodium **2,8,1**) or as a **diagram** (concentric circles with dots or crosses: 2 on the inner circle, 8 on the next, 1 on the outer). Questions may say "shells" or "energy levels" — they mean the same.

**Links:** number of shells = **period** (row); number of outer electrons = **group** (for Groups 1–7; Group 0 has a full outer shell).

::: terms
- **Atom**: the smallest part of an element that can exist.
- **Element**: a substance made of only one type of atom, all with the same number of protons.
- **Compound**: two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions; separated only by chemical reactions.
- **Mixture**: two or more elements or compounds not chemically combined, each keeping its own chemical properties.
- **Atomic number**: the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom.
- **Mass number**: the total number of protons and neutrons in an atom.
- **Isotopes**: atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
- **Relative atomic mass**: the average mass of an element's atoms, taking account of the abundance of each isotope.
- **Plum pudding model**: an early model of the atom as a ball of positive charge with negative electrons embedded in it.
- **Nuclear model**: a model with the mass and positive charge concentrated in a tiny central nucleus and electrons around it.
- **Energy level (shell)**: a fixed distance from the nucleus where electrons are found; inner levels fill first.
:::

::: method
**Command words on this topic**
- **State / give / name** — a single fact, no explanation: "2,8,3", "fractional distillation".
- **Describe** — say what happens or what something is like (e.g. describe the plum pudding model). No "because" needed.
- **Explain** — give the reason, linking evidence to conclusion: "*most alpha particles passed straight through* **so** *most of the atom is empty space*".
- **Compare** — give a similarity or difference **for both** things in the same sentence ("in the plum pudding model the positive charge is spread out, **whereas** in the nuclear model it is concentrated in the nucleus").
- **Suggest** — apply your knowledge to an unfamiliar mixture or element; use the data you are given.
- **Calculate** — show the working.

**Particle-counting questions:** protons = atomic number; neutrons = mass number − atomic number; electrons = protons for an atom; for an ion, **subtract** the positive charge or **add** the negative charge.

**Relative atomic mass calculations:** (1) multiply each mass number by its percentage abundance, (2) add them, (3) divide by 100 (or by the total abundance if it is not 100), (4) give the answer to the number of decimal places asked for, and check it lies between the isotope masses.

**Equations:** write the word equation first, then correct formulae, then balance with coefficients only. In Higher questions, check half equations balance for **charge** too.

**Separation questions:** identify the property that differs (solubility, boiling point) and name the technique **and** what is collected. For rock salt, give the steps in order.

**6-mark extended response — model of the atom.** Marked on levels: Level 3 (5–6 marks) gives a clear, logically ordered account linking each piece of evidence to the change in the model; Level 2 (3–4) describes several stages with some explanation; Level 1 (1–2) makes simple, unlinked statements. Plan: plum pudding model → alpha scattering method → each observation **linked** to its conclusion → nuclear model → Bohr's shells → protons → Chadwick's neutrons. Always use the word **evidence**.
:::

::: example Particles in an ion
Give the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in a magnesium ion, $^{24}_{12}\text{Mg}^{2+}$. (3)

Protons = atomic number = **12** (1).
Neutrons = 24 − 12 = **12** (1).
Electrons: the atom has 12; the 2+ charge means 2 electrons lost, so 12 − 2 = **10** (1).
:::

::: example Relative atomic mass
Copper has two isotopes: copper-63 (69.2%) and copper-65 (30.8%). Calculate the relative atomic mass of copper to 1 decimal place. (2)

$A_r = \dfrac{(63 \times 69.2) + (65 \times 30.8)}{100}$ (1)
$= \dfrac{4359.6 + 2002}{100} = 63.616 = $ **63.6** (1)

Check: between 63 and 65, closer to 63 (the more abundant isotope). ✓
:::

::: example Alpha scattering
Explain how the results of the alpha particle scattering experiment led scientists to replace the plum pudding model. (4)

Most alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil, so most of the atom must be empty space (1). A few were deflected through large angles, so there must be a concentration of positive charge that repelled them (1). A very small number bounced straight back, so the mass must be concentrated in a tiny region, the nucleus (1). The plum pudding model, with mass and positive charge spread throughout, could not explain these results, so it was replaced by the nuclear model (1).
:::

::: example Balancing an equation
Sodium reacts with water to form sodium hydroxide and hydrogen. Write a balanced symbol equation. (2)

Formulae: Na + H₂O → NaOH + H₂ (1 for correct formulae).
H₂ on the right needs 2 H from somewhere extra, so double the water and sodium hydroxide: Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂; now 2 Na are needed on the left:
**2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂** (1)
:::

::: example Choosing a separation method
A student has a mixture of water (boiling point 100 °C) and propanone (boiling point 56 °C), which mix completely. Suggest how to obtain pure propanone and explain your choice. (3)

Fractional distillation (1). The liquids are miscible, so filtration cannot be used, and they have different boiling points (1). Propanone has the lower boiling point so it boils, reaches the top of the fractionating column first and is condensed and collected at about 56 °C (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.** Do not give the mass number as the number of neutrons.
- **Ions:** only electrons change. A 2+ ion has **2 fewer** electrons than protons; a 2− ion has **2 more**.
- **Alpha scattering:** each observation must be **linked** to its conclusion ("most went straight through **so** mostly empty space"). Lists of observations without conclusions score little.
- **Plum pudding** is positive charge with electrons embedded — not "electrons orbiting". Bohr's model is the one with orbits at fixed distances.
- **Compounds** are chemically combined in **fixed proportions**; just "joined together" is too vague.
- Balance with **big numbers in front only**; never change a formula to make it balance.
- Relative atomic mass answers must lie **between** the isotope masses — if not, you have made a slip.
:::

Explain why an atom has no overall charge. (2)
---
It contains equal numbers of protons and electrons (1); the positive charges (+1 each) cancel the negative charges (−1 each) (1).

::: test
Bromine consists of bromine-79 (50.7%) and bromine-81 (49.3%). Calculate the relative atomic mass of bromine. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. (3)
---
$(79 \times 50.7) + (81 \times 49.3) = 4005.3 + 3993.3 = 7998.6$ (1); $\div 100 = 79.986$ (1); $= 80.0$ to 1 decimal place (1).

::: test
Element X has two isotopes, with mass numbers 69 and 71. Its relative atomic mass is 69.8. Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope. (3)
---
Let the abundance of X-69 be $x$%, so X-71 is $(100 - x)$% (1). $\dfrac{69x + 71(100 - x)}{100} = 69.8$, so $7100 - 2x = 6980$ (1). $x = 60$: **60% X-69 and 40% X-71** (1).
:::

The model of the atom has changed over time. Describe how and why the model of the atom developed from the plum pudding model to the modern model. (6)
---

Indicative content: the electron was discovered, so atoms were divisible; plum pudding model — a ball of positive charge with electrons embedded; alpha particles fired at thin gold foil; most passed straight through, so the atom is mostly empty space; some were deflected, so the centre is positively charged; a very few bounced back, so the mass is concentrated in a tiny nucleus; the nuclear model replaced plum pudding because it explained the results; Bohr suggested electrons orbit at specific distances (energy levels), and his calculations agreed with observations; later experiments showed the nuclear charge is made of whole-number particles of equal positive charge — protons; Chadwick provided evidence for neutrons in the nucleus about 20 years later; new evidence led to the model being changed.
