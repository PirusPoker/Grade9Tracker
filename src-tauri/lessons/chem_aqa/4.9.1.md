## What it is

This subsection is in **both** GCSE Chemistry 8462 and Combined Science: Trilogy 8464 (Trilogy 5.9.1). There is no Higher-tier-only content. It covers what the air is made of today, what scientists think the **early atmosphere** was like, and how **oxygen increased** and **carbon dioxide decreased** over about 4.6 billion years.

### The atmosphere today

For about the last **200 million years** the proportions of gases in the atmosphere have been much the same as they are now.

| Gas | Approximate proportion (dry air) | What AQA expects you to say |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen**, N₂ | 78% | "about four-fifths (approximately 80%)" |
| **Oxygen**, O₂ | 21% | "about one-fifth (approximately 20%)" |
| **Argon** and other noble gases | just under 1% | "small proportions" |
| **Carbon dioxide**, CO₂ | about 0.04% | "small proportions" |
| **Water vapour**, H₂O | varies, 0–4% | "small proportions" |

**Using ratios, fractions and percentages (MS 1c):** "four-fifths" is $\frac{4}{5} = 80\%$ and "one-fifth" is $\frac{1}{5} = 20\%$. In a 500 cm³ sample of air there is about $\frac{4}{5} \times 500 = 400$ cm³ of nitrogen and about $\frac{1}{5} \times 500 = 100$ cm³ of oxygen. The ratio of nitrogen to oxygen is about 4 : 1.

**Measuring the oxygen in air.** A classic experiment passes a known volume of air backwards and forwards between two gas syringes over heated copper. The copper reacts with the oxygen ($2\text{Cu} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CuO}$, pink copper turning black), so the gas volume goes down. If 100 cm³ of air shrinks to 79 cm³ after cooling, the oxygen was $100 - 79 = 21$ cm³, i.e. **21%**. The apparatus must be cooled to room temperature before the final reading because warm gas has a larger volume.

### The Earth's early atmosphere

Theories about the early atmosphere have **changed and developed over time**. **Evidence is limited** because of the huge time scale (4.6 billion years): no one was there to measure it, very few rocks survive from that time, and the gases have long since changed. Scientists use indirect evidence such as the chemistry of ancient rocks, the gases from present-day volcanoes and the atmospheres of other planets.

The theory AQA teaches (you need **no other theories**):

1. During the **first billion years**, there was **intense volcanic activity**. Volcanoes released the gases that formed the early atmosphere.
2. Volcanoes released lots of **water vapour**, which **condensed** as the Earth cooled to form the **oceans**.
3. The early atmosphere was probably like the atmospheres of **Mars and Venus** today: **mainly carbon dioxide with little or no oxygen**.
4. Volcanoes also produced **nitrogen**, which **gradually built up** in the atmosphere (nitrogen is very unreactive, so it was not removed).
5. There may have been **small proportions of methane and ammonia**.

| Early atmosphere (theory) | Atmosphere today |
|---|---|
| mainly CO₂ | about 0.04% CO₂ |
| little or no O₂ | about 21% O₂ |
| some N₂, building up | about 78% N₂ |
| water vapour (most condensed to form oceans) | small, varying amount |
| small amounts of CH₄ and NH₃ | traces only |

**Why compare with Mars and Venus?** Both planets have atmospheres that are about 95% carbon dioxide with some nitrogen and almost no oxygen. They have had volcanoes but no life and no liquid oceans, so their atmospheres may show what the Earth's looked like before oceans and photosynthesis changed it.

### How carbon dioxide first decreased: the oceans

When the **oceans formed**, **carbon dioxide dissolved in the water**. Dissolved carbon dioxide reacted to form **carbonates**, which were **precipitated**, producing **sediments** on the sea bed. This removed a large amount of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

### How oxygen increased

**Algae and plants** produced the oxygen now in the atmosphere by **photosynthesis**:

$$\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}$$

$$6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$$

- **Algae first produced oxygen about 2.7 billion years ago**, and soon after this oxygen appeared in the atmosphere.
- Over the **next billion years plants evolved** and the percentage of oxygen **gradually increased** to a level that **enabled animals to evolve** (animals need oxygen for aerobic respiration).

A simple demonstration (WS 1.2): put pondweed in water under a lamp, collect the gas in an inverted test tube and test it with a **glowing splint, which relights** — showing aquatic plants produce oxygen in daylight.

### How carbon dioxide decreased further

Carbon dioxide fell from "mainly CO₂" to about 0.04% because of:

1. **Photosynthesis** by algae and plants, which took in carbon dioxide (see the equation above).
2. **Dissolving in the oceans** and precipitation of carbonates (above).
3. The **formation of sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels** that contain carbon, which locked carbon away underground.

### Formation of limestone, coal, crude oil and natural gas

| Deposit | How it formed | Carbon locked away as |
|---|---|---|
| **Limestone** (sedimentary rock) | Marine organisms used carbon dioxide (as dissolved carbonate) to make **shells and skeletons of calcium carbonate**. When they died these settled on the sea bed, were buried by sediment and **compressed over millions of years** into rock. Precipitated carbonates added to these sediments. | calcium carbonate, CaCO₃ |
| **Coal** | **Plant material** (trees, ferns in swamp forests) died and was buried before it could decay fully (little oxygen). Layers of sediment **compressed** it and **heat and pressure** over millions of years turned it into coal. | mostly carbon |
| **Crude oil** and **natural gas** | Tiny **plankton** (marine plants and animals) died and settled in **mud** on the sea bed, where there was **no oxygen** so they did not fully decay. Burial under more layers gave **heat and pressure** over millions of years, forming crude oil and natural gas (mainly methane), trapped in porous rock under impermeable rock. | hydrocarbons |

In each case the carbon originally came from **carbon dioxide in the atmosphere**, taken in by photosynthesis (or used to make shells). Burying the remains removed the carbon from the atmosphere for millions of years — which is why burning fossil fuels today puts it back (topic 4.9.2).

### Summary timeline

| Time ago | Main event | Effect on atmosphere |
|---|---|---|
| 4.6 billion years | Earth forms | — |
| first billion years | intense volcanic activity | mainly CO₂, water vapour, some N₂, little or no O₂ |
| as Earth cooled | water vapour condenses to oceans | CO₂ dissolves; carbonates precipitate |
| about 2.7 billion years | algae photosynthesise | O₂ appears; CO₂ falls |
| next billion years | plants evolve | O₂ rises gradually; animals can evolve |
| hundreds of millions of years | sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels form | CO₂ locked away |
| last 200 million years | — | proportions roughly as today |

### Evaluating theories (WS 1.1–1.3, 3.5, 3.6, 4.1)

You may be given information about a different theory or a piece of evidence and asked to **evaluate** it. Useful points:

- **Limited evidence** — few rocks from that time; no direct measurement; so theories are uncertain and may change.
- A theory is **stronger** if it explains more of the evidence (for example, banded iron rocks that contain iron oxide formed about the time oxygen first appeared, which supports oxygen coming from early algae).
- Evidence from **other planets** (Mars, Venus) is indirect — they are different sizes and distances from the Sun.
- Theories are accepted when evidence is **repeatable** and has been **peer reviewed**; new evidence can lead to a theory being changed.

::: terms
- **Atmosphere**: the layer of gases surrounding the Earth, today about 80% nitrogen and 20% oxygen.
- **Early atmosphere**: the atmosphere in the Earth's first billion years, thought to be mainly carbon dioxide with little or no oxygen.
- **Volcanic activity**: eruptions that released carbon dioxide, water vapour, nitrogen and small amounts of methane and ammonia.
- **Photosynthesis**: the reaction in algae and plants that uses light to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.
- **Sedimentary rock**: rock such as limestone formed when layers of sediment are compressed over millions of years.
- **Fossil fuel**: coal, crude oil or natural gas, formed from the buried remains of organisms and containing carbon.
- **Precipitate**: an insoluble solid formed in a solution, as carbonates did when carbon dioxide dissolved in the oceans.
- **Algae**: simple aquatic organisms that photosynthesise; they first produced oxygen about 2.7 billion years ago.
:::

::: method
**Describe the changes over time:** give a clear sequence with the gas, the direction of change and the cause. "Carbon dioxide decreased because it dissolved in the oceans, was used in photosynthesis by algae and plants, and was locked up in sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels." Always name the process, not just "it went away".

**Explain the oxygen increase:** algae (from 2.7 billion years ago) and then plants **photosynthesised**, releasing oxygen. Give the word equation if asked; at GCSE the balanced equation is $6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$.

**Describe and explain deposits:** for limestone say **shells/skeletons of marine organisms made of calcium carbonate**; for coal say **plant material**; for crude oil and gas say **plankton buried in mud**. Then add **buried, compressed, heat and pressure, millions of years, little oxygen so no complete decay**.

**Percentages and fractions (MS 1c):** "four-fifths" = 80%, "one-fifth" = 20%. For a sample of volume V, nitrogen ≈ 0.8V and oxygen ≈ 0.2V. From an experiment: percentage of oxygen = (volume decrease ÷ starting volume) × 100.

**Evaluate a theory:** say what evidence **supports** it, what is **uncertain** (limited evidence over 4.6 billion years, no direct measurement), and finish with a **judgement**. Do not just repeat the theory.

**6-mark questions** ("Describe how the Earth's atmosphere has changed since it formed"): Level 3 answers cover the **early atmosphere** (volcanoes; CO₂, water vapour, N₂; little/no O₂), the **oceans** (condensation; CO₂ dissolving; carbonate sediments), **oxygen** (algae, plants, photosynthesis) and **carbon dioxide removal** (photosynthesis, sedimentary rocks, fossil fuels), in a logical order, ending with today's 80% N₂ / 20% O₂.
:::

::: example Proportions of gases
A sample of dry air has a volume of 250 cm³. Estimate the volume of nitrogen and of oxygen in the sample. (2)

Nitrogen is about four-fifths: $\frac{4}{5} \times 250 = 200$ cm³ (1). Oxygen is about one-fifth: $\frac{1}{5} \times 250 = 50$ cm³ (1).
:::

::: example Oxygen experiment
A student passes 120 cm³ of air over heated copper until the volume stops changing. After cooling, 95 cm³ of gas remains. Calculate the percentage of oxygen in the air and suggest why it differs from 21%. (3)

Volume of oxygen = 120 − 95 = 25 cm³ (1). Percentage = $\frac{25}{120} \times 100 = 20.8\%$, i.e. 21% to 2 significant figures (1). (If the result had been lower, likely reasons: not enough copper / not passed over enough times so not all the oxygen reacted, or the gas was not cooled to room temperature before reading; a leak in the apparatus would also give an error.) (1)
:::

::: example Where the water came from
Explain how the oceans formed. (2)

Volcanoes released water vapour into the early atmosphere (1). As the Earth cooled, the water vapour condensed to form the oceans (1).
:::

::: example Formation of coal
Describe how coal was formed and explain how this affected the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. (4)

Plants (such as trees in swamp forests) took in carbon dioxide for photosynthesis (1). When they died they were buried before they could decay completely (1). Over millions of years, heat and pressure from layers of sediment turned the plant material into coal (1). The carbon from the carbon dioxide was locked up underground in the coal, so the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere decreased (1).
:::

::: q
The atmosphere of Venus is about 96% carbon dioxide and 3.5% nitrogen. Suggest why scientists think the Earth's early atmosphere was similar. (2)
---
The Earth and Venus both had intense volcanic activity that released mainly carbon dioxide (and nitrogen) (1). Venus has no oceans and no photosynthesising organisms to remove carbon dioxide or produce oxygen, so its atmosphere may show what the Earth's was like before these changes (1).

::: watch
- **Getting the direction wrong.** Carbon dioxide went **down**; oxygen went **up**; nitrogen **built up**. Many answers say oxygen came from volcanoes — it did not; it came from **photosynthesis**.
- **Vague causes.** "Plants used it up" is weak. Say **algae and plants absorbed carbon dioxide for photosynthesis** and **released oxygen**.
- **Forgetting the oceans.** Carbon dioxide **dissolved** in the oceans and formed **carbonate sediments** — a separate cause from photosynthesis.
- **Muddling the fossil fuels.** Coal comes from **plants**; crude oil and natural gas come from **plankton** buried in **mud**; limestone comes from **shells and skeletons**.
- **Dropping the time scale.** Formation of rocks and fuels takes **millions of years** of **heat and pressure** — include it.
- **Percentages in a gas-syringe experiment.** Divide the **decrease** by the **starting** volume, not by the final volume.
:::

::: test
A 150 cm³ sample of air is passed repeatedly over hot copper. The final volume (at room temperature) is 119 cm³. (a) Calculate the percentage of oxygen in the sample. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. (b) Suggest why the gas must be cooled before the final volume is read. (3)
---
(a) Decrease = 150 − 119 = 31 cm³ (1). Percentage = 31 ÷ 150 × 100 = 20.67 = **21%** (1). (b) Hot gas expands / has a larger volume, so reading it hot would make the decrease (and the oxygen percentage) seem too small (1).
:::

Over millions of years, heat and pressure turned them into crude oil and natural gas (1). The carbon they had taken in (originally from atmospheric carbon dioxide via photosynthesis) was locked underground, reducing carbon dioxide in the atmosphere (1).

::: test
Some scientists suggested that much of the Earth's water came from comets hitting the Earth, rather than from volcanoes. Measurements show that the water in some comets has a different composition (ratio of hydrogen isotopes) from the water in the Earth's oceans. Evaluate what this evidence shows about the comet theory. (3)
---
The evidence does not support comets as the main source of the Earth's water, because the comet water is different from ocean water (1). However, only some comets have been measured, so other comets could still match / the sample is small (1). Conclusion: the evidence makes the comet theory less likely as the main source, but more data is needed; it is consistent with the volcano theory (1).
