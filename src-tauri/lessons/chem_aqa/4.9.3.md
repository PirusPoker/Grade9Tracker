## What it is

This subsection is in **both** GCSE Chemistry 8462 and Combined Science: Trilogy 8464 (Trilogy 5.9.3). There is no Higher-tier-only content. It covers which pollutants are produced when **fuels burn**, **how** each is formed, and the **problems** each causes.

### What is in a fuel?

The **combustion of fuels is a major source of atmospheric pollutants.** Most fuels, including **coal**, petrol, diesel and natural gas, contain **carbon and/or hydrogen** (hydrocarbons) and **may also contain some sulfur** as an impurity.

When a fuel burns, the gases released into the atmosphere may include:

- **carbon dioxide** and **water vapour** (the normal products)
- **carbon monoxide**
- **sulfur dioxide**
- **oxides of nitrogen** (NO and NO₂, written NOₓ)

**Solid particles** and **unburned hydrocarbons** may also be released, forming **particulates** in the atmosphere.

### Complete and incomplete combustion

| | Complete combustion | Incomplete combustion |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen supply | **plentiful** | **limited** (insufficient oxygen) |
| Carbon in the fuel becomes | **carbon dioxide**, CO₂ | **carbon monoxide**, CO, and/or **carbon (soot)**, C |
| Hydrogen in the fuel becomes | water, H₂O | water, H₂O |
| Flame | blue, hot | yellow, smoky, less heat released |

Equations for methane:

$$\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad \text{(complete)}$$

$$2\text{CH}_4 + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad \text{(incomplete — carbon monoxide)}$$

$$\text{CH}_4 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{C} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad \text{(incomplete — soot)}$$

### How each pollutant is produced

| Pollutant | How it is produced when fuels burn |
|---|---|
| **Carbon monoxide**, CO | **incomplete combustion** of a carbon-containing fuel in a **limited supply of oxygen** (e.g. a car engine, a faulty gas boiler or a blocked flue) |
| **Soot** (carbon particles) | **incomplete combustion** — some carbon in the fuel is not oxidised at all and is released as solid particles |
| **Sulfur dioxide**, SO₂ | **sulfur impurities in the fuel** react with oxygen when the fuel burns: $\text{S} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{SO}_2$ (coal and diesel are the main sources) |
| **Oxides of nitrogen**, NOₓ | **nitrogen and oxygen from the air** react together at the **high temperatures** in engines and furnaces: $\text{N}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{NO}$, then $2\text{NO} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{NO}_2$ |
| **Particulates** | soot and **unburned hydrocarbons**, especially from diesel engines and burning coal or wood |

**The key point about NOₓ:** the nitrogen does **not** come from the fuel. It comes from the **air**. Nitrogen is normally very unreactive, but the **high temperature** of combustion provides enough energy for it to react with oxygen. So even a fuel with no nitrogen in it — such as hydrogen — can produce oxides of nitrogen if it burns hot in air.

### Predicting the products of combustion (WS 1.2)

Given the **composition of a fuel** and the **conditions**, work out the products element by element:

| Element in fuel | Plenty of oxygen | Limited oxygen |
|---|---|---|
| carbon | CO₂ | CO and/or C (soot) |
| hydrogen | H₂O | H₂O |
| sulfur | SO₂ | SO₂ |
| (any fuel burned hot in **air**) | NOₓ | NOₓ |

**Mini-examples:**
- Coal (carbon with some sulfur) in a power station with plenty of air: **CO₂, SO₂, NOₓ** (plus soot/particulates if combustion is not perfect).
- Petrol (hydrocarbons) in a car engine with limited air: **CO₂, CO, H₂O, NOₓ**, soot and unburned hydrocarbons.
- Hydrogen in a fuel cell or engine: **water** only — plus NOₓ if burned at high temperature in air.
- Natural gas (methane, sulfur removed) in a well-ventilated cooker: **CO₂ and H₂O**.

### Properties and effects of the pollutants

| Pollutant | Properties | Problems caused |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon monoxide** | **toxic**, **colourless and odourless** — so it is **not easily detected** (CO detectors are needed) | combines with **haemoglobin** in red blood cells instead of oxygen, reducing the blood's ability to carry oxygen; causes drowsiness, unconsciousness and death |
| **Sulfur dioxide** | acidic gas, dissolves in water | **respiratory problems** in humans (e.g. triggering asthma); forms **acid rain** |
| **Oxides of nitrogen** | acidic gases | **respiratory problems**; form **acid rain**; contribute to photochemical smog |
| **Particulates** (soot, unburned hydrocarbons) | tiny solid particles that stay in the air | **global dimming** (they reflect sunlight back into space, so less reaches the surface); **health problems** — they are breathed deep into the **lungs**, causing lung damage, worsening asthma, and linked to heart disease and cancer |

### Acid rain

Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen **dissolve in water droplets in clouds** and react to form **sulfuric acid** and **nitric acid**, which fall as **acid rain**. Acid rain:

- damages **trees and plants** (kills leaves, removes nutrients from soil)
- makes **lakes and rivers acidic**, killing fish and other aquatic life
- **corrodes buildings and statues** made of **limestone** (calcium carbonate reacts with acid) and **metal** structures.

### Reducing pollution (context)

You do not need to learn these, but they help in "suggest" questions:
- **Catalytic converters** in cars convert CO to CO₂ and NOₓ to N₂.
- **Removing sulfur** from petrol and diesel before sale; using **low-sulfur fuels**.
- **Flue gas desulfurisation** in power stations — sulfur dioxide is removed from waste gases (e.g. with calcium carbonate or calcium oxide).
- **Particulate filters** on diesel exhausts.
- Making sure appliances have **enough air** (ventilation, servicing gas boilers) to avoid incomplete combustion.

::: terms
- **Atmospheric pollutant**: a substance released into the air that harms people or the environment, such as sulfur dioxide.
- **Complete combustion**: burning a fuel in plenty of oxygen so that carbon forms carbon dioxide and hydrogen forms water.
- **Incomplete combustion**: burning a fuel in a limited supply of oxygen, producing carbon monoxide and/or soot.
- **Carbon monoxide**: a toxic, colourless and odourless gas formed by incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels.
- **Oxides of nitrogen**: gases such as NO and NO₂ formed when nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at high temperatures.
- **Particulates**: tiny solid particles of soot and unburned hydrocarbons released into the air when fuels burn.
- **Acid rain**: rain made acidic when sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen dissolve in water droplets in clouds.
- **Global dimming**: a reduction in the sunlight reaching the Earth's surface because particulates reflect it.
:::

::: method
**"Describe how ... is produced":** always give the **source** and the **condition**.
- CO and soot → **incomplete combustion**, **limited oxygen**, carbon in the fuel.
- SO₂ → **sulfur impurities in the fuel** react with oxygen.
- NOₓ → **nitrogen and oxygen from the air** react at **high temperature** (in engines). The nitrogen is not from the fuel.

**"Predict the products":** go through the elements in the fuel one at a time (C, H, S), then decide whether oxygen is plentiful or limited, then add NOₓ if it burns hot in air. List every product.

**"Describe and explain the problems":** name the problem **and** explain it.
- CO — toxic; colourless and odourless so not detected; reduces oxygen carried by the blood.
- SO₂ and NOₓ — respiratory problems; dissolve in rainwater to form acid rain; acid rain damages trees, lakes and buildings.
- Particulates — global dimming (reflect sunlight); lung and health problems.

**Equations:** you may be asked to balance a combustion equation. Balance C first, then H, then O last. For incomplete combustion, count oxygen atoms carefully (each CO has one O).

**Extended responses (6 marks):** e.g. "Compare the pollutants from a diesel car and a hydrogen-powered car." Structure it pollutant by pollutant: what is produced, how, and its effect — then make a comparison and a conclusion.
:::

::: example Describing production
Describe how sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen are produced when diesel is burned in a car engine. (4)

Diesel contains sulfur as an impurity (1), which reacts with oxygen when the fuel burns to form sulfur dioxide (1). The high temperature in the engine (1) causes nitrogen and oxygen from the air to react, forming oxides of nitrogen (1).
:::

::: example Predicting products
A fuel contains carbon, hydrogen and a small amount of sulfur. It burns in a boiler with a limited supply of air. Predict the products. (3)

Carbon monoxide and/or carbon (soot), possibly some carbon dioxide (1); water vapour (1); sulfur dioxide (1). (Oxides of nitrogen may also form at high temperature.)
:::

::: example Balancing incomplete combustion
Balance the equation for the incomplete combustion of propane to form carbon monoxide and water: C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO + H₂O. (2)

Carbon: 3CO. Hydrogen: 8 H → 4H₂O. Oxygen on the right = 3 + 4 = 7 atoms, so $\frac{7}{2}$O₂; double everything:
$$2\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 + 7\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 6\text{CO} + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
(1 for correct formulae and products, 1 for balancing.)
:::

::: example Carbon monoxide danger
Explain why carbon monoxide from a faulty gas boiler is particularly dangerous. (3)

The boiler has a limited supply of air, so incomplete combustion produces carbon monoxide (1). Carbon monoxide is toxic — it reduces the amount of oxygen the blood can carry (1). It is colourless and odourless, so people cannot detect it without a carbon monoxide detector (1).
:::

::: watch
- **NOₓ from the fuel.** The most common mistake. Nitrogen comes from the **air** and reacts with oxygen at **high temperature**.
- **Mixing up acid rain and global warming.** SO₂ and NOₓ cause **acid rain**; CO₂ and methane cause **global warming**; particulates cause **global dimming**. Do not swap them.
- **Saying CO "causes breathing problems".** Carbon monoxide is **toxic** because it stops blood carrying oxygen; it is colourless and odourless. Respiratory problems are SO₂, NOₓ and particulates.
- **Forgetting the condition.** Carbon monoxide needs **limited oxygen** (incomplete combustion); SO₂ needs **sulfur in the fuel**.
- **Leaving out products.** When predicting products, include water and, if there is sulfur, SO₂.
:::

Incomplete combustion of a carbon-containing fuel (1) in a limited supply of oxygen (1). It is toxic — it reduces the amount of oxygen carried by the blood (and can be fatal) (1).

Balance the equation for the incomplete combustion of methane to produce carbon: CH₄ + O₂ → C + H₂O. (1)

::: test
Explain how burning coal in power stations can lead to damage to limestone buildings far away. (4)
---
Sulfur impurities in coal react with oxygen to form sulfur dioxide (1). Sulfur dioxide is carried by the wind and dissolves in water droplets in clouds (1), forming sulfuric acid / acid rain (1). The acid rain reacts with the calcium carbonate in limestone, wearing the buildings away (1).
:::

::: test
Diesel engines produce more particulates than petrol engines. Explain the health and environmental problems caused by particulates. (3)
---
Particulates are breathed in deep into the lungs (1), causing lung damage/respiratory problems (accept heart disease, cancer) (1). They reflect sunlight, causing global dimming (1).
:::

::: test
Hydrogen can be used as a fuel in car engines. A student says "a hydrogen car produces no pollution at all." Evaluate this statement. (3)
---
Hydrogen burns to form only water, so there is no carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, soot or sulfur dioxide from the exhaust (1). However, hydrogen burns at a high temperature in air, so nitrogen and oxygen from the air can still react to form oxides of nitrogen (1). Also, the hydrogen may have been made using fossil fuels, releasing pollutants elsewhere — so the statement is not fully correct (1).
:::

::: test
Fuel A is natural gas (methane, sulfur removed) burned in plenty of air. Fuel B is a high-sulfur diesel burned in an engine with a limited air supply. Compare the pollutants produced by the two fuels and the problems they cause. (6)
---
Levels of response. **Level 3 (5–6):** a detailed comparison covering most pollutants from both fuels, explaining how each forms and its effects, with a clear conclusion. **Level 2 (3–4):** identifies several pollutants from each fuel with some explanation of formation or effects. **Level 1 (1–2):** names some pollutants or effects with little explanation or comparison.

Indicative content: Fuel A — complete combustion gives carbon dioxide and water; no sulfur so no sulfur dioxide; little or no carbon monoxide or soot; some NOₓ if the flame is hot; CO₂ is a greenhouse gas. Fuel B — limited air means incomplete combustion: carbon monoxide (toxic, colourless, odourless, reduces oxygen carried by blood) and soot/particulates (global dimming, lung damage); sulfur impurities react with oxygen to give sulfur dioxide (respiratory problems, acid rain); high engine temperature makes nitrogen and oxygen from air form NOₓ (respiratory problems, acid rain); also CO₂ and water; unburned hydrocarbons. Conclusion: fuel B produces many more harmful pollutants; fuel A is cleaner but still releases CO₂.
:::
