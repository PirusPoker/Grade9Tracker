## What it is

This lesson covers the first block of Edexcel's grammar list (Appendix 2 of the 1FR1 spec): **nouns and articles**. They are small words that carry a lot of marks.
- **Paper 4, Q3 (translation into French, 10 marks):** "Health is important" needs *la santé*; "I don't have a bike" needs *pas de vélo*. Each slip is an accuracy error, and several of them pull AO3 down a band.
- **Paper 3 (Reading):** *pas de*, *aucun* or *ne... jamais de* is often the only word that tells you something is **missing**, and the A/B/C distractor is built on it.
- **Paper 2 (Dictation):** *le* and *les*, *du* and *des* sound different. The plural -s on the noun is silent, so the article tells you what to write.
- **Papers 1 and 4 (Speaking and Writing):** every noun you produce carries an article, and Pearson's AO3 grids reward accuracy.

**What Edexcel lists.** Everything below is examinable. The right-hand column is Higher only.

| Foundation + Higher | Higher only |
|---|---|
| feminine nouns: add -e; no change; -eur → -rice / -euse; -en → -enne | the partitive with uncountable nouns (*du lait*) |
| plurals: add -s; add -x after -eau / -eu; no change after -s / -x | *dans* + article, but *en* with no article (*dans le restaurant*, *en France*) |
| the infinitive as a noun, for English -ing | the negative determiner *aucun(e)* (*je n'ai aucune idée*) |
| articles agree in gender and number; *le / la* → *l'* | |
| article uses that differ from English (*la santé*, *le mercredi*) | |
| article + adjective used as a noun (*le seul*, *l'anglais*, *les Français*) | |
| the partitive for part and whole; *jouer du piano*; *faire de la danse* | |
| *de* after a negative and after quantities; *d'* before a vowel | |

**1. Feminine nouns.** Every noun is masculine or feminine, so learn each one with its article. Nouns for people usually have a feminine form, made by one of four patterns.

| Pattern | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| add -e | un ami, un voisin, un Français | une amie, une voisine, une Française |
| no change | un élève, un touriste, un enfant | une élève, une touriste, une enfant |
| -eur → -rice | un acteur, un directeur | une actrice, une directrice |
| -eur → -euse | un chanteur, un vendeur | une chanteuse, une vendeuse |
| -en → -enne | un Canadien, un musicien | une Canadienne, une musicienne |

**Which -eur pattern?** If the noun comes straight from a French verb (*chanter* → *chanteur*, *vendre* → *vendeur*, *danser* → *danseur*), it usually takes **-euse**. Most others take **-rice** (*acteur*, *directeur*). Learn the pairs you need. The list gives *médecin* and *professeur* as (m, f): *ma mère est médecin*.

**2. Plurals.**
- **Add -s** (silent): *un livre, des livres*.
- **Add -x** after **-eau** and **-eu**: *un gâteau, des gâteaux*; *un château, des châteaux*; *un jeu, des jeux*; *les cheveux*.
- **No change** after **-s** or **-x** (and -z): *un prix, des prix*; *un fils, des fils*; *un repas, des repas*; *un nez, des nez*.
- *Un animal, des animaux* and *les yeux* are irregular. Learn them as vocabulary; they are both on the list.

**3. The articles.**

| | masculine | feminine | before a vowel or silent h | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the | le | la | l' | les |
| a, an | un | une | un / une | des (some) |
| some, any (partitive) | du | de la | de l' | des |

*Le* and *la* become **l'** before a vowel or a silent h: *l'ami, l'école, l'hôtel, l'histoire*. *Les* never elides, but you hear a z-liaison: *les‿amis*. A few h words do not elide (*le hockey*), but they are rare.

**4. Where French and English differ.** French uses the **definite article** where English has none:
- **general statements and abstract nouns:** *La santé est importante.* *Les jeunes aiment la musique.* (Health is important. Young people like music.)
- **after verbs of liking**, even in the negative: *j'adore le chocolat*, *je déteste les maths*, *je n'aime pas le café*;
- **days and times for a habit:** *le mercredi* = on Wednesdays, *le soir* = in the evenings, *le week-end* = at weekends. Without *le*, *mercredi* means one particular Wednesday;
- **languages and countries:** *le français est utile*, *la France*, *le Canada*;
- **body parts:** *il a les yeux bleus*, *j'ai mal à la tête*.

And French **drops** the article where English has one: a job after *être* (*mon père est professeur* = my father is a teacher), and *deux fois par semaine* (twice **a** week).

**5. Article + adjective = a noun.** *le seul / la seule* (the only one), *le premier / la première* (the first one), *les jeunes* (young people), *le plus important* (the most important thing). **Languages** are the masculine adjective with *le*, a small letter and no agreement: *l'anglais, le français*. **Nationalities as people** take a capital letter: *les Français* (French people), *une Canadienne*. As adjectives they stay small: *il est français*.

**6. The partitive: part or whole.** *Du, de la, de l', des* mean "some" or "any", and English often leaves them out.
- **Part versus whole:** *je voudrais un pain* (a loaf) / *je mange du pain* (some bread, bread); *un gâteau* (a whole cake) / *du gâteau* (some cake).
- **Jouer de + instrument:** *jouer du piano, jouer de la guitare*. Compare *jouer au foot*: *à* for sports and games.
- **Faire de + activity:** *faire du sport, du vélo, de la danse, de la natation*.
- **Higher: uncountable nouns.** *Du lait, de l'eau, du sucre, de l'argent, du temps, du courage, de la chance*. *Pour réussir, il faut du courage.*

**7. De after negatives and quantities.** After a negative verb, *un, une, des, du, de la* and *de l'* all become **de** (**d'** before a vowel):
- *J'ai un stylo.* → *Je n'ai pas de stylo.*
- *Il y a des magasins.* → *Il n'y a pas de magasins.*
- *Je bois du café.* → *Je ne bois jamais de café.*
- *J'ai de l'argent.* → *Je n'ai plus d'argent.*

**Two things do not change.** The definite article stays: *je n'aime pas le café*. And after *être* the article stays: *ce n'est pas un problème*, *ce ne sont pas des amis*.

After **quantities**, use *de* with no article: *beaucoup de choses, trop de devoirs, assez de temps, (un) peu de sucre, plus de magasins, moins de bruit, combien de personnes ?*, and containers: *une bouteille d'eau, un verre de lait*. Before a vowel: *beaucoup d'animaux*. The Higher noun *la plupart* keeps *des*: *la plupart des élèves*.

**8. Higher: dans or en.** *Dans* takes an article (or *mon*, *ce*...): *dans le restaurant, dans la cuisine, dans ma chambre*. *En* takes **no** article: *en France, en ville, en classe, en été, en voiture*. *Dans France* is wrong. Masculine countries take *au* (*au Canada*), which is covered in G6.

**9. Higher: aucun(e).** *Ne... aucun(e)* + a **singular** noun means "no / not a single / not any". It agrees with the noun and replaces the article: *Je n'ai aucune idée* (*idée* is feminine). *Il n'y a aucun problème.* It can also be the subject, still with *ne*: *Aucun élève n'est venu.*

**10. The infinitive as a noun.** Where English uses -ing as the subject or object, French uses the **infinitive**: *Manger des légumes est bon pour la santé* (Eating vegetables is good for your health). *Lire, c'est ma passion* (Reading is my passion). The verb after it is third person singular, and any adjective is masculine singular: *Voyager est cher.* Never use the -ant form here: *en mangeant* means "by eating" (G3e).

::: terms
- **le genre**: gender; every noun is masculine or feminine, so learn it with le or la
- **l'article défini**: le, la, l', les = the; also used for general statements, likes and habits
- **l'article indéfini**: un, une = a, an; des = some (plural of un/une)
- **l'article partitif**: du, de la, de l', des = some or any, part of a whole: je mange du pain
- **l'élision**: le, la, de become l' and d' before a vowel or silent h: l'hôtel, pas d'argent
- **pas de**: after a negative verb, un/une/des/du/de la all become de: je n'ai pas de frère
- **l'expression de quantité**: beaucoup de, trop de, assez de, peu de + noun with no article
- **le pluriel en -x**: nouns in -eau and -eu add -x: des gâteaux, des jeux
- **l'infinitif comme nom**: the infinitive for English -ing as a subject: Lire est intéressant
- **l'adjectif nominalisé**: le/la + adjective as a noun: le seul, l'anglais, les Français (capital for people)
- **dans / en**: Higher; dans + article (dans le restaurant), en with no article (en France)
- **aucun(e)**: Higher; ne... aucun(e) + singular noun = not a single: je n'ai aucune idée
:::

::: method
1. **Find the noun.** Masculine or feminine? Singular or plural? Plural nouns add -s, or -x after -eau / -eu, and stay the same after -s / -x.
2. **Decide what you mean.** A particular thing, things in general, or something you like: *le, la, l', les*. One whole item: *un, une*. Some, any, an amount: *du, de la, de l', des*.
3. **Look for a negative.** After *ne... pas / jamais / plus*, change any indefinite or partitive article to *de / d'*. Keep *le / la / les*, and keep the article after *être*.
4. **Look for a quantity.** After *beaucoup, trop, assez, peu, plus, moins, combien* or a container, use *de / d'* with no article.
5. **Check the French-only uses.** *Le* + day for a habit, the article with abstract nouns, no article with a job after *être*, *jouer du / faire de la*.
6. **Higher.** *Dans* + article or *en* with none. *Aucun(e)* agrees and is singular.
7. **Elide and agree.** *L'* and *d'* before a vowel. Make adjectives agree with the noun you chose.
:::

::: example Translation into French: four article decisions (2)
Translate into French: "Health is important to me, so on Mondays I go swimming, but I never eat chips."

1. "Health" is an abstract noun used generally: **la santé**, and *importante* agrees.
2. "On Mondays" is a habit: **le lundi**.
3. "Go swimming" is *faire* + activity: **de la natation**.
4. "Never eat chips" is a negative, so *des frites* becomes **de frites**.

**Answer:** *La santé est importante pour moi, donc le lundi, je fais de la natation, mais je ne mange jamais de frites.*

Leaving out *la* or writing *jamais des frites* would each cost accuracy. In Pearson's terms they are minor errors, because the meaning is clear, but a run of them pulls AO3 down.
:::

::: example One noun, five articles (5)
*Le chocolat* changes with the meaning, which is exactly what translation questions test.
- *J'adore **le** chocolat.* = I love chocolate. (liking: definite article)
- *Je mange **du** chocolat le soir.* = I eat chocolate in the evening. (some: partitive)
- *Ma mère ne mange **pas de** chocolat.* = My mother doesn't eat chocolate. (negative: de)
- *Il y a **trop de** chocolat dans ce gâteau.* = There is too much chocolate in this cake. (quantity: de)
- *Je voudrais **une boîte de** chocolats.* = I'd like a box of chocolates. (container: de, and the noun is plural)

In the negative, the liking verb keeps its article: *je n'aime pas **le** chocolat*.
:::

::: example Feminine and plural forms by rule (6)
Give the feminine of: *un chanteur, un directeur, un Canadien, un voisin, un touriste*. Give the plural of: *le jeu, le prix*.

- *une chanteuse* (from *chanter*, so -euse)
- *une directrice* (-eur → -rice)
- *une Canadienne* (-en → -enne; a capital letter for a person)
- *une voisine* (add -e)
- *une touriste* (no change)
- *les jeux* (-eu + x)
- *les prix* (no change after -x)

Each rule gets 1 mark in a drill, and the same rules decide your spellings in the dictation.
:::

::: example Higher: partitive, dans / en and aucun (4)
Translate into French: (a) We spend the summer in France. (b) My brother is in the kitchen. (c) To succeed, you need courage. (d) There isn't a single shop in my village.

(a) *Nous passons l'été en France.* (1) *En* with no article.
(b) *Mon frère est dans la cuisine.* (1) *Dans* with an article.
(c) *Pour réussir, il faut du courage.* (1) A partitive with an uncountable noun.
(d) *Il n'y a aucun magasin dans mon village.* (1) *Aucun* is masculine singular to match *magasin*. *Il n'y a pas de magasin* gets the meaning but loses "single".
:::

::: example Reading: the article tells you the answer (2)
*Le samedi, Yanis fait du vélo avec son père. Il n'a pas de cours, mais ce n'est pas un jour calme : il a beaucoup de devoirs.*

What does Yanis do **on Saturdays**? Why is it **not** a quiet day?

- *Le samedi* is every Saturday, not one Saturday: he goes cycling with his dad (1).
- *Il n'a pas de cours* = he has no lessons. *Ce n'est pas un jour calme* keeps *un* after *être*. *Beaucoup de devoirs* = lots of homework, which is the reason (1).

A careless reader sees *calme* and answers "quiet". The negative is the point of the question.
:::

::: watch
- **Pas des / pas du / pas un** after a negative verb are wrong: it is *pas de* (*d'* before a vowel). Pearson treats these as minor errors, but they pile up.
- **Être keeps its article**: *ce n'est pas un problème*. Writing *ce n'est pas de problème* is wrong.
- **Liking verbs take le / la / les**, in the positive and the negative: *j'aime le lait*, *je n'aime pas le lait*. Never *j'aime du lait*.
- **Abstract and general nouns** need *le / la*: *la santé, la musique, les jeunes*. This is the commonest missing word in translations into French.
- **On Mondays = le lundi**; *lundi* alone is one Monday. Reading questions test the difference.
- **No article with a job** after *être*: *elle est chanteuse*.
- **Languages** are masculine, with a small letter: *le français*. **People** take a capital: *les Français*.
- **The plural -s is silent**, so in the dictation, listen for *le / les* and *du / des*, then make the noun match.
- **Higher**: *en France* but *dans la cuisine*; *aucune idée*, never *aucunes idées*.
:::

::: test
Translate the passage into English.

*Le week-end, ma famille et moi, nous allons souvent à la campagne. Ma mère fait du vélo et mon père fait de la natation dans le lac. Moi, je n'ai pas de vélo, donc je lis beaucoup de livres. Pour nous, la nature est importante, et passer du temps ensemble, c'est le plus important.* (10)
---
Holistic bands. 9–10: the meaning is fully transferred in natural English. 6–8: most of the meaning, with occasional wrong or missing words. 3–5: parts of the meaning, with tense errors or gaps. 1–2: isolated words or phrases.
**Model:** At weekends, my family and I often go to the countryside. My mother goes cycling and my father goes swimming in the lake. As for me, I don't have a bike, so I read lots of books. For us, nature is important, and spending time together is the most important thing.
**Deciding details:** *le week-end* = at weekends, not "this weekend"; *fait du vélo / de la natation* = goes cycling / swimming; *je n'ai pas de vélo* = I don't have a bike; *la nature* = nature, with no "the"; *passer du temps* = spending time (an infinitive as a noun); *le plus important* = the most important thing.

::: test
Translate the paragraph into French.

My sister is a singer. On Saturdays, she plays the guitar in a restaurant in town. Music is very important to her. She doesn't have a car, so she goes everywhere by bus. Last week, there was no bread at home, so I bought two loaves at the market. Singing is her passion, and she has no other job. (10)
---
**AO2 meaning (6):** 5–6 the meaning is transferred consistently, with rare lack of clarity; 3–4 most of it is transferred, with the occasional message unclear or missing; 1–2 some parts are transferred, and the message sometimes breaks down.
**AO3 accuracy (4):** 4 consistently accurate, any errors minor; 3 generally accurate, minor errors, major errors rare; 2 sometimes accurate, including some major errors; 1 limited accuracy, frequent major and minor errors.
**Model:** *Ma sœur est chanteuse. Le samedi, elle joue de la guitare dans un restaurant en ville. La musique est très importante pour elle. Elle n'a pas de voiture, donc elle va partout en bus. La semaine dernière, il n'y avait pas de pain à la maison, donc j'ai acheté deux pains au marché. Chanter est sa passion, et elle n'a aucun autre travail.*
**Key points:** no article with *chanteuse*; *le samedi*; *jouer de la guitare*; *la musique* with the article; *pas de voiture*; *en bus*; *pas de pain* and then *deux pains* (two whole loaves); *Chanter* as the subject; *aucun autre travail* (accept *pas d'autre travail*, and *il n'y a pas eu de pain*). Minor errors: *chanteur* for a woman, *la samedi*, *dans France*-type slips. Major errors: *elle a allé*, *Chantant est sa passion*, *j'ai acheter*.
:::
