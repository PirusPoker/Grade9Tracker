## What it is

This lesson covers four groups of verb structures from Appendix 2, all of which work differently from English:
- **impersonal verbs**, whose subject is an "it" that refers to nothing: *il y a, il fait, il pleut, il faut, il est huit heures*, and at Higher *il est important de, il manque, il vaut mieux, il vaut la peine de*;
- **reflexive verbs**, with a pronoun that matches the subject: *je m'appelle, nous nous entendons bien*, including the reciprocal meaning "each other": *ils se donnent des cadeaux*;
- the **passive** (Higher), in the present with *par*: *La ville est nettoyée par des jeunes*;
- the Higher time expressions **être en train de** (be in the middle of) and **venir de** (have just).

Where they earn marks:
- **Paper 1:** the picture task is built for *il y a* and *ils sont en train de* (both on the list for describing photos). *Il fait beau* places the scene.
- **Paper 4:** *il faut, il vaut mieux* and *il est important de* + infinitive give you ready-made "complex" opinions for Q2's "what makes a good..." bullet. A reflexive pronoun that does not match its subject (*je veux s'amuser*) is listed by Pearson as a **major error**.
- **Papers 2 and 3:** *il vient de partir* (he has just left) and *elle est en train de...* are classic time-frame traps, and the passive reverses who does what.

**1. Impersonal verbs (Foundation + Higher).** The subject is always *il*, which means "it" or "there".

| Structure | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| il y a / il y avait / il y aura | there is, are / there was, were, used to be / there will be | *Il y a un parc. Avant, il y avait une usine. Il y aura une piscine.* |
| il fait + adjective | weather | *Il fait beau, chaud, froid, mauvais.* |
| il + weather verb | weather | *Il pleut. Il neige.* (*Il a plu* = it rained.) |
| il faut + infinitive | you have to, we must, it is necessary to | *Il faut recycler. Il faut arriver à l'heure.* |
| il est + time | it is (time) | *Il est huit heures. Il est midi. Il est trois heures et demie.* |

*Il y a* + a time also means "ago": *il y a deux ans* = two years ago. *Il faut* has no person in it; *il ne faut pas* means "you must not": *Il ne faut pas fumer.*

**2. Impersonal verbs (Higher).**
- **il est + adjective + de + infinitive**: *Il est important de dormir. Il est difficile de trouver un emploi. Il est possible de réserver en ligne.*
- **il manque + noun** = something is missing / lacking: *Il manque deux élèves* (two pupils are missing). *Dans ma ville, il manque un cinéma* (my town lacks a cinema).
- **il vaut mieux + infinitive** = it's better to: *Il vaut mieux prendre le train.*
- **il vaut la peine de + infinitive** = it's worth (doing): *Il vaut la peine de visiter le musée.*

These four are the easiest way to show Higher grammar in Q2: *D'un côté, il est pratique d'avoir une voiture. D'un autre côté, il vaut mieux prendre les transports en commun.*

In other tenses, *il y avait / il y aura* and *il faisait / il fera* are on the list; *il fallait* and *il faudra* (it was / will be necessary) are not required, but credited if correct.

**3. Reflexive verbs.** The pronoun goes before the verb and **matches the subject**.

| | se reposer (to rest) | s'amuser (to have fun) |
|---|---|---|
| je | me repose | m'amuse |
| tu | te reposes | t'amuses |
| il / elle / on | se repose | s'amuse |
| nous | nous reposons | nous amusons |
| vous | vous reposez | vous amusez |
| ils / elles | se reposent | s'amusent |

- **Verbs on the list** include *s'appeler, se lever, s'habiller, se reposer, s'amuser, se sentir, se marier, s'entendre (avec), se disputer, se passer, se trouver*, and at Higher *s'ennuyer, s'occuper de, se souvenir de, se fier à, se plaindre, se taire*.
- **Reciprocal meaning** (each other), with plural subjects: *Nous nous écrivons* (we write to each other). *Ils se donnent des cadeaux. Mes parents s'aiment.*
- **Negative:** *ne* goes before the pronoun: *Je ne m'ennuie jamais. Ils ne se disputent pas.*
- **With an infinitive**, the pronoun still matches the subject of the first verb: *Je vais **me** reposer. Tu dois **te** lever. Nous voulons **nous** amuser.* Writing *je veux **s'**amuser* is a major error in Pearson's list.
- **In the perfect** they take *être*, and the participle agrees: *Elle s'est levée tôt. Nous nous sommes bien amusés.* That is taught in G3b.

**4. The passive (Higher): present tense with par.** In an active sentence, the subject does the action: *Des jeunes nettoient la plage.* In a passive one, the object becomes the subject, and the doer comes after **par**: *La plage **est nettoyée par** des jeunes* (The beach is cleaned by young people).
- Form: **être** in the present + **past participle** + (*par* + the doer).
- The participle **agrees with the subject**, like an adjective: *Le plastique est recyclé. La ville est protégée. Les déchets sont recyclés. Les bouteilles sont recyclées.*
- *Par* can be left out: *Le français est parlé au Canada.*
- **On** often replaces a passive: *On recycle le plastique* = plastic is recycled.
- Passives in other tenses (*a été construit*, was built) are not required; credited if correct, and recognisable from *été*.

**5. Être en train de + infinitive (Higher)** = to be in the middle of / busy doing. It stresses that an action is in progress right now: *Je suis en train de manger* (I'm in the middle of eating). It is the best phrase for a photo: *Sur la photo, les jeunes sont en train de jouer au foot.* The simple present (*ils jouent*) is also correct; *en train de* just adds emphasis. Never write *ils sont jouer*.

**6. Venir de + infinitive (Higher)** = to have just done something. Use the **present** of *venir* + *de* + infinitive: *Je viens de finir* (I've just finished). *Le train vient d'arriver* (The train has just arrived). *Nous venons de manger.* English uses a past ("have just finished"), but French uses the present: *j'ai venu de finir* is wrong. In the imperfect, *je venais de* = I had just (not required; credited if correct).

| venir (present) | + de + infinitive |
|---|---|
| je viens / tu viens / il, elle, on vient | de partir, d'arriver |
| nous venons / vous venez / ils, elles viennent | de manger, de finir |

::: terms
- **il y a / il y avait / il y aura**: there is, are / there was, were / there will be; with a time, il y a = ago
- **il faut + infinitive**: you must, it is necessary to; il ne faut pas = you must not
- **il est + adjective + de**: Higher; it is (important, difficult, possible) to: il est difficile de choisir
- **il manque**: Higher; something is missing or lacking: il manque un cinéma dans ma ville
- **il vaut mieux**: Higher; it is better to: il vaut mieux prendre le bus
- **il vaut la peine de**: Higher; it is worth (doing): il vaut la peine de visiter le château
- **le verbe pronominal**: a reflexive verb whose pronoun matches the subject: je me lève, nous nous levons
- **le sens réciproque**: plural reflexive meaning each other: ils se donnent des cadeaux
- **la voix passive**: Higher; être + agreeing participle + par: les déchets sont recyclés par la ville
- **être en train de**: Higher; to be in the middle of doing: elle est en train de lire
- **venir de**: Higher; present of venir + de + infinitive = have just done: je viens d'arriver
:::

::: method
1. **"There is / was / will be"**: *il y a / il y avait / il y aura*. "Ago": *il y a* + time.
2. **Weather**: *il fait* + adjective, or *il pleut / il neige*. **Time**: *il est* + hour.
3. **Advice or obligation**: *il faut*, *il vaut mieux*, *il est important de* + infinitive. The infinitive is never conjugated after them.
4. **Reflexive verb**: put the pronoun before the verb and match it to the subject, even after *vouloir, aller, devoir*. *Ne* goes before the pronoun.
5. **Passive**: find who does what. The thing done to is the subject. Use *est / sont* + participle, agree it, then *par*.
6. **"Have just"**: present of *venir* + *de* + infinitive. **"In the middle of"**: present of *être* + *en train de* + infinitive.
7. **Reading**: *vient de* = past (just happened), not future; *est en train de* = now; in a passive, the person after *par* is the one doing it.
:::

::: example Active to passive (4)
Rewrite in the passive, keeping the present tense. (a) La ville recycle les bouteilles. (b) Des bénévoles (volunteers) organisent la fête. (c) Les touristes visitent le château. (d) Mon père prépare le repas.

(a) *Les bouteilles sont recyclées par la ville.* (1) Feminine plural agreement.
(b) *La fête est organisée par des bénévoles.* (1)
(c) *Le château est visité par les touristes.* (1)
(d) *Le repas est préparé par mon père.* (1)

Check: the old object is now the subject, *être* agrees with it (*est / sont*), and so does the participle.
:::

::: example Picture task: describing with impersonal verbs and en train de (4)
A photo shows a family in a park on a sunny day. Two children are playing football, and their parents are eating at a table.

*Sur la photo, il y a une famille dans un parc. Il fait beau et il fait chaud, donc c'est peut-être l'été. Au premier plan, deux enfants sont en train de jouer au foot. À l'arrière-plan, les parents sont en train de manger. Ils ont l'air contents. À mon avis, il est important de passer du temps en famille.*

This covers Pearson's three points: the **people** (*une famille, deux enfants, les parents*), the **location** (*dans un parc*, the weather) and the **activity** (*en train de jouer / manger*). *Il est important de* adds an opinion with Higher grammar.
:::

::: example Venir de and the reflexive pronoun with an infinitive (4)
Translate into French: (a) I've just got up. (b) We have just arrived. (c) I want to have fun this weekend. (d) Are you going to rest? (*tu*)

(a) *Je viens de me lever.* (1) *Venir de* + a reflexive infinitive with *me*.
(b) *Nous venons d'arriver.* (1) Present tense of *venir*; *de* elides.
(c) *Je veux m'amuser ce week-end.* (1) *M'*, not *s'*.
(d) *Tu vas te reposer ?* (1) *Te* matches *tu*.
:::

::: example Reading: who does what, and when? (3)
*Le parc est entretenu (looked after) par les habitants du quartier. Ils viennent de planter des arbres, et maintenant, ils sont en train de construire une aire de jeux (playground). Il vaut la peine d'y aller !*

(a) Who looks after the park? (b) What have they done recently? (c) What are they doing now?

(a) The local residents / the people of the area (1): the doer comes after *par*. (b) Planted trees (1): *viennent de* = have just. (c) Building a playground (1): *en train de* = in the middle of. A student who writes "they are going to plant trees" has read *viennent de* as a future. That is wrong.
:::

::: watch
- **Venir de is present in French, past in English**: *il vient de partir* = he has just left. Don't read it as a future, and don't write *il est venu de partir*.
- **The reflexive pronoun matches the subject** even after another verb: *je vais me coucher*. *Je veux s'amuser* is a major error.
- **Passive participles agree** with the subject: *les bouteilles sont recyclées*. A missing ending is minor; a missing *être* changes the meaning.
- **Par marks the doer.** In reading, the noun after *par* is who does the action.
- **No "am -ing" in French**: *je suis en train de manger* or *je mange*, never *je suis manger*.
- **Il faut + infinitive**: *il faut recycler*, not *il faut recycle* or *il faut de recycler*.
- **Il est + adjective + de**: *il est important **de** dormir* (the *de* is required).
- **Il y a + time = ago**: *il y a trois ans* = three years ago, not "there are three years".
:::

Translate the passage into English.
