## What it is

A **sound-symbol correspondence (SSC)** is the link between a French sound and the letters that spell it. Pearson's Appendix 2 lists **35 SSCs**: the places where French spelling and sound differ from English. Anything that sounds roughly as it would in English (b, d, f, m, p, v and so on) is not listed. The same list is tested twice:
- **Paper 1, Task 1 (read aloud):** you read a 50–55-word text, marked on how clear your pronunciation is and how well you apply the SSCs. An English accent is fine as long as the SSCs are right.
- **Paper 2, Section B (dictation):** you hear six sentences and write them down. A word only counts if the SSCs in your spelling are right. Some words are deliberately off the vocabulary list, so you must spell them from the sound alone.

So learn every SSC **in both directions**: see the letters, say the sound; hear the sound, write the letters.

**How the sounds are described in this app.** There is no audio, so sounds are written in English letters. Use this key every time:

| written | the French sound | example |
|---|---|---|
| 'ay' | é, -er, -ez (like "day" without the y glide) | *été* 'ay-tay' |
| 'eh' | è, ê, ai (as in "bed") | *mère* 'mehr' |
| 'ee' | i, y | *midi* 'mee-dee' |
| 'uh' | the e of *je* | *le* 'luh' |
| 'ur' | eu, œu (the vowel of "fur", lips rounded; the r is not said) | *peu* 'pur' |
| 'oh' / 'o' | closed o (*eau*) / open o (as in "hot") | *beau* 'boh', *notre* 'notr' |
| 'oo' / 'ew' | ou / u (say 'ee' with lips rounded for 'oo') | *vous* 'voo', *tu* 'tew' |
| 'ahn', 'on', 'an', 'uhn' | the four nasal vowels: en/an, on, in/ain, un (the n is not said) | *dans* 'dahn', *bon* 'bon', *fin* 'fan', *un* 'uhn' |
| 'zh' | j, and g before e or i (the s of "measure") | *jouer* 'zhoo-ay' |
| 'ny' | -gn- (as in "onion") | *gagner* 'ga-nyay' |
| 'r' | the French r, from the back of the throat, always said | *rien* 'ryan' |

A hyphen joins syllables; a space separates words.

**The 35 SSCs, grouped.** Pearson's own example comes first, then another from the vocabulary list.

**Silent letters (3)**

| SSC | Pearson's example | another | what to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| silent final consonant | *tout* | *petit, trop, les* | Most final consonants are silent. *C, r, f, l* are often sounded (*avec, cher, neuf, il*); *-er* is the exception (*parler*). |
| silent final e | *elle* | *chaque* | The *-e* is not said, but it makes the consonant before it heard: *petit* 'puh-tee', *petite* 'puh-teet'. |
| h | *hôtel* | *homme, hier* | Never pronounced. Liaison happens before it: *les hôtels* 'lay-zoh-tel'. |

**The vowels (9)**

| SSC | Pearson's example | another | sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | *aller* | *la gare* | 'ah', short |
| i / y | *il / stylo* | *midi* | 'ee' |
| e | *je* | *le, demain* | 'uh', often almost swallowed: *petit* 'p'tee' |
| eu (closed) | *peu* | *deux, jeu* | 'ur' with tight rounded lips |
| open eu / œu | *leur / sœur* | *heure, jeune* | 'ur' with the mouth more open, usually before a sounded consonant |
| au / eau / closed o / ô | *autre / eau / nos / tôt* | *chaud, vélo, l'hôpital* | 'oh' |
| open o | *notre* | *la porte, l'école* | 'o' as in "hot" |
| ou | *vous* | *rouge, toujours* | 'oo' |
| u | *tu* | *la rue, la musique* | 'ew': never 'oo'. *Tu* and *tous* are different words. |

**The é and è families (2)**

| SSC | Pearson's example | another | sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| é / -er / -ez | *été / parler / avez* | *le café, manger, chez* | 'ay'. Three spellings, one sound: grammar decides which. |
| è / ê / ai | *collège / être / faire* | *la mère, la fête, le lait* | 'eh' |

**The nasal vowels (4).** The vowel goes through the nose and the *n* or *m* is not sounded, unless a vowel or another *n* / *m* follows (*bonne, animal, immeuble*).

| SSC | Pearson's example | another | sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| en / an / em / am | *entendre / dans / temps / jambe* | *l'enfant, la chambre* | 'ahn' |
| on / om | *mon / combien* | *bonjour, le nom* | 'on' |
| ain / in / aim / im | *pain / fin / faim / important* | *demain, le jardin* | 'an' |
| un | *un* | *lundi* | 'uhn' (many speakers say 'an') |

**Combinations (3)**

| SSC | Pearson's example | another | sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| oi / oy | *moi / moyen* | *trois, le voyage* | 'wah'; *oy* is 'wah-y' |
| -ien | *bien* | *le chien* | 'yan' |
| -tion | *pollution* | *la natation* | 'syon', never English 'shun' |

**The consonants (10)**

| SSC | Pearson's example | another | sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| ch | *cher* | *la chose, dimanche* | 'sh' |
| j | *jouer* | *jeune, déjeuner* | 'zh' |
| qu | *qui* | *quand, quatre* | 'k', no 'w' |
| ç / soft c | *reçu / cette* | *le garçon, ici, le cinéma* | 's'. *C* is soft before *e, i, y*; before *a, o, u* it needs a cedilla to be soft. |
| r | *rien* | *rouge, le frère* | throat r, always said (except in *-er*) |
| -s- | *faisons* | *la maison, la rose* | 'z' between two vowels; *-ss-* stays 's' (*le poisson* versus *le poison*) |
| th | *théâtre* | *les maths, le thé* | plain 't' |
| -gn- | *gagner* | *la montagne, la campagne* | 'ny' |
| -ill- / -ille | *billet / famille* | *la fille, juillet* | 'y'. Exceptions with an 'l' sound: *la ville, mille*. |
| -aill- / ail | *travailler / travail* | *la taille, le détail* | 'eye': *travail* 'tra-vye' |

**The four liaisons (4).** A silent final consonant is sounded when the next word starts with a vowel or a silent h.

| SSC | Pearson's example | another | sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| s-liaison | *vous avez* | *les enfants, nous avons* | 'z': 'voo-za-vay' |
| t-liaison | *on peut aller* | *c'est un, un petit ami* | 't': 'on pur-ta-lay' |
| n-liaison | *on a* | *mon ami, un enfant* | 'n': 'on-nah' |
| x-liaison | *deux heures* | *six ans, dix euros* | 'z': 'dur-zur' |

Never make a liaison after *et* (*et il* is 'ay eel'), and never before a word that starts with a sounded consonant.

**Homophones: same sound, different spelling.** The dictation tests these, and only grammar can decide.

| sound | spellings | test |
|---|---|---|
| 'ah' | *a* (has) / *à* (to, at) | if you can say *avait* instead, write *a* |
| 'ay' / 'eh' | *et* (and, 'ay') / *est* (is, 'eh'; many speakers make them sound alike) | if you can say *était*, write *est* |
| 'on' | *on* (we, one) / *ont* (have) | if you can say *avaient*, write *ont* |
| 'son' | *son* (his, her) / *sont* (are) | if you can say *étaient*, write *sont* |
| 'say' | *ces* (these) / *ses* (his, her, plural) / *c'est* (it is) / *s'est* (reflexive past) | *c'est* + noun or adjective; *s'est* + past participle; *ses* = belonging to him or her |
| 'oo' | *ou* (or) / *où* (where) | if you can say *ou bien*, write *ou* |
| 'ay' | *-er* / *-é* / *-ez* | after a preposition or a modal: *-er*; after *avoir* / *être*: *-é*; with *vous*: *-ez*. Swap in *vendre*: if *vendre* fits, *-er*; if *vendu* fits, *-é* |

**Silent grammar.** Many endings make no sound at all: the plural *-s* (*les petits chats*), the feminine *-e* (*allée* sounds exactly like *allé*), and the verb ending *-ent* (*ils parlent* is 'eel parl'). The dictation still expects them, but a slip here is **rewardable**: Pearson credits a word whose grammatical ending is wrong if the sound is unchanged (*je veut, deux maison*). What is **not** rewardable is a wrong SSC: *frere* for *frère* (no accent means a different sound), *violin* for *violon*. Careful: *-ent* is only silent on verbs. In *souvent, lentement, le parent* it is the nasal 'ahn'.

::: terms
- **SSC (sound-symbol correspondence)**: the link between a sound and its spelling; Pearson lists 35
- **la lettre muette**: a letter written but not said: final consonants (tout), final e (elle), h (hôtel)
- **la voyelle nasale**: a vowel said through the nose, the n or m silent: dans, mon, fin, un
- **la liaison**: a silent final s, t, n or x is said before a vowel or silent h: vous avez, deux heures
- **l'homophone**: words with the same sound and different spellings: a / à, et / est, son / sont
- **é / -er / -ez**: three spellings of 'ay'; grammar decides: j'ai mangé, je vais manger, vous mangez
- **è / ê / ai**: three spellings of 'eh': collège, être, faire
- **-tion**: said 'syon', never 'shun': la pollution, la natation
- **-ille / -ail**: 'y' and 'eye': la famille, le travail; but la ville and mille keep an l sound
- **rewardable word**: a dictation word that counts: exact, or a silent grammar slip, or the same sound spelt another recognisable way
- **SSC error**: a spelling that changes the sound, so the word does not count: frere, violin
- **read aloud**: Paper 1 Task 1, a 50–55-word text marked on clear, SSC-accurate pronunciation
:::

::: method
**Read aloud (Paper 1)**
1. In the preparation time, **cross out silent letters** in pencil: final consonants, final *-e*, *-ent* on verbs, every *h*.
2. **Draw a link** for each required liaison: after *les, des, mes, nous, vous, ils, elles, on, un, deux, trois, six, dix, c'est, très, petit, grand* before a vowel or silent h.
3. **Ring the nasals** and the traps: *-tion*, *-ille*, *-gn-*, *qu*, *ch*, *u* versus *ou*, *é* versus *è*.
4. Use the last minute in the room to **say the text aloud once**, slowly. Keep going if you slip; don't restart sentences.

**Dictation (Paper 2)**
1. **Hearing 1:** get the meaning and the grammar: who is the subject, singular or plural, which tense?
2. **Hearing 2:** write every sound with its most common spelling.
3. **Fix the homophones** with the tests above (*a / à, et / est, son / sont, ces / ses / c'est / s'est*).
4. **Fix the 'ay' endings**: *-er* after a preposition or second verb, *-é* after *avoir* / *être*, *-ez* with *vous*.
5. **Add silent grammar**: plural *-s*, feminine *-e*, *-ent* after *ils / elles*, participle agreement with *être*.
6. **Hearing 3:** check accents (*é, è, ê, à, ç*) and liaisons; never write the liaison sound as a letter (*les amis*, not *les zamis*).
7. **Unknown word:** build it sound by sound from the SSC table, so that anyone reading it aloud would say the right word.
:::

::: example Spelling a sentence from its sound (5)
Sounds like 'lay-zahn-fahn son ah lah pee-seen'. Write the sentence.

'lay-z...': a plural article with s-liaison: **les** (1).
'ahn-fahn': two nasal 'ahn' sounds, the list word **enfants**, plural because of *les*: silent *-ts* (1).
'son': a verb is needed after the subject, so **sont** (are), not *son* (his) (1).
'ah': a preposition (you can't say *avait* here), so **à** (1).
'lah pee-seen': **la piscine**: soft *c* before *i*, silent final *e* (1).

**Written:** *Les enfants sont à la piscine.*
:::

::: example Homophones by grammar (4)
Choose the right spelling: (a) Mon frère (a / à) quinze ans. (b) Ma sœur (et / est) malade. (c) Elle (c'est / s'est) levée tôt. (d) Tu préfères le thé (ou / où) le café ?

(a) **a** (1): "has" (*il avait quinze ans* works). (b) **est** (1): "is". (c) **s'est** (1): reflexive verb in the perfect, followed by a past participle. (d) **ou** (1): "or".
:::

::: example Marking a read-aloud text (6)
*L'été dernier, nous sommes allés au bord de la mer avec mes deux sœurs. Nous avons dormi dans un petit hôtel très calme près de la plage. Le matin, on prenait le petit-déjeuner au soleil, et l'après-midi, je faisais souvent du vélo. Je pense que les vacances en famille sont toujours importantes.* (52 words)

Mark three liaisons, and three other traps. (6)

**Liaisons (1 each):** *nous avons* 'noo-za-von' (s); *dans un* 'dahn-zuhn' (s); *petit hôtel* 'puh-tee-toh-tel' (t, because the *h* is silent). *Sommes allés* may be linked or not.
**Other traps (1 each, any three):** silent letters in *dernier, près, prenait, petit-déjeuner, toujours, sont*; *-ent* in *souvent* is nasal, not silent; *-ille* in *famille* is 'y'; *soleil* ends 'ay-y'; *-s-* in *faisais* is 'z'; *im-* and *-an-* in *importantes* are nasal, and the final *-es* is silent while the *t* is heard; *sœurs* is open 'ur' with a silent *s*; no liaison after *et*.
:::

::: example Rewardable or not? (4)
A student hears *Mes parents adorent la musique* and writes *Mes parent addore la mousique*. Which words count?

*Mes* and *la* count: exact (1). *Parent* counts: the missing plural *-s* is a grammar slip you cannot hear (1). *Addore* counts: the doubled *d* doesn't change the sound, and the missing *-nt* is a silent grammar slip (1). *Mousique* does not count: *ou* is 'oo', so it changes the sound of *u* (1). Score: 4 rewardable words out of 5.
:::

::: example A word you have never seen written (3)
The dictation includes 'ewn too-reest kah-nah-dyen' (*une* + two words; you may never have seen the feminine form written). Write it.

**Une touriste canadienne** (3). 'too' = *ou*; 'reest' = *-iste* with silent *e*; 'kah-nah' = *ca-na*; 'dyen' with a sounded *n* is feminine, *-ienne* (the masculine *-ien* would be the nasal 'yan'). Any spelling a French reader would pronounce the same way, such as *canadiene*, would also count.
:::

::: watch
- **Accents are SSCs**: *frere* for *frère* or *ecole* for *école* changes the sound, so the word does not count in the dictation.
- **-ent is silent on verbs only**: *ils jouent* 'eel zhoo', but *souvent* and *lentement* end in 'ahn'.
- **Don't write liaisons as letters**: *les amis*, never *les zamis*.
- **u is not ou**: *tu* and *tous*, *rue* and *roue* are different words; an English 'oo' for *u* changes the word in the read-aloud.
- **-tion is 'syon'**, never 'shun'; **th is 't'**; **qu is 'k'**; **h is silent**.
- **Homophones need a grammar test**: *a / à, et / est, son / sont, on / ont, ou / où, ces / ses / c'est / s'est*.
- **Silent grammar slips are forgiven, SSC slips are not**: *je veut* counts, *violin* for *violon* does not.
- **Ville and mille** keep an 'l' sound; every other *-ille* is 'y'.
:::

**Dictation simulation 2.** Someone is talking about school and free time.

Sentences 1 and 2: write the missing word in each gap.
1. *Ma ____ ____ est le ____.* The gaps sound like 'ma-tyehr', 'pray-fay-ray', 'frahn-seh'.
2. *Le ____, je joue au ____ avec mon ____.* The gaps sound like 'mehr-kruh-dee', 'bas-ket', 'frehr'.

Sentences 3 to 6: write the whole sentence.
3. 'mon pro-feh-sur duh mat eh zhahn-tee'
4. 'noo-za-von boh-koo duh duh-vwahr'
5. 'ee-yehr swahr noo som-za-lay oh see-nay-ma'
6. 'plew tar zhuh voo-dreh duh-vuh-neer an-feer-myehr' (10)
---
**Transcript:** 1 Ma *matière préférée* est le *français*. 2 Le *mercredi*, je joue au *basket* avec mon *frère*. 3 Mon professeur de maths est gentil. 4 Nous avons beaucoup de devoirs. 5 Hier soir, nous sommes allés au cinéma. 6 Plus tard, je voudrais devenir infirmière.
**Rewardable words: 30** (gaps 6; sentences 3–6: 6 + 5 + 7 + 6), on the same sliding scale (28–30 = 10 … 1–3 = 1).
**Off-list words:** *gentil* and *infirmière* are not on the vocabulary list, so you spell them from the rules: *gentil* ends in a silent *l*; *infirmière* has nasal *in*, *è* and a silent final *e*.
**Count as rewardable:** *préféré* or *préférés* for *préférée* (silent agreement); *allé* or *allées* for *allés*; *devoir* for *devoirs*; *math* for *maths*; *proffesseur* (doubled letter, same sound).
**Don't count:** *francais* (without the cedilla it is 'k'), *frere* and *infirmiere* (no grave accent changes the vowel), *cinema* ('see-nuh-ma'), *profeseur* (a single *s* between vowels is 'z'), *gentille* ('zhahn-tee-y', a different sound).
