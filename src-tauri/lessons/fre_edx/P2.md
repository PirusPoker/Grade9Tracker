## What it is

**Paper 2 Listening** lasts **60 minutes at Higher**, including **5 minutes' reading time** at the start, and is worth **50 marks, 25% of the GCSE**. Everything is recorded, and **each extract is played three times**. No dictionary.

- **Section A: listening comprehension (40 marks).** Nine questions, set and answered **in English**. The extracts add up to roughly 700–850 words of French: monologues, conversations, announcements, adverts, podcasts and news reports, across all six thematic contexts. The sample paper's tariffs run 3, 5, 3, 4, 3, 3, 6, 6, 7, so a single question can carry up to 7 marks.
- **Section B: dictation (10 marks).** Six short sentences on one subject, each heard three times, with **2 minutes to check** at the end.

**The question types in Section A.**

| Type | What you do | How it's marked |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple choice | "Complete the sentences by putting a cross in the correct box": A, B or C | 1 per item |
| Word-box gap-fill | Complete English sentences with words from a box; there are **more words than gaps** | 1 per gap; each word fits only one gap |
| Multiple response | "Put a cross in each one of the **three** correct boxes" from six | 1 per correct cross; cross exactly three |
| Table | "Complete the table in English. You do not need to write in full sentences" | 1 per cell; one detail is enough |
| Short answer | Questions in English, answered in English | 1 per point; isolated words that don't answer the question score 0 |

**This app has no audio.** Every listening item is a **transcript**. Read it **once, at speaking pace, as if you were hearing it**, then **cover it** and answer. That trains the real skill: holding on to details after the words have gone.

**Using the 5 minutes' reading time.** Read every question, underline the question word (*when, why, which two, what does she NOT…*), and **predict the French you'll hear**. A question about transport means listening for *en train, en voiture, à vélo, à pied*; one about times means *heures, et demie, moins le quart*. Note the names: two or three speakers often share one question.

**The three hearings.**
1. **First hearing: gist.** Who is speaking, about what, in which time frame? Don't write yet, or just jot key words.
2. **Second hearing: answer.** Write or cross your answers.
3. **Third hearing: check.** Check the details that the traps below target, especially negatives and time words.

**The traps Pearson builds in.**
- **Distractors:** the wrong options are usually **mentioned** too. *Je voulais prendre le train, mais il était complet, donc j'y suis allé en voiture.* "Train" is heard, but the answer is the car.
- **Negatives:** *ne… pas* (not), *ne… plus* (no longer), *ne… jamais* (never), *ne… rien* (nothing), *ne… personne* (nobody), *ne… que* (only). *Je ne mange plus de viande* means they **used to**.
- **Time frames:** *avant, quand j'étais petit, l'année dernière* (past) / *maintenant, en ce moment* (present) / *l'année prochaine, plus tard, bientôt* (future). The tense matters as much as the activity.
- **Contrast words** that reverse what came before: *mais, par contre, cependant, pourtant, sauf* (except), *sinon* (otherwise).
- **Numbers, times and prices:** *soixante-dix* = 70, *quatre-vingts* = 80, *quatre-vingt-dix* = 90, *soixante-quinze* = 75. The 24-hour clock (*dix-sept heures quarante-cinq* = 5.45 pm). *Et demie* = half past, *et quart* = quarter past, *moins le quart* = quarter to. *Sept euros cinquante* = €7.50.
- **Opinions:** a question may ask whether a speaker is positive, negative, or both. Listen for *mais* and *par contre* inside one person's turn.
- **Paraphrase:** the English question rarely uses the cognate of the French word. "Free" might be *gratuit*; "fully booked" might be *complet*.

**Answering in English.** Give **enough detail to answer the question asked**, and no more than one answer per line. A true detail that answers a different question scores 0, and so does an isolated word: if the answer is "to help poor families", "families" alone is not enough. Don't write French unless it is a name. Spelling in English is not marked if the meaning is clear.

**Section B: the dictation.** Instructions in the paper: for **sentences 1 and 2**, write the missing words in the gaps, **one French word per gap**; for **sentences 3 to 6**, write the **whole sentence**, which has between 4 and 8 words. Each sentence is announced by its number and heard three times.
- About **30 words are rewardable**, counted and converted on a sliding scale: **28–30 = 10, 25–27 = 9, 22–24 = 8, 19–21 = 7, 16–18 = 6, 13–15 = 5, 10–12 = 4, 7–9 = 3, 4–6 = 2, 1–3 = 1**.
- A word **counts** if it is spelt exactly; or if its only mistake is grammatical and can't be heard (*je veut, deux maison*); or if it uses another spelling of the same sound and is still recognisable (a doubled consonant, an extra silent letter).
- A word **does not count** if a sound-symbol correspondence is misapplied, so that it would be read aloud differently (*frere* for *frère*, *violin* for *violon*), or if it is so phonetic that you wouldn't recognise it on its own.
- **Some words are deliberately not on the vocabulary list.** You can't have learnt them, so spell them from the sound rules in G8.

Dictation in this app uses the **sound key from G8**: 'ay' = é, 'eh' = è / ai, 'uh' = the e of *je*, 'ur' = eu, 'oo' = ou, 'ew' = u, 'ahn' / 'on' / 'an' / 'uhn' = the four nasals, 'zh' = j, 'ny' = gn; a hyphen joins syllables. Read the description, write the French, then compare with the answer.

::: terms
- **la compréhension orale**: Section A, 40 marks, nine questions in English, each extract heard three times
- **le temps de lecture**: the 5 minutes before the recording: read the questions and predict the French
- **le distracteur**: a wrong option that is also mentioned in the extract, often negated or in another time frame
- **la réponse multiple**: cross exactly the number of boxes asked for, usually three from six
- **la grille de mots**: a word-box gap-fill; there are more words than gaps
- **la négation**: ne… plus = no longer, ne… jamais = never, ne… que = only; they reverse the obvious answer
- **le marqueur temporel**: a time word that fixes past, present or future: avant, maintenant, l'année prochaine
- **le mot de contraste**: mais, par contre, cependant, pourtant: what follows often matters more
- **la dictée**: Section B, 10 marks, six sentences; 1–2 are gap-fills, 3–6 whole sentences of 4–8 words
- **le barème progressif**: the sliding scale turning about 30 rewardable words into 10 marks (28–30 = 10)
- **le mot récompensé**: a dictation word that counts: exact, an inaudible grammar slip, or the same sound spelt recognisably
- **le mot hors liste**: a dictation word not on the vocabulary list, to be spelt from the sound rules
:::

::: method
**Section A**
1. **Reading time:** underline the question words, note the names, and predict three or four French words for each question.
2. **Hearing 1:** listen for who, what and when. Note the time frame.
3. **Hearing 2:** answer. For multiple choice, cross out options as you hear them negated or placed in another time.
4. **Hearing 3:** check the negatives, the contrast words and any numbers.
5. **Short answers:** answer the exact question in English with the key detail; avoid a single word that doesn't answer it.
6. **Multiple response:** cross exactly the number asked; never leave a box blank if you can make an informed guess.
7. **In this app:** read each transcript once at speaking pace, cover it, then answer.

**Section B**
1. **Hearing 1:** meaning, subject (singular or plural), tense.
2. **Hearing 2:** write every sound with its most common spelling.
3. **Hearing 3 and the 2 minutes:** fix homophones (*a / à, et / est, son / sont*), *-er / -é / -ez*, silent endings and accents.
:::

::: example A multiple-choice question with distractors (3)
**Listening practice (read the transcript once, then cover it).** Emma is talking about her Saturdays.

*Le samedi matin, je fais de la natation avec mon club, mais je n'aime pas trop ça parce que l'eau est froide. L'après-midi, je préfère aller en ville avec ma sœur. On regarde les vêtements, mais on n'achète pas grand-chose, car c'est trop cher. Le soir, je reste à la maison et je regarde une série.*

Put a cross in the correct box. (a) On Saturday mornings, Emma… A — goes swimming; B — goes running; C — goes to town. (b) In town, Emma and her sister… A — buy lots of clothes; B — look at clothes; C — go to the cinema. (c) In the evening, Emma… A — goes out with friends; B — watches a series at home; C — reads. (3)

(a) A (1). (b) B (1): A is the distractor, because *on n'achète pas grand-chose* means they don't buy much. (c) B (1).
:::

::: example Spotting the distractor (2)
**Listening practice.** *Je voulais prendre le train, mais il était complet, donc finalement, j'y suis allé en voiture avec mon oncle.*

How did the speaker travel? A — by train; B — by car; C — by bus. Which option is the distractor, and why? (2)

B (1). A is the distractor: he **wanted** to take the train (*je voulais*), but it was full (*complet*) (1). *Finalement* signals the real answer.
:::

::: example Negatives change the answer (3)
**Listening practice.** *Je ne mange plus de viande depuis deux ans. Je ne bois jamais de coca et, le matin, je ne prends qu'un fruit.*

Answer in English. (a) What has the speaker stopped eating? (b) How often does he drink cola? (c) What does he have in the morning? (3)

(a) Meat (1): *ne… plus* = no longer. (b) Never (1). (c) Only a piece of fruit (1): *ne… que* = only. "A fruit" is accepted; "fruit and cola" is not.
:::

::: example A dictation sentence, sound by sound (4)
Sentence 3 sounds like 'mez-a-mee son par-tee ah lah mon-ta-ny'. Write it.

'mez-a-mee' = **mes amis**: s-liaison, plural (1). 'son' after a plural subject = **sont** (1). 'par-tee' after *sont* = past participle, plural masculine: **partis** (silent *s*; *parti* would still count as a silent grammar slip) (1). 'ah' = preposition **à**; 'mon-ta-ny' = **montagne** (1). **Written:** *Mes amis sont partis à la montagne.*
:::

---

During the reading time you see: "Why did Mehdi leave the club?" List four French words or phrases you might hear. (2)
---
Any four sensible predictions, e.g. *parce que, car, ennuyeux, trop cher, loin, je n'avais pas le temps, j'ai quitté*. 2 = four; 1 = two or three.

Write the gaps from their sounds (dictation sentence 1): *Le ____, je ____ au ____.* Gaps: 'mehr-kruh-dee', 'zhoo', 'ten-nees'. (2)
---
*Le mercredi, je joue au tennis.* 2 = three gaps right; 1 = two. These would also count: *jous* or *jouent* (a grammar slip you can't hear), *tenis* (same sound, still recognisable).
:::

::: q
A student writes *Ma sœur est allé au marche.* for *Ma sœur est allée au marché.* How many of the six words are rewardable? (2)
---
Five (2). *Allé* for *allée* is a grammar slip you can't hear, so it counts; *marche* without the accent ends in a silent *e* instead of 'ay', so it does not. 1 mark for four.
:::

::: watch
- **Picking the first option you hear**: wrong options are mentioned too, often negated or in another time frame.
- **Missing a negative**: *ne… plus, ne… jamais, ne… que* turn the obvious answer round.
- **Ignoring the time frame**: "what will she do" is not answered by what she did.
- **Isolated words**: "families" is not "to help poor families". Give the detail that answers the question.
- **Too many crosses**: cross exactly three in a "three correct boxes" question.
- **Numbers**: 70, 80 and 90 are built from 60 and 4 × 20; check prices and times on the third hearing.
- **Dictation accents**: *é, è, à, ç* are sounds; leaving them out loses the word.
- **Dictation homophones**: *a / à, et / est, son / sont, ces / ses / c'est*; decide by grammar.
- **Leaving dictation gaps blank**: an attempt spelt from the rules can still count.
:::
