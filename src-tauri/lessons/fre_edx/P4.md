## What it is

**Paper 4 Writing** lasts **1 hour 20 minutes at Higher** and is worth **50 marks, 25% of the GCSE**. Instructions are in English; you write in French, **in full sentences**, with no dictionary. There are three questions.

| Question | Marks | Task | Grids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 18 | Choose **one of two** informal tasks: write to a friend, **80–90 words**, **four bullets** | AO2 13 + AO3 5 |
| Q2 | 22 | Choose **one of two** formal tasks: an article for a magazine or website, **130–150 words**, **four bullets** | AO2 17 + AO3 5 |
| Q3 | 10 | Translate a short English paragraph into French (about five sentences, getting harder) | AO2 6 + AO3 4 |

A sensible split: **Q1 about 20 minutes, Q2 about 35, Q3 about 15, and 10 minutes to check.** Word counts are **guidance only**: nothing is deducted for writing more or less, but every word you write is marked, and extra length usually adds errors.

**The four bullets.** Both tasks have four bullet points, usually in this pattern:
- Q1: a **present** description, an **opinion with reasons**, a **past** event, a **future** plan;
- Q2: "what makes a good…", "**the pros and cons** of…", a **past** event, a **future** plan.

A bullet is "addressed" if there is some response to it, in any order; they don't need equal length. But the top two AO2 bands in both questions require **all four bullets**. **Miss one and the best you can get for AO2 is the 8–10 band**, however good the rest is.

**Q1 grids, paraphrased.**
- **AO2, response to the task (13):** 11–13 all four bullets, frequent development, comprehensible with only the occasional unclear message; 8–10 three or more bullets, some development, generally comprehensible; 5–7 two or more bullets, occasional brief development, the message sometimes breaks down; 1–4 at least one bullet, little extra detail.
- **AO3, language (5):** 5 some variety and some extended sentences with occasional complex language, three time frames generally successful, generally accurate; 3–4 occasional variety, at least two time frames with some success, some accurate language with some major errors; 1–2 short simple sentences, limited success with time frames, frequent errors.

**Q2 grids, paraphrased.**
- **AO2 (17):** 14–17 all four bullets, consistent development, easily comprehensible; 11–13 all four bullets, frequent development; 8–10 three or more bullets, some development; 5–7 two or more, occasional brief development; 1–4 at least one, very brief.
- **AO3 (5):** 4–5 a wide variety, frequent extended sentences and frequent complex language, three time frames consistently successful, consistently accurate (any errors minor); 2–3 some variety and some complex language, at least two time frames generally successful, mostly minor errors with occasional major ones; 1 occasional variety, occasional success with time frames, frequent errors.

**Development** means extra detail, a reason, a justification or an example. **Complex language**, in Pearson's sense: longer sentences linked with *et, ou, mais, parce que, quand, si*; relative clauses (*qui, que, où*); infinitive structures (*pour, sans, avant de* + infinitive, *après avoir* + past participle); a range of pronouns (*je les aime, j'y vais, il m'a aidé*); several conjugated verbs in one sentence; and structures unlike English (*depuis* + present, *venir de, il vaut mieux, ce qui était…*). **Time frames** can be shown by any tense that works: *je vois mes amis samedi prochain* is future.

**Register.** Q1 is **informal**: *Salut !*, *tu*, *Et toi ?*, *À bientôt !* Q2 is a **formal article**: no greeting, no *tu*; use *on*, *les jeunes*, *à mon avis*, *d'un côté… d'un autre côté…* (both Higher short phrases), *selon moi*, *pourtant*, *en conclusion*.

**Errors** (Pearson's two categories, paraphrased):
- **minor** = the meaning is still clear: a wrong gender or adjective agreement (*la mer est vert*), a missing contraction (*près de le parc*), word order (*je n'ai mangé pas*), *dans France*, a relative pronoun slip;
- **major** = the meaning is blocked: a wrong verb form (*j'alle, je recève*), the wrong tense for the time (*demain je suis allé*), an infinitive instead of a conjugated verb (*il avoir un accident*), a reflexive mismatch (*je veux s'amuser*), an English word used as French (*un office*).

**Inclusive language.** Pearson credits non-binary pronouns (such as *iel*) and agreements when they are used correctly and consistently, in the same way as masculine and feminine forms.

**Q3, the translation.** The sentences get harder, typically ending with *depuis*, a pronoun, *après avoir* or two time frames in one sentence. **AO2 meaning (6):** 5–6 consistently transferred; 3–4 most of it transferred, the occasional message unclear or incomplete; 1–2 some parts, the message sometimes breaks down. **AO3 accuracy (4):** 4 consistently accurate, any errors minor; 3 generally accurate, major errors rare; 2 sometimes accurate, some major errors; 1 limited accuracy. Common traps: *j'ai quinze ans* (age with *avoir*); the perfect with *être* and agreement (*elle est allée*); *depuis* + present; "used to" = imperfect; pronouns before the verb (*je les vois, je leur parle*); *à* / *au* / *en* with places (*à Paris, au Canada, en France*); *jouer à* a sport, *jouer de* an instrument; *après avoir mangé*; no article for jobs (*elle est médecin*).

**A model Q1 (88 words).** *Write to your friend about your free time:* what you do in your free time; your opinion of sports clubs, with reasons; what you did last weekend; your plans for next weekend.

*Salut Enzo ! Pendant mon temps libre, je joue de la guitare et je retrouve mes amis en ville.* [present] *À mon avis, les clubs de sport sont super, parce qu'on peut se faire de nouveaux amis et rester en forme.* [opinion + two reasons] *Le week-end dernier, je suis allée à la piscine avec ma sœur, puis nous avons mangé une pizza. C'était génial !* [past + opinion] *Le week-end prochain, je vais aller au concert de mon groupe préféré et, si je ne suis pas trop fatiguée, je passerai la nuit chez ma cousine.* [future + *si* clause] *Et toi ?*

**A model Q2 (about 135 words).** *Write about school trips for a school website:* what makes a good school trip; the pros and cons of trips abroad; a trip you went on recently; a trip you would like to go on in the future.

*À mon avis, une bonne sortie scolaire est à la fois intéressante et amusante : on apprend des choses sans s'en rendre compte.* [bullet 1, infinitive clause] *D'un côté, les voyages à l'étranger permettent de découvrir une autre culture et de parler une langue étrangère. D'un autre côté, ils coûtent souvent cher, et certaines familles ne peuvent pas payer.* [pros and cons] *L'année dernière, ma classe est partie trois jours en Normandie. Nous avons visité des plages historiques et un musée sur la Seconde Guerre mondiale. Après avoir vu le film du musée, beaucoup d'élèves étaient émus. Le soir, nous avons fait un grand jeu à l'auberge de jeunesse, ce qui était inoubliable.* [past: perfect, imperfect, *après avoir*, *ce qui*] *L'année prochaine, j'aimerais participer au voyage en Espagne, car je voudrais améliorer mon espagnol. Si c'est possible, je vais aussi aider à organiser une sortie pour les plus jeunes élèves.* [future and conditional]

**A model Q3.** *My brother is fifteen years old. He likes playing video games in his bedroom. Yesterday, we went to the cinema with our cousins. I have been learning to swim for two years. After finishing my homework, I am going to help my parents in the garden.*

*Mon frère a quinze ans. Il aime jouer aux jeux vidéo dans sa chambre. Hier, nous sommes allés au cinéma avec nos cousins. J'apprends à nager depuis deux ans. Après avoir fini mes devoirs, je vais aider mes parents dans le jardin.*

::: terms
- **la tâche informelle**: Q1, 18 marks: 80–90 words to a friend, four bullets, tu and a friendly tone
- **la tâche formelle**: Q2, 22 marks: a 130–150-word article for a magazine or website, four bullets
- **la traduction vers le français**: Q3, 10 marks: AO2 meaning 6 + AO3 accuracy 4
- **la puce**: one of the four bullet points; missing one caps AO2 at the 8–10 band
- **le développement**: extra detail, a reason, a justification or an example added to a point
- **la langue complexe**: linked clauses, qui / que, pour / avant de + infinitive, pronouns, depuis, venir de
- **les trois périodes**: past, present and future used successfully, needed for the top AO3 band
- **les avantages et les inconvénients**: the "pros and cons" bullet: d'un côté… d'un autre côté…
- **l'erreur mineure**: an error that doesn't block meaning: gender, agreement, près de le parc
- **l'erreur majeure**: an error that blocks meaning: j'alle, the wrong tense, an infinitive for a verb
- **le registre**: informal (tu, Salut !) for Q1; formal (on, les jeunes, no greeting) for Q2
- **la vérification**: the last 10 minutes: verbs, agreements, accents, every bullet ticked off
:::

::: method
1. **Choose quickly:** pick the task whose four bullets you can all answer, especially the past and future ones.
2. **Plan each bullet** in a few French words: the verb and tense you'll use, one reason or detail, one complex structure.
3. **Q1:** two or three sentences per bullet, with an opinion and *parce que*. Use *tu* and a friendly opening and close.
4. **Q2:** three or four sentences per bullet. Balance the pros and cons with *d'un côté… d'un autre côté…*; include at least one *qui / que* clause, one *après avoir* or *avant de*, and one *si* sentence.
5. **Spread the time frames:** at least two past verbs (perfect + imperfect *c'était*), present, and two future or conditional forms (*je vais*, *je voudrais*, *-erai*).
6. **Q3:** translate sentence by sentence; underline the grammar point in each English sentence before you write it.
7. **Check:** a tick against each bullet; every verb agrees with its subject and fits its time; *être* verbs agree; accents and genders.
:::

::: example Turning a flat sentence into a developed one (3)
*J'aime le cinéma.*

*J'aime le cinéma* → *J'adore aller au cinéma avec mes amis* (detail, 1) *parce que l'écran est énorme et que l'ambiance est géniale* (reason, 1). *Samedi dernier, nous avons vu un film d'horreur qui m'a fait peur !* (past + *qui* clause, 1)
:::

::: example Why a translation sentence loses marks (4)
"I have been learning to swim for two years." Four attempts:

*J'apprends à nager depuis deux ans.* (full marks: present + *depuis*.) *J'ai appris à nager depuis deux ans.* (a major tense error, but the meaning mostly survives.) *J'apprends nager depuis deux ans.* (a minor error: *apprendre à*.) *Je suis apprendre à nager pour deux ans.* (two major errors: *je suis* + infinitive and "for" translated as *pour*.) (4)

::: watch
- **Missing a bullet** caps AO2 at the 8–10 band in both questions. Tick each bullet off as you write.
- **Using one time frame** keeps AO3 low; the top bands want three, used successfully.
- **Wrong register**: *tu* and *Salut !* in the Q2 article, or a stiff *vous* to a friend in Q1.
- **Major errors**: *j'alle, je recève*, an infinitive for a verb, a past verb with *demain*, *je veux s'amuser*, English words like *un office*.
- **Opinions with no reason**: always add *parce que* or *car* plus a detail.
- **Pros and cons with only one side**: the bullet asks for both.
- **Translation traps**: *avoir* for age, *être* verbs and agreement, *depuis* + present, pronouns before the verb, *au* / *en* / *à* with places, *après avoir*.
- **Over-length**: nothing is gained by 200 words, and errors and repetition creep in.
- **Learnt paragraphs** that don't answer the bullet earn no AO2 credit for it.
:::
