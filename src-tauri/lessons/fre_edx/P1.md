## What it is

**Paper 1 Speaking** is worth **50 marks, 25% of the GCSE**. Your teacher conducts and records it in April or May, and Pearson marks the recording. At Higher it lasts **10–12 minutes**, after **15 minutes' supervised preparation**.

**Preparation.** You get the read-aloud card, the role-play card and the picture card.
- **14 minutes** in a preparation room, in silence. You may make notes on **one sheet of A4** and **annotate the read-aloud card**, but you must **not** write on the role-play or picture cards. No dictionary.
- **1 minute** in the exam room to practise the read-aloud **out loud** (this is recorded, and the teacher gives no help).
- You keep your notes during the test and hand them in at the end.

The tasks always come in this order: **read aloud (12 marks, 2–2.5 min), role play (10, 1–1.5 min), picture task (28: description 2–2.5 min, then a 5–5.5-minute conversation)**.

**Task 1: read aloud (12 = 8 + 2 + 2).** Pearson picks the thematic context. The text is marked **holistically for pronunciation**: how clear it is, and how well you apply the sound-symbol correspondences (G8). In outline: 7–8 = consistently clear, any SSC lapses don't affect the message; 5–6 = clear, lapses have minimal impact; 3–4 = generally clear, lapses have limited impact; 1–2 = sometimes clear, lapses occasionally make it hard to follow. An English accent is fine. Then the teacher takes the card and asks **two questions in the present tense** about your likes, dislikes or opinions on the text's subject. Each is marked **2** (fully communicated), **1** (partly communicated, some ambiguity) or **0**. **A one-word answer can score only 1.** The questions are not rephrased; they may be repeated.

**Task 2: role play (10 = 5 × 2).** The setting is one of ten: café / restaurant, shop / market / shopping centre, hotel, train station, tourist information office, cinema / theatre / concert hall, campsite, leisure centre, doctor's / hospital, in town. The card has a scenario and **five bullet prompts in English**. The teacher reads an introduction, then asks the first question, word for word. At Higher:
- **two prompts say "Ask a question about…"**: your answer must be a real question. A statement scores 0, however good the French;
- **one prompt needs the future** (the teacher's question uses *aller* + infinitive or a future word); answer in the future;
- everything else in the present, or *je voudrais*. *Vous* is not required.

It is marked for **communication only**: 2 = fully communicated, 1 = partly, 0 = nothing relevant. A grammar slip that leaves the meaning clear can still score 2. A one-word answer scores 1 at most.

**Task 3: picture task (28).** Two weeks before, you choose one thematic context from two that Pearson generates. On the day, the card has **two photos**; you pick one. The teacher asks which photo you have chosen, then asks you to describe it.
- **Description (AO2 4 + AO3 4):** cover **the people, the location, the activity**. The teacher may nudge you with *(Et) les personnes ? (Et) c'est où ? (Et) que font-ils ? Autre chose ?* AO2 rewards covering all three and **developing** them in detail; AO3 rewards a wide range of accurate language.
- **Two compulsory questions (2 + 2)** about your own experience of the photo's subject. **At Higher, the second is in a past tense.**
- **Conversation (AO1 12 + AO3 4):** 5–5.5 minutes on the same thematic context, starting from a **set subject** given on the teacher's card. AO1 (10–12 band) wants consistently relevant answers, ideas developed with **extended sequences of speech**, and easy comprehension. AO3 (4) wants a wide range including **complex language**, **three time frames used successfully**, and accuracy with only minor errors.

**Errors.** Pearson separates **minor** errors (meaning still clear: wrong gender, adjective agreement, *près de le parc*, *dans France*, a wrong preposition) from **major** errors (meaning blocked: *j'alle*, the wrong tense for the time, *Lucie avoir un accident*, *je veux s'amuser*, an English word such as *un office* for an office). AO3 tolerates minor errors in the top band.

**"Complex language"** means: longer sentences joined with *parce que, quand, mais, si*; relative clauses (*qui, que*); infinitive structures (*pour + infinitive, avant de, après avoir*); pronouns (*je les aime, j'y vais*); several conjugated verbs in one sentence; and structures unlike English (*depuis* + present, *venir de*, *il vaut mieux*).

**Inclusive language.** Pearson credits non-binary pronouns (such as *iel*) and agreements when they are used correctly and consistently, in the same way as masculine and feminine forms.

**Using the 15 minutes** (timings in the method): your A4 sheet holds notes, not a script. Reading out a long paragraph sounds flat and is easy to lose your place in.

**A model read-aloud, with the traps marked.**

*Mes parents pensent que je passe trop de temps sur mon portable. C'est vrai que j'utilise souvent les réseaux sociaux pour discuter avec mes amis. Cependant, je trouve que la technologie est aussi très utile pour mes études. Le week-end prochain, je vais essayer de laisser mon téléphone dans ma chambre.* (51 words)

- **silent:** *-ent* of *pensent*; *-ps* of *temps*; *-p* of *trop*; *-s* of *mes parents, réseaux, sociaux, amis, études*; *-t* of *cependant*;
- **liaisons:** *mes amis* 'may-za-mee'; *mes études* 'may-zay-tewd';
- **not silent:** *souvent* ends in nasal 'ahn' (it is not a verb);
- **sounds:** *ch* in *technologie* is 'k' (an exception: say 'tek-no-lo-zhee'); *ph* in *téléphone* is 'f'; *-s-* in *j'utilise* and *réseaux* is 'z'; *u* in *utilise, sur, utile* is 'ew'; *ai* in *laisser, vrai* is 'eh'; nasals in *pensent, temps, mon, cependant, prochain, chambre*.

**Follow-up questions** (they ask for likes, dislikes or opinions, in the present):
- *Qu'est-ce que tu aimes faire sur ton portable ?* → *J'aime regarder des vidéos drôles et envoyer des messages à mes amis, parce que c'est rapide.* (2: clear, with a reason)
- *Quel est ton avis sur les réseaux sociaux ?* → *Je pense qu'ils sont utiles pour rester en contact, mais on peut y passer trop de temps.* (2) *Bien.* would score 1 at most.

**Station:** *Je voudrais aller à Lyon. — Un aller-retour, s'il vous plaît. — Le train part à quelle heure ? — À Lyon, je vais rendre visite à mon oncle.* (future) *— Le train part de quel quai ?*
**Doctor's:** *J'ai mal à la gorge et j'ai de la fièvre. — Je suis malade depuis deux jours. — Où est la pharmacie la plus proche ? — Ce week-end, je vais rester au lit.* (future) *— Est-ce que je peux jouer au foot samedi ?*

**Questions:** rising intonation (*Le musée est ouvert ?*), *est-ce que*, or a question word (*à quelle heure, combien, où*).

**Picture description toolkit.** *Sur la photo, il y a… ; au premier plan* (in the foreground); *à l'arrière-plan* (in the background); *à gauche / à droite / au centre*; *ils sont en train de* + infinitive (Higher: they are in the middle of…); *il fait beau / il pleut*; *ils ont l'air contents* (they look happy); *je pense que…, peut-être parce que…*

**Model description** (a photo of a family picnic in a park): *Sur la photo, il y a une famille de cinq personnes : les parents, deux enfants et une grand-mère. Ils ont l'air contents parce qu'ils rient. Ils sont dans un grand parc et, à l'arrière-plan, on peut voir des arbres et un lac. Il fait beau et il y a du soleil. Ils sont en train de faire un pique-nique : au premier plan, il y a du pain, du fromage et des fruits. À droite, un garçon joue au foot avec son père. Je pense que c'est le week-end, parce que tout le monde est détendu.* People, location and activity are each developed.

**Transferable sentences** that fit many conversation subjects:
- *Quand j'étais petit(e), je… mais maintenant, je…* (past vs present)
- *Le week-end dernier, je suis allé(e)… C'était…, parce que…*
- *À l'avenir, je voudrais…, parce que… ; l'année prochaine, je vais…*
- *Si j'avais plus de temps, je ferais…* (credited if correct)
- *Je … depuis trois ans* (present + *depuis*); *je viens de…* (Higher)
- *D'un côté…, d'un autre côté…; il vaut mieux* + infinitive; *avant de* + infinitive; *après avoir* + past participle.

::: terms
- **le texte à lire à haute voix**: Task 1, a 50–55-word text marked out of 8 on clear, SSC-accurate pronunciation
- **les questions de suivi**: the two unprepared present-tense questions after the read-aloud, 2 marks each
- **le jeu de rôle**: Task 2, five English prompts in a transactional setting, 2 marks each for communication
- **le point d'interrogation**: a role-play prompt "Ask a question about…": a statement scores 0
- **le point futur**: the one role-play prompt you answer in a future time frame (je vais + infinitive)
- **la description de photo**: people, location and activity, marked AO2 4 (coverage, development) + AO3 4 (language)
- **les questions obligatoires**: two questions on your own experience after the photo; the second is in the past
- **la conversation**: 5–5.5 minutes from a set starter subject: AO1 12 (relevance, development) + AO3 4
- **la réponse développée**: an answer with extra detail, a reason, an example or a contrast
- **les trois périodes**: past, present and future used successfully: needed for the top AO3 band
- **la fiche A4**: the one sheet of notes you may write in the 15 minutes' preparation and keep in the room
- **la langue inclusive**: Pearson credits non-binary pronouns and agreements used correctly and consistently
:::

::: method
1. **Prepare in order (14 minutes):** read-aloud (3), role play (4), photo (6), reread your notes (1). The 15th minute is in the exam room: read the text aloud once.
2. **Read aloud:** steady pace, every liaison you marked, no restarting; then answer each question with **an opinion + a reason** (*J'aime… parce que…*).
3. **Role play:** listen to the teacher's question, match it to the prompt, answer in a short full sentence. For "Ask…", start with *Est-ce que…* or a question word. For the future prompt, use *je vais* + infinitive.
4. **Describe the photo** in a fixed order: people (who, how many, clothes, mood), location (where, weather, foreground / background), activity (*ils sont en train de…*), then an opinion or guess.
5. **Compulsory questions:** listen for the tense. If the question has *as-tu fait, es-tu allé(e)* or *hier*, answer in the perfect, and add a detail plus *c'était…*.
6. **Conversation:** aim for 3–4 sentences per answer: answer, reason, example, a different time frame. Use each of past, present and future at least twice.
7. **If you don't understand,** ask: *Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?* Then answer what you can; silence earns nothing.
:::

::: example Two follow-up answers at 2, 1 and 0 (4)
After a text about sport, the teacher asks: (a) *Qu'est-ce que tu aimes faire comme sport ?* (b) *Quel est ton avis sur les sports d'équipe ?*

(a) **2:** *J'aime nager, parce que c'est relaxant.* **1:** *La natation.* (one word or phrase: ambiguous). **0:** *Le sport* (repeats the question).
(b) **2:** *Je les adore, car on travaille ensemble et on se fait des amis.* **1:** *C'est équipe.* (unclear). **0:** *Oui.*
Both 2-mark answers give a clear opinion and a reason. (4)
:::

::: watch
- **A statement when the prompt says "Ask"** scores 0. Start every question with *Est-ce que* or a question word.
- **The future prompt in the present tense** loses the mark: *je vais* + infinitive is the safest form.
- **One-word answers** cap a 2-mark question at 1, in the follow-up, role-play and compulsory questions alike.
- **Skipping one of people, location, activity** in the description limits AO2.
- **Answering the second compulsory question in the present**: listen for *as-tu*, *es-tu* or *hier*.
- **Short conversation answers**: AO1's top band wants consistently extended sequences of speech, not one sentence per question.
- **Only one time frame** in the conversation keeps AO3 in the lower bands; use past, present and future several times.
- **Reading a script from the A4 sheet**: it sounds stilted and you lose your place; write prompts, not paragraphs.
- **Read-aloud slips** that change the word: *-ent* said on verbs, *u* said as 'oo', *-tion* said as 'shun', liaisons missed.
:::
