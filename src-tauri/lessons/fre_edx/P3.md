## What it is

**Paper 3 Reading** lasts **1 hour at Higher** and is worth **50 marks, 25% of the GCSE**. No dictionary.

- **Section A: reading comprehension (40 marks).** Eight questions, set and answered **in English**. The sample paper's tariffs are 3, 5, 7, 3, 7, 5, 5, 5. The texts cover all six thematic contexts: blogs, forum posts, emails, adverts, brochures, articles, diaries and school projects. Names come from Pearson's own list (Léa, Hugo, Inès…).
- **Section B: translation into English (10 marks).** One French paragraph of about 60 words. Pearson suggests spending **about 10 minutes** on it, so plan **50 minutes for Section A**.

**The question types in Section A.**

| Type | The instruction | How it's marked |
|---|---|---|
| Word-box gap-fill | "Complete the gap in each sentence using a word from the box. There are more words than gaps." | 1 per gap. The box holds extra, plausible words. |
| Multiple choice | "Put a cross in the correct box": A, B or C | 1 per item |
| Multiple response | "Put a cross next to each one of the **two** / **three** correct statements" | 1 per correct cross. Cross exactly the number asked; extra crosses can cost marks. |
| Short answers | "Answer the following questions in English. You do not need to write in full sentences." | 1 per point. Isolated words that don't answer the question score 0. |
| Table | "Complete the table in English." Each cell wants **one detail**. | 1 per cell |
| Inferred word | "Which of these is the best translation of the word X?" A / B / C | 1 each; two per paper |

**Vocabulary in the texts.** Almost every word comes from the Edexcel vocabulary list, plus words derived from it by the rules in G7 (*cinquième, heureusement, l'organisateur*). A small number of **cognates** that are not on the list may appear unglossed (*le yoga, la catastrophe*). Any other unlisted word is **glossed**: its English meaning is given under the text. The exception is the **two inferred words** per paper: single words deliberately left off the list and unglossed, which you must work out from context.

**Inferring a word.** Read the whole sentence and the one after it. Decide the word class (an article before it = noun; after *il est* = adjective; after *je* = verb). Then ask what the situation needs: *j'étais complètement mouillé* after a day of rain without a jacket can only be "wet". Test each option in the sentence; two of them usually contradict something in the text.

**Reading for time frames.** Many questions ask what someone **used to** do, **does now**, or **will** do. Tenses help (*jouais* / *joue* / *vais jouer*), but so do time words: *avant, quand j'étais petit, l'année dernière* / *maintenant, en ce moment, depuis* / *bientôt, plus tard, l'année prochaine*. A present tense with *demain* is a future plan.

**Negatives and contrasts** reverse meanings: *ne… plus* (no longer), *ne… jamais* (never), *ne… que* (only), *ne… personne* (nobody), *ne… rien* (nothing); *mais, par contre, cependant, pourtant, sauf*. A statement that matches the words but ignores *ne… plus* is the classic distractor.

**True, but not the answer.** Mark schemes reject answers that are correct about the text but answer a different question. *Thomas n'aime pas son collège, parce que les journées sont trop longues. Pourtant, ses professeurs sont sympas.* "Why doesn't Thomas like his school?" "The teachers are nice" is true, but it is not the reason. Answer the question word: **why** wants a reason, **when** a time, **how** a means.

**Section B: translating into English.** It is marked holistically on one grid. In outline:
- **9–10:** the meaning is fully and naturally transferred; any errors don't affect the message;
- **6–8:** mostly transferred; occasional wrong or missing words or awkward word order blur parts;
- **3–5:** parts transferred; some errors block the message: wrong words, missing phrases, wrong tenses;
- **1–2:** only odd fragments transferred.

**Translate every word and every tense, then make it sound English.**

| French | Natural English |
|---|---|
| *J'habite ici depuis deux ans.* | I have lived / have been living here for two years. |
| *Il y a deux ans, …* | Two years ago, … |
| *Je viens de finir.* | I have just finished. |
| *Je ne mange que des légumes.* | I only eat vegetables. |
| *On aime faire du vélo.* | We like cycling. (*on* = we, people, you) |
| *Je jouais / je regardais* | I used to play / I was watching |
| *J'irais / je voudrais* | I would go / I would like |
| *Le samedi, je travaille.* | On Saturdays, I work. (*samedi* alone = this Saturday) |
| *chez elle* | at her house |
| *une voiture rouge* | a red car (adjective moves) |
| *Je suis en train de lire.* | I'm (in the middle of) reading. |

Don't add or drop details, don't leave French words in the English (except names), and don't leave gaps: a sensible guess may still carry meaning.

::: terms
- **la compréhension écrite**: Section A, 40 marks, eight questions in English on French texts
- **la grille de mots**: a word-box gap-fill with more words than gaps; each word is used once at most
- **la réponse multiple**: put a cross next to exactly two or three correct statements
- **le tableau**: complete a table in English, one detail per cell
- **le mot à déduire**: one of two unglossed off-list words you work out from context, 1 mark each
- **le mot glosé**: an off-list word translated for you under the text
- **le mot apparenté**: a cognate; a few unlisted ones may appear without a gloss
- **la réponse hors sujet**: an answer true to the text that does not answer the question asked: 0
- **la traduction vers l'anglais**: Section B, about 60 words, 10 marks on one holistic grid, about 10 minutes
- **depuis + présent**: translate as "have been -ing for / since": il joue depuis un an = he has been playing for a year
- **venir de + infinitif**: "have just": elle vient d'arriver = she has just arrived
- **ne… que**: "only": je ne bois que de l'eau = I only drink water
:::

::: method
1. **Read the title, the English introduction and the questions first.** They tell you who, what and which details to look for.
2. **Find the section of text** for each question; the questions follow the order of the text.
3. **Check the time frame and any negative** before choosing; cross out options the text contradicts.
4. **Short answers:** answer the question word exactly, in English, with the key detail; never a lone word that doesn't answer it.
5. **Multiple response:** find evidence for each statement; cross exactly the number asked.
6. **Inferred words:** word class, then context, then test each option in the sentence.
7. **Translation (10 minutes):** first draft word by word, keeping every tense; then reread it as English and smooth it; check you haven't left out a word or a time phrase.
:::

::: example A word-box gap-fill (3)
Read Léa's blog. *La semaine dernière, ma classe est partie à Paris pour trois jours. Nous avons dormi dans un hôtel près de la gare. Le premier jour, nous avons visité un musée d'art, mais c'était un peu ennuyeux. Le deuxième jour, on a visité la ville en bateau : c'était génial !*

Complete the gap in each sentence using a word from the box. There are more words than gaps. Box: station — beach — boring — exciting — boat — bus — museum — hotel
(a) Léa's class stayed near the ____. (b) She found the art museum ____. (c) She loved seeing the city by ____. (3)

(a) station (1): "hotel" is where they stayed, not what it was near. (b) boring (1): *un peu ennuyeux*. (c) boat (1). "Bus" and "beach" are never mentioned; "exciting" is the trap for (b).
:::

::: example An inferred word (1)
*Il a plu toute la journée et je n'avais pas de veste, donc quand je suis arrivé à la maison, j'étais complètement mouillé.*

Which is the best translation of the word *mouillé*? A — wet; B — tired; C — late. (1)

A (1). After *j'étais* it is an adjective describing the speaker. The context (rain all day, no jacket) points only to "wet". "Tired" and "late" are possible adjectives but nothing in the text supports them.
:::

::: example One translation, three bands (10)
*Il y a deux ans, j'habitais au Canada. Maintenant, j'habite en France depuis six mois et je viens de commencer au lycée. Je ne connais que trois élèves.*

**9–10:** Two years ago, I lived in Canada. Now I have been living in France for six months and I have just started at sixth form. I only know three students.
**6–8:** Two years ago, I lived in Canada. Now I live in France for six months and I have just started at school. I know three students. (The *depuis* tense is wrong and *ne… que* is lost, but most of the meaning is there.)
**3–5:** There are two years, I live in Canada. Now I live in France since six months and I come to start the high school. I don't know three students. (Tenses, *il y a*, *venir de* and *ne… que* all break the message.)
:::

::: example True but not the answer (2)
*Maria aime son nouveau collège, parce qu'il y a beaucoup de clubs. Par contre, il est loin de chez elle, donc elle doit prendre le bus à sept heures.*

(a) Why does Maria like her new school? (b) What is the disadvantage? (2)

(a) There are lots of clubs (1). "She takes the bus" is true but not a reason for liking it. (b) It is far from her home (1). Accept "she has to get the bus at seven"; reject "the bus" alone.
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *Je joue au tennis depuis l'âge de huit ans.* (2)
---
I have been playing / have played tennis since I was eight (since the age of eight). (2) 1 = "I play tennis since…" (tense not rendered).
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *Mes parents viennent d'acheter une nouvelle voiture.* (2)
---
My parents have just bought a new car. (2) 1 = "My parents come to buy…" or "are going to buy".
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *On ne peut utiliser son portable que pendant la pause.* (2)
---
We can only use our phones during break. / You can only use your phone at break time. (2) 1 = *ne… que* lost or treated as a negative ("We can't use our phones during break").
:::

::: q
Which word in each set is the odd one out for time frame? (a) avant — l'année dernière — demain (b) bientôt — l'année prochaine — hier (2)
---
(a) *demain* (future; the others are past) (1) (b) *hier* (past; the others are future) (1).
:::

::: q
*Le samedi, Hugo travaille dans une boulangerie, mais ce samedi, il va au match de foot.* Answer in English: (a) What does Hugo usually do on Saturdays? (b) What is he doing this Saturday? (2)
---
(a) works in a bakery (1) (b) going to a football match (1). *Le samedi* = every Saturday; *ce samedi* = this one.
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *Avant, je ne mangeais jamais de légumes, mais maintenant, j'en mange tous les jours.* (2)
---
Before, I never used to eat vegetables, but now I eat them every day. (2) 1 = one element lost (the imperfect, *jamais*, or *en*).
:::

::: watch
- **Isolated words** that don't answer the question score 0: "bottles" is not "glass bottles".
- **True but not the answer**: answer the question word (why, when, how, who).
- **Ignoring a negative**: *ne… plus* means "no longer", so a statement that says they still do it is false.
- **Time frames mixed up**: *avant / maintenant / l'année prochaine* decide which detail answers the question.
- **Too many crosses** in a multiple-response question.
- **Inferred words**: an option that fits the grammar but not the situation is wrong.
- **Translation**: *depuis* + present is "have been -ing"; *venir de* is "have just"; *ne… que* is "only"; *il y a* + time is "ago"; *le samedi* is "on Saturdays".
- **Missing words in the translation**: every adverb and time phrase (*déjà, toujours, encore, l'été dernier*) carries meaning.
- **Leaving French words in the English** or leaving gaps: write a sensible guess.
:::

::: test
Read Hugo's email.

*Salut ! Depuis septembre, je travaille le samedi dans une boulangerie. Je commence à six heures du matin, ce qui est difficile, mais j'aime bien parler avec les clients. Je gagne dix euros de l'heure. Avec cet argent, je vais acheter un ordinateur portable, parce que le mien est trop vieux. Plus tard, je ne voudrais pas travailler dans un magasin : je préfère l'idée de devenir médecin.*

Put a cross in the correct box for each question.
(i) Hugo has worked in the bakery since… A — September; B — June; C — January.
(ii) What does he like about the job? A — the early start; B — the money; C — talking to customers.
(iii) He is saving up for… A — a phone; B — a laptop; C — a bike. (3)
---
(i) A (1) (ii) C (1): the early start is *difficile* (iii) B (1): *ordinateur portable*.
:::

::: test
Read the text again. What does Hugo want to do later? Put a cross in the correct box. A — work in a shop; B — become a doctor; C — open a bakery. (1)
---
B (1). A is the distractor: *je ne voudrais pas travailler dans un magasin*.
:::

::: test
*Après avoir couru vingt kilomètres, Clément était épuisé : il a dormi douze heures !*

Which is the best translation of the word *épuisé*? Put a cross in the correct box. A — hungry; B — exhausted; C — proud. (1)
---
B (1). He ran twenty kilometres and then slept twelve hours.
:::

::: test
*Ma tante est très généreuse, mais mon oncle est plutôt avare : il ne veut jamais rien acheter pour les autres, même pas pour ses enfants.*

Which is the best translation of the word *avare*? Put a cross in the correct box. A — mean; B — lazy; C — rude. (1)
---
A (1). *Mais* contrasts him with a generous aunt, and he never wants to buy anything for others.
:::

::: test
Read the forum.

*Maria : J'utilise les réseaux sociaux tous les jours pour rester en contact avec ma famille au Canada. Je ne mets jamais de photos de moi en ligne, parce que je pense que c'est dangereux.*
*Yanis : Moi, j'ai arrêté d'utiliser les réseaux sociaux l'année dernière : je perdais trop de temps. Maintenant, je lis plus et je dors mieux.*

Put a cross next to each one of the **three** correct statements. A — Maria uses social media every day. B — Maria often puts photos of herself online. C — Some of Maria's family live in Canada. D — Yanis still uses social media. E — Yanis used to waste too much time. F — Yanis sleeps worse now. (3)
---
A (1), C (1), E (1). B: *ne… jamais*. D: *j'ai arrêté*. F: *je dors mieux* = better.
:::

::: test
Read the article.

*Le festival de musique de notre ville aura lieu du 3 au 5 juillet. Cette année, il y aura des concerts gratuits dans le parc pour les familles. Pour aller au festival, il vaut mieux prendre le bus, car il est très difficile de trouver une place pour sa voiture. Attention : on n'a pas le droit d'apporter des bouteilles en verre.*

Answer the following questions in English. You do not need to write in full sentences.
(a) When does the festival take place? (1)
(b) What is on offer for families this year? (1)
(c) Why is it better to take the bus? (1)
(d) What are people not allowed to bring? (1) (4)
---
(a) 3rd to 5th July (1) (b) free concerts (in the park) (1) (c) it is (very) difficult to find a (parking) space for your car (1); reject "the bus is free" (not stated) (d) glass bottles (1); isolated "bottles" scores 0.
:::

::: test
Read the posts.

*Camille : Pour être en forme, je fais du yoga deux fois par semaine. Mon problème, c'est que je mange trop de sucre, surtout quand je suis stressée.*
*Sofiane : Je joue au basket dans un club depuis cinq ans. Par contre, je ne dors pas assez, parce que je joue sur mon portable tard le soir.*

Complete the table in English. You do not need to write in full sentences.
Camille: (a) healthy habit (b) unhealthy habit. Sofiane: (c) healthy habit (d) unhealthy habit. (4)
---
(a) yoga (twice a week) (1) (b) eats too much sugar (1); reject "stress" on its own (c) plays basketball (in a club) (1) (d) doesn't sleep enough / plays on his phone late (1).
:::

::: test
*Mon grand-père a quatre-vingts ans, mais il est encore très actif. Il ne conduit plus, alors il va partout à pied ou en bus. Le matin, il fait les courses au marché, et l'après-midi, il s'occupe de son jardin.*

Put a cross next to each one of the **two** correct statements. A — He is ninety. B — He is still very active. C — He still drives. D — He walks or takes the bus everywhere. E — He gardens in the morning. (2)
---
B (1), D (1). A: *quatre-vingts* = 80. C: *ne… plus*. E: the garden is in the afternoon.
:::

::: test
Read Lola's blog.

*Quand j'avais dix ans, je voulais être chanteuse. Maintenant, j'apprends deux langues au lycée, et je les adore. Après le lycée, je vais faire un stage à l'étranger, puis je chercherai un emploi dans le tourisme.*

Answer in English. (a) What did Lola want to be at the age of ten? (b) What is she doing at the moment? (c) What will she do first after the lycée? (3)
---
(a) a singer (1) (b) learning two languages (at sixth form) (1) (c) a work placement abroad (1); reject "look for a job in tourism", which comes after.
:::

::: test
*Thomas n'aime pas son collège, parce que les journées sont trop longues. Pourtant, ses professeurs sont sympas.*

"Why doesn't Thomas like his school?" Two students answer: (a) "His teachers are nice." (b) "The days are too long." Which answer scores the mark, and why does the other one not? (2)
---
(b) scores (1). (a) is true but answers a different question: it is a positive point introduced by *pourtant* (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English (*déménager* = to move house): *Il y a trois ans, nous avons déménagé à Lyon, et j'y suis très heureux.* (2)
---
Three years ago, we moved to Lyon, and I am very happy there. (2) 1 = one element wrong or missing ("there are three years", *y* left out).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *Si j'avais assez d'argent, je voyagerais en Afrique avec mes amis.* (2)
---
If I had enough money, I would travel to Africa with my friends. (2) 1 = one tense lost ("I travel", "if I have").
:::

::: test
A student translates *Je ne vais plus à la piscine depuis mon accident* as "I don't go to the swimming pool because of my accident." Name the two errors and give a correct version. (3)
---
*Ne… plus* = no longer / not any more, not just "don't" (1). *Depuis* = since, not "because of" (1). Correct: "I haven't been going to the swimming pool since my accident." / "I no longer go to the swimming pool since my accident." (1)
:::

::: test
Translate the passage into English.

*Ma meilleure amie s'appelle Jade. Elle est très amusante et elle m'écoute toujours. Nous nous connaissons depuis l'âge de six ans. Le week-end, on aime faire du vélo à la campagne ou regarder des films chez elle. L'été dernier, nous sommes allées en Martinique avec sa famille, et c'était génial ! L'année prochaine, elle va étudier au Canada, mais nous allons rester en contact.* (10)
---
Holistic bands. 9–10: the meaning is fully and naturally transferred. 6–8: mostly transferred, with occasional wrong or missing words. 3–5: parts transferred; wrong tenses or missing phrases block the message. 1–2: isolated fragments.
**Model:** My best friend is called Jade. She is very funny and she always listens to me. We have known each other since we were six. At weekends, we like cycling in the countryside or watching films at her house. Last summer, we went to Martinique with her family, and it was great! Next year, she is going to study in Canada, but we are going to stay in touch.
**Deciding details:** *m'écoute toujours* = always listens to me; *nous nous connaissons depuis* = we have known each other since (not "we know each other since"); *on* = we; *faire du vélo* = cycling; *chez elle* = at her house; *nous sommes allées* = we went; *va étudier* = is going to study; *rester en contact* = stay in touch.
:::
