## What it is

This is the **Higher-only** pronoun set from Appendix 2. It builds on G1b (*me, te, le, la, lui, nous, vous, moi, qui*) and adds seven things:
- **y** (there, to it) and **en** (some, of it, of them, any);
- **les** (them) and **leur** (to them), the extra object pronouns;
- **eux** (them), the extra emphatic pronoun, for after a preposition;
- **personne ne** (nobody) and **rien ne** (nothing) as the **subject** of a verb;
- the relative pronouns **que** (whom, which, that) and **où** (where, when).

Edexcel also lists *dont*, *moi-même* and *le mien / le tien / le sien* as **Higher vocabulary**. You need to recognise them in reading and listening; you are not tested on producing them, and using them correctly is credited.

Why it matters: these are the structures Pearson means by "varied pronouns" and "relative clauses" in its description of complex language for Paper 4 Q1 and Q2 and the Paper 1 conversation. In Paper 3, *il n'y en a plus* or *personne ne* is often the whole answer.

**1. Y = there, to it.** *Y* replaces a place introduced by *à, en, dans, chez, sur*... It also replaces *à* + a thing after verbs such as *penser à*.
- *Je vais **à Paris** demain.* → *J'**y** vais demain.* (I'm going there tomorrow.)
- *Elle habite **en France** depuis un an.* → *Elle **y** habite depuis un an.*
- *Je pense souvent **à mes vacances**.* → *J'**y** pense souvent.* (I often think about it / them.)

**2. En = some, any, of it, of them.** *En* replaces *de* + a noun, including *du, de la, des* + noun, a noun after a number or a quantity, and *de* + a thing after verbs such as *parler de* and *avoir besoin de*.
- *Tu veux **du gâteau** ?* → *Oui, j'**en** veux.* (Yes, I want some.)
- *J'ai **deux sœurs**.* → *J'**en** ai deux.* (I have two of them.) The number stays at the end.
- *Il mange **beaucoup de fruits**.* → *Il **en** mange beaucoup.*
- *Nous parlons **de l'environnement**.* → *Nous **en** parlons.* (We talk about it.)
- *Je n'ai pas **de voiture**.* → *Je n'**en** ai pas.*

English often has no word at all for *en*: *Combien de frères as-tu ? — J'en ai un.* = I have one. You cannot leave it out in French.

**3. Position of y and en.** Exactly like the other object pronouns:
- before the verb: *j'y vais, j'en ai deux*;
- with *ne... pas* round both: *je n'y vais pas, je n'en ai pas*;
- in the perfect, before the auxiliary: *j'y suis allé(e), j'en ai acheté*;
- with *aller* or a modal + infinitive, before the infinitive: *je vais y aller, je veux en acheter*.

**Never put y or en next to another object pronoun**, with three exceptions that are fixed phrases: **il y en a** (there is / are some), **il y en avait** (there was / were some), **il y en aura** (there will be some). *Des magasins ? Il y en a trois.* *Il n'y en a pas.* *Avant, il y en avait beaucoup.*

**4. Les and leur.**
- **Les** = them (direct object, people or things): *Je regarde **les séries** le soir.* → *Je **les** regarde le soir.* Perfect: *Je les ai vus.*
- **Leur** = to them / for them (indirect object, people): *Je téléphone **à mes grands-parents**.* → *Je **leur** téléphone.*
- Both go before the verb, like *le* and *lui*. **Never put them next to another object pronoun**: *Tu les montres à tes amis* (keep *à tes amis*), not *tu les leur montres*. *Elle vous achète des cadeaux*, not *elle vous les achète*.
- **Leur the pronoun never adds -s**: *je leur parle*. Only the possessive adjective has a plural: *leur maison* (their house), *leurs parents* (their parents). Ask: is it in front of a verb (pronoun, no -s) or a noun (possessive, agreeing)?

**5. Eux.** The emphatic pronoun for "them", masculine or mixed: after prepositions (*avec eux, chez eux, pour eux, sans eux*), and for emphasis (*Eux, ils préfèrent la campagne*). For an all-female group, use *elles*: *avec elles*.

**6. Personne ne and rien ne as subjects.** When "nobody" or "nothing" is the **subject**, it comes first and *ne* goes before the verb. There is **no pas**, and the verb is singular.
- *Personne ne m'écoute.* (Nobody listens to me.) *Personne n'est venu.* (Nobody came.)
- *Rien n'est impossible.* (Nothing is impossible.) *Rien ne change ici.* (Nothing changes here.)

Compare the object forms in G2a, where the negative goes round the verb: *Je n'écoute personne* (I don't listen to anybody). *Je ne fais rien* (I don't do anything).

**7. Relative clauses: qui, que and où.**
- **Qui** (G1b) is the subject of the next verb: *le garçon **qui** habite ici*.
- **Que / qu'** is the **object**: a new subject follows it. *Le film **que** j'ai vu* (the film that I saw). *La fille **que** je connais.* *C'est une ville **qu'**on aime.* *Que* becomes *qu'* before a vowel; *qui* never changes.
- **The test:** after the relative, is there a verb straight away (*qui*), or a subject and then a verb (*que*)?
- English often drops "that": "the book I'm reading" = *le livre **que** je lis*. French never drops it.
- **Où** = where, for places, and **when** after a time noun: *la ville **où** j'habite*, *le jour **où** je suis arrivé(e)*, *l'année **où** j'ai commencé le collège*. "The day when" is *le jour où*, never *le jour quand*.
- After *que* in the perfect, the participle agrees with the noun before it: *la série que j'ai regardée*. That rule is taught in G3b.

**8. Recognition only.** *Dont* = of which, whose, about which: *le livre dont je parle* (the book I'm talking about), *une amie dont le frère est médecin* (a friend whose brother is a doctor). *Moi-même, lui-même, nous-mêmes* = myself, himself, ourselves (for emphasis: *je l'ai fait moi-même*). *Le mien, la mienne, les miens, les miennes* = mine; *le tien* = yours; *le sien* = his or hers. They agree with the thing owned: *Ton vélo est rouge ; le mien est bleu.* (Not required; credited if correct.)

::: terms
- **y**: there, to it; replaces à/en/dans + a place, or à + a thing: j'y vais, j'y pense
- **en**: some, any, of it, of them; replaces de + noun: j'en veux, j'en ai deux
- **il y en a**: there is / are some; with il y en avait and il y en aura, the only allowed pronoun pair
- **les**: them (direct object); before the verb: je les vois tous les jours
- **leur (pronoun)**: to them, for them; before the verb, never with -s: je leur parle
- **leur / leurs (possessive)**: their; agrees with the noun: leur maison, leurs enfants
- **eux**: them, after a preposition or for emphasis, masculine or mixed: avec eux, chez eux
- **personne ne**: nobody as the subject; singular verb, no pas: Personne ne m'écoute
- **rien ne**: nothing as the subject; singular verb, no pas: Rien n'est impossible
- **que / qu'**: whom, which, that; the object of the next verb, followed by a subject: le film que j'ai vu
- **où**: where, and when after a time noun: la ville où j'habite, le jour où je suis arrivé
- **dont**: recognition only; of which, whose, about which: le livre dont je parle
:::

::: method
1. **Replacing a place?** Use *y*. **Replacing something after *de*, *du*, *des*, a number or a quantity?** Use *en*, and keep the number or quantity after the verb.
2. **Replacing "them"?** Direct (no *à*): *les*. To or for them (*à* + people): *leur*, with no -s.
3. **Place the pronoun** before the verb, before the auxiliary in the perfect, or before the infinitive with *aller* or a modal. *Ne* goes in front of it.
4. **One object pronoun only**, except in *il y en a / avait / aura*.
5. **After a preposition**, use *eux* or *elles*, not *les*.
6. **"Nobody" or "nothing" as the subject?** *Personne ne* / *Rien ne* + a singular verb, with no *pas*.
7. **Joining two clauses:** a verb straight after the relative → *qui*; a subject then a verb → *que*; a place or a time → *où*.
8. **Reading:** for every *y, en, les, leur*, find the noun it replaces before answering. For *personne ne / rien ne*, the answer is "nobody / nothing".
:::

::: example Y and en in three time frames (6)
Replace the words in bold with *y* or *en*.
(a) Je vais **au stade** le samedi. (b) Elle a acheté **des chaussures**. (c) Nous allons aller **en Martinique** cet été. (d) Il y a **trois piscines** dans ma ville. (e) Je ne mange jamais **de viande**. (f) Tu es allé **à Paris** ?

(a) *J'y vais le samedi.* (1)
(b) *Elle en a acheté.* (1) No participle agreement with *en*.
(c) *Nous allons y aller cet été.* (1) Before the infinitive.
(d) *Il y en a trois dans ma ville.* (1) The fixed phrase, with the number at the end.
(e) *Je n'en mange jamais.* (1)
(f) *Tu y es allé ?* (1) Before the auxiliary.
:::

::: example Qui, que or où? (4)
Complete: (a) C'est le prof ___ m'aide toujours. (b) C'est le prof ___ je préfère. (c) Voici le collège ___ j'étudie. (d) Je n'oublierai jamais le jour ___ j'ai rencontré Léa.

(a) **qui** (1): *m'aide* is a verb, and the prof is its subject.
(b) **que** (1): *je* is the subject, so the prof is the object.
(c) **où** (1): a place.
(d) **où** (1): a time noun, so "when" = *où*.
:::

::: example Leur or leurs, pronoun or possessive (3)
*Mes voisins sont sympas. Je **leur** dis bonjour tous les matins, et je joue avec **leurs** enfants. **Leur** jardin est grand.*

- *Je leur dis* = I say (hello) **to them**. A pronoun in front of a verb, so no -s.
- *leurs enfants* = **their** children. A possessive in front of a plural noun, so -s.
- *Leur jardin* = **their** garden. A possessive in front of a singular noun.

The dictation can test exactly this: the three sound the same, so the grammar decides the spelling.
:::

::: example Translation into French with personne ne and eux (2)
Translate into French: "My parents are strict. Nobody goes out without them and nothing changes."

1. "Nobody" is the subject: **Personne ne sort**, singular, no *pas*.
2. "without them": a preposition, so **sans eux** (*parents* is masculine or mixed).
3. "nothing changes": **rien ne change**.

**Answer:** *Mes parents sont stricts. Personne ne sort sans eux et rien ne change.* Writing *Personne ne sort pas* adds a second negative, which is wrong.
:::

::: watch
- **En has no English word, but French needs it**: *J'en ai deux* (I've got two). *J'ai deux* is incomplete.
- **Keep the number after the verb**: *j'en ai trois*, *il y en a beaucoup*.
- **Only one object pronoun at a time**, except in *il y en a / avait / aura*. *Je les leur donne* is beyond the list; keep a noun instead.
- **Leur the pronoun never takes -s**; *leurs* is only the possessive before a plural noun.
- **After a preposition, eux, not les**: *avec eux*, *chez eux*.
- **Personne ne / Rien ne** take no *pas*: *Personne n'est venu*. The verb is singular.
- **Qui or que**: a verb straight after → *qui*; a subject first → *que*. *Que* elides (*qu'il*); *qui* never does.
- **"When" after a time noun is où**: *le jour où*, not *le jour quand*.
- **Agreement in the perfect**: the participle agrees with *les* (*je les ai vus*, G3b) but never with *en* (*j'en ai acheté*).
:::
