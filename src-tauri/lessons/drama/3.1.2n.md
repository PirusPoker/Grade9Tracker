## What it is

*The Empress* by Tanika Gupta (prescribed edition: **Methuen Drama, Student Editions**) is a **historical drama** that AQA describes as **ensemble** theatre. It follows two Indians who arrive in England in 1887: **Rani**, a sixteen-year-old ayah (nanny), and **Abdul Karim**, a young man who becomes Queen Victoria's teacher and companion. Through them the play shows the British Empire from the other side: the palace and the docks, the privileged and the abandoned. It was first staged by the **Royal Shakespeare Company** in **2013** and revived by the RSC in 2023. Section B asks four questions on an extract (4, 8, 12 and 20 marks).

This lesson keeps to details that are certain. Where you want more (exact scene order, minor characters), use your own copy, and keep uncertain plot references general in the exam.

### Context

| | What it is | What it gives a theatre maker |
|---|---|---|
| **Period** | **1887 to 1901**: from Queen Victoria's **Golden Jubilee** to her death. AQA frames it as **the last fourteen years (1887-1901) of Queen Victoria's rule**. | Late Victorian Britain at the height of empire: royal splendour, strict class divisions, the busy, dangerous London docks, poverty in the East End. |
| **Empire and India** | India was ruled by Britain; Victoria was proclaimed **Empress of India** in 1876 | Indians in Britain were servants, sailors and students; they met fascination, prejudice and exploitation. |
| **Ayahs and lascars** | **Ayahs** were Indian nannies brought to Britain by families returning from India, and sometimes abandoned on arrival. **Lascars** were Indian sailors working on British ships, often in harsh conditions. | The docks, lodging houses and streets where these workers struggled to survive. |
| **Abdul Karim** | A real man who came to serve at the Jubilee and became the Queen's **munshi** (teacher), teaching her Urdu; resented by the royal household. | The palace world of protocol and jealousy, and a friendship that crossed empire and class. |
| **Dadabhai Naoroji** | A real Indian politician, elected in **1892** as the first Asian MP in Britain (for a London seat) | Politics and the struggle for Indian voices to be heard. |

### Style: ensemble

- **Ensemble**: the company works as a group, doubling roles and becoming crowds, sailors, servants, the sea, the docks.
- **Many locations**: a ship, Tilbury docks, lodging houses, grand houses, the royal court, London streets, political meetings.
- **Episodic** scenes across fourteen years, with acts headed by their years.
- **Music and movement** carry transitions and create the world (Indian and English music side by side).
- A **rough theatre** or storytelling feel suits the docks and streets: trunks, ropes and crates transformed by the ensemble.

### Plot outline

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1887, arrival** | Rani travels from India with the English family she works for, and on the voyage she befriends **Hari**, a young lascar. Abdul Karim arrives to serve at the Queen's Golden Jubilee. At **Tilbury docks** Rani's employer, Susan, dismisses her: the family already has an English nanny. Rani is alone in a foreign country. |
| **Rani's struggle** | With Hari's help Rani finds lodging, but is horrified by its conditions and determined to make her own way. She finds a new position in the household of **Lord and Lady Oakham**. Lord Oakham begins an affair with her, promising protection and money she needs to send home; later he throws her out. Desperate, with a baby, she reaches her lowest point and is saved by the kindness of **Firoza**. |
| **Abdul's rise** | Abdul becomes the Queen's munshi, teaching her Urdu and becoming her close companion. The royal household resents his rise and questions his status. |
| **Politics and endings** | Rani supports **Dadabhai Naoroji**'s campaign to become an MP, wanting to give a voice to those who are ignored, and is devastated when he later returns to India. Hari, who never forgot her, writes to her over the years, and they are reunited about thirteen years after they first met. The Queen's death in 1901 leaves Abdul exposed to the household's hostility. |

### Characters

| Character | Performance note |
|---|---|
| **Rani** | Sixteen at the start, educated, trusting and excited by England; betrayal after betrayal hardens her into a woman who speaks the truth to power. Physically she grows from open, eager movement to a guarded, upright strength. |
| **Abdul Karim** | Proud, dignified, clever, ambitious; he learns to navigate court protocol; formal posture and careful speech in the palace, more relaxed with other Indians. |
| **Queen Victoria** | Elderly, grieving, lonely, imperious but warm with Abdul; her status shown by stillness, a seated position and others' deference; small signs of age and frailty. |
| **Hari** | A lascar: kind, loyal, hard-working, physically strong from ship life; his lasting love for Rani is the play's hopeful thread. |
| **Susan** | Rani's first employer: cold, dismissive, anxious to be rid of her; clipped, upper-middle-class voice. |
| **Lord Oakham** | Wealthy, charming when he wants something, ruthless when he is done. |
| **Dadabhai Naoroji** | Distinguished, principled, a persuasive public speaker. |
| **Firoza** | A woman who shows Rani kindness at her lowest point. |

### Key moments and how to stage them

1. **The voyage and arrival.** The ensemble sways as the ship's deck; ropes and a gangplank; the sea projected or created with fabric. Rani and Hari laugh together, then the ship docks to the noise of Tilbury: shouting, cranes, horses.
2. **Rani dismissed at the docks.** Susan keeps her distance, children clinging to Rani; trunks and luggage as obstacles. Evening light fading as the family leaves and Rani is left alone with a single bag.
3. **Abdul at court.** A formal space: a raised chair for the Queen, courtiers standing stiffly around the edges; Abdul bowing low, then invited closer, while the household exchange looks. Ceremony and hierarchy shown through positions.
4. **Rani in the Oakham house.** The contrast of grand furniture and a servant's place; the affair played with the power imbalance clear (he sits or stands higher; she is never fully at ease).
5. **Rani's lowest point.** A bare street at night, cold blue light, rain; Rani with the baby; Firoza's arrival brings warmth into the light.
6. **Naoroji's campaign.** A political meeting: a platform, a crowd of the ensemble facing him, banners; Rani among them, then helping.
7. **Endings.** Hari and Rani's reunion after thirteen years; the Queen's death marked by bells and black drapes.

### Design across the play

A **permanent, adaptable set** suits the ensemble style: a wooden deck-like platform, trunks, crates, ropes and rails for the docks and ship; drapes, a throne-like chair and a rich carpet flown or rolled in for the palace. Costume must show **late Victorian** dress for both British and Indian characters and class within each: Susan's travelling dress with bustle and bonnet; the Queen in black mourning silk with a white widow's cap; Rani's cotton sari and shawl, later a maid's uniform; Hari's lascar work clothes; Abdul's turban and fine tunic at court. Lighting and sound separate the palace (warm, rich, formal music) from the docks and streets (grey, smoky, noisy).

::: terms
- **Ayah**: an Indian nanny or nursemaid employed by British families, like Rani.
- **Lascar**: an Indian sailor working on British ships, like Hari.
- **Munshi**: a teacher or secretary; Abdul Karim became Queen Victoria's munshi, teaching her Urdu.
- **Golden Jubilee**: the 1887 celebration of fifty years of Queen Victoria's reign, when the play begins.
- **Empress of India**: the title Queen Victoria took in 1876, which gives the play its name.
- **Ensemble**: a company working as a group, playing many roles and creating the world together.
- **Tilbury docks**: the Thames docks east of London where ships from India arrived.
- **Rough theatre**: a simple, inventive staging style using a few objects and the actors' skills rather than elaborate sets.
- **Dadabhai Naoroji**: an Indian politician elected in 1892 as Britain's first Asian MP.
:::

::: method
1. **Place the extract**: the year, the location (ship, docks, palace, house, street) and Rani's or Abdul's situation.
2. **For the 4-marker, design for 1887-1901**: late Victorian period detail for British characters, accurate Indian dress, and class shown clearly.
3. **For the line**, show power and status: employer and servant, Queen and munshi, British and Indian; voice (accent, register) and body (bowing, stillness, distance).
4. **For the shaded section**, use space to show hierarchy and exclusion: who has the centre, who waits at the edge.
5. **For the 20-marker**, trace the character's journey across the fourteen years, or a design that serves many locations.
6. **Use the ensemble**: crowds, doubling, the ship and docks created by bodies and objects.
:::

::: example Setting for the palace (4 marks)
You are designing a setting for a scene in which Abdul teaches the Queen. The setting must reflect the context of *The Empress*, set in the last fourteen years (1887-1901) of Queen Victoria's rule. Describe your design ideas for the setting. (4)

A rich red and gold patterned carpet rolled out over the bare deck-like floor, so the palace is laid over the permanent set (1). A carved, dark mahogany writing table with a green leather top, an inkstand, and an open exercise book in which the Queen practises Urdu script (1). A high-backed chair with deep crimson velvet upholstery for the Queen, and a plain upright chair for Abdul, placed a little lower, showing status (1). Behind them, heavy draped curtains in dark green with gold tassels, a framed portrait of Prince Albert, and a vase of lilies; everything heavy and formal, reflecting late Victorian taste and the Queen's mourning (1).
:::

::: example Performing Rani (8 marks)
You are performing the role of Rani. When she realises she has been abandoned at the docks, she asks where she can go. Describe how you would use your vocal and physical skills to perform this line and explain the effects you want to create. (8)

I would say it quietly at first, voice shaking, pitch high with disbelief (1). A pause after the first words as the reality lands (1), then a rise in volume and pace, as panic takes over (1), with stress on the words about being a stranger (1). Physically I would clutch my small bag to my chest (1) and turn in a full circle, looking at the busy dock around me (1). The effect: the audience sees how young and alone she is (1), and feels anger at Susan's cruelty and the injustice of how ayahs were treated (1).
:::

::: example Space and interaction at court (12 marks, outline)
You are performing the role of Abdul in a scene at court, when he is first noticed by the Queen. Explain how you might use the performance space and interact with the actor playing the Queen to show his respect and his ambition. (12)

He enters upstage right among the servants, carrying a dish, eyes lowered. The Queen sits downstage centre; courtiers form a half-circle. He stops at the edge of the circle and bows deeply, holding it. When she speaks to him, he raises his head but not his eyes, then meets her gaze briefly and confidently. He answers in careful, formal English with a little more volume than the other servants. When she beckons, he crosses into the circle, one measured step at a time, and kneels close by her chair. The courtiers' exchanged glances show resentment; his steady posture shows he knows exactly what he is doing.
:::

::: example Costume across the play (20 marks, outline)
Plan costume for the docks scene and the play as a whole. (20)

Extract: Susan in a dark plum wool travelling dress with a bustle, fitted jacket and bonnet, gloves; the children in sailor suits; Rani in a pale cotton sari with a thin border, a shawl and simple sandals, damp at the hem from the dock; Hari in loose cotton trousers, a faded shirt and a cloth tied at the head, barefoot or in worn shoes. Whole play: the Queen in black mourning silk and a white lace cap; Abdul's growing splendour at court (silk tunic, embroidered sash, turban); Rani's maid's uniform in the Oakham house, then ragged clothes on the street; Naoroji in a Victorian frock coat. Costume shows empire, class and each character's rise or fall.
:::

::: watch
- Design for **1887-1901**, for **both** British and Indian characters; avoid modern or generic "Indian" costume.
- Show **class and power** in every answer (employer and servant, Queen and courtiers).
- Use the **ensemble** style: many locations suggested, not built.
- Treat the history with care: prejudice and exploitation should be **shown truthfully**, not played for laughs.
- Don't invent plot details; keep uncertain events general.
- Link every choice to the audience's understanding of empire.
:::

::: test
You are performing the role of the Queen. When a courtier objects to Abdul's promotion, the Queen defends him sharply. Describe how you would use your vocal and physical skills to perform her reply and explain the effects you want to create. (8)
---
Levels. 7–8: exact vocal and physical choices on specific words; effects fully right for the Queen and the moment. 5–6: clear, detailed, effects appropriate. 3–4: some detail. 1–2: vague. Voice or body only cannot go above 3–4.

Indicative content: vocal - low, cold, deliberate; slow pace; each word crisp; a pause before the rebuke; no raised volume (she doesn't need it). Physical - remaining seated; a small lift of the chin; a cold stare; a slight gesture of dismissal with the hand; a cane tapped on the floor. Effects: shows her power and loyalty to Abdul; courtiers' humiliation; tension in the court; the audience sees the jealousy that will follow.
