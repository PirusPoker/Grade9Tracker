## What it is

*Things I Know to Be True* by Andrew Bovell (prescribed edition: **Nick Hern Modern Plays**) follows one Australian family, the Prices, through a year of crises. AQA describes it as a **suburban drama** in the style of **Australian realism**. It mixes realistic family scenes in the kitchen and garden with **direct-address monologues** and passages of **physical theatre**, because it was first made by the State Theatre Company of South Australia with the UK physical theatre company **Frantic Assembly** in **2016**. Section B asks four questions on an extract (4, 8, 12 and 20 marks).

This lesson keeps plot details to what the play clearly establishes. Where you want more (exact sequence of scenes, minor details), check your own copy.

### Context

| | What it is | What it gives a theatre maker |
|---|---|---|
| **Setting** | A contemporary **working-class suburb** of Adelaide, South Australia | Bob and Fran's modest single-storey house, with a kitchen opening onto a much-loved back garden: Bob's roses, a lemon tree, a big old eucalyptus, a shed, a rotary washing line, patio chairs. The Australian seasons (a hot summer, cooler winter) shape the light and mood. |
| **Class and generation** | Bob and Fran worked hard all their lives (Fran as a nurse, Bob in paid work until recently) to give their four children chances they never had | The parents' pride and sacrifice versus the children's more educated, mobile, uncertain lives. Money, work and independence are live issues. |
| **Contemporary issues** | Gender identity, marriage breakdown, financial crime, ageing and the fear of loss | The play treats these with care; performances must be truthful, never played for shock or laughs. |
| **Theatre-making** | Co-created by a realist playwright and a physical theatre company; directed by Geordie Brookman and Scott Graham | Movement sequences, lifts and moments of heightened physicality can express what the characters cannot say. |

AQA's 4-mark question frames the context as **a contemporary working-class Australian suburb**. Design for ordinary, practical, modern life, not for glamour.

### Structure and style

- **Seasons**: the play's sections are named after the seasons of a year, and the garden changes with them. Time passing is part of the story.
- **Direct address**: each of the four children has a monologue spoken **to the audience**, telling their own crisis. These are not spoken to another character.
- **Realism**: the family scenes are naturalistic: cooking, arguing, drinking tea in the garden.
- **Physical theatre**: movement expresses emotion and memory, often in transitions or at climaxes.
- **The garden** is the central image: Bob's care for his roses is his care for his family, and the garden reflects the family's state.

### Plot outline

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| **The return** | Rosie, the youngest, tells the audience about her travels in Europe and a heartbreak in Berlin, and comes home early to find the family much as she left it, or so it seems. |
| **The children's crises** | Over the year each child brings a crisis home. **Pip**, the eldest, leaves her marriage and takes a job overseas, clashing bitterly with Fran. **Mark** tells the family that she is a trans woman and becomes **Mia**. **Ben**, who seems the most successful, has been stealing money from his employer. **Rosie** cannot decide what to do with her life, and in time decides to move to Brisbane to study. |
| **Fran and Bob** | Fran, the family's strongest force, has secrets and longings of her own, and her relationship with Bob is tested when they come out. Bob is bewildered and hurt as the life he built changes around him. |
| **The ending** | Fran is killed in a car accident. The family, especially Bob, is left grieving. Rosie tells the audience what happened and says that her childhood ended with her mother's death. |

### Characters

| Character | Performance note |
|---|---|
| **Bob Price** | Gentle, loyal, a creature of habit; no longer working, he pours himself into the garden. Slow, steady movement, a soft Australian voice; confusion shows in stillness, as if he has forgotten what to do next. |
| **Fran Price** | A nurse; practical, sharp-tongued, fiercely loving, hard on her children; her love comes out as criticism and advice. Quick, efficient movement around the kitchen; a tone that can cut. |
| **Pip** | The eldest, a mother herself; clever, restless, desperate to be her own person; clashes with Fran because they are alike. |
| **Mark / Mia** | Gentle and thoughtful; the scene in which Mark tells the family is one of the most delicate in the play. Play with truthfulness and dignity: the performance is about courage, love and fear of rejection. |
| **Ben** | Charming, flashy, anxious under the surface; energy that is a little too bright; collapse when the truth comes out. |
| **Rosie** | The youngest, dreamy and romantic, the family's baby; she wants to fix everyone. Her direct address frames the play; she grows up by the end. |

### Key moments and how to stage them

1. **Rosie's opening monologue.** Alone downstage in a cool, pale special, suitcase beside her, speaking straight to the audience. A physical sequence can show the journey home: the ensemble passes her from one to another, as though she is carried back.
2. **The kitchen as battleground.** Fran working at the bench (chopping, stirring) while she talks: the task keeps her busy and avoids eye contact. The table is where confessions are made.
3. **Pip and Fran.** Mother and daughter circle each other in the garden; the more alike they are, the fiercer the fight. Proxemics: close, face to face, then turning away at the same moment.
4. **Mark's news.** Stillness. Mark sits apart; the family freezes in a tableau; a slow physical sequence can show the shock rippling through each body. Lighting narrows to a tight warm pool around the family.
5. **Ben's confession.** Energy drains out of him: from bouncing on his toes to sitting on the ground; Bob stands over him, then kneels to his level.
6. **The ending.** Rosie's direct address; the family moves in slow motion; Bob alone in the garden; rain or falling leaves.

### Design across the play

A **composite set** of kitchen and garden: the kitchen bench and table on one side, the garden with Bob's rose bed, a patio and the washing line on the other, with a door or open frame between. Seasonal lighting (hot white-gold summer, amber autumn, cold blue winter) and **falling leaves or rain** mark time. Sound: magpies, cicadas in summer, a lawnmower, distant traffic, the radio. Costume: practical, contemporary, modest: Fran's nurse's scrubs or cardigan and jeans, Bob's shorts and gardening clothes, the children in city clothes that show they have moved away.

::: terms
- **Suburban drama**: a play about family life in the suburbs, focusing on domestic relationships and ordinary homes.
- **Australian realism**: a naturalistic style presenting everyday Australian life and speech truthfully.
- **Direct address**: speaking straight to the audience; each Price child tells their story this way.
- **Monologue**: a long speech by one character, here spoken to the audience rather than to another character.
- **Physical theatre**: using movement, lifts and gesture to express emotion or ideas beyond words.
- **Frantic Assembly**: the British physical theatre company that co-created the first production.
- **Composite set**: one set showing more than one location at once, such as the kitchen and garden.
- **Motif**: a repeated image; Bob's roses and the changing garden are the play's main motif.
- **Transgender**: having a gender identity different from the sex recorded at birth, as Mia shares with her family.
:::

::: method
1. **Place the extract**: the season, who is home, and which crisis has or has not come out yet.
2. **For the 4-marker, design for a contemporary working-class Australian suburb**: practical, modern, lived-in, and right for the season and time of day.
3. **For the line**, capture the family's way of speaking: Australian accent, casual rhythms, love hidden in blunt words.
4. **For the shaded section**, use the kitchen and garden: tasks (chopping, gardening) give actors something to do while the sub-text plays.
5. **For the 20-marker**, trace the character or design through the seasons, and use the play's mix of realism, direct address and movement.
6. **Treat sensitive subjects truthfully**; the marks are for truthful, detailed choices, not melodrama.
:::

::: example Costume for Bob (4 marks)
You are designing a costume for Bob to wear in the garden in summer. The costume must reflect the context of *Things I Know to Be True*, set in a contemporary working-class Australian suburb. Describe your design ideas for the costume. (4)

Faded navy cotton work shorts with deep pockets holding secateurs, and a sweat-stained grey cotton T-shirt, because he spends his days in the garden in the heat (1). A battered wide-brimmed cotton bush hat against the Australian sun (1). Worn leather work boots with no socks, or rubber thongs (flip-flops) that he kicks off at the kitchen door (1). Tanned forearms and a sunburnt neck with make-up, soil on his knees, and a plain gold wedding ring he never takes off (1).
:::

::: example Performing Fran (8 marks)
You are performing the role of Fran. When one of her children announces a big decision, Fran answers with a blunt piece of advice. Describe how you would use your vocal and physical skills to perform her line and explain the effects you want to create. (8)

I would keep chopping as I speak, not looking up (1). My voice would be flat, brisk and low, in a broad Australian accent, with the stress on the practical words (1). A short pause before the final phrase, knife still, lets the sting land (1). Then I would look up and hold eye contact for a beat (1), before going back to the chopping board (1). The effect: the audience sees Fran's love coming out as toughness (1), her fear for her child hidden under practical advice (1), and the tension of a family where feelings are rarely said directly (1).
:::

::: example Space and interaction: Bob and Rosie (12 marks, outline)
You are performing the role of Bob when Rosie tells her parents she is going to drive to Brisbane to start a new life. Explain how you might use the performance space and interact with the actor playing Rosie to show Bob's concern for her. (12)

Bob starts at the rose bed upstage, pruning; Rosie stands on the patio with Fran near the kitchen door. As Rosie explains, Bob stops cutting but does not turn. When he does turn, he walks slowly towards her, still holding the secateurs, and stops short, as if closeness would make it real. His questions come quickly, pitch rising, about the road and where she will stay; he glances at Fran for support. Rosie crosses to him; he looks away, then reaches out and holds her by both shoulders. His voice drops and slows. The journey across the garden shows worry giving way to a father letting go.
:::

::: example Lighting through the seasons (20 marks, outline)
Plan lighting for an autumn scene and across the play. (20)

Extract: late afternoon autumn light, warm amber from a low side angle through a leaf gobo, so long shadows stretch across the garden; a cooler blue wash rising in the kitchen as evening falls. For direct address, a tight, neutral special downstage that cuts the speaker out of the family scene. Whole play: hard, bright white-gold summer light; the same garden in cold blue-grey winter with rain; soft golden spring light at the end, bittersweet after Fran's death. Physical theatre sequences use side light to sculpt bodies. The seasonal cycle carries the passing of time and the family's changes.
:::

::: watch
- The monologues are **direct address**; don't play them to another character.
- Design for **contemporary working-class** Australia: practical, modern, not smart or glamorous.
- Use the **seasons**; lighting and set should say which part of the year it is.
- Mia's story needs **truth and dignity**; avoid stereotyped or comic choices.
- Remember **physical theatre** is part of the style; use it in transitions and climaxes.
- Don't invent plot details you are unsure of; keep them general.
:::
