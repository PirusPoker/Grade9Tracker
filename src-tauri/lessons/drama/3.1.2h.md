## What it is

*Noughts and Crosses* is Dominic Cooke's stage adaptation of Malorie Blackman's novel (prescribed edition: **Nick Hern Modern Plays**). AQA calls it a **teen drama** in an **epic** style. It tells the love story of Sephy, a Cross, and Callum, a nought, in a society divided by colour, in many short scenes that race across locations. Section B asks four questions on an extract (4, 8, 12 and 20 marks). For this play AQA's short design question is framed differently from the others: your design must reflect **the conventions of contemporary dystopian theatre used in Noughts and Crosses**, not a real place and date.

Be careful which script you revise from. Other stage versions exist (a later adaptation toured with a different script, and there was a TV series). Your exam extract comes from **Cooke's** version.

### Context

| | What it is | What it gives a theatre maker |
|---|---|---|
| **The world of the play** | A modern, Britain-like society where the dark-skinned **Crosses** hold power and the pale-skinned **noughts** are second-class: poorer schools, poorer jobs, harsher police and courts | A **dystopia**: familiar enough to recognise (schools, shopping centres, TV news, prisons) but with the power reversed, so the audience sees racism afresh. Designs should make that hierarchy visible. |
| **What it draws on** | Real history of racial injustice: segregation and the US civil rights movement, apartheid in South Africa, and racism in Britain | Images of protest, segregated schooling, armed resistance and the death penalty all echo real events, so the play feels urgent rather than fantastical. |
| **Writing** | Novel published 2001; Cooke's adaptation first staged by the Royal Shakespeare Company in **2007** | Written for young audiences; the **epic** form (short scenes, direct address, quick changes) keeps the audience thinking about the society as well as the love story. |

Get the reversal right: **Crosses are dark-skinned and powerful; noughts are pale-skinned and oppressed**. Mixing this up ruins a design or context answer.

### Style: epic, episodic, fast

- **Episodic structure**: two acts of many short scenes in many places (a beach, two very different homes, a school, a shopping centre, a courtroom, a prison, a hideout).
- **Direct address**: the novel alternates between Sephy's and Callum's points of view; the play keeps this by letting characters speak their thoughts straight to the audience.
- **Minimal, transformable staging**: a few pieces of furniture, levels, projection and sound shift location in seconds; doubling (one actor, several small roles) is common.
- **Contemporary** costume and technology: phones, TV news, uniforms, riot police.

### Plot outline

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Act One** | Sephy Hadley, daughter of the powerful Cross politician Kamal Hadley, and Callum McGregor, a nought, have been friends since childhood, meeting secretly on the beach near the Hadleys' home. Callum's mother Meggie once worked for Sephy's mother Jasmine and was sacked. Callum is one of the few noughts admitted to Sephy's school, Heathcroft, and faces hatred from protesters and pupils; Sephy's attempts to show loyalty go wrong. Callum's father Ryan and older brother Jude are involved with the **Liberation Militia** (LM). A bomb explodes at a shopping centre; Ryan is arrested and put on trial; Sephy's mother secretly helps pay for his defence. |
| **Act Two** | Ryan is sentenced to death and does not survive prison. Callum's sister Lynette dies. Callum leaves school and is drawn into the LM, while Sephy is sent away to boarding school. The LM kidnaps Sephy with Callum's involvement; alone together, they finally admit their love. Callum is later captured, and he is executed. Sephy, pregnant with his child, is left to raise the baby in the divided world that killed its father. |

(The order of some events is quick and compressed; learn the sequence from your own copy and keep plot references general when unsure.)

### Characters

| Character | Performance note |
|---|---|
| **Sephy Hadley** | A Cross, privileged, idealistic, sometimes naive about her own privilege; quick, bright energy, speaks directly to the audience. Grows from a sheltered girl into a woman who pays a terrible price. |
| **Callum McGregor** | A nought, clever and hopeful at first; controlled posture at Heathcroft, where every move is watched; bitterness hardens his voice and body as his family is destroyed. |
| **Jude McGregor** | Callum's older brother: angry, mocking, a militant; restless energy and a sneer; he pulls Callum towards violence. |
| **Meggie McGregor** | Proud, practical, protective; wants her children safe more than equal; grief breaks her after Lynette's death. |
| **Ryan McGregor** | Callum's father, drinks, involved with the LM; tired, defeated body that stiffens into defiance at his trial. |
| **Lynette McGregor** | Callum's sister, damaged by a past attack; distant, dreamy, fragile. |
| **Kamal Hadley** | A senior Cross politician: public charm, private coldness; upright, expensive, controlled. |
| **Jasmine Hadley** | Sephy's mother: wealthy, unhappy, drinks; shows unexpected guilt and kindness towards the McGregors. |
| **Minerva** | Sephy's older sister, sharper and more worldly. |

### Key moments and how to stage them

1. **The beach (opening).** Two actors on a bare stage with sand-coloured light and the sound of gentle waves; they sit close, then Sephy reaches for his hand and Callum pulls back, aware that someone could see. Direct address shows what each is really thinking.
2. **Callum's first day at Heathcroft.** A wall of protesters (the ensemble, facing out) chanting as he walks the gauntlet; slow motion for the moment he is struck; projection of news cameras.
3. **The shopping centre bomb.** Sound builds (crowd chatter, shop music), then a huge bang from surround speakers, strobe, dust haze and blackout, and then emergency sirens. Callum pulling Sephy away beforehand creates dramatic irony.
4. **Ryan's trial.** A raised dock isolates him upstage; Kamal or the court sits on a higher level; the McGregors crowd together on a low bench. Cold, even white light.
5. **The kidnap.** A cramped hideout: a single bare bulb, Sephy on a chair; Callum hides his face, then reveals himself. The shift from captor to lover must be slow and careful.
6. **The ending.** Callum's execution staged with restraint (a sound cue, a change of light, the family's reaction) rather than shown; Sephy alone downstage speaks to the audience about the child.

### Design across the play

A **permanent, adaptable set**: a bare raised platform or steps, perhaps a grey concrete-textured floor, with projection screens or a cyclorama for locations (beach, school corridor, prison bars). Divide the world in design: warm, rich colours and good fabric for the Crosses' world; faded greys and cheap synthetic fabrics for the noughts. Uniforms (school, police, prison) make the power structure visible. Lighting and sound carry the fast transitions and the violence.

::: terms
- **Nought**: a pale-skinned person in the play's society, treated as second-class.
- **Cross**: a dark-skinned person in the play's society, belonging to the ruling group.
- **Dystopia**: an imagined society, often in the present or future, in which injustice is built into everyday life.
- **Liberation Militia (LM)**: the armed nought group fighting the Cross government, which Ryan, Jude and later Callum join.
- **Heathcroft**: the elite school that admits a few noughts, including Callum, for the first time.
- **Epic theatre**: a style using short scenes, direct address and visible staging so the audience thinks about society as well as feeling for characters.
- **Episodic structure**: a play built from many short, separate scenes rather than a few long ones.
- **Direct address**: a character speaking straight to the audience, often revealing private thoughts.
- **Doubling**: one actor playing more than one role.
:::

::: method
1. **Place the extract**: act, which characters, and what we know about each family at this point.
2. **For the 4-marker, design for the play's dystopian conventions**: contemporary, suggestive rather than fully naturalistic, and with the Cross/nought power divide visible.
3. **For the line**, show the character's place in the hierarchy through voice (accent, pitch, tone) and body (status, posture, eye contact).
4. **For the shaded section**, remember that touch, closeness and eye contact between a nought and a Cross are dangerous in this world; use space to show who is watched and who has power.
5. **For the 20-marker**, trace the character's arc or the design's journey from the beach to the end.
6. **Use the epic tools**: direct address, quick transitions, projection, ensemble.
:::

::: example Setting for the beach (4 marks)
You are designing a setting for the opening beach scene. The setting must reflect the conventions of contemporary dystopian theatre used in *Noughts and Crosses*. Describe your design ideas for the setting. (4)

A bare thrust stage with a raked platform covered in a pale sand-coloured floor cloth, so the scene can change in seconds (1). Upstage, a cyclorama lit in soft pinks and gold with a projection of slowly moving waves; a single low, grey rock (a painted foam block) where the two sit (1). Downstage, a strip of coiled wire fencing marks the edge of the Hadleys' private land, a quiet reminder that their meeting is forbidden (1). Everything else is bare, because the epic style needs the same space to become the school and the prison later; the fence stays through the whole play (1).
:::

::: example Performing Jude (8 marks)
You are performing the role of Jude. When Callum defends Sephy, Jude mocks him for thinking a Cross could care about a nought. Describe how you would use your vocal and physical skills to perform his taunt and explain the effects you want to create. (8)

I would lean back against the wall, arms folded, a slow smile before speaking (1). The tone is mock-sweet at first, stretching the vowels to imitate Callum's hope (1), then drops to a hard, flat delivery on the final words (1). I would push away from the wall and step into Callum's space, nose to nose (1), and flick his collar or cheek with a finger (1). The effect: the audience sees Jude's contempt and his resentment of his brother's hope (1), and also the pain underneath, since he has learned that hope is dangerous (1). This builds tension and foreshadows the violence Jude draws Callum into (1).
:::

::: example Space and interaction in the kidnap (12 marks, outline)
You are performing the role of Callum when he first reveals himself to Sephy in the hideout. Explain how you might use the performance space and interact with the actor playing Sephy to show his conflicting feelings. (12)

He begins in shadow upstage, face covered, circling behind her chair so she cannot see him. When he removes the mask he stays at a distance, by the door, as if ready to run. She turns and stands; he steps back. Voice hard and clipped when he gives orders, then breaking on her name. He places water beside her without looking at her. When she moves towards him he raises a hand to stop her but does not push her. Every move balances his LM loyalty against his love.
:::

::: example Costume design, the whole play (20 marks, outline)
Plan costume for the trial scene and across the play. (20)

Extract: Kamal in a sharp tailored dark suit of fine wool, a gold tie-pin and a lapel badge of the ruling party; Ryan in a stiff grey prison jumpsuit with a number stencilled on the back, too big for him; Meggie in her one good coat, a cheap, pilled navy wool, worn with dignity. Whole play: Heathcroft uniform (blazer with a crest) for Sephy and Callum, but Callum's second-hand and ill-fitting; Jude in a dark hooded jacket and combat trousers, LM armband; Sephy's clothes getting plainer as her world falls apart; Callum's final costume stripped to a plain white shirt. Colour divides the two groups throughout.
:::

::: watch
- Crosses are **dark-skinned and powerful**; noughts are **pale-skinned and oppressed**. Never reverse this.
- The 4-marker asks for **dystopian conventions**, not a real place and date.
- Revise **Cooke's** script, not another adaptation or the TV series.
- Do not over-build locations; the epic style needs **fast change**.
- Violence should be **staged safely and precisely** (sound, light, slow motion), not described vaguely.
- Link every choice to the power divide and the audience's response.
:::

::: test
You are performing the role of Sephy. After Callum's first day at Heathcroft, Sephy tries to comfort him but says something thoughtless. Describe how you would use your vocal and physical skills to perform her line and explain the effects you want to create. (8)
---
Levels. 7–8: exact vocal and physical choices on specific words; effects fully right for Sephy and the moment. 5–6: clear, detailed, effects appropriate. 3–4: some detail. 1–2: vague. Voice or body only cannot go above 3–4.

Indicative content: vocal - bright, earnest tone; quick pace; stress on words she thinks are kind; a catch or silence as she realises the hurt. Physical - reaching to touch his arm; he pulls away; her hand frozen in the air; eyes widening; stepping back. Effects: shows her privilege and blindness; dramatic irony; tension between the friends; audience sympathy for both.
:::

::: test
You are performing the role of Callum. In Act Two, Callum tells the audience that love between a nought and a Cross can never work. Describe how you would use your vocal and physical skills to perform this direct address and explain the effects you want to create. (8)
---
Levels as above.

Indicative content: vocal - flat, controlled tone; slow pace; bitter stress on key words; volume dropping on the final phrase. Physical - standing downstage centre alone, looking directly at the audience; arms folded or hands in pockets; a turn away at the end. Effects: shows his despair turning to bitterness; confides in the audience, so they feel complicit; foreshadows his joining the LM.
:::

::: test
