## What it is

This half of Theme B is about **human life**: where it came from, why it is valuable, and whether it can ever be right to end it. You need **Christian** and **Muslim** teachings (with the differences within each), **non-religious** views (atheist and humanist), and the **ethical arguments** based on the **sanctity of life** and **quality of life**. **Abortion** and **euthanasia** are two of the three Theme B issues on which you must be able to explain **contrasting beliefs**: the specification requires **Christianity and at least one other religious tradition** (here, Islam). Differences within Christianity and a non-religious view can be added, but a non-religious view can never be the only contrast.

### The origins of human life

**The scientific view — evolution.** Life on earth began over 3.5 billion years ago as simple single-celled organisms. **Charles Darwin**, in *On the Origin of Species* (1859), explained how species change over millions of years by **natural selection**: individuals best suited to their environment survive and pass on their characteristics. Humans (*Homo sapiens*) evolved from earlier primates and share a common ancestor with apes. The evidence includes fossils and DNA.

**Christian teaching.** "So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them" (Genesis 1:27). In the second account, "the Lord God formed a man from the dust of the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being" (Genesis 2:7); Eve is made from Adam's rib (Genesis 2:21-22). Humans are created last, deliberately, with a **soul**, and given responsibility for the world.

| Christian interpretation | Belief about human origins | Relationship with evolution |
|---|---|---|
| **Literal / creationist** | God made Adam and Eve directly, in their present form, a few thousand years ago | evolution is rejected because it contradicts Genesis; some argue "intelligent design" shows life is too complex to have evolved by chance |
| **Non-literal** | Genesis 1–2 teach religious truths: humans are made by God, in his image, with a special relationship to him | evolution is the process God used; God guided it and gave humans a soul. The **Catholic Church** accepts this: Pope John Paul II said in 1996 that evolution is "more than a hypothesis", while teaching that each human soul is created directly by God |

**Muslim teaching.** Allah created **Adam** from clay — "We created man from sounding clay, from mud moulded into shape" (Qur'an 15:26) — and breathed his spirit into him. The Qur'an compares Jesus' creation to Adam's: Allah "created him from dust; then He said to him, 'Be,' and he was" (Qur'an 3:59). Humans are created "in the best of stature" (Qur'an 95:4) and made **khalifah** on earth (Qur'an 2:30). The Qur'an also says Allah "made from water every living thing" (Qur'an 21:30).

- **Most Muslims** reject the idea that humans evolved from earlier primates, because Adam was created directly by Allah as the first human and first prophet.
- **Some Muslims** accept that other living things developed over time, and a minority of Muslim scientists accept human evolution guided by Allah — but all agree that humans exist because Allah willed it and that the soul comes from him.

**Non-religious views.** Atheists and humanists accept evolution as the best explanation, supported by overwhelming evidence. Scientists such as **Richard Dawkins** argue that natural selection explains the appearance of design without any need for God. Humanists believe humans are the product of natural processes, but that human life is still valuable because of our ability to think, love and create meaning.

### Sanctity of life and quality of life

| | **Sanctity of life** | **Quality of life** |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaning** | human life is **sacred** (holy) because it is created by God and belongs to him | the general **well-being** of a person — health, happiness, ability to live a fulfilling life |
| **Consequence** | only God can decide when life begins and ends; it is wrong to take innocent life | the value of a life can depend on its quality; it may be kinder to end great suffering |
| **Christian support** | humans are made in God's image (Genesis 1:27); "you knit me together in my mother's womb" (Psalm 139:13); "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you" (Jeremiah 1:5); "You shall not murder" (Exodus 20:13); the body is a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19) | some Christians use **agape** (selfless love): Jesus healed and relieved suffering; Joseph Fletcher's **situation ethics** says do the most loving thing in each situation |
| **Muslim support** | "do not kill the soul which Allah has forbidden, except by right" (Qur'an 17:33); whoever kills an innocent soul, it is as if he killed all mankind (Qur'an 5:32); no soul dies except by Allah's permission at a decreed time (Qur'an 3:145) | Islam values compassion and the relief of suffering, but quality of life is not a reason to end a life |
| **Who stresses it** | most Christians and Muslims | humanists, many atheists, and some liberal Christians |

A **humanist** believes life is valuable, not because it is sacred, but because of the experiences, relationships and choices it makes possible. So a humanist may say that where there is no quality of life left, a person should have the right to decide about ending it.

### Abortion

**Abortion** is the deliberate removal of a fetus from the womb to end a pregnancy. In Great Britain the **Abortion Act 1967** allows abortion if **two doctors** agree that continuing would risk the physical or mental health of the woman or her existing children; since 1990 the limit is **24 weeks**. There is **no time limit** where the woman's life is at risk, or there is a risk of grave permanent injury to her, or a substantial risk the baby would be born with a serious disability.

The key question is **when life begins**. Many religious people say at **conception**; others say later — at **ensoulment**, at **viability** (when the fetus could survive outside the womb) or at birth.

| Tradition | View on abortion | When the mother's life is at risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Catholic Church** | life begins at conception, so abortion is always gravely wrong: it kills an innocent human being (Catechism 2270-2271). Rape does not change this, though the Church offers support to the woman; adoption is encouraged | direct abortion is still not allowed, but the **doctrine of double effect** permits treatment to save the mother — such as removing a cancerous womb or an ectopic pregnancy — even if the fetus dies as a side effect, because the aim is to save life, not to kill |
| **Church of England** | strongly opposes abortion, but accepts that in strictly limited conditions it may be morally preferable to the alternative — the "lesser of two evils" | accepted when the mother's life or health is seriously threatened |
| **Other Protestants** (e.g. many Methodists) and liberal Christians | abortion is wrong in general, but may be the most loving action (situation ethics) in cases such as rape or severe disability; many support a woman's right to make the decision | accepted |
| **Islam** | life is a gift from Allah; abortion is generally haram. "Do not kill your children for fear of poverty" (Qur'an 17:31). A Hadith teaches the soul is breathed into the fetus after 120 days (Bukhari and Muslim), so many scholars permit abortion before then only for serious reasons, such as a severe abnormality or a pregnancy from rape; some (for example many Maliki scholars) forbid it from conception | after ensoulment, allowed only to save the mother's life: the mother is the "root" and the fetus the "branch", so her life takes priority — the lesser of two evils |
| **Humanists** | **pro-choice**: the woman should decide, as it is her body and she will live with the consequences; the fetus's interests grow as it develops; quality of life of the mother, family and child matters. Humanists UK supports legal access to abortion | the mother's life comes first |

**Ethical arguments about abortion**

| Arguments against (sanctity of life / pro-life) | Arguments for (quality of life / pro-choice) |
|---|---|
| the fetus is a human life from conception, sacred and created by God (Psalm 139:13) | the woman has the right to control her own body |
| abortion is killing an innocent person — "You shall not murder" (Exodus 20:13) | the child's quality of life may be very poor (severe disability) |
| disabled people have equal value; aborting for disability is discrimination | a pregnancy from rape can cause severe mental harm |
| adoption is an alternative; many couples want to adopt | the family's circumstances (poverty, other children) matter |
| it can cause the woman guilt and regret; the father has no legal say | banning abortion leads to unsafe illegal abortions |

Pro-life groups in Britain include **SPUC** (the Society for the Protection of Unborn Children) and **LIFE**; pro-choice groups include **BPAS** (British Pregnancy Advisory Service), which provides abortions.

### Euthanasia

**Euthanasia** means a gentle or easy death — ending a life to relieve suffering, often called "mercy killing".

- **Voluntary** — at the person's own request. **Non-voluntary** — the person cannot ask (e.g. in a coma). **Involuntary** — against the person's wishes (seen by almost everyone as murder).
- **Active** — deliberately causing death (e.g. a lethal injection). **Passive** — withdrawing or withholding treatment so that the person dies naturally. **Assisted suicide** — helping someone to end their own life.

In the UK active euthanasia is illegal (treated as murder or manslaughter) and assisting suicide is a crime under the **Suicide Act 1961**. Some Britons travel to **Dignitas** in Switzerland. It is legal in some form in countries such as the **Netherlands**, **Belgium** and **Canada**. A bill to allow assisted dying for terminally ill adults in England and Wales passed the House of Commons in 2025 but ran out of time in the House of Lords in 2026, so the debate continues.

| Tradition | View on euthanasia |
|---|---|
| **Catholic Church** | active euthanasia is murder and always wrong (Catechism 2277). But patients may refuse "extraordinary" or burdensome treatment that only prolongs dying (Catechism 2278), and painkillers that may shorten life are allowed if the aim is to relieve pain, not to kill (double effect). Supports **hospices** and palliative care |
| **Church of England and most Protestants** | oppose active euthanasia and assisted suicide, fearing vulnerable people would feel pressured to die; accept that doctors need not prolong life at all costs. **Dame Cicely Saunders**, a Christian nurse and doctor, founded **St Christopher's Hospice** in London in 1967, the start of the modern hospice movement |
| **Some liberal Christians** | support voluntary euthanasia as the most loving thing (agape; situation ethics), and argue God gave humans free will and compassion — "Blessed are the merciful" (Matthew 5:7) |
| **Islam** | euthanasia is **haram**: Allah decides the time of death (Qur'an 3:145), life is sacred (Qur'an 17:33) and suffering may be a test (Qur'an 2:155) that brings reward; the hardship of caring for the sick is also a test. Many scholars accept that **futile** treatment need not be continued when there is no hope of recovery |
| **Humanists** | support **assisted dying** for people who are terminally ill or incurably suffering and who clearly choose it, with safeguards: people should have **autonomy** over their own lives and a right to die with **dignity**. Humanists UK campaigns for a change in the law |

**Ethical arguments about euthanasia**: *for* — compassion, relief of unbearable suffering, autonomy (the right to choose), dying with dignity, the quality of life argument, strict safeguards work abroad. *Against* — sanctity of life, the "slippery slope" (pressure on the elderly, disabled or depressed to "choose" death), possible misdiagnosis or new cures, damage to the trust between doctor and patient, and that good palliative care can control most pain.

### Death and the afterlife

**Christianity**: death is not the end. Jesus said, "I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in me will live, even though they die" (John 11:25). After death people will be **judged** by God (the Parable of the Sheep and the Goats, Matthew 25:31-46) and go to **heaven** (eternal life with God) or **hell** (separation from God). Catholics also believe in **purgatory**, where souls are cleansed before heaven. Many believe in the **resurrection of the body** (1 Corinthians 15); some believe the soul goes straight to God.

**Islam**: belief in **akhirah** (life after death) is one of the six articles of faith. After death the soul waits in **barzakh**; on the **Day of Judgement** everyone is resurrected, the book of their deeds is opened and their deeds weighed. The righteous enter **Jannah** (paradise), the wicked **Jahannam** (hell). Allah "created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed" (Qur'an 67:2).

**Humanists and atheists**: there is no evidence for life after death; death is the end of the person. This life is the only one we have.

**The impact of these beliefs on the value of human life**

- For Christians and Muslims, life is a **gift and a test** that will be judged — so it must be used well, treated as sacred and not ended early. It also means death is not the worst thing, which can comfort the dying and bereaved.
- Belief that God alone decides death supports opposition to abortion and euthanasia.
- For humanists, because there is only **one life**, it is precious and should be lived as fully and happily as possible — which is why they stress **quality** of life and the right to choose a dignified death when that quality is gone.

::: terms
- **Evolution**: the scientific theory that living things, including humans, developed gradually over millions of years by natural selection.
- **Sanctity of life**: the belief that human life is sacred because it is created by God and belongs to him.
- **Quality of life**: the general well-being of a person — their health, happiness and ability to live a fulfilling life.
- **Abortion**: the deliberate removal of a fetus from the womb in order to end a pregnancy.
- **Ensoulment**: the moment the soul enters the body; many Muslims say 120 days after conception.
- **Viability**: the point at which a fetus could survive outside the womb.
- **Euthanasia**: ending someone's life to relieve suffering, sometimes called mercy killing; it may be voluntary, non-voluntary, active or passive.
- **Palliative care**: care for the dying that relieves pain and supports patient and family, as in a hospice, without aiming to cause death.
- **Doctrine of double effect**: the Catholic teaching that an action meant to do good (saving the mother, easing pain) may be allowed even if a bad effect, such as death, follows.
- **Akhirah**: the Muslim belief in life after death, judgement, paradise and hell.
:::

::: method
**Paper 2A gives each theme one five-part question: 1, 2, 4, 5 and 12 marks (24 in all).** Allow about 25 minutes per theme.

1. **1 mark — multiple choice**: four options A–D; choose one. Know the pairs that are easy to confuse: sanctity v quality of life; active v passive; voluntary v non-voluntary euthanasia.
2. **2 marks — "Give two..."**: two short, correct, *different* points. Only the first two are marked.
3. **4 marks — "Explain two contrasting (or similar) religious beliefs about..." — "In your answer you must refer to one or more religious traditions."** Each belief: 1 mark simple, 2 marks detailed. **Abortion** and **euthanasia** are named "contrasting beliefs" issues: the specification requires **Christianity and at least one other religious tradition**, so set a Christian view against a Muslim one — e.g. liberal Christians who accept euthanasia as the most loving act against Islam's "haram", or the Catholic "abortion is always wrong from conception" against the Muslim teaching that it may be allowed before 120 days for serious reasons. Differences within Christianity or a humanist view can be added, but never as the only contrast. If both beliefs say the same thing, only one is credited. If the question says "similar", keep both beliefs on the same side.
4. **4 marks — "Explain two ways / reasons..."**: 2 + 2 — make the point, then develop it with detail, an example or the effect on how believers act.
5. **5 marks — "Explain two... Refer to sacred writings or another source of religious belief and teaching in your answer."** Two developed points (4) plus **1 mark for a relevant, accurate source**: Genesis 1:27, Psalm 139:13, Exodus 20:13, Qur'an 17:33 or 3:145, the Catechism, a Hadith. A paraphrase with the reference is fine, but tie it to the point.
6. **12 marks — "'Statement.' Evaluate this statement. In your answer you should give reasoned arguments in support of this statement; should give reasoned arguments to support a different point of view; should refer to religious arguments; may refer to non-religious arguments; should reach a justified conclusion."** Plus **3 SPaG** marks. Spell euthanasia, sanctity, palliative and conception correctly.

**How the 12-mark levels are reached** (the whole answer goes in one level):
- **Level 1 (1–3)**: a point of view with reasons stated.
- **Level 2 (4–6)**: one point of view argued with a logical chain, **or** different views recognised, each with reasons. **Capped at Level 2 with no reference to religion.**
- **Level 3 (7–9)**: reasoned consideration of **different** points of view, with logical chains that draw on knowledge, and **clear reference to religion**.
- **Level 4 (10–12)**: a well-argued response — different views weighed with logical chains **leading to a justified judgement**, with religion *applied* to the issue.

On abortion and euthanasia, the strongest answers name the **ethical principle** behind each argument (sanctity of life, quality of life, autonomy, compassion, slippery slope) and show the **diversity** inside a religion — for example the Catholic Church's double effect, or Islam's 120-day teaching. Always decide in the conclusion and say why the winning argument is stronger.
:::

::: example 4-mark contrasting beliefs
Explain two contrasting beliefs about euthanasia. In your answer you must refer to one or more religious traditions. (4)

Muslims believe euthanasia is haram (forbidden) (1). This is because Allah alone decides when a person dies — the Qur'an says no soul dies except by Allah's permission at a decreed time — and suffering may be a test from Allah, so a Muslim should be cared for until they die naturally rather than have their life ended (1).

In contrast, some liberal Christians believe voluntary euthanasia can be right for a terminally ill person who asks for it (1). They follow situation ethics, which says the most loving action (agape) is right in each case, and Jesus taught "Blessed are the merciful" (Matthew 5:7), so they argue that helping someone in unbearable pain to die with dignity can be an act of Christian love (1).
:::

::: example 5-mark with a source
Explain two religious beliefs about the sanctity of life. Refer to sacred writings or another source of religious belief and teaching in your answer. (5)

Christians believe human life is sacred because every person is made in the image of God (1). Genesis 1:27 says "God created mankind in his own image", so each life has a special value that does not depend on health or usefulness, which is why many Christians oppose abortion and euthanasia (1) (source 1).

Muslims believe life is sacred because it is a gift from Allah and belongs to him (1). Only Allah has the right to decide when a life ends, and the Qur'an teaches that killing one innocent person is like killing the whole of humanity (Qur'an 5:32), so Muslims believe they must protect life and never take it without just cause (1).
:::

::: example 12-mark evaluation
"Abortion is always wrong." Evaluate this statement. In your answer you should give reasoned arguments in support of this statement; should give reasoned arguments to support a different point of view; should refer to religious arguments; may refer to non-religious arguments; should reach a justified conclusion. (12)

Many people agree, especially Catholic Christians. They believe life begins at conception, so from that moment the embryo is a human being made in God's image (Genesis 1:27). Psalm 139:13 says God knits each person together in the mother's womb, which shows that God already knows and values the unborn child. Therefore abortion is the killing of an innocent person, which breaks the commandment "You shall not murder". Even in cases of disability, pro-life Christians argue that every life has equal value, and that adoption is a loving alternative.

However, others disagree. Muslims believe life is sacred, yet many scholars teach that the soul enters the fetus after 120 days, and they allow abortion when the mother's life is in danger, because she is already a full person with responsibilities to her family — the lesser of two evils. The Church of England takes a similar view, and even the Catholic Church allows treatment to save the mother that may end the pregnancy under the doctrine of double effect, so the word "always" is questioned even within strict traditions. Humanists go further and argue that the woman should decide, since it is her body, and that a child's future quality of life matters. Liberal Christians using situation ethics say that in a case of rape the most loving action might be to allow an abortion.

In conclusion, I disagree with the statement. The sanctity of life argument is powerful and shows that abortion should never be taken lightly. But when the mother's life is at risk, both lives are sacred and one must be chosen, and almost every religious tradition, including Islam and the Church of England, accepts abortion then. Because there is at least one situation where abortion is widely seen as right, it cannot be "always" wrong.

(Level 4: different views, including diversity within Christianity and Islam, argued in chains; teachings applied; a justified judgement.)
:::

Indicative content: Christians and Muslims both believe life is a gift from God and belongs to him — so only God should end it (Qur'an 3:145; Job 1:21); both believe humans have special value — made in God's image (Genesis 1:27) / created in the best of stature (Qur'an 95:4); both forbid taking innocent life — "You shall not murder" (Exodus 20:13), Qur'an 17:33 and 5:32; both therefore generally oppose abortion and euthanasia; both believe people will be judged on how they treated life.
