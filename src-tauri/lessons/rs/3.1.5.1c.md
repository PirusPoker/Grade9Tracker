## What it is

This topic is about **authority** in Islam: where Muslims believe guidance comes from. God is the source of all guidance, and he has given it in three ways that this topic covers: through **prophets** (Risalah), through **holy books**, and — for Shi'a Muslims — through the **Imams**. In Sunni Islam, belief in the prophets and belief in the books are two of the **six articles of faith**. In Shi'a Islam, **Nubuwwah** (prophethood) and **Imamah** (the imamate) are two of the **five roots of Usul ad-Din**. So both branches share belief in prophets and books; the imamate is the key difference.

### Risalah (prophethood)

**Risalah** means "message" and refers to the way God communicates with humans through chosen people. A **nabi** is a prophet; a **rasul** is a messenger — a prophet who was also given a book or a new law to pass on. Every rasul is a nabi, but not every nabi is a rasul.

- God sent guidance to every people: "And We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying], 'Worship Allah and avoid Taghut'" (Qur'an 16:36). A hadith (in the Musnad of Ahmad) gives the number of prophets as 124,000; **25** are named in the Qur'an.
- All the prophets brought the **same basic message**: Tawhid (there is one God), how to worship him and how to live, and the reminder of the Day of Judgement. Islam is not seen as a new religion but as the original religion of all the prophets.
- Prophets were human, not divine, but chosen and protected by God from serious sin so they could be trusted examples.
- **Muhammad is the last prophet**: "Muhammad is not the father of [any] one of your men, but [he is] the Messenger of Allah and seal of the prophets" (33:40). The **Seal of the Prophets** (Khatam an-Nabiyyin) means no prophet will come after him. Both Sunni and Shi'a Muslims hold this.

The spec names three prophets you must know in detail.

#### Adam — the first man and first prophet

- God created Adam from clay (15:26) and breathed his spirit into him (15:29), making him his **khalifah** (steward, vicegerent) on earth (2:30).
- "And He taught Adam the names — all of them" (2:31): God gave humans knowledge and reason, which even the angels did not have. God commanded the angels to bow to Adam; they obeyed, but **Iblis** refused out of pride (2:34), which is the origin of Shaytan's enmity to humans.
- Adam and his wife **Hawwa** (Eve) lived in the Garden but were tempted by Shaytan to eat from the forbidden tree (7:20-22). The Qur'an blames them both, not Hawwa alone.
- Adam **repented** and God forgave him: "Then Adam received from his Lord [some] words, and He accepted his repentance" (2:37). This is why Islam has **no doctrine of original sin** — every baby is born pure (in a state of **fitrah**).
- Adam was sent to earth and taught his children to worship the one God. Some Muslim traditions say he built the first house of worship on the site of the Ka'aba.

**Importance**: Adam shows that humans are special (given knowledge, the angels bowed to him), that they are responsible as God's khalifah, that sin comes from giving in to temptation, and that God always forgives those who sincerely repent. Guidance began with the very first human.

#### Ibrahim — the friend of God

- Ibrahim grew up among idol-worshippers; his father **Azar** made idols (6:74). He reasoned his way to God by watching a star, the moon and the sun set and concluding that only their Creator deserved worship (6:75-79).
- He **smashed the idols** of his people and was thrown into a fire, but God said, "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham" (21:69).
- "And Allah took Abraham as an intimate friend" (4:125) — he is called **Khalilullah**, the friend of God.
- He was willing to **sacrifice his son** (Isma'il, according to most Muslims) when God commanded it; God stopped him and provided a ram (37:99-111). Muslims remember this at **Id-ul-Adha**.
- With Isma'il he **rebuilt the Ka'aba** in Makkah as a house for worshipping the one God (2:127). Many actions of hajj go back to Ibrahim, his wife Hajar and Isma'il.
- He received the **Scrolls of Abraham** (87:18-19).
- He is the ancestor of many prophets: through **Isma'il** came Muhammad; through **Ishaq** came Musa, Dawud and Isa. The Qur'an says he "was neither a Jew nor a Christian" but a **hanif**, a pure monotheist who submitted to God (3:67). God also made him "a leader (imam) for the people" (2:124).

**Importance**: Ibrahim is the model of total **submission** (islam) and of Tawhid — he gave up his family's religion, faced death and was ready to give up his son. He links Islam, Judaism and Christianity as "Abrahamic" faiths, and his story is re-lived every year in hajj and Id-ul-Adha.

#### Muhammad — the Seal of the Prophets

| Date (CE) | Event |
|---|---|
| c. 570 | Born in Makkah, of the Quraysh tribe; orphaned young and raised by his grandfather and then his uncle Abu Talib |
| as a young man | Worked as a merchant; known as **al-Amin**, "the trustworthy"; married **Khadijah** |
| 610 | While meditating in the **Cave of Hira** on Mount Nur, visited by the angel Jibril on the **Night of Power** — "Recite in the name of your Lord who created" (96:1) |
| 610–622 | Preached Tawhid in Makkah; faced mockery and persecution from the Quraysh, who made money from the idols at the Ka'aba |
| 622 | The **Hijrah**: migrated to **Madinah**, where he led the first Muslim community; year 1 of the Islamic calendar |
| 630 | Returned to Makkah peacefully, removed the idols from the Ka'aba and dedicated it to God alone |
| 632 | Performed his farewell hajj; died in Madinah |

**Importance**:
- He received the **Qur'an**, God's final and complete revelation, so his message will never be replaced.
- His life is the perfect example to follow: "There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of Allah an excellent pattern" (33:21). His words and actions (the **Sunnah**), recorded in the **Hadith**, are the second source of authority after the Qur'an. Muslims copy him in how they pray, wash, eat, treat family and neighbours.
- He was sent "as a mercy to the worlds" (21:107) — for all humanity, not just Arabs.
- Muslims show him great respect, saying "peace be upon him" after his name, and never make pictures of him, to avoid anything that could become worship of a human.

### The holy books (kutub)

Muslims believe God revealed books to several messengers. Believing in all of them is part of faith (2:285; 4:136). All were revealed from the same heavenly source, which the Qur'an calls the "Mother of the Book" (Umm al-Kitab) (43:4).

| Book | Revealed to | Qur'an reference | Status today, in Muslim belief |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Suhuf Ibrahim** (Scrolls of Abraham) | Ibrahim | 87:18-19; 53:36-37 | lost — no copy survives |
| **Tawrat** (Torah) | Musa (Moses) | 3:3; 5:44 | original message revealed by God, but the text has been changed or lost over time |
| **Zabur** (Psalms) | Dawud (David) | 4:163; 17:55 | as above |
| **Injil** (Gospel) | Isa (Jesus) | 5:46; 57:27 | as above; the Injil was one message given to Isa, not the four Gospels written about him |
| **Qur'an** | Muhammad | 2:2; 15:9 | the final, complete and unchanged word of God |

#### The Qur'an: revelation

- Revealed to Muhammad by **Jibril** over about **23 years**, from the Night of Power in 610 CE to 632 CE, in **Arabic** (12:2), in parts as situations arose.
- Muslims believe Muhammad could not read or write, so they see the beauty of the Qur'an as proof it came from God. The Qur'an challenges anyone who doubts it to produce a single surah like it (2:23).
- Muhammad's companions **memorised** it (a person who has memorised all of it is a **hafiz**) and wrote it down on whatever was available. After his death it was gathered into one volume under the first caliph **Abu Bakr**, and a standard copy was sent out under the third caliph **Uthman**. It has **114 surahs**. Sunni and Shi'a Muslims use the same Qur'an.

#### The Qur'an: authority

- It is the **literal word of God**, not Muhammad's words: "This is the Book about which there is no doubt, a guidance for those conscious of Allah" (2:2).
- It is **unchanged and protected by God**: "Indeed, it is We who sent down the message, and indeed, We will be its guardian" (15:9).
- It is **final and complete**: it confirms the earlier books and acts as a criterion over them (5:48), correcting where the earlier texts were changed.
- It is the **highest source of authority** — the basis of Shari'ah (Islamic law) with the Sunnah. Where they need to apply it to new situations, Sunni Muslims also use the agreement of scholars (ijma) and reasoning by analogy (qiyas); Shi'a Muslims look to the teachings of the Imams and to qualified scholars.
- Because it is God's word **in Arabic**, a translation is only an interpretation. Muslims learn to recite it in Arabic, perform wudu before touching it (many cite 56:79), keep it wrapped and on a high shelf, and many try to memorise it.

#### The authority of the earlier books

- Muslims **respect** the Torah, Psalms, Gospel and Scrolls because they came from God; Jews and Christians are called **Ahl al-Kitab**, "People of the Book".
- But most Muslims believe the texts as they now exist are **not in their original form**: the Scrolls are lost, and the Torah and Gospel were altered over time (**tahrif**; the Qur'an speaks of people who "distort words from their [proper] usages", 5:13). They were also given to particular peoples for a particular time.
- So they have **less authority** than the Qur'an. Muslims may read them with respect, but where they differ from the Qur'an, the Qur'an is followed. Some Muslims hold that only the **meaning** was changed by interpretation rather than the words themselves; all agree the Qur'an is the final authority.

### The imamate in Shi'a Islam

The split between Sunni and Shi'a Islam began with the question: **who should lead the Muslims after Muhammad died in 632?**

| | Sunni Islam | Shi'a Islam |
|---|---|---|
| Who succeeded Muhammad | **Abu Bakr**, chosen by the leading companions; then Umar, Uthman and Ali — the four "Rightly Guided Caliphs" | **Ali**, Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law, **appointed by Muhammad on God's command** |
| Evidence | Muhammad did not name a successor, so the community chose the best person (shura, consultation) | At **Ghadir Khumm** (632) Muhammad said, "For whomever I am his mawla (master), Ali is his mawla" (a hadith also found in Sunni collections) |
| The leader's role | A political leader who protects the community; not infallible and cannot add to religion | An **Imam**: religious and spiritual guide as well as leader |
| "Imam" also means | the person who leads prayer in a mosque | this, and the divinely chosen successors of Muhammad |

**What Shi'a Muslims believe about the Imams**

- **Imamah** is one of the five roots of Usul ad-Din: believing in the Imams is part of faith.
- The Imams are **appointed by God**, each named by the one before, and come from the **Ahl al-Bayt**, the Prophet's family, through Ali and Muhammad's daughter **Fatimah**.
- They are **ma'sum** — protected from sin and error — and have God-given knowledge to interpret the Qur'an and Sunnah correctly. They are **not** prophets and bring no new revelation; Muhammad is the Seal of the Prophets.
- Qur'anic support Shi'a Muslims cite: God made Ibrahim "a leader (imam) for the people" and said the covenant would not reach "the wrongdoers" among his descendants (2:124); "obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you" (4:59), which Shi'a understand as the Imams (Sunni Muslims usually read it as rightful rulers and scholars); and the hadith that Muhammad left "two weighty things", the Qur'an and his family (the hadith of the thaqalayn, a version of which is in Sahih Muslim).
- **Twelver** (Ithna Ashari) Shi'a — the large majority — recognise **twelve Imams**, beginning with Ali, then his sons **Hasan** and **Husayn**. Husayn was killed at **Karbala** in 680 CE, remembered each year at **Ashura**. The twelfth Imam, **Muhammad al-Mahdi**, went into **occultation** (ghaybah, hiding) in the 9th century; he is alive but hidden and will return as **the Mahdi**, with Isa, to bring justice before the Day of Judgement.
- **Isma'ili** Shi'a follow a different line after the sixth Imam and have a living, present Imam, the **Aga Khan**.

**Role and significance today**

- While the Twelfth Imam is hidden, Twelver Shi'a follow senior scholars (**mujtahids**; the most senior is a **marja' at-taqlid**, "source of emulation", such as Grand Ayatollah Ali al-Sistani in Najaf) who interpret the law on the Imam's behalf.
- The Imams are **models** of faith, justice and courage — above all Husayn, who died rather than accept an unjust ruler.
- Shi'a Muslims visit the shrines of the Imams, for example at Karbala and Najaf in Iraq and Mashhad in Iran, and hope for their **intercession** on the Day of Judgement.
- Belief in the Mahdi's return gives **hope** that injustice will end.
- Sunni Muslims love and respect the Prophet's family too, and many also believe a Mahdi will come — but they do not believe in divinely appointed, sinless Imams. This is one of the main differences between the two branches.

**Influence of the whole topic on Muslims today**: Muslims read and recite the Qur'an daily and base decisions on it; they follow the Sunnah of Muhammad in everyday life; they respect Jews and Christians as People of the Book; they learn from the stories of Adam (repentance) and Ibrahim (submission); Shi'a Muslims follow their marja' and commemorate the Imams.

::: terms
- **Risalah**: prophethood — the way God communicates his message to humans through chosen prophets.
- **Nabi and rasul**: a nabi is a prophet; a rasul is a messenger who was also given a book or law to pass on.
- **Seal of the Prophets**: Muhammad's title (33:40), meaning he is the last prophet and no other will follow him.
- **Sunnah**: the example of Muhammad — what he said, did and approved — recorded in the Hadith.
- **Khalifah**: steward or vicegerent — the role God gave Adam and all humans, to look after the earth.
- **Kutub**: the holy books revealed by God: the Scrolls of Abraham, Torah, Psalms, Gospel and Qur'an.
- **Tahrif**: the belief that earlier scriptures were changed or corrupted over time, so only the Qur'an is unchanged.
- **Ahl al-Kitab**: "People of the Book" — Jews and Christians, who received earlier revelations from God.
- **Imamah**: the imamate — the Shi'a belief in divinely appointed, sinless Imams descended from Ali; one of the five roots.
- **Ma'sum**: protected from sin and error — a quality Shi'a Muslims believe the Imams possess.
- **Ghaybah**: occultation — the hiding of the Twelfth Imam, who Twelver Shi'a believe will return as the Mahdi.
- **Ahl al-Bayt**: the family of the Prophet Muhammad, especially Ali, Fatimah, Hasan and Husayn.
:::

::: method
Paper 1 (Islam) has two five-part questions: **Q1 Beliefs** (this topic sits here) and **Q2 Practices**. Each is 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 12 = 24 marks. **SPaG (3 marks)** is assessed on the 12-mark question in the **Beliefs** section, so this topic's 12-mark is marked out of 12 + 3.

1. **1 mark — multiple choice.** Four options A–D; tick one. Distractors are usually other Arabic terms or other prophets (e.g. Zabur/Injil, Dawud/Isa). Check which book goes with which prophet.
2. **2 marks — "Give two…"** Two short, separate, accurate facts (e.g. "Give two holy books other than the Qur'an"). No explanation needed; only your first two answers count.
3. **4 marks — "Explain two ways…" / "Explain two reasons…" / "Explain two contrasting beliefs…"** Two points; each earns 1 for a simple explanation and 2 for a detailed one. For **"influence"** questions the second mark must show what a Muslim **does** because of the belief (e.g. follows the Sunnah by praying as Muhammad did). For **"contrasting"** questions the two points must genuinely differ — here, the obvious contrast is **Sunni vs Shi'a** views on leadership after Muhammad, or the Qur'an vs the earlier books. If both points say the same thing, only one is credited.
4. **5 marks — "Explain two… Refer to sacred writings or another source of Muslim belief and teaching in your answer."** Two developed points (2 + 2) **plus 1** for a relevant, accurate source: a Qur'an reference (33:40, 15:9, 2:2, 2:37, 21:69, 87:18-19, 2:124, 4:59) or a hadith (Ghadir Khumm, the thaqalayn). A precise paraphrase with the reference is safest.
5. **12 marks — "'Statement.' Evaluate this statement. In your answer you should: refer to Muslim teaching; give reasoned arguments to support this statement; give reasoned arguments to support a different point of view; reach a justified conclusion."** Levels:
   - **Level 1 (1–3)**: a point of view with a reason.
   - **Level 2 (4–6)**: one view with a logical chain of reasoning, OR different views each with a reason. **Maximum Level 2 if there is no reference to religion.**
   - **Level 3 (7–9)**: different points of view, each developed with logical chains and **clear reference to Muslim teaching**.
   - **Level 4 (10–12)**: a well-argued response — both sides developed, teaching applied accurately, arguments **weighed** (say which is stronger and why) and a conclusion that follows from the argument.
   Structure: argument for (teaching + "because… this means…"), argument against (same), then evaluation and conclusion. For SPaG, spell key terms correctly (Risalah, Imamah, Qur'an, Sunnah) and write in paragraphs. Showing **diversity** — Sunni and Shi'a views — is an easy way to show "different points of view".
:::

::: example 4-mark influence
Explain two ways in which belief in Muhammad as the Seal of the Prophets influences Muslims today. (4)

First, Muslims believe Muhammad was the last prophet, so his message is final and will never be replaced (1). This means they treat the Qur'an he received as the complete guide for life and reject anyone who claims to be a new prophet (1).

Second, Muslims believe Muhammad is the perfect example, "an excellent pattern" (33:21) (1). So they follow his Sunnah in everyday life — for example praying in the way he prayed, washing before prayer as he did, and being honest in business as he was known as al-Amin (1).
:::

::: example 5-mark with source
Explain two Muslim beliefs about the authority of the Qur'an. Refer to sacred writings or another source of Muslim belief and teaching in your answer. (5)

Muslims believe the Qur'an is the literal word of God, revealed to Muhammad by the angel Jibril over 23 years (1). It is not Muhammad's own words, so it has the highest authority of any source and is the foundation of Shari'ah law (1). The Qur'an says of itself, "This is the Book about which there is no doubt, a guidance for those conscious of Allah" (2:2) (1 for source).

Muslims also believe the Qur'an has been kept unchanged since it was revealed (1). Unlike the Torah and Gospel, which they believe were altered over time, God has promised to protect it — "indeed, We will be its guardian" (15:9) — so it can be completely trusted and overrides earlier books where they differ (1).
:::

::: example 4-mark contrasting
Explain two contrasting beliefs about who should have led the Muslim community after Muhammad. (4)

Sunni Muslims believe the community was right to choose Abu Bakr as the first caliph (1). They hold that Muhammad did not name a successor, so the leading companions chose the most suitable person by consultation, followed by Umar, Uthman and Ali — the Rightly Guided Caliphs (1).

Shi'a Muslims believe Muhammad appointed his cousin and son-in-law Ali on God's command (1). They point to Ghadir Khumm, where Muhammad said, "For whomever I am his mawla, Ali is his mawla", and believe leadership should pass through God-chosen, sinless Imams from the Prophet's family (1).
:::

::: example 12-mark evaluation
"The Qur'an is the only holy book Muslims need." Evaluate this statement. (12 + 3 SPaG)

**For the statement**: Muslims believe the Qur'an is the final and complete revelation, given to Muhammad, the Seal of the Prophets (33:40). It is the literal word of God and, unlike every earlier book, has been protected from change — "We will be its guardian" (15:9). The Scrolls of Abraham are lost, and most Muslims believe the Torah and Gospel were altered (tahrif), so they cannot be fully trusted. Because the Qur'an confirms what was true in the earlier books and corrects what was changed (5:48), everything a Muslim needs from them is already contained in it. So in practice a Muslim can live by the Qur'an alone as far as holy books are concerned.

**Against the statement**: believing in all the revealed books is itself an article of faith (2:285; 4:136), so a Muslim cannot simply dismiss the others — they were genuine revelations and show that God has always guided humanity. Reading them can help Muslims understand the stories of Musa, Dawud and Isa and build respect with Jews and Christians as People of the Book. More importantly, the Qur'an itself points Muslims to other sources: it commands them to follow the Prophet (33:21), and without the **Sunnah** in the Hadith Muslims would not know how to pray five times a day or how to perform hajj, because the Qur'an does not give every detail. Shi'a Muslims add that the Qur'an needs to be interpreted correctly by the Imams and, today, by qualified scholars, citing the hadith that Muhammad left two weighty things — the Qur'an and his family.

**Conclusion**: the statement is right that the Qur'an is the only **holy book** with complete and unchanged authority, so Muslims do not need to rely on the earlier scriptures. But it is misleading if it means the Qur'an is the only source Muslims need: the Qur'an itself tells them to follow Muhammad's example, and Muslims of both branches rely on the Sunnah (and for Shi'a the Imams) to put it into practice. So I partly agree: the Qur'an is supreme, but it is not used alone.
:::

::: watch
- Muddling the books and prophets: **Tawrat–Musa, Zabur–Dawud, Injil–Isa, Suhuf–Ibrahim, Qur'an–Muhammad**. One wrong pairing loses a 1- or 2-mark answer.
- Saying Muslims "reject" the Bible. Muslims believe the Torah, Psalms and Gospel were revealed by God; they believe the texts were later changed, so they have less authority than the Qur'an.
- Writing that Shi'a Imams are prophets. They are not — Muhammad is the Seal of the Prophets; the Imams interpret, they do not bring new revelation.
- 4-mark influence questions answered with belief only: the second mark needs what Muslims **do**.
- A vague source in the 5-mark ("the Qur'an says Muhammad is important") earns nothing. Learn 33:40, 33:21, 15:9, 2:2 and 2:124.
- 12-mark answers with no Muslim teaching are capped at Level 2; forgetting Sunni/Shi'a diversity wastes an easy "different point of view".

Indicative content — for: the split began over who should succeed Muhammad (Abu Bakr chosen vs Ali appointed at Ghadir Khumm); Imamah is one of the five roots of Usul ad-Din but not a Sunni article of faith; belief in sinless, divinely guided Imams affects authority — Shi'a follow the Imams' teachings and today a marja' at-taqlid; it explains other differences, such as Ashura commemorating Husayn, pilgrimage to the Imams' shrines at Karbala and Najaf, and belief in the hidden Twelfth Imam.
Against: Sunni and Shi'a share far more than divides them — Tawhid, prophethood with Muhammad as the Seal (33:40), the same Qur'an, angels, the Day of Judgement, salah, sawm, zakah and hajj; other differences also matter, such as Adalat as a root, Khums, the Ten Obligatory Acts, and differences in prayer (e.g. combining prayers, the turbah); many Muslims say the differences are of emphasis and all are one ummah; Sunni Muslims also honour Ali and the Ahl al-Bayt and many expect a Mahdi.
Conclusion: justified, e.g. the imamate is the root of the historical split and of several other differences, so it is the most important difference — but the differences are small compared with shared core beliefs.
