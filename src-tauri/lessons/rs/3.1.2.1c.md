## What it is

At the centre of Christianity is **Jesus Christ**: Christians believe he is God the Son who became human, died on the cross, rose from the dead and ascended to heaven — and that through him humans are **saved** from sin and brought back into a right relationship with God. The spec asks for five things: the **incarnation** and Jesus as **Son of God**; the **crucifixion, resurrection and ascension**; **sin**, including **original sin**; the **means of salvation** (law, grace and Spirit); and the **role of Christ in salvation**, including **atonement**.

### The incarnation and Jesus as the Son of God

- **Incarnation** means "becoming flesh": God the Son took on a human body and nature in Jesus. John's Gospel says, "The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us" (John 1:14).
- **The virgin birth**: Matthew and Luke teach that Jesus was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit, not by a human father. The angel tells Mary, "The Holy Spirit will come on you" (Luke 1:35), and Matthew links the birth to Isaiah's prophecy that he will be called **Immanuel**, "God with us" (Matthew 1:18–23; Isaiah 7:14).
- **Fully God and fully human**: the Council of Chalcedon (451) taught that Jesus is one person with two natures, divine and human. The Nicene Creed calls him "true God from true God", sharing the same being as the Father, who by the Holy Spirit was made flesh of the Virgin Mary and became man.
- **Son of God**: this title means Jesus has a unique relationship with God the Father and is the second Person of the Trinity. At his baptism a voice from heaven says, "You are my Son, whom I love" (Mark 1:11); at the crucifixion the centurion says, "Surely this man was the Son of God!" (Mark 15:39).
- **Why it matters**: God is not distant — he shares human life, suffering and death; Jesus shows what God is like ("Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father", John 14:9) and is a perfect example of how to live; only someone both human and divine could reconcile humans to God.
- **Different views**: most Christians accept the virgin birth literally; some liberal Christians see it as a symbolic way of saying Jesus came from God, while still believing God was uniquely present in him.

### The crucifixion, resurrection and ascension

| Event | What happened (in the Gospels) | Why it matters to Christians |
|---|---|---|
| **Crucifixion** | Jesus was arrested, tried by the Jewish council and Pontius Pilate, and crucified on Good Friday. He said, "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing" (Luke 23:34) and cried, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Mark 15:34). He told the criminal beside him he would be with him in paradise (Luke 23:43). | His death was a sacrifice that took away the sin of the world; it shows God's love and forgiveness; God shares human suffering, so Christians who suffer know God understands. The cross is the central Christian symbol. |
| **Resurrection** | On the third day (Easter Sunday) women found the tomb empty; the risen Jesus appeared to Mary Magdalene, the disciples and more than five hundred people (Mark 16; Luke 24; John 20; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8). | It shows Jesus' victory over sin and death and proves he is the Son of God; it gives hope of eternal life to believers; Paul says without it faith is useless (1 Corinthians 15:14). It is celebrated every Sunday and at Easter. |
| **Ascension** | Forty days after the resurrection Jesus was taken up into heaven in the disciples' sight and a cloud hid him (Luke 24:50–53; Acts 1:9–11). | Jesus returned to the Father and is "seated at the right hand of the Father" (Creeds), ruling with God; it prepares for the coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost; angels promise he will come again (Acts 1:11). |

Most Christians believe these events literally happened. Some liberal Christians interpret the resurrection and ascension more spiritually — the disciples experienced Jesus as alive and with God — while still seeing them as central.

### Sin, including original sin

- **Sin** is any thought or action that separates people from God — breaking God's law or failing to love God and neighbour. Paul writes, "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).
- **Original sin** is the tendency to sin that all humans are born with, traced back to Adam and Eve's disobedience in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3). Through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin (Romans 5:12).
- **Different views**: the **Catholic Church** and many Protestants (following **St Augustine**) teach that all humans inherit original sin, which is why baptism — even of infants — is needed to wash it away. The **Orthodox** Church speaks of **ancestral sin**: humans inherit the consequences of Adam's sin (death and a tendency to sin) but not his personal guilt. Many liberal Christians read Genesis 3 as a symbolic story about human nature rather than history.
- **Consequences**: sin damages humans' relationship with God and each other, and "the wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23).

### The means of salvation: law, grace and Spirit

**Salvation** means being saved from sin and its consequences and restored to a right relationship with God, leading to eternal life.

- **Law**: God gave the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20) and Jesus taught the greatest commandments — love God and love your neighbour (Mark 12:29–31). Keeping God's law shows faith and love, and some Christians stress that salvation involves living a good life. James writes that "faith by itself, if it is not accompanied by action, is dead" (James 2:17). But most Christians agree no one can keep the law perfectly, so the law alone cannot save.
- **Grace**: salvation is God's free, undeserved gift, not something earned: "For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith — and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God — not by works, so that no one can boast" (Ephesians 2:8–9). "The wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 6:23).
- **Spirit**: the Holy Spirit is God's power within believers, helping them turn from sin, have faith and grow in holiness. Jesus says no one can enter the kingdom of God unless born of water and the Spirit (John 3:5). The Spirit produces "fruit" — love, joy, peace, patience… (Galatians 5:22–23).

| Tradition | Emphasis on salvation |
|---|---|
| **Protestant** (from Martin Luther) | Saved by **grace alone through faith alone** (sola gratia, sola fide); good works follow as a result of salvation but do not earn it. |
| **Catholic** | Saved by God's grace, received through faith **and** expressed in good works and the sacraments (baptism, Eucharist, reconciliation), which are channels of grace. |
| **Orthodox** | Salvation is a lifelong process of becoming more like God (**theosis**), through grace, faith, the sacraments and the Holy Spirit. |
| **Evangelical / Pentecostal** | Stress a personal decision to accept Jesus as saviour and being "born again" by the Spirit. |

### The role of Christ in salvation, including atonement

- **Atonement** means "at-one-ment": restoring the relationship between God and humans broken by sin. Christians believe Jesus' death and resurrection achieved this.
- Jesus said he came "to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45). God "so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life" (John 3:16). Jesus is called the "atoning sacrifice for our sins" (1 John 2:2).
- **Ways Christians understand atonement**:
  - **Sacrifice / substitution**: Jesus, who was sinless, took the punishment for human sin in humanity's place, like the sacrifices of the Old Testament but once for all.
  - **Ransom / victory**: Jesus' death and resurrection defeated sin, death and evil and set humanity free.
  - **Moral example**: Jesus' self-giving love on the cross shows how much God loves humans and moves them to repent and love in return.
- **Resurrection completes it**: through rising again Jesus conquered death, opening the way to eternal life for all who believe.
- **Influence today**: Christians are grateful and try to follow Jesus' example of love and forgiveness; they confess sins trusting they will be forgiven; they share the "good news" (gospel); the Eucharist remembers Christ's sacrifice.

::: terms
- **Incarnation**: God the Son becoming flesh — taking on a human body and nature as Jesus, fully God and fully human.
- **Son of God**: a title for Jesus showing his unique relationship with God the Father as the second Person of the Trinity.
- **Crucifixion**: the execution of Jesus by being nailed to a cross, on Good Friday, under Pontius Pilate.
- **Resurrection**: Jesus rising from the dead on the third day (Easter Sunday), showing victory over sin and death.
- **Ascension**: Jesus being taken up into heaven forty days after the resurrection, to be with the Father.
- **Sin**: any thought or action that separates humans from God, such as breaking God's law or failing to love.
- **Original sin**: the tendency to sin that all humans are born with, traced to Adam and Eve's disobedience (Genesis 3).
- **Salvation**: being saved from sin and its consequences and restored to God, leading to eternal life.
- **Grace**: God's free, undeserved love and gift of salvation, which cannot be earned.
- **Atonement**: "at-one-ment" — restoring the relationship between God and humans through Jesus' death and resurrection.
:::

::: method
**Paper 1 (Christianity), Beliefs question: five parts worth 1, 2, 4, 5 and 12 marks.** About 25 minutes for the whole question.

1. **1 mark — multiple choice**: four options, tick one. Separate lookalikes: incarnation vs resurrection vs ascension; atonement vs salvation; grace vs law.
2. **2 marks — "Give two…"**: two short, separate, correct points ("Jesus was born of a virgin"; "Jesus was fully God and fully human"). Only the first two count.
3. **4 marks — "Explain two ways…" / "Explain two contrasting beliefs…"**: two points, each **developed** (1 simple + 1 detailed). Develop with a teaching, an example or the effect on a believer. **Contrasting** pairs for this topic: salvation by faith alone (Protestant) vs faith and good works/sacraments (Catholic); inherited guilt (Augustine, Catholic) vs ancestral sin (Orthodox); literal vs symbolic readings of the virgin birth or the Fall; atonement as substitution vs as moral example.
4. **5 marks — "Explain two… Refer to sacred writings or another source of Christian belief and teaching in your answer"**: two developed points (4) **+ 1 for a relevant, accurate source**. Strong sources here: John 1:14, John 3:16, Mark 10:45, Romans 3:23, Romans 6:23, Ephesians 2:8–9, James 2:17, Luke 23:34, Acts 1:9–11, the Nicene Creed. Link the source to the point it supports.
5. **12 marks — "'Statement.' Evaluate this statement."** Paper 1 bullets: *refer to Christian teaching; give reasoned arguments to support this statement; give reasoned arguments to support a different point of view; reach a justified conclusion.* (The themes paper says "refer to religious arguments; may refer to non-religious arguments".) The Beliefs 12-mark carries **+3 SPaG** — spell incarnation, crucifixion, atonement, resurrection correctly.

**How the 12-mark levels are reached** (the whole answer is placed in a level):
- **Level 1 (1–3)**: a point of view with a reason stated.
- **Level 2 (4–6)**: one view argued with a logical chain of reasoning, **or** different views recognised, each with reasons. **Maximum Level 2 with no reference to religion.**
- **Level 3 (7–9)**: reasoned consideration of **different** points of view, with logical chains drawing on knowledge, and **clear reference to religion**.
- **Level 4 (10–12)**: a **well-argued** response weighing different views with logical chains **leading to a justified judgement**, with Christian teaching *applied* to the issue.

For "most important" statements ("The resurrection is the most important event in Jesus' life"), argue for the named event, then for at least one rival (incarnation, crucifixion), then conclude by showing **how they depend on each other** and which is the key — that weighing earns Level 4.
:::

::: example 4-mark — contrasting beliefs about salvation
Explain two contrasting Christian beliefs about how people are saved. (4)

Many Protestants believe people are saved by faith alone (1): following Martin Luther, they teach that salvation is God's free gift of grace which cannot be earned, and good works are simply the result of being saved (1). In contrast, Catholics believe that although salvation comes through God's grace, faith must be shown through good works and the sacraments (1), because "faith by itself, if it is not accompanied by action, is dead" (James 2:17), so receiving the Eucharist and loving one's neighbour are part of the way to salvation (1).
:::

::: example 5-mark — the incarnation
Explain two Christian beliefs about the incarnation. Refer to sacred writings or another source of Christian belief and teaching in your answer. (5)

Christians believe that in the incarnation God the Son became a human being (1). John's Gospel says "The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us" (John 1:14), which means God did not stay distant but shared human life, including suffering and death (1 + 1 source). Christians also believe Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary (1), so he was fully God and fully human at the same time — which is why his death could reconcile humans to God, as only a human could represent humanity and only God could save it (1).
:::

::: example 12-mark — faith and salvation
"Belief in Jesus is all that Christians need to be saved from sin." Evaluate this statement. In your answer you should: refer to Christian teaching; give reasoned arguments to support this statement; give reasoned arguments to support a different point of view; reach a justified conclusion. (12 + 3 SPaG)

Many Protestant Christians would agree. Paul teaches that "it is by grace you have been saved, through faith… not by works, so that no one can boast" (Ephesians 2:8–9). Because "all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23), no one could ever be good enough to earn salvation; Jesus has already paid the price through his atoning death, so all that is needed is to accept this gift by believing in him. John 3:16 promises eternal life to "whoever believes in him". The criminal on the cross did no good works, yet Jesus told him he would be in paradise (Luke 23:43), which shows faith alone was enough.

However, Catholics and many other Christians would say belief on its own is not enough. James writes that "faith by itself, if it is not accompanied by action, is dead" (James 2:17): real belief must change how a person lives. In the Parable of the Sheep and the Goats (Matthew 25) people are judged on whether they fed the hungry and visited prisoners, not just on what they believed. Catholics also teach that God's grace comes through the sacraments — baptism removes original sin and the Eucharist and confession strengthen the believer — and that the Holy Spirit works in a Christian throughout life. "Belief" could also be misunderstood as just agreeing Jesus existed, which even non-Christians do.

In conclusion, the statement is only partly true. All Christians agree salvation is a gift of God's grace through Jesus rather than a reward for being good. But the stronger view is that true belief in Jesus is not just an opinion — it is trust that shows itself in love, the life of the Spirit and, for many, the sacraments — so "all that Christians need" is too simple.
:::

::: q
Give two Christian beliefs about Jesus' incarnation. (2)
---
Any two (1 each): God the Son became flesh / took human form ("The Word became flesh", John 1:14); Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary; Jesus is fully God and fully human; he is "Immanuel", God with us (Matthew 1:23); the incarnation was so that Jesus could save humans from sin; God shares human suffering.
:::

::: q
What is original sin, and how do Catholic and Orthodox Christians understand it differently? (3)
---
Original sin is the tendency to sin that all humans are born with, going back to Adam and Eve's disobedience in Genesis 3 (1). Catholics (following Augustine) believe all humans inherit the guilt of original sin, which baptism washes away, including in infants (1). The Orthodox speak of ancestral sin: people inherit the consequences of Adam's sin — death and a tendency to sin — but not his personal guilt (1).
:::

::: q
Explain the meaning of the ascension and why it is important to Christians. (3)
---
Forty days after the resurrection Jesus was taken up into heaven while the disciples watched (Luke 24:50–53; Acts 1:9–11) (1). It shows Jesus returned to the Father and now reigns with him, "seated at the right hand of the Father" (Creeds) (1); it prepared for the Holy Spirit to come at Pentecost and includes the promise that he will come again (Acts 1:11), so Christians look forward to the Second Coming (1).
:::

::: q
Explain two ways in which belief in the crucifixion influences Christians today. (4)
---
Any two, developed (1 + 1 each). Christians forgive others (1) because Jesus prayed for those crucifying him, "Father, forgive them" (Luke 23:34), so they try to forgive even people who have seriously hurt them (1). It gives comfort in suffering (1): God himself suffered on the cross, so Christians believe he understands their pain and is with them in it (1). It is remembered in worship (1): the cross or crucifix is displayed in churches and the Eucharist recalls Jesus' sacrifice (1).
:::

::: q
Explain what Christians mean by "atonement" and give two ways they understand how Jesus achieved it. (4)
---
Atonement means "at-one-ment" — restoring the broken relationship between God and humans caused by sin (1); Christians believe Jesus achieved this through his death and resurrection (1). Any two (1 each): substitution — Jesus took the punishment for sin in humanity's place; sacrifice — his death was the perfect, once-for-all sacrifice ("atoning sacrifice for our sins", 1 John 2:2); ransom/victory — he gave his life "as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45) and defeated sin and death; moral example — his self-giving love moves people to repent and love.
:::

::: q
Explain the roles of law, grace and the Spirit in salvation. (3)
---
Law: God's commandments (the Ten Commandments; love God and neighbour) show how to live, but no one keeps them perfectly, so the law alone cannot save (1). Grace: salvation is God's free, undeserved gift through Jesus — "by grace you have been saved, through faith" (Ephesians 2:8) (1). Spirit: the Holy Spirit works within believers, bringing them to faith, helping them turn from sin and live holy lives (John 3:5; Galatians 5:22–23) (1).
:::

::: q
Why is the resurrection important for Christian beliefs about salvation? (3)
---
It shows Jesus has defeated death and sin, so his sacrifice on the cross was accepted by God (1). It proves Jesus is the Son of God, as he claimed (1). It promises that believers will also be raised to eternal life — Paul says that without it Christian faith would be useless (1 Corinthians 15:14) (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Incarnation** (becoming human, at Christmas) is not **resurrection** (rising from the dead, at Easter) or **ascension** (going up to heaven) — keep the events straight.
- Do not write that "Christians believe Jesus was only a good teacher" — Christians believe he is **God the Son**, fully divine and fully human.
- **Contrasting** questions on salvation must show a real difference — Protestant faith alone vs Catholic faith with works and sacraments — not two versions of the same idea.
- **Atonement** answers must say **how** Jesus' death restores the relationship (sacrifice, substitution, ransom, example), not just "he died for our sins".
- Quote scripture accurately with book, chapter and verse, or paraphrase — a garbled quotation loses the source mark.
- Original sin: be careful to say the Orthodox view differs; do not claim all Christians believe in inherited guilt.
:::
