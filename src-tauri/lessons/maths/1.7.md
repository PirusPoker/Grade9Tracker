## What it is

A **ratio** compares parts: mixing squash $1 : 4$ means 1 part squash to 4 parts water — 5 parts in all, of which squash is $\frac15$. Ratios simplify like fractions (divide every part by the same number) and can be written with a 1 on one side: $3 : 8 = 1 : 2.67$ or $0.375 : 1$.

**Direct proportion**: two quantities are in direct proportion when one is a fixed multiple of the other — double one, the other doubles. Cost and quantity, distance and time at constant speed. The graph is a straight line through the origin.

Almost every question is one of four types: **simplify** a ratio, **share** in a ratio, find a **missing part** given one part or the difference, or **scale** a recipe / price / map (the unitary method).

::: method
1. **Simplify:** same units first (convert $2$ m $: 40$ cm to $200 : 40$), then divide by the HCF: $5 : 1$. Fractions in a ratio? Multiply through to clear them.
2. **Share £$T$ in the ratio $a : b : c$:** total parts $= a + b + c$; one part $= T \div (a + b + c)$; multiply up. Check the shares add back to $T$.
3. **Given one share, not the total:** find one part from the share you know, then scale. "Ali gets £45 in the ratio $3 : 5$" → one part is £15 → Bo gets £75.
4. **Given the difference:** the difference in parts is $b - a$; one part $=$ difference $\div (b - a)$.
5. **Unitary method:** find the value of *one* unit, then multiply. 8 pens cost £6.40 → one pen 80p → 15 pens £12.00.
6. **Combining ratios:** $a : b = 2 : 3$ and $b : c = 4 : 5$ — make the $b$s match: $8 : 12$ and $12 : 15$, so $a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15$.
7. **Best value:** cost per unit, or units per pound — compare like with like.
:::

::: example Simplifying and the $1 : n$ form
Write $45 : 27$ in its simplest form, and $8 : 5$ in the form $1 : n$.

HCF of 45 and 27 is 9: $5 : 3$.

$8 : 5 = 1 : \dfrac58 = 1 : 0.625$.
:::

::: example Sharing
Share £560 between three people in the ratio $2 : 3 : 5$.

$2 + 3 + 5 = 10$ parts; one part $= 56$. Shares: £112, £168, £280. (Check: $112 + 168 + 280 = 560$ ✓)
:::

::: example Given the difference
Two numbers are in the ratio $4 : 7$ and differ by 27. Find them.

$7 - 4 = 3$ parts $= 27$, so one part $= 9$. Numbers: $36$ and $63$.
:::

::: example Combining two ratios
In a school, the ratio of Year 10 to Year 11 is $5 : 4$, and Year 11 to Year 12 is $6 : 5$. There are 300 in Year 11. Find the ratio Year 10 : Year 11 : Year 12 and the number in Year 12.

Match Year 11: $5 : 4 = 15 : 12$ and $6 : 5 = 12 : 10$. So $15 : 12 : 10$.

Year 11 is 12 parts $= 300$, one part $= 25$. Year 12 $= 250$.
:::

::: example Unitary method and scaling a recipe
A recipe for 6 people uses 450 g of flour and 4 eggs. How much flour for 15 people, and how many eggs for 9 people?

Flour per person: $75$ g; for 15: $1125$ g.

Eggs: $4 \div 6 = \frac23$ per person; for 9: $6$ eggs.
:::

::: example Best buy
Cereal: 500 g for £2.20 or 750 g for £3.15. Which is better value?

Per 100 g: $2.20 \div 5 = 44$p; $3.15 \div 7.5 = 42$p. The 750 g box is better value.
:::

::: q
Simplify $2.5 \text{ km} : 750 \text{ m}$.
---
$2500 : 750 = 10 : 3$.
:::

::: q
Write $12 : 30$ in the form $1 : n$ and in the form $n : 1$.
---
$1 : 2.5$ and $0.4 : 1$.
:::

::: q
Share 84 sweets in the ratio $3 : 4$.
---
7 parts, one part 12: $36$ and $48$.
:::

::: q
£1200 is shared between A, B and C in the ratio $1 : 3 : 4$. How much more does C get than A?
---
One part $= 150$; C $- $ A $= 3$ parts $= \pounds 450$.
:::

::: q
The ratio of boys to girls in a club is $5 : 7$. There are 42 girls. How many members are there?
---
One part $= 6$; total $12 \times 6 = 72$.
:::

::: q
Sam and Jo share money in the ratio $2 : 9$. Jo gets £63 more than Sam. How much do they share?
---
$7$ parts $= 63$, one part $= 9$; total $11 \times 9 = \pounds 99$.
:::

::: q
$a : b = 3 : 4$ and $b : c = 6 : 7$. Find $a : b : c$.
---
$b$ to 12: $9 : 12$ and $12 : 14$. $a : b : c = 9 : 12 : 14$.
:::

::: q
12 identical books weigh 9 kg. How much do 20 of them weigh?
---
One book $0.75$ kg; $20 \times 0.75 = 15$ kg.
:::

::: q
A map has scale $1 : 25\,000$. Two villages are 6.4 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they in km?
---
$6.4 \times 25000 = 160\,000$ cm $= 1.6$ km.
:::

::: q
Paint is mixed from blue and white in the ratio $3 : 5$. How much blue is needed to make 6 litres of paint, and how much white must be added to 1.2 litres of blue?
---
Blue $= \frac38 \times 6 = 2.25$ litres. With 1.2 litres blue (one part $0.4$): white $= 2$ litres.
:::

::: q
Which is better value: 6 cans for £2.70 or 10 cans for £4.60?
---
$45$p per can vs $46$p per can. The 6-pack.
:::

::: watch
- **Same units before simplifying.** $2 \text{ m} : 40 \text{ cm}$ is $5 : 1$, not $1 : 20$.
- Sharing: divide by the **total number of parts**, not by the number of people.
- If you are given one **share** or a **difference**, find the value of one part first — do not divide the number you are given by the total parts.
- Check shares add to the total. It is a five-second check that catches the commonest slip.
- Combining ratios: scale so the **shared** quantity has the same number in both.
- Best value: compare **per unit** (per 100 g, per can) — never just the prices.
:::
