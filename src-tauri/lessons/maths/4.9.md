## What it is

**Perimeter** is the distance round the edge; **area** is the space inside. The formulae to know:

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $l \times w$ |
| Triangle | $\frac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$ |
| Parallelogram | $\text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$ |
| Trapezium | $\frac12(a + b)h$ — half the sum of the parallel sides, times the distance between them |
| Circle | $\pi r^2$; circumference $2\pi r$ (or $\pi d$) |
| Sector of angle $\theta°$ | $\dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2$; arc length $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ |

Compound shapes are split into these pieces (or a big piece with a hole cut out). A **semicircle** is half a circle — half the area, but its perimeter is half the circumference **plus the diameter**.

Units: area is in square units; converting $1$ m$^2 = 10\,000$ cm$^2$ (the length factor squared).

::: method
1. **Identify the shape(s).** Split a compound shape with dashed lines into rectangles, triangles, semicircles; or subtract a hole from a whole.
2. **Perpendicular height** — the height must be at right angles to the base. A slanted side of a parallelogram or triangle is not the height (though it may give it via Pythagoras or trig).
3. **Perimeter:** walk round the outside and add every edge. Missing lengths in an L-shape come from the opposite sides. Do not include the internal dashed lines.
4. **Circles:** $r$ or $d$? Halve a diameter before squaring. Leave $\pi$ in the answer if asked for exact.
5. **Sectors:** fraction of the circle $= \dfrac{\theta}{360}$. Perimeter of a sector $=$ arc $+ 2r$.
6. **Working backwards:** given the area, set the formula equal to it and solve for the missing length.
7. **Units:** convert lengths to the same unit before calculating; write the unit squared on an area.
:::

::: example Trapezium and parallelogram
(a) A trapezium has parallel sides 8 cm and 14 cm, 5 cm apart. (b) A parallelogram has base 9 cm, slant side 6 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm. Find each area.

(a) $\dfrac12(8 + 14) \times 5 = 55$ cm$^2$.

(b) $9 \times 4 = 36$ cm$^2$ (the 6 cm is not used).
:::

::: example An L-shape
An L-shape has outer dimensions 10 cm by 8 cm with a 4 cm by 5 cm rectangle removed from one corner. Find its area and perimeter.

Area $= 80 - 20 = 60$ cm$^2$.

Perimeter: the outline still has the same total horizontal and vertical extents, so $2(10 + 8) = 36$ cm (the notch adds nothing to an L's perimeter).
:::

::: example Circle and semicircle
A semicircle has diameter 12 cm. Find its area and perimeter.

$r = 6$: area $= \dfrac12 \pi \times 36 = 18\pi = 56.5$ cm$^2$.

Perimeter $= \dfrac12 \times 2\pi \times 6 + 12 = 6\pi + 12 = 30.8$ cm.
:::

::: example Sector
A sector has radius 9 cm and angle $140°$. Find its arc length, perimeter and area.

Arc $= \dfrac{140}{360} \times 2\pi \times 9 = 7\pi = 22.0$ cm. Perimeter $= 22.0 + 18 = 40.0$ cm.

Area $= \dfrac{140}{360} \times \pi \times 81 = 31.5\pi = 99.0$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example Working backwards
A circle has area 200 cm$^2$. Find its circumference.

$\pi r^2 = 200 \Rightarrow r = \sqrt{200/\pi} = 7.98$. Circumference $= 2\pi \times 7.98 = 50.1$ cm.
:::

::: example A shape with a hole
A square of side 20 cm has a circle of diameter 20 cm cut from it. Find the area left, exactly.

$400 - \pi \times 100 = 400 - 100\pi$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
Find the area of a triangle with base 14 cm and height 9 cm, and of a parallelogram with base 7.5 cm and height 6 cm.
---
$63$ cm$^2$; $45$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A trapezium has parallel sides 6 cm and 11 cm and area 68 cm$^2$. Find the distance between the parallel sides.
---
$\dfrac12(17)h = 68 \Rightarrow h = 8$ cm.
:::

::: q
Find the circumference and area of a circle of radius 4.5 cm.
---
$9\pi = 28.3$ cm; $20.25\pi = 63.6$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A circular pond has circumference 31.4 m. Find its diameter and area.
---
$d = 10.0$ m; area $= 78.5$ m$^2$.
:::

::: q
Find the perimeter and area of a quarter circle of radius 8 cm.
---
Arc $= 4\pi$; perimeter $= 4\pi + 16 = 28.6$ cm; area $= 16\pi = 50.3$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A sector of radius 6 cm has area $15\pi$ cm$^2$. Find its angle.
---
$\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 36\pi = 15\pi \Rightarrow \theta = 150°$.
:::

::: q
A running track is a rectangle 100 m by 60 m with a semicircle on each 60 m end. Find the total perimeter and area.
---
Perimeter $= 200 + \pi \times 60 = 388$ m. Area $= 6000 + \pi \times 900 = 8830$ m$^2$ (3 s.f.).
:::

::: q
An equilateral triangle has side 10 cm. Find its area.
---
Height $= 5\sqrt3$; area $= \dfrac12 \times 10 \times 5\sqrt3 = 25\sqrt3 = 43.3$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
Convert $3.2$ m$^2$ to cm$^2$, and $45\,000$ mm$^2$ to cm$^2$.
---
$32\,000$ cm$^2$; $450$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A rectangle has perimeter 40 cm and length 12 cm. Find its area.
---
Width $8$; area $96$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A circle of radius 5 cm is inscribed in a square. Find the exact area of the square not covered by the circle.
---
Square side 10: $100 - 25\pi$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: watch
- **Perpendicular height**, always. A slanted side in a parallelogram is a distractor.
- Semicircle perimeter includes the **diameter**; sector perimeter includes **two radii**.
- Halve the diameter before $\pi r^2$. Using $d$ in $\pi r^2$ gives four times the area.
- Trapezium: half the **sum** of the parallel sides times the height — not the product.
- Area units are **squared**; converting area between units uses the **square** of the length factor.
- "Exact" answers keep $\pi$ (and surds). $18\pi$, not $56.5$.
:::

::: test
A circle has radius 6.5 cm. Find its circumference and its area, to 3 significant figures. (2)
---
$2\pi \times 6.5 = 40.8$ cm (1); $\pi \times 6.5^2 = 133$ cm$^2$ (1).
:::

::: test
A sector has radius 8 cm and angle $135^\circ$. Find its arc length and its area, to 3 significant figures. (3)
---
Fraction $= \dfrac{135}{360} = 0.375$ (1); arc $= 0.375 \times 2\pi \times 8 = 18.8$ cm (1); area $= 0.375 \times \pi \times 64 = 75.4$ cm$^2$ (1).
:::

::: test
A trapezium has parallel sides 9 cm and 15 cm and height 7 cm. Find its area. (1)
---
$\dfrac{1}{2}(9 + 15) \times 7 = 84$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: test
A shape is a 12 cm by 8 cm rectangle with a semicircle of diameter 8 cm attached to one 8 cm side. Find its area, to 3 significant figures. (2)
---
$96 + \dfrac{1}{2}\pi \times 4^2$ (1) $= 121$ cm$^2$ (1).
:::

::: test
Find the perimeter of the same shape, to 3 significant figures. (2)
---
Three straight sides $12 + 8 + 12 = 32$, plus the arc $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \pi \times 8 = 12.57$ (1); $44.6$ cm (1).
:::

::: test
A circle has area 200 cm$^2$. Find its radius to 3 significant figures. (2)
---
$r = \sqrt{\dfrac{200}{\pi}}$ (1) $= 7.98$ cm (1).
:::

::: test
A sector of radius 4 cm has arc length 10 cm. Find the angle of the sector, to 1 decimal place. (2)
---
$\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi \times 4 = 10$ (1); $\theta = 143.2^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends an angle of $60^\circ$ at the centre. Find the area of the minor segment cut off by the chord, to 3 significant figures. (3)
---
Sector area $= \dfrac{60}{360} \times \pi \times 100 = 52.36$ (1); triangle area $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 10^2 \times \sin 60^\circ = 43.30$ (1); segment $= 9.06$ cm$^2$ (1).
:::
