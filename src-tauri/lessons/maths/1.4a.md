## What it is

$a^n$ means $a$ multiplied by itself $n$ times: $2^5 = 32$. The **index laws** let you combine powers of the same base without working them out:

$$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n} \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$

$$a^0 = 1 \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \qquad a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a} \qquad a^{m/n} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m$$

**Square numbers** $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225$ and **cube numbers** $1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000$ are worth knowing by sight — they are how you spot roots without a calculator. A **square root** undoes a square ($\sqrt{144} = 12$); a **cube root** undoes a cube ($\sqrt[3]{343} = 7$).

Prime factorisation ties in: $360 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$, and a number is a perfect square exactly when every power in its factorisation is even.

::: terms
- **Square number**: the result of multiplying an integer by itself, e.g. 49 = 7².
- **Cube number**: the result of multiplying an integer by itself three times, e.g. 64 = 4³.
- **Square root**: a number that multiplies by itself to give the original number; √49 = 7.
- **Index (power)**: the small number showing how many times the base is multiplied by itself.
- **Multiplying powers**: add the indices: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ.
- **Dividing powers**: subtract the indices: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ.
- **Power of a power**: multiply the indices: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ.
- **Zero index**: a⁰ = 1 for any non-zero a.
- **Negative index**: a⁻ⁿ = 1 ÷ aⁿ — the reciprocal.
- **Fractional index**: a^(1/n) is the nth root of a; a^(m/n) is the nth root of a, raised to the power m.
:::

::: method
1. **Same base only.** The laws work for $2^3 \times 2^4$, not for $2^3 \times 3^4$. If bases differ, either make them the same ($4^3 = (2^2)^3 = 2^6$) or work the numbers out.
2. **Multiply → add the powers. Divide → subtract. Power of a power → multiply.** A number in front is handled separately: $3x^2 \times 4x^5 = 12x^7$.
3. **Negative power = reciprocal:** $5^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{25}$; $\left(\dfrac23\right)^{-1} = \dfrac32$ — flip the fraction, then apply the positive power.
4. **Fractional power = root:** the bottom of the fraction is the root, the top is the power. Do the root **first**: $27^{2/3} = (\sqrt[3]{27})^2 = 3^2 = 9$.
5. **Solving $2^x = 64$-type equations:** write both sides as powers of the same base and equate the powers.
6. **Prime factors for roots:** $\sqrt{2^4 \times 3^2 \times 5^2} = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5 = 60$ — halve each power. For a cube root, divide each power by 3.
7. **HCF / LCM** from prime factors as in 1.1: smallest powers for HCF, largest for LCM.
:::

::: example Index laws with numbers
Simplify, leaving your answer as a power of 3: (a) $3^4 \times 3^5$, (b) $3^8 \div 3^2$, (c) $(3^2)^5$, (d) $\dfrac{3^7 \times 3^{-2}}{3^3}$.

(a) $3^9$. (b) $3^6$. (c) $3^{10}$. (d) $3^{7 - 2 - 3} = 3^2$.
:::

::: example Index laws in algebra
Simplify (a) $2a^3 \times 5a^4$, (b) $\dfrac{12x^6}{3x^2}$, (c) $(2y^3)^4$, (d) $\left(\dfrac{a^2}{b}\right)^3$.

(a) $10a^7$. (b) $4x^4$. (c) $2^4 y^{12} = 16y^{12}$. (d) $\dfrac{a^6}{b^3}$.
:::

::: example Negative and fractional indices
Evaluate (a) $4^{-2}$, (b) $\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^{-2}$, (c) $64^{2/3}$, (d) $16^{-3/4}$.

(a) $\dfrac{1}{16}$. (b) $\left(\dfrac52\right)^2 = \dfrac{25}{4}$. (c) $(\sqrt[3]{64})^2 = 4^2 = 16$. (d) $\dfrac{1}{(\sqrt[4]{16})^3} = \dfrac{1}{8}$.
:::

::: example Equating powers
Solve $8^x = 32$.

$8 = 2^3$ and $32 = 2^5$: $2^{3x} = 2^5$, so $3x = 5$, $x = \dfrac53$.
:::

::: example Roots from prime factors
$N = 2^6 \times 3^4 \times 5^2$. Find $\sqrt N$, and the smallest integer $k$ such that $Nk$ is a perfect cube.

$\sqrt N = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 360$.

A cube needs every power to be a multiple of 3: $2^6$ is fine, $3^4$ needs two more 3s, $5^2$ needs one more 5. $k = 3^2 \times 5 = 45$.
:::

::: example Writing a number as a power
Write $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{8}}$ as a power of 2.

$\sqrt 8 = 8^{1/2} = (2^3)^{1/2} = 2^{3/2}$, so $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt 8} = 2^{-3/2}$.
:::

::: q
Simplify $5^3 \times 5^6 \div 5^4$, leaving your answer as a power of 5.
---
$5^{3 + 6 - 4} = 5^5$.
:::

::: q
Simplify (a) $4p^2q \times 3pq^4$, (b) $\dfrac{18m^5}{6m^{-1}}$, (c) $(3t^2)^3$.
---
(a) $12p^3q^5$. (b) $3m^{6}$. (c) $27t^6$.
:::

::: q
Evaluate $7^0$, $3^{-3}$ and $\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^{-2}$.
---
$1$; $\dfrac{1}{27}$; $\dfrac{16}{9}$.
:::

::: q
Evaluate $125^{1/3}$, $81^{3/4}$ and $32^{-2/5}$.
---
$5$; $(\sqrt[4]{81})^3 = 27$; $\dfrac{1}{(\sqrt[5]{32})^2} = \dfrac14$.
:::

::: q
Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{27}{8}\right)^{-2/3}$.
---
$\left(\dfrac{8}{27}\right)^{2/3} = \left(\dfrac23\right)^2 = \dfrac49$.
:::

::: q
Solve $2^{3x - 1} = 16$.
---
$2^{3x-1} = 2^4 \Rightarrow 3x - 1 = 4 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac53$.
:::

::: q
Solve $9^x = 27$.
---
$3^{2x} = 3^3 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac32$.
:::

::: q
Write $\sqrt[3]{16}$ as a power of 2, and $\dfrac{1}{25\sqrt5}$ as a power of 5.
---
$16^{1/3} = 2^{4/3}$; $25\sqrt5 = 5^2 \times 5^{1/2} = 5^{5/2}$, so $5^{-5/2}$.
:::

::: q
$M = 2^4 \times 3^2 \times 7^2$. Write down $\sqrt M$, and find the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $Mn$ is a perfect square and a multiple of 5.
---
$\sqrt M = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7 = 84$. $M$ is already a square; to be a multiple of 5 and still square, multiply by $5^2$: $n = 25$.
:::

::: q
Find the HCF and LCM of $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$ and $2 \times 3^3 \times 7$, as products of prime factors.
---
HCF $= 2 \times 3^2 = 18$; LCM $= 2^3 \times 3^3 \times 5 \times 7 = 7560$.
:::

::: q
Which is larger, $2^{40}$ or $3^{25}$? Justify without a calculator's full display.
---
$2^{40} = (2^8)^5 = 256^5$ and $3^{25} = (3^5)^5 = 243^5$. Since $256 > 243$, $2^{40}$ is larger.
:::

::: watch
- **Add powers when multiplying, multiply powers when raising a power.** $(2^3)^4 = 2^{12}$, not $2^7$.
- The laws need the **same base**. $2^3 \times 2^4 = 2^7$, but $2^3 \times 4^2$ must become $2^3 \times 2^4$ first.
- A negative power is a reciprocal, **not a negative number**: $4^{-2} = \frac{1}{16}$.
- Fractional power: **root first**, then power. $27^{2/3}$ via $\sqrt[3]{27} = 3$ is easy; via $27^2 = 729$ is not.
- $x^0 = 1$ for any $x \ne 0$ — including $(-5)^0$ and $(3x^2)^0$.
- The coefficient is not part of the power: $(2y^3)^4 = 16y^{12}$ — the 2 gets the power too.
:::
