## What it is

Number in everyday life: **units** (metric and the conversions between them), **time** (the 24-hour clock, timetables, durations), **money** (bills, best buys, wages, tax) and **currency** exchange. None of it is hard mathematics; all of it is where careless marks vanish, because the arithmetic hides inside reading the question.

The metric conversions you must know:

| Length | Mass | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ km $= 1000$ m | $1$ t (tonne) $= 1000$ kg | $1$ l $= 1000$ ml |
| $1$ m $= 100$ cm | $1$ kg $= 1000$ g | $1$ l $= 1000$ cm$^3$ |
| $1$ cm $= 10$ mm | $1$ g $= 1000$ mg | $1$ ml $= 1$ cm$^3$ |

Area and volume units convert by the **square** and **cube** of the length factor: $1$ m$^2 = 100^2 = 10\,000$ cm$^2$; $1$ m$^3 = 100^3 = 1\,000\,000$ cm$^3$.

::: method
1. **Convert before calculating.** Put everything into the same unit, and write the unit on every line.
2. **Time is base 60.** $2.5$ hours is $2$ h $30$ min; $1$ h $45$ min is $1.75$ h. Never write $1$ h $45$ min as $1.45$. To find a duration, count up to the next whole hour, then on.
3. **Currency:** identify the rate as "1 of this $=$ so many of that". To go *from* the 1-side, multiply; to go *to* it, divide. Write the rate as a fraction with units to see which way.
4. **Money:** keep pence as decimals of pounds (£3.05, not £3.5), round to 2 d.p. at the end only, and check whether an answer must be rounded *up* (how many boxes are needed) or *down* (how many can be bought).
5. **Timetables:** read across the row for one train, down the column for one station; check the day/night boundary with the 24-hour clock.
6. **Reading a scale:** work out what one small division is worth (the gap between labelled marks divided by the number of spaces), then count.
:::

::: example Converting area and volume
Convert $3.5$ m$^2$ to cm$^2$, and $250\,000$ cm$^3$ to m$^3$.

$3.5 \times 10\,000 = 35\,000$ cm$^2$.

$250\,000 \div 1\,000\,000 = 0.25$ m$^3$.
:::

::: example Time and speed
A train leaves at 09:47 and arrives at 12:23. Its journey is 208 km. Find the journey time and the average speed in km/h.

09:47 → 10:00 is 13 min; 10:00 → 12:23 is 2 h 23 min. Total 2 h 36 min $= 2.6$ h.

Speed $= 208 \div 2.6 = 80$ km/h.
:::

::: example Currency both ways
The exchange rate is £1 = 1.27 US dollars. (a) Convert £350 to dollars. (b) Convert 508 dollars to pounds.

(a) Going *from* the pound side, multiply: $350 \times 1.27 = 444.50$ dollars.

(b) Going *to* the pound side, divide: $508 \div 1.27 = 400$, so £400.
:::

::: example Rounding up or down
Tiles are sold in boxes of 8 at £14.99 a box. A floor needs 90 tiles. How much do the tiles cost?

$90 \div 8 = 11.25$, so 12 boxes are needed (round **up** — 11 boxes is not enough). $12 \times 14.99 = \pounds 179.88$.
:::

::: example A bill with a percentage
A restaurant bill is £84.50 plus a 12.5% service charge, shared equally by four people. How much does each pay?

$84.50 \times 1.125 = 95.0625$; $\div 4 = 23.765\ldots$, so £23.77 each (to the nearest penny; in practice the group pays £95.06 or £95.07).
:::

::: example Wages
Kim earns £11.40 an hour for a 37.5-hour week, and time and a half for overtime. One week she works 42 hours. Find her pay.

Basic: $37.5 \times 11.40 = 427.50$. Overtime: $4.5 \times 17.10 = 76.95$. Total £504.45.
:::

::: q
Convert $2.35$ km to metres, $480$ g to kg, and $0.7$ litres to ml.
---
$2350$ m; $0.48$ kg; $700$ ml.
:::

::: q
Convert $4.2$ m$^2$ to cm$^2$ and $6\,800\,000$ cm$^3$ to m$^3$.
---
$42\,000$ cm$^2$; $6.8$ m$^3$.
:::

::: q
A film starts at 19:35 and lasts 2 hours 48 minutes. When does it finish?
---
19:35 + 2 h $= 21{:}35$; $+ 48$ min $= 22{:}23$.
:::

::: q
Write 3 hours 20 minutes as a decimal number of hours, and 4.15 hours in hours and minutes.
---
$3\frac{20}{60} = 3.33$ h (3 s.f.); $0.15 \times 60 = 9$ min, so 4 h 9 min.
:::

::: q
€1 $=$ £0.86. Convert €250 to pounds and £129 to euros.
---
$250 \times 0.86 = \pounds 215$; $129 \div 0.86 = €150$.
:::

::: q
A coach holds 53 people. 400 students are going on a trip. How many coaches are needed?
---
$400 \div 53 = 7.5\ldots$, so 8 coaches.
:::

::: q
Pens cost 65p each. What is the greatest number that can be bought for £20, and how much change is left?
---
$2000 \div 65 = 30.7$, so 30 pens for £19.50; change 50p.
:::

::: q
An electricity bill: 1450 units at 28.4p per unit, plus a standing charge of £0.62 per day for 91 days, plus 5% VAT. Find the total.
---
Units: $1450 \times 0.284 = 411.80$. Standing: $91 \times 0.62 = 56.42$. Subtotal $468.22$; $\times 1.05 = \pounds 491.63$.
:::

::: q
A scale has labelled marks at 200 g and 300 g with 4 spaces between them. The pointer is 3 spaces past 200 g. What is the reading?
---
Each space is $25$ g: $275$ g.
:::

::: q
A car uses 6.4 litres of fuel per 100 km. Fuel costs £1.52 per litre. Find the fuel cost of a 350 km journey.
---
$3.5 \times 6.4 = 22.4$ litres; $22.4 \times 1.52 = \pounds 34.05$.
:::

::: watch
- **Time is not decimal.** 2 h 36 min is $2.6$ h, not $2.36$. Convert minutes by dividing by 60.
- Area units: square the factor (m$^2$ → cm$^2$ is $\times 10\,000$). Volume: cube it ($\times 1\,000\,000$).
- Currency: check which way the rate goes by writing the rate with units, e.g. "1.27 dollars per £1": multiply to leave the £1 side, divide to return to it.
- "How many boxes/coaches are needed" → round **up**. "How many can be bought" → round **down**. Write the reason.
- Keep money in pounds with two decimal places. £23.765 is not an answer.
- Units on the answer line: a bare "80" for a speed is incomplete.
:::
