## What it is

Two ways quadratics arrive in disguise.

**From context.** A rectangle whose area is known and whose length is 3 more than its width; a number problem ("the product of two consecutive integers is 132"); a projectile's height; a journey where speed is unknown and $\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$. You define a letter, translate the sentence into an equation, and find that it has an $x^2$ in it. Then it is 2.7a — except that one of the two roots usually makes no sense and must be **rejected with a reason**.

**One linear and one quadratic simultaneous equation.** A line and a curve (often a circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, or a parabola) meet where both equations hold. Substitute the linear into the quadratic; the quadratic you get has up to two solutions, each giving a point. The number of solutions tells you whether the line cuts the curve twice, touches it once (a **tangent**), or misses.

::: terms
- **Forming a quadratic**: writing an equation from a context (area, speed, consecutive numbers) that simplifies to ax² + bx + c = 0.
- **Rejecting a solution**: discarding a root that is impossible in the context, such as a negative length.
- **Linear and quadratic simultaneous equations**: substitute the linear equation into the quadratic, solve the quadratic, then find the matching values.
- **Intersection of a line and a curve**: the solutions of their simultaneous equations — up to two points for a line and a quadratic or circle.
:::

::: method
1. **Define the variable** in a sentence with units. Choose the thing the question asks for, or the simplest quantity, and express everything else in terms of it.
2. **Write the equation** from the fact given (area $=$, product $=$, total time $=$, Pythagoras). Expand and rearrange to $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
3. **Solve** by factorising or the formula. **Reject** any root that is negative when the quantity is a length, or otherwise impossible — and say why in words.
4. **Answer the question**: sometimes it wants the length (use $x$), sometimes the area (use both dimensions).
5. **Linear + quadratic:** make $y$ (or $x$) the subject of the linear equation; substitute into the quadratic; expand carefully (a bracket squared has a middle term); solve; find the **other** coordinate for each root; present as pairs.
6. **Tangent questions:** the resulting quadratic has a repeated root, so its discriminant is zero.
:::

::: example Area of a rectangle
A rectangle is $x$ cm wide and $(x + 5)$ cm long. Its area is 84 cm$^2$. Find its perimeter.

$x(x + 5) = 84 \Rightarrow x^2 + 5x - 84 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 12)(x - 7) = 0$.

$x = -12$ is rejected (a width cannot be negative), so $x = 7$: sides 7 and 12, perimeter $38$ cm.
:::

::: example Consecutive numbers
The product of two consecutive positive integers is 156. Find them.

$n(n + 1) = 156 \Rightarrow n^2 + n - 156 = 0 \Rightarrow (n + 13)(n - 12) = 0 \Rightarrow n = 12$ (reject $-13$, not positive). The integers are 12 and 13.
:::

::: example A journey with unknown speed
A cyclist rides 30 km at $v$ km/h, then returns at $(v - 5)$ km/h. The whole trip takes 5 hours. Find $v$.

$\dfrac{30}{v} + \dfrac{30}{v - 5} = 5$. Multiply by $v(v - 5)$: $30(v - 5) + 30v = 5v(v - 5)$.

$60v - 150 = 5v^2 - 25v \Rightarrow 5v^2 - 85v + 150 = 0 \Rightarrow v^2 - 17v + 30 = 0 \Rightarrow (v - 15)(v - 2) = 0$.

$v = 2$ would make the return speed negative, so $v = 15$ km/h.
:::

::: example Right-angled triangle
The sides of a right-angled triangle are $x$, $x + 7$ and $x + 8$ cm. Find $x$.

$x^2 + (x + 7)^2 = (x + 8)^2 \Rightarrow x^2 + x^2 + 14x + 49 = x^2 + 16x + 64 \Rightarrow x^2 - 2x - 15 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 5)(x + 3) = 0$.

$x = 5$ (reject $-3$).
:::

::: example Line and circle
Solve $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ and $y = x + 1$.

$x^2 + (x + 1)^2 = 25 \Rightarrow 2x^2 + 2x + 1 = 25 \Rightarrow x^2 + x - 12 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 4)(x - 3) = 0$.

$x = 3, y = 4$; $\ x = -4, y = -3$. The line cuts the circle at $(3, 4)$ and $(-4, -3)$.
:::

::: example Line and parabola
Solve $y = x^2 - 3x + 1$ and $y = 2x - 5$.

$x^2 - 3x + 1 = 2x - 5 \Rightarrow x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$.

$(2, -1)$ and $(3, 1)$.
:::

::: q
A rectangle has length $(x + 3)$ cm and width $x$ cm, and area 40 cm$^2$. Find $x$.
---
$x^2 + 3x - 40 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 8)(x - 5) = 0$: $x = 5$ (reject $-8$).
:::

::: q
The product of two consecutive even numbers is 224. Find them.
---
$n(n + 2) = 224 \Rightarrow n^2 + 2n - 224 = 0 \Rightarrow (n + 16)(n - 14) = 0$: $14$ and $16$ (or $-16$ and $-14$ if negatives allowed).
:::

::: q
A square of side $x$ has a 2 cm border added all round, making a square of area 144 cm$^2$. Find $x$.
---
$(x + 4)^2 = 144 \Rightarrow x + 4 = 12$ (positive root): $x = 8$ cm.
:::

::: q
The sides of a right-angled triangle are $x$, $2x - 1$ and $2x + 1$. Find $x$.
---
$x^2 + (2x - 1)^2 = (2x + 1)^2 \Rightarrow x^2 - 4x + 1 = 4x + 1 \Rightarrow x^2 - 8x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 8$ (reject 0).
:::

::: q
A ball's height is $h = 20t - 5t^2$ metres after $t$ seconds. When is it 15 m high?
---
$5t^2 - 20t + 15 = 0 \Rightarrow t^2 - 4t + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow t = 1$ or $t = 3$ seconds (on the way up and on the way down).
:::

::: q
A train travels 120 km at $v$ km/h. If it went 20 km/h faster it would take 30 minutes less. Find $v$.
---
$\dfrac{120}{v} - \dfrac{120}{v + 20} = \dfrac12 \Rightarrow 240(v + 20) - 240v = v(v + 20) \Rightarrow v^2 + 20v - 4800 = 0 \Rightarrow (v + 80)(v - 60) = 0$: $v = 60$ km/h.
:::

::: q
Solve $x^2 + y^2 = 20$ and $y = x - 2$.
---
$x^2 + (x - 2)^2 = 20 \Rightarrow 2x^2 - 4x - 16 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 - 2x - 8 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 4)(x + 2) = 0$: $(4, 2)$ and $(-2, -4)$.
:::

::: q
Solve $y = x^2 + 2x - 3$ and $y = x + 3$.
---
$x^2 + x - 6 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 3)(x - 2) = 0$: $(-3, 0)$ and $(2, 5)$.
:::

::: q
Solve $x^2 + y^2 = 10$ and $x + 2y = 5$.
---
$x = 5 - 2y$: $(5 - 2y)^2 + y^2 = 10 \Rightarrow 5y^2 - 20y + 15 = 0 \Rightarrow y^2 - 4y + 3 = 0$: $y = 1, x = 3$; $y = 3, x = -1$.
:::

::: q
Show that the line $y = 2x + 5$ is a tangent to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 5$.
---
$x^2 + (2x + 5)^2 = 5 \Rightarrow 5x^2 + 20x + 20 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 + 4x + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 2)^2 = 0$: one repeated solution, $x = -2$, so the line touches the circle at $(-2, 1)$ only. ∎
:::

::: q
$xy = 12$ and $x + y = 7$. Find $x$ and $y$.
---
$y = 7 - x$: $x(7 - x) = 12 \Rightarrow x^2 - 7x + 12 = 0$: $(3, 4)$ or $(4, 3)$.
:::

::: watch
- **Reject the impossible root and say why**: "$x = -12$ is not possible because a length cannot be negative." The rejection is a mark.
- Expanding $(x + 1)^2$ inside a substitution: it is $x^2 + 2x + 1$. Dropping the $2x$ is the most common error in line-and-circle questions.
- Substitute the **linear** into the **quadratic**, never the other way.
- Give solutions as **pairs** $(x, y)$, and match each $y$ to its own $x$ — do not mix them.
- Clear fractions by multiplying **every** term by the product of the denominators.
- Answer the question actually asked — the perimeter, the two numbers, the speed — not just "$x = 7$".
:::

::: test
A rectangle has length $(x + 5)$ cm and width $x$ cm. Its area is 84 cm$^2$. Find $x$ and the dimensions of the rectangle. (3)
---
$x(x + 5) = 84$, so $x^2 + 5x - 84 = 0$ (1); $(x + 12)(x - 7) = 0$ (1); $x = 7$ (reject $-12$), so 12 cm by 7 cm (1).
:::

::: test
Solve the simultaneous equations $y = x + 1$ and $y = x^2 - 5x + 6$. (3)
---
$x^2 - 5x + 6 = x + 1$, so $x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$ (1); $(x - 1)(x - 5) = 0$ (1); $x = 1, y = 2$ or $x = 5, y = 6$ (1).
:::

::: test
Solve the simultaneous equations $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ and $y = x + 1$. (4)
---
$x^2 + (x + 1)^2 = 25$, so $2x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0$ (1); $x^2 + x - 12 = 0$, $(x + 4)(x - 3) = 0$ (2); $x = 3, y = 4$ or $x = -4, y = -3$ (1).
:::

::: test
A ball is thrown upwards. Its height after $t$ seconds is $h = 20t - 5t^2$ metres. Find the times at which it is 15 m high, and explain why there are two answers. (3)
---
$20t - 5t^2 = 15$, so $t^2 - 4t + 3 = 0$ (1); $(t - 1)(t - 3) = 0$, $t = 1$ or $t = 3$ (1). It passes 15 m on the way up and again on the way down (1).
:::

::: test
Two consecutive positive odd numbers have a product of 195. Find them. (3)
---
$n(n + 2) = 195$, so $n^2 + 2n - 195 = 0$ (1); $(n + 15)(n - 13) = 0$ (1); $n = 13$, so the numbers are 13 and 15 (1).
:::

::: test
A right-angled triangle has shorter sides $x$ cm and $(x + 7)$ cm and hypotenuse $(x + 8)$ cm. Find $x$. (3)
---
$x^2 + (x + 7)^2 = (x + 8)^2$: $2x^2 + 14x + 49 = x^2 + 16x + 64$ (1); $x^2 - 2x - 15 = 0$, $(x - 5)(x + 3) = 0$ (1); $x = 5$ (1).
:::

::: test
The line $y = 2x + k$ is a tangent to the curve $y = x^2 + 4x + 5$. Find $k$. (3)
---
$x^2 + 4x + 5 = 2x + k$, so $x^2 + 2x + (5 - k) = 0$ (1). Tangent means one solution: discriminant $4 - 4(5 - k) = 0$ (1); $k = 4$ (1).
:::

::: test
Show that the line $y = x - 3$ does not meet the curve $y = x^2 - 2x + 1$. (2)
---
$x^2 - 2x + 1 = x - 3$ gives $x^2 - 3x + 4 = 0$ (1); discriminant $9 - 16 = -7 < 0$, so there are no real solutions and no intersection (1).
:::
