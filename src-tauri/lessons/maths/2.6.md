## What it is

Two equations, two unknowns, one pair of values that satisfies both — that is **simultaneous equations**. Each linear equation is a straight line; the solution is the point where the lines cross. If the lines are parallel there is no solution; if they are the same line, every point works.

Two algebraic methods:

- **Elimination:** scale the equations so one unknown has matching coefficients, then add or subtract to remove it.
- **Substitution:** rearrange one equation to $y = \dots$ (or $x = \dots$), substitute into the other.

Elimination is fastest when both equations are in the form $ax + by = c$; substitution when one already has a letter on its own. Word problems — ticket prices, two kinds of coin, costs of pens and pencils — turn into a pair of equations and then it is the same again.

::: terms
- **Simultaneous equations**: two equations with two unknowns that are both true at the same time.
- **Elimination method**: make the coefficients of one unknown the same, then add or subtract the equations to remove it.
- **Substitution method**: rearrange one equation for one unknown and substitute it into the other.
- **Graphical solution**: the coordinates of the point where the two lines cross.
:::

::: method
1. **Line the equations up** as $ax + by = c$, number them (1) and (2).
2. **Match a coefficient:** multiply (1) and/or (2) so that the coefficients of one letter are equal in size. Multiply *every* term.
3. **Same signs → subtract; different signs → add.** ("SSS": same sign subtract.) The letter disappears; solve for the other.
4. **Substitute back** into either original equation for the second letter.
5. **Check** in the equation you did *not* substitute into.
6. **Substitution method:** from the simpler equation write $y = \dots$; replace $y$ in the other; solve; back-substitute.
7. **From words:** define two letters with units, write two facts as two equations, then solve.
8. **Graphically:** draw both lines; read the crossing point. Only accurate to the grid — use algebra when exact values are needed.
:::

::: example Elimination, one multiply
Solve $3x + 2y = 16$ and $x - 2y = -4$.

$y$ coefficients are already $+2$ and $-2$: different signs, so **add**: $4x = 12 \Rightarrow x = 3$.

(1): $9 + 2y = 16 \Rightarrow y = 3.5$. Check (2): $3 - 7 = -4$ ✓.
:::

::: example Elimination, both multiplied
Solve $4x + 3y = 18$ and $3x + 5y = 19$.

Match the $x$ coefficients: $(1) \times 3$ gives $12x + 9y = 54$; $(2) \times 4$ gives $12x + 20y = 76$.

Same signs, **subtract** (second from first): $-11y = -22 \Rightarrow y = 2$.

Then $4x + 6 = 18 \Rightarrow x = 3$. Check in (2): $9 + 10 = 19$ ✓.
:::

::: example Substitution
Solve $y = 2x - 5$ and $3x + 4y = 13$.

$3x + 4(2x - 5) = 13 \Rightarrow 11x - 20 = 13 \Rightarrow x = 3$, $y = 1$.
:::

::: example A word problem
Three coffees and two teas cost £11.40; two coffees and five teas cost £13.10. Find the cost of each.

$3c + 2t = 11.4$ (1), $2c + 5t = 13.1$ (2).

$(1) \times 2$: $6c + 4t = 22.8$; $(2) \times 3$: $6c + 15t = 39.3$. Subtract: $11t = 16.5 \Rightarrow t = 1.5$.

$3c + 3 = 11.4 \Rightarrow c = 2.8$. Coffee £2.80, tea £1.50.
:::

::: example Reading the crossing point
The lines $y = 2x - 1$ and $x + y = 5$ are drawn on a grid. Where do they meet?

Algebraically: $x + 2x - 1 = 5 \Rightarrow x = 2$, $y = 3$. They meet at $(2, 3)$ — the graphical reading should agree.
:::

::: q
Solve $x + y = 9$ and $x - y = 3$.
---
Add: $2x = 12$, $x = 6$, $y = 3$.
:::

::: q
Solve $2x + 3y = 12$ and $2x - y = 4$.
---
Subtract: $4y = 8$, $y = 2$, $x = 3$.
:::

::: q
Solve $5x + 2y = 1$ and $3x - 4y = 11$.
---
$(1) \times 2$: $10x + 4y = 2$; add to (2): $13x = 13$, $x = 1$, $y = -2$.
:::

::: q
Solve $3x + 4y = 7$ and $2x + 5y = 7$.
---
$\times 2$: $6x + 8y = 14$; $\times 3$: $6x + 15y = 21$. Subtract: $7y = 7$, $y = 1$, $x = 1$.
:::

::: q
Solve $y = 3x + 1$ and $2x + y = 11$.
---
$2x + 3x + 1 = 11 \Rightarrow x = 2$, $y = 7$.
:::

::: q
Solve $4x - 3y = 18$ and $x = 2y + 7$.
---
$4(2y + 7) - 3y = 18 \Rightarrow 5y = -10 \Rightarrow y = -2$, $x = 3$.
:::

::: q
Two adult and three child tickets cost £43; three adult and one child ticket cost £36.50. Find the price of each.
---
$2a + 3c = 43$, $3a + c = 36.5 \Rightarrow c = 36.5 - 3a$. $2a + 109.5 - 9a = 43 \Rightarrow a = 9.5$, $c = 8$. Adult £9.50, child £8.
:::

::: q
The sum of two numbers is 27 and their difference is 9. Find the numbers.
---
$x + y = 27$, $x - y = 9$: $x = 18$, $y = 9$.
:::

::: q
A rectangle's perimeter is 34 cm. Its length is 5 cm more than its width. Find its area.
---
$2l + 2w = 34$, $l = w + 5$: $2w + 10 + 2w = 34 \Rightarrow w = 6$, $l = 11$. Area $66$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
Find the point of intersection of the lines $2x + y = 8$ and $y = x - 1$.
---
$2x + x - 1 = 8 \Rightarrow x = 3$, $y = 2$: $(3, 2)$.
:::

::: q
Explain why $2x + 4y = 6$ and $x + 2y = 5$ have no solution.
---
The second is $2x + 4y = 10$ when doubled — same left side, different right side: parallel lines that never meet.
:::

::: watch
- **Multiply every term**, including the right-hand side, when scaling an equation.
- Same signs **subtract**, different signs **add** — and when subtracting, subtract the *whole* equation: $54 - 76 = -22$.
- Substitute back into an **original** equation, and check in the **other** one.
- Answers are a **pair**: $x = 3$ *and* $y = 3.5$. One value is half an answer.
- Word problems: define the letters with units ("$c$ = cost of a coffee in £") and keep money in pounds throughout.
- Fractional answers happen. Do not "fix" them — check them.
:::

::: test
Solve $3x + 2y = 16$ and $x - 2y = 0$. (2)
---
Add: $4x = 16$, $x = 4$ (1); $y = 2$ (1).
:::

::: test
Solve $2x + 3y = 13$ and $5x - 2y = 4$. (3)
---
$\times 2$ and $\times 3$: $4x + 6y = 26$, $15x - 6y = 12$ (1). Add: $19x = 38$, $x = 2$ (1); $y = 3$ (1).
:::

::: test
Solve $4x - y = 11$ and $3x + 2y = 11$. (3)
---
$y = 4x - 11$ (1); $3x + 2(4x - 11) = 11$, $11x = 33$ (1); $x = 3$, $y = 1$ (1).
:::

::: test
Three coffees and two teas cost £11.20. One coffee and four teas cost £9.40. Find the cost of a coffee and of a tea. (3)
---
$3c + 2t = 11.2$ and $c + 4t = 9.4$ (1). $c = 9.4 - 4t$: $28.2 - 12t + 2t = 11.2$, $10t = 17$ (1). Tea £1.70, coffee £2.60 (1).
:::

::: test
Solve $y = 2x - 1$ and $3x + y = 14$. (2)
---
$3x + 2x - 1 = 14$, $x = 3$ (1); $y = 5$ (1).
:::

::: test
A straight line passes through $(2, 7)$ and $(5, 16)$. By forming and solving two simultaneous equations in $m$ and $c$, find its equation. (3)
---
$7 = 2m + c$ and $16 = 5m + c$ (1). Subtract: $9 = 3m$, $m = 3$ (1); $c = 1$, so $y = 3x + 1$ (1).
:::

::: test
Solve $\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{y}{3} = 4$ and $x - y = 3$. (3)
---
$\times 6$: $3x + 2y = 24$ (1). $x = y + 3$: $3y + 9 + 2y = 24$, $y = 3$ (1); $x = 6$ (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why the equations $2x + 4y = 10$ and $x + 2y = 3$ have no solution. (1)
---
Doubling the second gives $2x + 4y = 6$, which contradicts $2x + 4y = 10$: the lines are parallel and never meet.
:::

::: test
Solve $y = 4 - x$ and $3x - 2y = 13$. (2)
---
$3x - 2(4 - x) = 13$, $5x = 21$ (1); $x = 4.2$, $y = -0.2$ (1).
:::

::: test
Solve $5x + 3y = 21$ and $2x + 3y = 12$. (3)
---
Subtract: $3x = 9$ (1); $x = 3$ (1); $y = 2$ (1).
:::

::: test
Solve $3x - 4y = 2$ and $5x + 6y = 35$. (4)
---
$9x - 12y = 6$ and $10x + 12y = 70$ (1). Add: $19x = 76$ (1), $x = 4$ (1). $12 - 4y = 2$, $y = 2.5$ (1).
:::

::: test
The lines $y = 3x - 5$ and $2y + x = 11$ meet at $P$. Find the coordinates of $P$. (3)
---
$2(3x - 5) + x = 11$ (1); $7x = 21$, $x = 3$ (1); $P = (3, 4)$ (1).
:::

::: test
Explain, without solving, why $y = 2x + 1$ and $y = 2x - 3$ have no solution. (1)
---
Both lines have gradient 2, so they are parallel and never meet.
:::

::: test
3 adult tickets and 2 child tickets cost £41. 2 adult tickets and 5 child tickets cost £53. Find the cost of each ticket. (4)
---
$3a + 2c = 41$ and $2a + 5c = 53$ (1). $6a + 4c = 82$ and $6a + 15c = 159$ (1). $11c = 77$, $c = 7$ (1). $a = 9$: adult £9, child £7 (1).
:::

::: test
A jar holds only 20p and 50p coins. There are 34 coins with a total value of £11.30. How many of each coin are in the jar? (5)
---
$x + y = 34$ (1) and $20x + 50y = 1130$ (1). $20x + 20y = 680$, so $30y = 450$ (1). $y = 15$ fifty-pence coins (1) and $x = 19$ twenty-pence coins (1).
:::
