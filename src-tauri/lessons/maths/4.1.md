## What it is

Angle facts are the building blocks of every geometry question. Each has a name, and in the exam the **name is the reason** you write next to your working.

- Angles on a **straight line** add to $180°$; angles at a **point** add to $360°$.
- **Vertically opposite** angles (where two lines cross) are equal.
- With **parallel lines** cut by a transversal: **alternate** angles (Z) are equal; **corresponding** angles (F) are equal; **co-interior** angles (C, between the parallels on the same side) add to $180°$.
- Angles in a **triangle** add to $180°$; the **exterior angle** of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
- **Isosceles** triangle: two equal sides and the two base angles equal. **Equilateral**: all sides equal, all angles $60°$. **Right-angled**: one angle $90°$.

Angle types by size: **acute** $< 90°$, **right** $= 90°$, **obtuse** between $90°$ and $180°$, **reflex** between $180°$ and $360°$.

::: method
1. **Mark what you know** on the diagram: equal sides (isosceles tick marks), parallel arrows, right angles.
2. **Work from the angle you have to the one you want**, one fact at a time, writing each new angle on the diagram as you find it.
3. **Write the reason** for every step: "angles on a straight line add to 180°", "alternate angles are equal", "base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal". Use the standard wording.
4. **Isosceles triangles:** decide which two angles are the base angles — the ones opposite the equal sides. Then $180° - $ apex, halved.
5. **Algebra in angles:** write the sum equation (triangle $= 180$, straight line $= 180$, point $= 360$), solve for $x$, then find the angle asked for.
6. **Parallel lines:** find the Z, F or C. If none is obvious, extend a line or draw a third parallel line through the corner.
:::

::: example Parallel lines
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is $112°$. Find the alternate angle, the corresponding angle and the co-interior angle to it.

Alternate $= 112°$ (alternate angles are equal). Corresponding $= 112°$ (corresponding angles are equal). Co-interior $= 180° - 112° = 68°$ (co-interior angles add to $180°$).
:::

::: example Isosceles triangle
Triangle $ABC$ has $AB = AC$ and angle $A = 44°$. Find angle $B$.

$AB = AC$, so angles $B$ and $C$ are equal (base angles of an isosceles triangle). $B = \dfrac{180° - 44°}{2} = 68°$.
:::

::: example Exterior angle
In triangle $PQR$, angle $P = 37°$ and angle $Q = 71°$. Side $QR$ is extended to $S$. Find angle $PRS$.

Exterior angle $= $ sum of the interior opposite angles: $37° + 71° = 108°$.
:::

::: example Chaining facts
$AB$ is parallel to $CD$. A transversal meets $AB$ at $E$ and $CD$ at $F$, and angle $AEF = 58°$ ($A$ and $C$ on the same side). Find angle $EFC$ and angle $EFD$, with reasons.

Angle $EFD$ is alternate to angle $AEF$ (a Z shape), so $EFD = 58°$ — alternate angles are equal.

Angle $EFC = 180° - 58° = 122°$ — angles on a straight line add to $180°$. (Equally, $AEF$ and $EFC$ are co-interior and add to $180°$.)
:::

::: example Algebra
The angles of a triangle are $2x$, $3x + 10$ and $4x - 19$ degrees. Find the size of the largest angle.

$2x + 3x + 10 + 4x - 19 = 180 \Rightarrow 9x = 189 \Rightarrow x = 21$. Angles $42°, 73°, 65°$: largest $73°$.
:::

::: q
Two angles on a straight line are $x$ and $x + 40$. Find $x$.
---
$2x + 40 = 180 \Rightarrow x = 70$.
:::

::: q
Three angles at a point are $95°$, $140°$ and $y$. Find $y$.
---
$y = 360 - 235 = 125°$.
:::

::: q
Two lines cross; one of the angles formed is $37°$. Write down the other three.
---
$37°$ (vertically opposite), $143°$ and $143°$ (straight line).
:::

::: q
In an isosceles triangle the apex angle is $26°$. Find each base angle.
---
$(180 - 26) \div 2 = 77°$.
:::

::: q
In an isosceles triangle one base angle is $54°$. Find the apex angle.
---
$180 - 108 = 72°$.
:::

::: q
A triangle has angles $x$, $2x$ and $3x$. Find them and state what kind of triangle it is.
---
$6x = 180$, $x = 30$: $30°, 60°, 90°$ — right-angled.
:::

::: q
Parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One co-interior angle is $3x$ and the other is $2x + 15$. Find $x$.
---
$5x + 15 = 180 \Rightarrow x = 33$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, angle $A = 52°$ and angle $B = 61°$. $BC$ is extended to $D$. Find angle $ACD$, giving a reason.
---
$52 + 61 = 113°$ (exterior angle equals the sum of the interior opposite angles).
:::

::: q
Triangle $ABC$ is isosceles with $AB = BC$. Angle $A = 3x - 5$ and angle $C = 2x + 15$. Find angle $B$.
---
Base angles equal: $3x - 5 = 2x + 15 \Rightarrow x = 20$; base angles $55°$; $B = 70°$.
:::

::: q
$AB$ is parallel to $CD$. $E$ lies between the lines; angle $BAE = 40°$ and angle $DCE = 35°$. Find angle $AEC$ (the angle at $E$ between $EA$ and $EC$).
---
Draw a line through $E$ parallel to $AB$: the two parts of angle $AEC$ are alternate to $40°$ and $35°$. $AEC = 75°$.
:::

::: q
Classify these angles: $89°$, $90°$, $91°$, $181°$, $359°$.
---
Acute, right, obtuse, reflex, reflex.
:::

::: watch
- **Write the reason** in the standard words. "Because it's a Z angle" is not accepted; "alternate angles are equal" is.
- Co-interior angles **add to 180°** — they are not equal. The C shape is the one that catches people.
- Isosceles: the equal angles are **opposite the equal sides**. Check which side is the odd one out before halving.
- Do not assume angles are equal because they look it. Only use marked facts.
- Show the sum equation in algebra questions, and answer with the **angle**, not just $x$.
- Angles at a point: $360°$; on a line: $180°$. Do not mix them up when a diagram has both.
:::

::: test
Two angles on a straight line are $(2x + 15)^\circ$ and $(3x - 10)^\circ$. Find $x$. (2)
---
$5x + 5 = 180$ (1); $x = 35$ (1).
:::

::: test
The angles of a triangle are $x$, $2x$ and $3x$. Find the three angles. (2)
---
$6x = 180$, $x = 30$ (1); $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One of the angles formed is $118^\circ$. Write down the size of the angle alternate to it, and of the angle co-interior with it. (2)
---
Alternate: $118^\circ$ (1); co-interior: $62^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
An exterior angle of a triangle is $125^\circ$. One of the interior opposite angles is $48^\circ$. Find the other. (1)
---
$125 - 48 = 77^\circ$.
:::

::: test
An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of $40^\circ$. Find each base angle. (1)
---
$(180 - 40) \div 2 = 70^\circ$.
:::

::: test
Three angles around a point are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$. Find the three angles. (2)
---
9 parts $= 360^\circ$, one part $= 40^\circ$ (1); $80^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $160^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = AC$, angle $BAC = 4x$ and angle $ABC = x + 30$. Find $x$ and the three angles. (3)
---
Base angles equal: $4x + 2(x + 30) = 180$ (1); $6x + 60 = 180$, $x = 20$ (1); angles $80^\circ$, $50^\circ$, $50^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
Two straight lines cross. One pair of vertically opposite angles is $(5x - 20)^\circ$ and $(3x + 30)^\circ$. Find $x$ and the size of the other pair of angles. (2)
---
$5x - 20 = 3x + 30$, $x = 25$, so the pair is $105^\circ$ (1); the other pair is $180 - 105 = 75^\circ$ (1).
:::
