## What it is

**Constructions** are accurate drawings made with a ruler and a pair of compasses — no protractor except where the question allows one. The compass arcs are the evidence; leave them on the page.

The four standard constructions:

- **Perpendicular bisector** of a line segment: the line through its midpoint at right angles. Every point on it is the same distance from both ends.
- **Angle bisector**: the line that splits an angle in half. Every point on it is the same distance from both arms.
- **Perpendicular from a point to a line** (and at a point on a line).
- **Triangles** from three sides (SSS) with compasses, or from sides and angles (SAS, ASA) with a protractor.

A **locus** is the path of all points obeying a rule. The rules that come up: a fixed distance from a point (a circle); a fixed distance from a line (two parallel lines, with semicircular ends if it is a segment); equidistant from two points (the perpendicular bisector); equidistant from two lines (the angle bisector). Regions — "closer to A than to B and less than 3 cm from C" — are shaded overlaps of loci.

**Scale drawings** turn a real situation into a drawing at a stated scale ($1 : 50\,000$, or 1 cm to 2 m), so you can measure lengths and bearings off the page.

::: terms
- **Scale drawing**: an accurate drawing with every length reduced (or enlarged) by the same scale.
- **Scale**: the ratio of a length on the drawing to the real length, e.g. 1 : 50 000.
- **Perpendicular bisector**: the line that cuts a segment in half at right angles; every point on it is equidistant from the two ends.
- **Angle bisector**: the line that cuts an angle in half; every point on it is equidistant from the two arms.
- **Locus**: the set of points that satisfy a rule, such as being a fixed distance from a point (a circle).
- **Construction**: an accurate drawing using only a ruler and compasses.
:::

::: method
1. **Perpendicular bisector of AB:** compasses open to more than half $AB$; arcs from A above and below; same radius from B; join the two crossings.
2. **Angle bisector:** arc from the vertex cutting both arms; from each cut, arcs of equal radius crossing inside the angle; join vertex to crossing.
3. **Perpendicular from a point P to a line:** arc from P cutting the line twice; from those two points, equal arcs crossing on the far side; join P to the crossing.
4. **Triangle SSS:** draw the base; arc from each end with radius the other two sides; join the crossing to both ends.
5. **Locus questions:** translate each rule into its shape (circle / parallel lines / perpendicular bisector / angle bisector); draw each accurately; shade the region where all conditions hold; use dashed lines for "less than" boundaries if the question distinguishes.
6. **Scale drawings:** convert the real length with the scale, draw, measure what is asked, convert back. Measure bearings with a protractor from a north line drawn at the point.
7. **Accuracy:** lines to the nearest mm, angles to the nearest degree; leave every arc visible.
:::

::: example Equidistant from two points
Points A and B are 6 cm apart. Draw the locus of points equidistant from A and B, and shade the region of points closer to A than to B.

Construct the perpendicular bisector of AB (arcs from A and B, radius 4 cm say, crossing above and below; join). The locus is that line. Shade the side containing A.
:::

::: example Fixed distance from a line segment
Draw the locus of points exactly 2 cm from a line segment PQ of length 5 cm.

Two lines parallel to PQ, 2 cm either side, joined by semicircles of radius 2 cm centred at P and at Q — a stadium shape.
:::

::: example A combined region
A garden is a rectangle ABCD, 8 m by 5 m (scale 1 cm to 1 m). A tree is to be planted at least 3 m from corner A and closer to side AB than to side AD. Show the region.

Circle centre A radius 3 cm: the region is *outside* it. Angle bisector of angle A (the diagonal direction from A): points closer to AB are below the bisector. Shade the part of the rectangle outside the circle and on the AB side of the bisector.
:::

::: example Constructing a triangle
Construct triangle ABC with $AB = 7$ cm, $AC = 5$ cm and $BC = 6$ cm.

Draw AB 7 cm. Compasses 5 cm from A, arc above; compasses 6 cm from B, arc crossing it. The crossing is C; join AC and BC. Leave the arcs.
:::

::: example A scale drawing with a bearing
A ship sails 12 km on a bearing of $050°$ then 8 km on a bearing of $140°$. Using a scale of 1 cm to 2 km, find by drawing the distance and bearing of the ship from its start.

Draw north at the start; measure $50°$ clockwise; draw 6 cm. At the end draw another north line; measure $140°$; draw 4 cm. Join start to finish: about 7.2 cm, so about 14.4 km; the bearing measures about $084°$. (By calculation: the turn is $90°$, so distance $= \sqrt{144 + 64} = 14.4$ km and the bearing is $050° + \tan^{-1}\frac{8}{12} = 083.7°$.)
:::

::: q
Describe the locus of points 3 cm from a fixed point O.
---
A circle, centre O, radius 3 cm.
:::

::: q
Describe the locus of points equidistant from two intersecting lines.
---
The two angle bisectors of the angles between the lines (perpendicular to each other).
:::

::: q
Describe how to construct the perpendicular bisector of a 7 cm line AB, and state the compass radius you would use.
---
Any radius more than 3.5 cm (say 5 cm); arcs from A above and below the line; same radius from B; join the two crossing points.
:::

::: q
Describe how to construct an angle of $60°$ with compasses only, and hence $30°$.
---
Draw a line; arc from one end cutting it; same radius from the cut, arc crossing the first; join — an equilateral triangle corner gives $60°$. Bisect it for $30°$.
:::

::: q
Points P and Q are 5 cm apart. Shade the region of points less than 3 cm from P and less than 4 cm from Q.
---
The overlap (lens) of the circle centre P radius 3 cm and the circle centre Q radius 4 cm.
:::

::: q
A goat is tethered by a 4 m rope to the corner of a 6 m by 3 m shed. Describe the region it can graze (scale drawing at 1 cm to 1 m).
---
A three-quarter circle of radius 4 m at the corner, plus, round the short (3 m) side, a quarter circle of radius 1 m at the next corner where the rope bends.
:::

::: q
On a map with scale $1 : 25\,000$, two churches are 5.6 cm apart. Find the real distance in km.
---
$5.6 \times 25\,000 = 140\,000$ cm $= 1.4$ km.
:::

::: q
A room 7.5 m by 4.2 m is drawn at a scale of 1 cm to 50 cm. Find the dimensions of the drawing.
---
$15$ cm by $8.4$ cm.
:::

::: q
Town B is 20 km from A on a bearing of $120°$. Using 1 cm to 5 km, describe how to plot B and how to find the bearing of A from B.
---
Draw a north line at A, measure $120°$ clockwise, mark B 4 cm along. Bearing of A from B: $120 + 180 = 300°$ (check with a protractor from a north line at B).
:::

::: q
Construct the perpendicular from a point P to a line, and explain why the construction works.
---
Arc from P cutting the line at X and Y; equal arcs from X and Y crossing at Z; PZ is the perpendicular. It works because P and Z are both equidistant from X and Y, so PZ is the perpendicular bisector of XY.
:::

::: watch
- **Leave the construction arcs.** No arcs, no marks — the examiner needs to see the method.
- Perpendicular bisector: the compass radius must be **more than half** the segment, and the **same** from both ends.
- "Closer to A than to B" is a **region** — shade the correct side of the perpendicular bisector, not the line itself.
- A locus "3 cm from a line segment" has **rounded ends**; from a full line, it is just two parallel lines.
- Scale drawings: convert with the scale **both ways** and state units; measure to the nearest mm.
- Bearings on drawings: draw the north line first, then measure clockwise with the protractor — check that $120°$ lands south of east.
:::

::: test
Describe how to construct the perpendicular bisector of a line segment $AB$ using a ruler and compasses. (2)
---
With the compasses set to more than half of $AB$, draw arcs from $A$ and from $B$ that cross above and below the line (1); join the two crossing points with a ruler (1).
:::

::: test
Describe the locus of points that are 3 cm from a fixed point $P$. (1)
---
A circle of radius 3 cm with centre $P$.
:::

::: test
Describe the locus of points equidistant from two fixed points $A$ and $B$. (1)
---
The perpendicular bisector of $AB$.
:::

::: test
Describe the locus of points equidistant from two intersecting straight lines. (1)
---
The bisectors of the angles between the lines.
:::

::: test
Describe the locus of points that are exactly 2 cm from a line segment $AB$. (2)
---
Two straight lines parallel to $AB$, 2 cm either side (1), joined by semicircles of radius 2 cm centred at $A$ and at $B$ — a running-track shape (1).
:::

::: test
A map has scale $1 : 50\,000$. How long on the map is a footpath of length 3.5 km? (2)
---
$3.5$ km $= 350\,000$ cm (1); $350\,000 \div 50\,000 = 7$ cm (1).
:::

::: test
A rectangular garden 12 m by 8 m is drawn to a scale of 1 cm to 2 m. A tree is to be planted more than 3 m from the house wall (one long side) and no more than 4 m from one corner of that wall. Give the dimensions of the drawing and describe the region where the tree can go. (3)
---
Drawing 6 cm by 4 cm (1). The tree lies on the far side of a line 1.5 cm from the wall (1) and inside (or on) a circle of radius 2 cm centred at the corner (1).
:::

::: test
Describe how to construct an angle of $60^\circ$ at a point $A$ on a line, using compasses. (2)
---
Draw an arc from $A$ cutting the line at $B$; with the same radius, draw an arc from $B$ to cut the first arc at $C$ (1). Join $AC$: angle $CAB = 60^\circ$ because $ABC$ is equilateral (1).
:::
