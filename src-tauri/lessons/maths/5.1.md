## What it is

A **vector** has size and direction — a journey, not a position. On a grid it is a **column vector** $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$: 3 right, 2 down. In a diagram it is an arrow, written $\overrightarrow{AB}$ (from $A$ to $B$) or a bold letter $\mathbf{a}$ (underlined when handwritten: $\underline{a}$).

- **Add** vectors component by component — or nose to tail on a diagram: $\overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{AC}$.
- **Subtract**: $\mathbf a - \mathbf b$ is $\mathbf a + (-\mathbf b)$; $-\mathbf b$ is $\mathbf b$ reversed. $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB}$.
- **Multiply by a scalar**: $3\mathbf a$ is three times as long in the same direction; $-\frac12\mathbf a$ is half as long, reversed. $k\mathbf a$ is always **parallel** to $\mathbf a$.
- **Magnitude** (length): $\left|\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\right| = \sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{13}$ — Pythagoras.
- **Resultant**: the single vector equal to several added together.

The geometry questions are all one idea: **express a vector as a route** through the diagram in terms of $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$, then compare. If $\overrightarrow{PQ} = k\overrightarrow{RS}$, the lines are parallel; if $\overrightarrow{AB} = k\overrightarrow{AC}$, the points $A$, $B$, $C$ are on a straight line (**collinear**).

::: terms
- **Vector**: a quantity with magnitude (size) and direction.
- **Column vector**: a vector written with its horizontal component on top and its vertical component below.
- **Magnitude (modulus)**: the length of a vector: √(x² + y²).
- **Scalar multiple**: multiplying a vector by a number changes its length but not its direction (a negative reverses it).
- **Resultant**: the single vector equal to two or more vectors combined, found by adding them.
- **Parallel vectors**: one is a scalar multiple of the other.
- **Collinear points**: points on the same straight line; shown by parallel vectors that share a point.
:::

::: method
1. **Column vectors:** add, subtract and scale each component. $2\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 11 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. **Magnitude:** square root of the sum of squares of the components. Leave as a surd if asked for exact.
3. **Routes:** to write $\overrightarrow{XY}$, walk from $X$ to $Y$ along arrows you know, adding forwards and subtracting backwards. Write the route down first: "$\overrightarrow{XY} = \overrightarrow{XO} + \overrightarrow{OY}$".
4. **Fractions of lines:** if $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $\overrightarrow{AM} = \frac12\overrightarrow{AB}$. If $P$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $2 : 1$, $\overrightarrow{AP} = \frac23\overrightarrow{AB}$.
5. **Simplify** by collecting $\mathbf a$s and $\mathbf b$s, then **factorise** to show a multiple: $\frac23\mathbf a + \frac43\mathbf b = \frac23(\mathbf a + 2\mathbf b)$.
6. **Parallel:** show one vector is a scalar multiple of the other and say so. **Collinear:** show $\overrightarrow{AB} = k\overrightarrow{BC}$ (parallel *and* sharing $B$) and say both facts.
7. **Ratio of lengths:** the scalar $k$ *is* the ratio: $\overrightarrow{AB} = \frac23\overrightarrow{AC}$ means $AB : AC = 2 : 3$.
:::

::: example Column vectors
$\mathbf p = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf q = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $\mathbf p + \mathbf q$, $3\mathbf p - 2\mathbf q$ and $|\mathbf p|$.

$\mathbf p + \mathbf q = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$. $\quad 3\mathbf p - 2\mathbf q = \begin{pmatrix} 6 + 8 \\ 15 - 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 14 \\ 13 \end{pmatrix}$. $\quad |\mathbf p| = \sqrt{4 + 25} = \sqrt{29}$.
:::

::: example A route in a parallelogram
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf c$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $\overrightarrow{OB}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$.

$\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf a + \mathbf c$. $\quad \overrightarrow{AC} = -\mathbf a + \mathbf c = \mathbf c - \mathbf a$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf c$, so $\overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf a + \frac12\mathbf c$.
:::

::: example Proving parallel
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. $P$ and $Q$ are the midpoints of $OA$ and $OB$. Show that $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$ and half its length.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a$. $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{PO} + \overrightarrow{OQ} = -\frac12\mathbf a + \frac12\mathbf b = \frac12(\mathbf b - \mathbf a) = \frac12\overrightarrow{AB}$.

A scalar multiple, so parallel; the scalar $\frac12$ makes $PQ$ half the length of $AB$. ∎
:::

::: example Proving collinear
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. The point $C$ has $\overrightarrow{OC} = 3\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a$. Show that $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a$.

$\overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{OC} - \overrightarrow{OB} = 3\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a - \mathbf b = 2\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a = 2(\mathbf b - \mathbf a) = 2\overrightarrow{AB}$.

$\overrightarrow{BC}$ is a multiple of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, so the two are parallel; they share the point $B$; hence $A$, $B$, $C$ lie on one straight line, with $AB : BC = 1 : 2$. ∎
:::

::: example A ratio on a line
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$, and $P$ is on $AB$ with $AP : PB = 1 : 3$. Find $\overrightarrow{OP}$.

$\overrightarrow{AP} = \frac14\overrightarrow{AB} = \frac14(\mathbf b - \mathbf a)$.

$\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf a + \frac14(\mathbf b - \mathbf a) = \frac34\mathbf a + \frac14\mathbf b$.
:::

::: example Resultant and magnitude
A boat's velocity is $\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and the current is $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ (m/s). Find the resultant velocity and its magnitude.

Resultant $= \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$; magnitude $\sqrt{36 + 4} = \sqrt{40} = 2\sqrt{10} = 6.32$ m/s.
:::

::: q
$\mathbf a = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf b = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $\mathbf a + \mathbf b$, $\mathbf a - \mathbf b$ and $2\mathbf a + 3\mathbf b$.
---
$\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$; $\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}$; $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}$.
:::

::: q
Find the magnitude of $\begin{pmatrix} -6 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}$ and of $\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$ (exact).
---
$10$; $5\sqrt2$.
:::

::: q
$A = (1, 2)$ and $B = (7, -1)$. Write $\overrightarrow{AB}$ as a column vector and find $|\overrightarrow{AB}|$.
---
$\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$; $\sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt5$.
:::

::: q
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$.
---
$\mathbf b - \mathbf a$; $\frac12(\mathbf a + \mathbf b)$.
:::

::: q
$OABC$ is a parallelogram, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf c$. $N$ is the midpoint of $BC$. Find $\overrightarrow{ON}$ and $\overrightarrow{AN}$.
---
$\overrightarrow{ON} = \mathbf c + \frac12\mathbf a$; $\overrightarrow{AN} = \mathbf c - \frac12\mathbf a$.
:::

::: q
$\overrightarrow{OA} = 2\mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = 2\mathbf b$, $P$ is the midpoint of $OA$, $Q$ is the midpoint of $OB$. Prove $PQ \parallel AB$.
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a$; $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a = \frac12\overrightarrow{AB}$: a multiple, so parallel. ∎
:::

::: q
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \frac32\mathbf b - \frac12\mathbf a$. Show that $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear.
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf b - \mathbf a$; $\overrightarrow{AC} = \frac32\mathbf b - \frac12\mathbf a - \mathbf a = \frac32\mathbf b - \frac32\mathbf a = \frac32(\mathbf b - \mathbf a) = \frac32\overrightarrow{AB}$. Parallel and both through $A$: collinear. ∎
:::

::: q
$P$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $2 : 3$, with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. Find $\overrightarrow{OP}$.
---
$\mathbf a + \frac25(\mathbf b - \mathbf a) = \frac35\mathbf a + \frac25\mathbf b$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf a$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf b$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $X$ is the point on $OM$ with $OX : XM = 2 : 1$. Find $\overrightarrow{OX}$ and show that $\overrightarrow{AX} = \frac13(\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a)$.
---
$\overrightarrow{OM} = \frac12(\mathbf a + \mathbf b)$; $\overrightarrow{OX} = \frac13(\mathbf a + \mathbf b)$. $\overrightarrow{AX} = \frac13\mathbf a + \frac13\mathbf b - \mathbf a = \frac13\mathbf b - \frac23\mathbf a = \frac13(\mathbf b - 2\mathbf a)$. ∎
:::

::: q
Two forces $\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ N and $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$ N act on an object. Find the resultant and its magnitude.
---
$\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$; $5$ N.
:::

::: q
Find the value of $k$ such that $\begin{pmatrix} k \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$ is parallel to $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$.
---
$\begin{pmatrix} k \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} = 2\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \Rightarrow k = 4$.
:::

::: watch
- **$\overrightarrow{AB}$ is from $A$ to $B$**: $\mathbf b - \mathbf a$ (end minus start). Reversing it flips every sign.
- Going backwards along an arrow is a **minus**. Write the route before the algebra.
- A ratio $m : n$ along $AB$ gives the fraction $\dfrac{m}{m + n}$ — $1 : 3$ is one **quarter** of the way.
- To prove parallel you must **factorise** to show the scalar multiple and then **say** "so they are parallel". For collinear, also say they share a point.
- Magnitude needs the **square root**: $\left|\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\right| = 5$, not $25$.
- Column vectors are not coordinates: $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ is a movement, not a point.
:::

::: test
$\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $3\mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b}$. (1)
---
$\begin{pmatrix} 6 - 2 \\ 9 + 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 11 \end{pmatrix}$.
:::

::: test
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. (2)
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ (1); $\overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf{a} + \dfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \dfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$ (1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$, and $P$ lies on $AB$ with $AP : PB = 1 : 2$. Find $\overrightarrow{OP}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. (2)
---
$\overrightarrow{AP} = \dfrac{1}{3}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$ (1); $\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{a} + \dfrac{1}{3}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \dfrac{2}{3}\mathbf{a} + \dfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{b}$ (1).
:::

::: test
Show that $\mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ are parallel. (1)
---
$\mathbf{q} = 1.5\mathbf{p}$ — one is a scalar multiple of the other.
:::

::: test
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = 2\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = 2\mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $OA$ and $N$ is the midpoint of $OB$. Prove that $MN$ is parallel to $AB$ and half its length. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{MN} = -\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ (1); $\overrightarrow{AB} = -2\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b} = 2(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$ (1). $\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\overrightarrow{MN}$, so they are parallel and $MN$ is half of $AB$ (1).
:::

::: test
$A$ is the point $(1, 2)$ and $B$ is $(4, 8)$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$, its magnitude in simplest surd form, and the coordinates of the point $C$ such that $\overrightarrow{AC} = 3\overrightarrow{AB}$. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$ (1); $|\overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}$ (1); $C = (1 + 9, 2 + 18) = (10, 20)$ (1).
:::
