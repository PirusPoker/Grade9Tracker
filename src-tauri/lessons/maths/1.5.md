## What it is

A **set** is a collection of things — numbers, people, letters — written in curly brackets: $A = \{2, 4, 6, 8\}$. The things in it are its **elements**; $4 \in A$ means "4 is an element of $A$", and $n(A) = 4$ is how many elements it has.

The notation you must read and write:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mathscr{E}$ | the **universal set** — everything under consideration |
| $\varnothing$ or $\{\}$ | the **empty set** |
| $A'$ | the **complement** of $A$: everything in $\mathscr E$ that is *not* in $A$ |
| $A \cap B$ | **intersection**: in *both* $A$ and $B$ |
| $A \cup B$ | **union**: in $A$ *or* $B$ or both |
| $A \subset B$ | $A$ is a **subset** of $B$: every element of $A$ is in $B$ |
| $\{x : x > 3\}$ | the set of $x$ **such that** $x > 3$ (set-builder notation) |

A **Venn diagram** draws the universal set as a rectangle and each set as a circle inside it; overlapping circles share their intersection. Numbers written in the regions are usually *counts* of elements, not the elements themselves — read the question.

::: method
1. **Read the definition into a list** when the set is given algebraically: $\{x : x \text{ is an integer}, 1 \le x < 6\} = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$.
2. **Draw the Venn diagram from the inside out.** Fill the intersection first ($A \cap B$), then the parts of $A$ and $B$ that are *only* in one, then whatever is in neither (inside the rectangle, outside both circles). Every element of $\mathscr E$ must appear exactly once.
3. **Counting with two sets:** $n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$. If you are told $n(A)$, $n(B)$ and the total, and asked for the overlap, let the overlap be $x$ and fill the regions as $n(A) - x$, $x$, $n(B) - x$, then add up.
4. **Three sets:** start with the centre (in all three), then the pairwise overlaps *minus the centre*, then the singles, then the outside.
5. **Shading:** $A \cap B'$ is "in $A$ and not in $B$" — the crescent of $A$ outside $B$. $(A \cup B)'$ is everything outside both circles. Build it up one symbol at a time.
6. **Probability from a Venn diagram:** $P(\text{in region}) = \dfrac{\text{count in region}}{n(\mathscr E)}$.
:::

::: example Listing from set notation
$\mathscr E = \{x : x \text{ is an integer}, 1 \le x \le 12\}$, $A = \{\text{factors of } 12\}$, $B = \{\text{even numbers}\}$. List $A \cap B$, $A \cup B$ and $A'$.

$A = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\}$, $B = \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$.

$A \cap B = \{2, 4, 6, 12\}$. $\quad A \cup B = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$. $\quad A' = \{5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11\}$.
:::

::: example Finding an overlap
In a class of 30, 18 study French, 14 study Spanish and 5 study neither. How many study both?

Let $x$ study both. French only: $18 - x$; Spanish only: $14 - x$.

$(18 - x) + x + (14 - x) + 5 = 30 \Rightarrow 37 - x = 30 \Rightarrow x = 7$.
:::

::: example Three sets
$\mathscr E$ is 40 students. 20 play football ($F$), 18 hockey ($H$), 15 tennis ($T$). 8 play football and hockey, 6 hockey and tennis, 7 football and tennis, and 3 play all three. How many play none?

Centre: 3. $F \cap H$ only: $8 - 3 = 5$; $H \cap T$ only: $6 - 3 = 3$; $F \cap T$ only: $7 - 3 = 4$.

Football only: $20 - 5 - 4 - 3 = 8$; hockey only: $18 - 5 - 3 - 3 = 7$; tennis only: $15 - 4 - 3 - 3 = 5$.

Total in at least one: $8 + 7 + 5 + 5 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 35$. None: $40 - 35 = 5$.
:::

::: example Describing a shaded region
On a Venn diagram of two sets, the region inside $A$ but outside $B$ is shaded. Write it in set notation, and also write the notation for everything outside both circles.

Inside $A$, outside $B$: $A \cap B'$.

Outside both: $(A \cup B)'$, which is the same as $A' \cap B'$.
:::

::: example Subsets and the empty set
$P = \{\text{prime numbers less than } 10\}$ and $Q = \{\text{odd numbers less than } 10\}$. Is $P \subset Q$? What is $P \cap \{\text{even numbers}\}$?

$P = \{2, 3, 5, 7\}$, $Q = \{1, 3, 5, 7, 9\}$. $2 \in P$ but $2 \notin Q$, so $P$ is **not** a subset of $Q$.

$P \cap \{\text{even}\} = \{2\}$ — not empty, because 2 is the even prime.
:::

::: q
$\mathscr E = \{1, 2, 3, \dots, 10\}$, $A = \{\text{multiples of } 3\}$, $B = \{x : x \le 5\}$. List $A$, $B$, $A \cap B$, $A \cup B$ and $B'$.
---
$A = \{3, 6, 9\}$; $B = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$; $A \cap B = \{3\}$; $A \cup B = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9\}$; $B' = \{6, 7, 8, 9, 10\}$.
:::

::: q
Write $\{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13\}$ using set-builder notation.
---
$\{x : x \text{ is a prime number}, x < 15\}$ (or $x \le 13$).
:::

::: q
$n(\mathscr E) = 50$, $n(A) = 28$, $n(B) = 19$ and $n(A \cap B) = 9$. Find $n(A \cup B)$ and $n((A \cup B)')$.
---
$28 + 19 - 9 = 38$; $50 - 38 = 12$.
:::

::: q
Of 60 people, 35 like tea, 27 like coffee and 10 like neither. How many like both?
---
$35 + 27 - x + 10 = 60 \Rightarrow x = 12$.
:::

::: q
Draw a Venn diagram for $\mathscr E = \{1, \dots, 12\}$, $A = \{\text{factors of } 8\}$, $B = \{\text{factors of } 12\}$, and write down $n(A \cap B)$.
---
$A = \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$, $B = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12\}$. Overlap $\{1, 2, 4\}$; $A$ only $\{8\}$; $B$ only $\{3, 6, 12\}$; outside $\{5, 7, 9, 10, 11\}$. $n(A \cap B) = 3$.
:::

::: q
Shade $A' \cap B$ on a two-set Venn diagram and describe it in words.
---
The part of $B$ that is outside $A$ — "in $B$ but not in $A$" (the crescent of $B$ away from the overlap).
:::

::: q
In a group of 100, 45 own a bike ($B$), 52 own a scooter ($S$), 30 own a skateboard ($K$); 15 own bike and scooter, 12 scooter and skateboard, 10 bike and skateboard, and 5 own all three. Find how many own exactly one of the three.
---
Centre 5. $B \cap S$ only 10; $S \cap K$ only 7; $B \cap K$ only 5. Bike only $45 - 10 - 5 - 5 = 25$; scooter only $52 - 10 - 7 - 5 = 30$; skateboard only $30 - 5 - 7 - 5 = 13$. Exactly one: $25 + 30 + 13 = 68$.
:::

::: q
Using the previous question, how many own none of the three?
---
At least one: $68 + 10 + 7 + 5 + 5 = 95$. None: $5$.
:::

::: q
$\mathscr E = \{\text{students in a year}\}$, $G = \{\text{students who wear glasses}\}$, $L = \{\text{left-handed students}\}$. Write in set notation: (a) students who wear glasses and are right-handed, (b) students who are neither.
---
(a) $G \cap L'$. (b) $G' \cap L'$, i.e. $(G \cup L)'$.
:::

::: q
A number is picked at random from $\mathscr E = \{1, \dots, 20\}$. $A = \{\text{multiples of } 4\}$, $B = \{\text{multiples of } 6\}$. Find $P(A \cap B)$ and $P(A \cup B)$.
---
$A = \{4, 8, 12, 16, 20\}$, $B = \{6, 12, 18\}$, $A \cap B = \{12\}$. $P(A \cap B) = \dfrac{1}{20}$; $n(A \cup B) = 5 + 3 - 1 = 7$, $P = \dfrac{7}{20}$.
:::

::: watch
- **Elements or counts?** A Venn diagram of *counts* has numbers in regions that add to $n(\mathscr E)$; a diagram of *elements* lists each element once. Don't mix them.
- Fill the **intersection first**. Writing $n(A)$ in the $A$-only region is the most common error — the "only" region is $n(A) - n(A \cap B)$.
- $\cap$ is "and" (the narrow one, like an "n" for "aNd"); $\cup$ is "or" (the cup that holds everything).
- The complement is relative to $\mathscr E$: $A'$ includes the outside region too, not just the rest of $B$.
- Don't forget the people/elements in **neither** set — they sit inside the rectangle, outside the circles, and they count towards the total.
- $(A \cup B)' = A' \cap B'$: "not in either" is the same as "not in $A$ and not in $B$".
:::

Describe in words the set $\{x : x \text{ is an integer}, -2 \le x < 3\}$ and list its members. (2)
---
The integers from $-2$ up to, but not including, 3 (1): $\{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$ (1).

::: test
$A \subset B$. Write down $A \cap B$ and $A \cup B$ in terms of $A$ and $B$. (2)
---
$A \cap B = A$ (1); $A \cup B = B$ (1).
:::

::: test
100 people were asked which drinks they like. 60 like tea, 45 like coffee and 30 like juice; 20 like tea and coffee, 15 like tea and juice, 10 like coffee and juice; 5 like all three. How many like none of the three? (3)
---
Fill the Venn diagram from the middle: all three = 5, so the two-only regions are 15, 10, 5 (1). Tea only $= 60 - 15 - 10 - 5 = 30$, coffee only $= 45 - 15 - 5 - 5 = 20$, juice only $= 30 - 10 - 5 - 5 = 10$ (1). Total inside $= 30 + 20 + 10 + 15 + 10 + 5 + 5 = 95$, so 5 like none (1).
:::

::: test
$n(A) = 12$, $n(B) = 9$ and $n(A \cup B) = 15$. Find $n(A \cap B)$ and $n(A \cap B')$. (2)
---
$n(A \cap B) = 12 + 9 - 15 = 6$ (1); $n(A \cap B') = 12 - 6 = 6$ (1).
:::
