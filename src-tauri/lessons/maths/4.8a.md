## What it is

In a right-angled triangle the longest side, opposite the right angle, is the **hypotenuse**. **Pythagoras' theorem** links the three sides:

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \qquad (c \text{ the hypotenuse})$$

Use it when you know two sides and want the third — no angles involved.

**Trigonometry** links sides to angles. Label the sides relative to the angle $\theta$ you are using: **opposite** (across from it), **adjacent** (next to it, not the hypotenuse), **hypotenuse**. Then

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$$

— SOH CAH TOA. Use it when the question involves an angle: to find a side from an angle and another side, or an angle from two sides (with $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$, $\tan^{-1}$).

Exact values worth knowing: $\sin 30° = \cos 60° = \frac12$; $\sin 45° = \cos 45° = \frac{\sqrt2}{2}$; $\sin 60° = \cos 30° = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}$; $\tan 45° = 1$; $\tan 30° = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}$; $\tan 60° = \sqrt3$.

**Angle of elevation**: looking up from the horizontal. **Angle of depression**: looking down from the horizontal. The two are equal (alternate angles) when measured between the same two points.

::: method
1. **Right angle? Two sides, no angle?** → Pythagoras. Hypotenuse: add the squares and root. Shorter side: subtract the squares and root.
2. **Right angle? An angle involved?** → Trig. Label O, A, H from the angle. Pick the ratio that contains the two sides in play. Cover up what you want in the triangle diagram to see whether to multiply or divide.
3. **Finding a side:** e.g. $\sin 35° = \dfrac{x}{12} \Rightarrow x = 12\sin 35°$; or $\cos 35° = \dfrac{12}{x} \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{12}{\cos 35°}$ (unknown on the bottom — divide).
4. **Finding an angle:** ratio $= \dfrac{\text{side}}{\text{side}}$, then the inverse function: $\theta = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{5}{8}\right)$. Degrees mode!
5. **No right angle in sight?** Draw one in — drop a perpendicular, or use the isosceles triangle's line of symmetry — and split the problem into right-angled triangles.
6. **Word problems:** ladders, shadows, ramps, kites on strings, angles of elevation. Sketch, mark the right angle, label the sides.
7. **Accuracy:** keep the calculator value until the end; round the final answer to 3 s.f. (or 1 d.p. for angles).
:::

::: example Pythagoras both ways
(a) A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 7 cm and 24 cm. Find the hypotenuse. (b) A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 13 cm and one side 5 cm. Find the other side.

(a) $\sqrt{49 + 576} = \sqrt{625} = 25$ cm.

(b) $\sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144} = 12$ cm.
:::

::: example Finding a side with trig
A ladder 6 m long leans against a wall at $68°$ to the ground. How far up the wall does it reach?

The height is opposite the $68°$; the ladder is the hypotenuse: $\sin 68° = \dfrac{h}{6} \Rightarrow h = 6\sin 68° = 5.56$ m.
:::

::: example Unknown on the bottom
In a right-angled triangle, the angle is $41°$ and the side adjacent to it is 9 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

$\cos 41° = \dfrac{9}{H} \Rightarrow H = \dfrac{9}{\cos 41°} = 11.9$ cm.
:::

::: example Finding an angle
A ramp rises 1.2 m over a horizontal distance of 7.5 m. Find the angle of the ramp.

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{1.2}{7.5} = 0.16 \Rightarrow \theta = \tan^{-1}(0.16) = 9.1°$.
:::

::: example Angle of elevation
From a point 40 m from the base of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is $32°$. Find the height of the tower.

$\tan 32° = \dfrac{h}{40} \Rightarrow h = 40\tan 32° = 25.0$ m.
:::

::: example An isosceles triangle
An isosceles triangle has two sides of 10 cm and a base of 12 cm. Find its height and the angle at the apex.

Split down the middle: right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 10 and base 6. Height $= \sqrt{100 - 36} = 8$ cm.

Half the apex angle: $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{6}{10} \Rightarrow \alpha = 36.87°$; apex $= 73.7°$.
:::

::: example Exact values
A right-angled triangle has an angle of $30°$ and hypotenuse 8. Find the other two sides exactly.

Opposite $30°$: $8\sin 30° = 4$. Adjacent: $8\cos 30° = 8 \times \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = 4\sqrt3$.
:::

The side opposite a $63°$ angle is 11 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

::: q
A right-angled triangle has sides 7 cm and 11 cm around the right angle. Find the smaller angle.
---
$\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{7}{11}\right) = 32.5°$.
:::

::: q
A kite string is 25 m long and makes $58°$ with the ground. How high is the kite?
---
$25\sin 58° = 21.2$ m.
:::

::: q
From the top of a 50 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is $18°$. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff?
---
$\tan 18° = \dfrac{50}{d} \Rightarrow d = \dfrac{50}{\tan 18°} = 154$ m.
:::

::: q
A rectangle is 12 cm by 5 cm. Find the angle between a diagonal and the longer side.
---
$\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{5}{12}\right) = 22.6°$.
:::

::: q
Is a triangle with sides 8, 15, 17 right-angled? Show how you decide.
---
$8^2 + 15^2 = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17^2$. Yes (converse of Pythagoras).
:::

::: q
Find the exact area of an equilateral triangle of side 6 cm.
---
Height $= 6\sin 60° = 3\sqrt3$; area $= \dfrac12 \times 6 \times 3\sqrt3 = 9\sqrt3$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: watch
- **Hypotenuse is the longest side, opposite the right angle** — the labels O and A depend on which angle you use; H never changes.
- Pythagoras for a **shorter** side: subtract. $13^2 - 5^2$, not $13^2 + 5^2$.
- Unknown on the **bottom** of the ratio → divide: $H = \dfrac{9}{\cos 41°}$, not $9\cos 41°$.
- Finding an angle: use the **inverse** ($\sin^{-1}$ etc.), and check the calculator is in **degrees**.
- Do not round early. $\tan^{-1}(0.16)$ from the exact fraction, not from $\tan\theta \approx 0.2$.
- Angle of depression is measured **from the horizontal at the top**, not from the vertical.
:::
