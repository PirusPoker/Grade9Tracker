## What it is

A **transformation** moves or changes a shape on a grid. The original is the **object**, the result the **image**. Four kinds:

| Transformation | What you must state to describe it fully |
|---|---|
| **Translation** | the column vector $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ (right/left, up/down) |
| **Reflection** | the equation of the mirror line ($x = 2$, $y = -1$, $y = x$, $y = -x$) |
| **Rotation** | the angle, the direction (clockwise / anticlockwise), and the centre |
| **Enlargement** | the scale factor and the centre |

Translation, reflection and rotation keep the shape **congruent** (same size and shape). Enlargement keeps the angles but scales the lengths by the factor; a negative scale factor puts the image on the other side of the centre, upside down; a fractional factor makes it smaller.

"Describe fully" means every item in the right-hand column — a rotation with no centre, or an enlargement with no centre, loses the marks.

::: terms
- **Translation**: a slide, described by a column vector.
- **Reflection**: a flip in a mirror line, described by the equation of the line.
- **Rotation**: a turn, described by the centre, the angle and the direction (anticlockwise is positive).
- **Enlargement**: a change of size, described by the centre and the scale factor.
- **Negative scale factor**: an enlargement in which the image is on the opposite side of the centre and upside down.
- **Congruence under transformation**: translations, reflections and rotations keep a shape congruent; enlargements keep angles but change lengths.
:::

::: method
1. **Translation:** move every vertex by the vector; a vector $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ is 3 left, 2 up.
2. **Reflection:** for each vertex, measure its perpendicular distance to the mirror line and mark the same distance on the other side. For $y = x$, swap the coordinates: $(a, b) \to (b, a)$. For $y = -x$: $(a, b) \to (-b, -a)$.
3. **Rotation:** use tracing paper — trace the shape and the centre, pin the pencil on the centre, turn. Or by coordinates about the origin: $90°$ anticlockwise sends $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$; $180°$ sends $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$; $90°$ clockwise sends $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$.
4. **Enlargement:** from the centre, the vector to each image vertex is $k$ times the vector to the object vertex. Draw rays from the centre through the vertices; measure $k$ times along. Negative $k$: go the other way through the centre.
5. **Finding the centre of an enlargement:** join corresponding vertices and extend; the lines meet at the centre. Scale factor $= \dfrac{\text{image length}}{\text{object length}}$.
6. **Finding the centre of a rotation:** perpendicular bisectors of the lines joining corresponding points meet at the centre — or use tracing paper and try likely points.
7. **Finding a mirror line:** it is the perpendicular bisector of the line joining any point to its image.
8. **Combined transformations:** do them in the order given; describe the *single* transformation with the same overall effect if asked.
:::

::: example Describing a translation and a reflection
Triangle $A$ has vertices $(1, 1), (3, 1), (1, 4)$. Triangle $B$ has vertices $(-2, -3), (0, -3), (-2, 0)$. Triangle $C$ has vertices $(1, -1), (3, -1), (1, -4)$. Describe the transformations $A \to B$ and $A \to C$.

$A \to B$: every vertex moves 3 left and 4 down: translation by $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$.

$A \to C$: $y$-coordinates change sign, $x$ unchanged: reflection in the $x$-axis ($y = 0$).
:::

::: example A rotation by coordinates
Rotate the point $(3, 1)$ through $90°$ anticlockwise about the origin, then $180°$ about the origin.

$90°$ anticlockwise: $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$: $(3, 1) \to (-1, 3)$. $180°$: $(3, 1) \to (-3, -1)$.
:::

::: example Enlargement with a fractional and a negative factor
Enlarge the triangle with vertices $(2, 2), (6, 2), (2, 6)$ by scale factor $\frac12$ about $(0, 0)$, and by scale factor $-1$ about $(0, 0)$.

$\times\frac12$: $(1, 1), (3, 1), (1, 3)$. $\times(-1)$: $(-2, -2), (-6, -2), (-2, -6)$ — the same size, on the opposite side, upside down.
:::

::: example Finding the centre and scale factor
Triangle $P$ has vertices $(1, 2), (3, 2), (1, 3)$; triangle $Q$ has vertices $(1, 2), (7, 2), (1, 5)$. Describe fully the transformation $P \to Q$.

Lengths triple ($2 \to 6$, $1 \to 3$): scale factor 3. The vertex $(1, 2)$ does not move, so it is the centre. Enlargement, scale factor 3, centre $(1, 2)$.
:::

::: example Describing a rotation
Shape $A$ has vertices $(1, 1), (4, 1), (4, 2)$. Shape $B$ has vertices $(-1, 1), (-1, 4), (-2, 4)$. Describe the transformation $A \to B$.

$(4, 1) \to (-1, 4)$ and $(1, 1) \to (-1, 1)$: the rule $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$, i.e. rotation of $90°$ anticlockwise about the origin $(0, 0)$.
:::

::: example Combining two transformations
Triangle $T$ is reflected in the $x$-axis and the image is then reflected in the $y$-axis. Describe the single transformation equivalent to the two together.

Reflecting in $y = 0$ sends $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$; then in $x = 0$: $(x, -y) \to (-x, -y)$. That is a rotation of $180°$ about the origin.
:::

::: q
Translate the point $(-2, 5)$ by $\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -7 \end{pmatrix}$.
---
$(2, -2)$.
:::

::: q
Reflect $(3, -2)$ in (a) the $y$-axis, (b) the line $y = x$, (c) the line $y = 1$.
---
(a) $(-3, -2)$; (b) $(-2, 3)$; (c) $(3, 4)$.
:::

::: q
Rotate $(2, 5)$ through $90°$ clockwise about the origin.
---
$(x, y) \to (y, -x)$: $(5, -2)$.
:::

::: q
Rotate $(4, 1)$ through $180°$ about the point $(1, 1)$.
---
The image is as far beyond the centre as the object is before it: $(-2, 1)$.
:::

::: q
Enlarge the point $(5, 3)$ by scale factor 2 about the centre $(1, 1)$.
---
Vector from centre $\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$, doubled: $(9, 5)$.
:::

::: q
Enlarge $(6, 4)$ by scale factor $-\frac12$ about the origin.
---
$(-3, -2)$.
:::

::: q
Shape $A$ has vertices $(2, 1), (5, 1), (5, 3)$. Shape $B$ has vertices $(2, -1), (5, -1), (5, -3)$. Describe fully $A \to B$.
---
Reflection in the $x$-axis.
:::

::: q
Shape $A$ has vertices $(1, 1), (3, 1), (3, 2)$. Shape $B$ has vertices $(3, 3), (7, 3), (7, 5)$. Describe fully $A \to B$.
---
Lengths double; joining $(1,1) \to (3,3)$ and $(3,1) \to (7,3)$ and extending back, the lines meet at $(-1, -1)$. Enlargement, scale factor 2, centre $(-1, -1)$.
:::

::: q
A shape is rotated $90°$ clockwise about the origin and then reflected in the $x$-axis. What single transformation is this equivalent to?
---
$(x, y) \to (y, -x) \to (y, x)$: reflection in the line $y = x$.
:::

::: q
Point $P(2, 3)$ is mapped to $P'(2, -5)$ by a reflection. Find the equation of the mirror line.
---
Midpoint of $PP'$ is $(2, -1)$ and $PP'$ is vertical, so the mirror is $y = -1$.
:::

::: q
Which transformations produce a congruent image? Which produce a similar but not congruent image?
---
Translation, reflection, rotation: congruent. Enlargement (scale factor $\ne \pm 1$): similar only.
:::

::: watch
- **"Describe fully" means every detail**: rotation → angle *and* direction *and* centre; enlargement → scale factor *and* centre; reflection → the line's **equation**; translation → a **vector**.
- Reflection in $y = x$ **swaps** coordinates; in the $x$-axis it changes the sign of $y$ (not $x$).
- Rotation direction: anticlockwise is the usual positive; state it explicitly.
- Enlargement measures from the **centre**, not from the shape's own corner. Draw the rays.
- A negative scale factor flips the image through the centre — and the image is still an enlargement, not a rotation.
- Use tracing paper for rotations in the exam; it is allowed and it is faster than guessing.
:::

::: test
Triangle $A$ has vertices $(1, 1)$, $(3, 1)$ and $(1, 4)$. Triangle $B$ has vertices $(-1, 1)$, $(-3, 1)$ and $(-1, 4)$. Describe fully the single transformation that maps $A$ onto $B$. (1)
---
Reflection in the $y$-axis (the line $x = 0$).
:::

::: test
The point $(2, 5)$ is rotated $90^\circ$ clockwise about the origin. Find its image. (1)
---
$(5, -2)$.
:::

::: test
Translate the point $(3, -2)$ by the vector $\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$. (1)
---
$(-1, 4)$.
:::

::: test
The point $(4, 2)$ is enlarged with scale factor 3, first with centre $(0, 0)$ and then, separately, with centre $(1, 1)$. Find the two images. (2)
---
Centre origin: $(12, 6)$ (1). Centre $(1, 1)$: the vector from the centre $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is tripled, giving $(1 + 9, 1 + 3) = (10, 4)$ (1).
:::

::: test
An enlargement with scale factor $-2$ and centre the origin is applied to the point $(1, 3)$. Find its image. (1)
---
$(-2, -6)$ — on the opposite side of the centre, twice as far.
:::

::: test
Describe the transformation that maps every point $(x, y)$ onto $(y, x)$. (1)
---
Reflection in the line $y = x$.
:::

::: test
Reflect the point $(3, 1)$ in the line $y = -x$. (1)
---
$(-1, -3)$ (swap the coordinates and change both signs).
:::

::: test
A shape is reflected in the $x$-axis and the image is then reflected in the $y$-axis. Describe the single transformation equivalent to the two reflections. (2)
---
$(x, y) \to (x, -y) \to (-x, -y)$ (1): a rotation of $180^\circ$ about the origin (1).
:::
