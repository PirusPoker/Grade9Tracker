## What it is

When two things happen one after the other, a **tree diagram** lays out every route. Each branch carries a probability; the branches from any one point add to 1.

- **Multiply along a route** to get the probability of that whole route ("and").
- **Add the routes** that give the outcome you want ("or").

Two events are **independent** if one does not affect the other (two coins, a dice and a spinner, picking *with* replacement): $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$, and the second set of branches has the same numbers as the first.

Events are **dependent** when the first changes the second — picking sweets from a bag **without replacement**: after one red is taken there are fewer reds and fewer sweets in total, so the second branches have different fractions. That is **conditional probability**: $P(B \text{ given } A)$, written $P(B \mid A)$, and it also appears in two-way tables and Venn diagrams ("given that the student is a girl, what is the probability she walks?" — restrict to the girls' row).

::: terms
- **Independent events**: events where the outcome of one does not affect the other; P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).
- **Tree diagram**: a diagram showing the outcomes and probabilities of successive events; multiply along branches, add between them.
- **Without replacement**: the item is not put back, so the probabilities on the second branches change.
- **Conditional probability**: the probability of an event given that another has happened, P(A | B).
- **"At least one"**: often found as 1 − P(none).
:::

::: method
1. **Draw the tree**: first event's branches, then from each end the second event's branches. Label every branch with its probability and check each set adds to 1.
2. **With replacement / independent:** second-stage probabilities are the same as the first.
3. **Without replacement:** reduce the numerator of the item taken **and** the denominator by 1 for the second pick. Write the fractions unsimplified so the pattern is visible ($\frac{5}{12}$ then $\frac{4}{11}$).
4. **Multiply along, add across.** For "both the same": add the RR and BB routes. For "at least one": either add every route that qualifies, or use $1 - P(\text{none})$.
5. **"Exactly one of each" / "different colours":** there are usually two routes (RB and BR) — count both.
6. **Conditional from a table or Venn diagram:** the "given" part becomes the new total. $P(\text{walks} \mid \text{girl}) = \dfrac{\text{girls who walk}}{\text{all girls}}$.
7. **Three picks:** the tree has three levels; the same rules. Keep fractions unsimplified until the final answer.
:::

::: example Independent events
A bag has 3 red and 5 blue counters. One is picked, replaced, and another picked. Find $P(\text{both red})$ and $P(\text{one of each})$.

$P(RR) = \dfrac38 \times \dfrac38 = \dfrac{9}{64}$.

$P(\text{one of each}) = P(RB) + P(BR) = \dfrac38 \times \dfrac58 + \dfrac58 \times \dfrac38 = \dfrac{30}{64} = \dfrac{15}{32}$.
:::

::: example Without replacement
Same bag, but the first counter is **not** replaced. Find $P(\text{both red})$ and $P(\text{both the same colour})$.

$P(RR) = \dfrac38 \times \dfrac27 = \dfrac{6}{56} = \dfrac{3}{28}$.

$P(BB) = \dfrac58 \times \dfrac47 = \dfrac{20}{56}$. Same colour: $\dfrac{6 + 20}{56} = \dfrac{26}{56} = \dfrac{13}{28}$.
:::

::: example At least one
The probability that Amy passes her driving test is $0.7$ and that Ben passes his is $0.6$, independently. Find $P(\text{at least one passes})$.

$1 - P(\text{both fail}) = 1 - 0.3 \times 0.4 = 1 - 0.12 = 0.88$.
:::

::: example Conditional from a two-way table
Of 120 students, 70 are girls; 45 girls and 30 boys walk to school. A student is picked at random. Find $P(\text{walks} \mid \text{girl})$ and $P(\text{girl} \mid \text{walks})$.

$P(\text{walks} \mid \text{girl}) = \dfrac{45}{70} = \dfrac{9}{14}$.

$P(\text{girl} \mid \text{walks}) = \dfrac{45}{75} = \dfrac35$.
:::

::: example Three picks without replacement
A box has 4 white and 6 black balls. Three are taken without replacement. Find $P(\text{all black})$ and $P(\text{exactly one white})$.

$P(BBB) = \dfrac{6}{10} \times \dfrac59 \times \dfrac48 = \dfrac{120}{720} = \dfrac16$.

Exactly one white: three routes (WBB, BWB, BBW), each $\dfrac{4 \times 6 \times 5}{720} = \dfrac{120}{720}$: total $\dfrac{360}{720} = \dfrac12$.
:::

::: example Conditional from a Venn diagram
In a class of 30, 18 play football, 12 play tennis, 8 play both. Given that a student plays football, find the probability they also play tennis.

$P(\text{tennis} \mid \text{football}) = \dfrac{8}{18} = \dfrac49$.
:::

::: example Working backwards
A bag has $n$ counters, 4 of them green. Two are taken without replacement and $P(\text{both green}) = \dfrac{2}{15}$. Find $n$.

$\dfrac{4}{n} \times \dfrac{3}{n - 1} = \dfrac{2}{15} \Rightarrow 12 \times 15 = 2n(n - 1) \Rightarrow n^2 - n - 90 = 0 \Rightarrow (n - 10)(n + 9) = 0 \Rightarrow n = 10$.
:::

::: q
A coin is flipped and a dice rolled. Find $P(\text{head and a 6})$ and $P(\text{head or a 6})$.
---
$\dfrac{1}{12}$; $\dfrac12 + \dfrac16 - \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{7}{12}$.
:::

::: q
A spinner lands on red with probability $0.4$. It is spun twice. Find $P(\text{red then not red})$ and $P(\text{exactly one red})$.
---
$0.4 \times 0.6 = 0.24$; $2 \times 0.24 = 0.48$.
:::

::: q
A bag has 7 white and 3 black balls. Two are taken without replacement. Find $P(\text{both white})$.
---
$\dfrac{7}{10} \times \dfrac69 = \dfrac{42}{90} = \dfrac{7}{15}$.
:::

::: q
Same bag: find $P(\text{different colours})$.
---
$\dfrac{7}{10} \times \dfrac39 + \dfrac{3}{10} \times \dfrac79 = \dfrac{42}{90} = \dfrac{7}{15}$.
:::

::: q
Same bag: find $P(\text{at least one black})$.
---
$1 - P(WW) = 1 - \dfrac{7}{15} = \dfrac{8}{15}$.
:::

::: q
The probability it rains on any day is $0.3$, independently. Find the probability it rains on exactly one of two days.
---
$0.3 \times 0.7 + 0.7 \times 0.3 = 0.42$.
:::

::: q
Of 80 people, 50 own a car; 35 of the car owners and 10 of the others own a bike. Find $P(\text{bike} \mid \text{car})$ and $P(\text{car} \mid \text{bike})$.
---
$\dfrac{35}{50} = \dfrac{7}{10}$; $\dfrac{35}{45} = \dfrac79$.
:::

::: q
Cards numbered 1 to 10. Two are drawn without replacement. Find $P(\text{both even})$.
---
$\dfrac{5}{10} \times \dfrac49 = \dfrac{2}{9}$.
:::

::: q
A box has 5 red and 3 green pens. Three are taken without replacement. Find $P(\text{all red})$.
---
$\dfrac58 \times \dfrac47 \times \dfrac36 = \dfrac{60}{336} = \dfrac{5}{28}$.
:::

::: q
A bag has $n$ sweets, 5 of them mint. Two are eaten. $P(\text{both mint}) = \dfrac{5}{33}$. Find $n$.
---
$\dfrac{5}{n} \cdot \dfrac{4}{n - 1} = \dfrac{5}{33} \Rightarrow 20 \times 33 = 5n(n - 1) \Rightarrow n^2 - n - 132 = 0 \Rightarrow (n - 12)(n + 11) = 0$: $n = 12$.
:::

::: q
$P(A) = 0.5$, $P(B) = 0.4$ and $P(A \text{ and } B) = 0.2$. Are $A$ and $B$ independent?
---
$0.5 \times 0.4 = 0.2 = P(A \text{ and } B)$: yes.
:::

::: watch
- **Without replacement: both numbers change** on the second pick — the count of that colour and the total.
- Multiply **along** a route, add **across** routes. "And" multiplies; "or" adds.
- "One of each" has **two** routes. "Both the same" has two routes too (RR and BB).
- "At least one" → $1 - P(\text{none})$.
- Conditional probability: the "given" event is the **new denominator**. $P(A \mid B) \ne P(B \mid A)$.
- Keep fractions unsimplified on the tree; simplify the final answer only.
:::

::: test
A bag holds 4 red and 6 blue counters. Two are taken at random, with replacement. Find the probability that both are red. (2)
---
$0.4 \times 0.4$ (1) $= 0.16$ (1).
:::

::: test
Two counters are taken from the same bag without replacement. Find the probability that both are red. (2)
---
$\dfrac{4}{10} \times \dfrac{3}{9}$ (1) $= \dfrac{12}{90} = \dfrac{2}{15}$ (1).
:::

::: test
Two counters are taken without replacement. Find the probability of one of each colour. (2)
---
$\dfrac{4}{10} \times \dfrac{6}{9} + \dfrac{6}{10} \times \dfrac{4}{9}$ (1) $= \dfrac{48}{90} = \dfrac{8}{15}$ (1).
:::

::: test
The probability of rain on each of two days is 0.3, independently. Find the probability that it rains on at least one of the days. (2)
---
$P(\text{no rain either day}) = 0.7 \times 0.7 = 0.49$ (1); $1 - 0.49 = 0.51$ (1).
:::

::: test
60% of students study French. Of those, 40% also study Spanish. Of the students who do not study French, 25% study Spanish. Find the probability that a student chosen at random studies Spanish. (3)
---
$0.6 \times 0.4 = 0.24$ (1); $0.4 \times 0.25 = 0.10$ (1); $0.34$ (1).
:::

::: test
Given that a student studies Spanish, find the probability that they also study French. (2)
---
$\dfrac{0.24}{0.34}$ (1) $= \dfrac{12}{17} \approx 0.706$ (1).
:::

::: test
Three cards are drawn without replacement from a pack containing 3 aces and 2 kings. Find the probability that all three are aces. (2)
---
$\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{2}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{3}$ (1) $= \dfrac{6}{60} = \dfrac{1}{10}$ (1).
:::

::: test
The probability of passing a test at the first attempt is 0.7. Anyone who fails takes it again, and the probability of passing at the second attempt is 0.6. Find the probability of passing within two attempts. (2)
---
$0.7 + 0.3 \times 0.6$ (1) $= 0.88$ (1).
:::
