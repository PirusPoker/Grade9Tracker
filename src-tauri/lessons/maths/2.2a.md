## What it is

**Expanding** removes brackets by multiplying everything inside by what is outside; **factorising** is the reverse — putting brackets back in. They are the two directions of one road, and the whole of quadratic algebra runs along it.

- Single bracket: $3x(2x - 5) = 6x^2 - 15x$. Reverse: $6x^2 - 15x = 3x(2x - 5)$ — take out the **highest common factor**.
- Two brackets: $(x + 3)(x - 5) = x^2 - 5x + 3x - 15 = x^2 - 2x - 15$. Reverse: $x^2 - 2x - 15 = (x + 3)(x - 5)$ — find two numbers that **multiply to $-15$ and add to $-2$**.
- Three special patterns worth recognising on sight:
  - $(x + a)^2 = x^2 + 2ax + a^2$ (there is *always* a middle term)
  - $(x + a)(x - a) = x^2 - a^2$ — the **difference of two squares**
  - $x^2 - a^2 = (x + a)(x - a)$ — the same thing backwards, and it also works for $4x^2 - 9 = (2x + 3)(2x - 3)$.

At Higher tier you also expand **three** brackets (do two, then multiply the result by the third) and factorise quadratics with a number in front of $x^2$, like $6x^2 + 11x - 10$.

::: terms
- **Expand**: multiply out brackets.
- **Factorise**: put an expression into brackets by taking out common factors — the reverse of expanding.
- **Quadratic expression**: an expression whose highest power of x is 2, e.g. x² + 5x + 6.
- **Difference of two squares**: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).
- **Product of two brackets**: multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second.
:::

::: method
1. **Single bracket:** multiply every term inside by the term outside; watch the sign of the outside term. $-2(x - 4) = -2x + 8$.
2. **Two brackets (FOIL):** First, Outer, Inner, Last, then collect the middle terms. Or use a grid.
3. **Three brackets:** expand two of them first, then multiply every term of that answer by both terms of the third bracket. Collect carefully — there are usually six products before collecting.
4. **Factorise a single bracket:** find the HCF of the numbers *and* the lowest power of each shared letter; write it outside; divide each term by it to find what goes inside. Check by expanding.
5. **Factorise $x^2 + bx + c$:** two numbers with product $c$ and sum $b$. If $c$ is positive both numbers have the sign of $b$; if $c$ is negative they have different signs.
6. **Factorise $ax^2 + bx + c$ ($a \ne 1$):** find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$; split the middle term with them; factorise the two halves in pairs; take out the common bracket.
7. **Difference of two squares:** if it is (something)$^2 - $(something)$^2$ with no middle term, write $(\ + \ )(\ - \ )$ straight away.
8. **Always take out a common factor first** — $2x^2 - 8 = 2(x^2 - 4) = 2(x + 2)(x - 2)$.
:::

::: example Expanding two brackets
Expand and simplify $(2x - 3)(x + 4)$ and $(3x - 1)^2$.

$(2x - 3)(x + 4) = 2x^2 + 8x - 3x - 12 = 2x^2 + 5x - 12$.

$(3x - 1)^2 = (3x - 1)(3x - 1) = 9x^2 - 3x - 3x + 1 = 9x^2 - 6x + 1$.
:::

::: example Three brackets
Expand and simplify $(x + 1)(x - 2)(x + 3)$.

First two: $(x + 1)(x - 2) = x^2 - x - 2$.

Then $(x^2 - x - 2)(x + 3) = x^3 + 3x^2 - x^2 - 3x - 2x - 6 = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6$.
:::

::: example Common factors
Factorise fully $12x^3y - 18x^2y^2$.

HCF of 12 and 18 is 6; lowest powers $x^2$ and $y$: $6x^2y(2x - 3y)$.
:::

::: example Simple quadratics
Factorise $x^2 + 7x + 12$, $x^2 - 5x + 6$ and $x^2 + 3x - 28$.

Product 12, sum 7: $3, 4$ → $(x + 3)(x + 4)$.

Product 6, sum $-5$: $-2, -3$ → $(x - 2)(x - 3)$.

Product $-28$, sum $3$: $7, -4$ → $(x + 7)(x - 4)$.
:::

::: example Harder quadratics
Factorise $6x^2 + 11x - 10$.

$ac = -60$, $b = 11$: numbers $15$ and $-4$. Split: $6x^2 + 15x - 4x - 10$.

Pairs: $3x(2x + 5) - 2(2x + 5) = (2x + 5)(3x - 2)$.

Check the outer/inner terms: $-4x + 15x = 11x$ ✓.
:::

::: example Difference of two squares, with a factor first
Factorise fully $3x^2 - 48$ and $25a^2 - 16b^2$.

$3x^2 - 48 = 3(x^2 - 16) = 3(x + 4)(x - 4)$.

$25a^2 - 16b^2 = (5a + 4b)(5a - 4b)$.
:::

::: q
Expand and simplify $4(2x - 3) - 3(x - 5)$.
---
$8x - 12 - 3x + 15 = 5x + 3$.
:::

::: q
Expand and simplify $(x - 6)(x + 2)$ and $(2x + 5)(3x - 1)$.
---
$x^2 - 4x - 12$; $6x^2 + 13x - 5$.
:::

::: q
Expand $(x + 4)^2$ and $(2x - 3y)^2$.
---
$x^2 + 8x + 16$; $4x^2 - 12xy + 9y^2$.
:::

::: q
Expand and simplify $(x - 1)(x + 2)(x - 4)$.
---
$(x^2 + x - 2)(x - 4) = x^3 - 4x^2 + x^2 - 4x - 2x + 8 = x^3 - 3x^2 - 6x + 8$.
:::

::: q
Expand and simplify $(2x + 1)^3$.
---
$(4x^2 + 4x + 1)(2x + 1) = 8x^3 + 4x^2 + 8x^2 + 4x + 2x + 1 = 8x^3 + 12x^2 + 6x + 1$.
:::

::: q
Factorise fully $15a^2b + 20ab^3$.
---
$5ab(3a + 4b^2)$.
:::

::: q
Factorise $x^2 - 9x + 20$ and $x^2 - 2x - 35$.
---
$(x - 4)(x - 5)$; $(x - 7)(x + 5)$.
:::

::: q
Factorise $2x^2 + 7x + 3$ and $3x^2 - 10x + 8$.
---
$(2x + 1)(x + 3)$; $(3x - 4)(x - 2)$.
:::

::: q
Factorise $4x^2 - 4x - 15$.
---
$ac = -60$, $b = -4$: $-10, 6$. $4x^2 - 10x + 6x - 15 = 2x(2x - 5) + 3(2x - 5) = (2x - 5)(2x + 3)$.
:::

::: q
Factorise fully $x^2 - 81$, $2y^2 - 50$ and $9p^2 - 4q^2$.
---
$(x + 9)(x - 9)$; $2(y + 5)(y - 5)$; $(3p + 2q)(3p - 2q)$.
:::

::: q
Factorise fully $x^3 - x$.
---
$x(x^2 - 1) = x(x + 1)(x - 1)$.
:::

::: q
Use factorising to evaluate $57^2 - 43^2$ without a calculator.
---
$(57 + 43)(57 - 43) = 100 \times 14 = 1400$.
:::

::: watch
- **$(x + a)^2$ has a middle term.** $(x + 4)^2 = x^2 + 8x + 16$, never $x^2 + 16$.
- A minus outside a bracket changes **every** sign inside: $-3(x - 5) = -3x + 15$.
- "Factorise **fully**" means keep going: take out the common factor, then look for a difference of two squares or a quadratic inside.
- Check a factorisation by expanding the outer and inner terms — five seconds, catches most sign errors.
- For $ax^2 + bx + c$, the two numbers multiply to **$ac$**, not to $c$.
- Difference of two squares needs a **minus** and **no middle term**: $x^2 + 9$ does not factorise.
:::
