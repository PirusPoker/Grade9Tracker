## What it is

Two shapes are **similar** if one is an enlargement of the other: the same shape, all angles equal, every length multiplied by the same **scale factor** $k$. A photo and its enlargement; a model car and the real one; two triangles with the same angles.

Lengths scale by $k$. But areas and volumes do not:

$$\text{length} \times k \qquad \text{area} \times k^2 \qquad \text{volume} \times k^3$$

Double the lengths of a box and its surface area is 4 times bigger, its volume 8 times. This is the whole topic — and it works backwards too: if the areas are in the ratio $9 : 25$, the lengths are in the ratio $\sqrt9 : \sqrt{25} = 3 : 5$, and the volumes $27 : 125$.

Similar triangles are where you prove similarity (two pairs of equal angles is enough, since the third pair follows) and then use matching sides to find a missing length — including the standard picture of a small triangle nested inside a bigger one with parallel sides.

::: method
1. **Find the scale factor** from a pair of corresponding lengths: $k = \dfrac{\text{new}}{\text{old}}$. Corresponding means "in the same position" — the longest side matches the longest side, the side opposite the equal angle matches the side opposite the equal angle.
2. **Missing length:** multiply the corresponding old length by $k$ (or divide if going the other way).
3. **Area from lengths:** multiply by $k^2$. **Volume:** by $k^3$.
4. **Lengths from areas:** $k = \sqrt{\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}}$. **From volumes:** $k = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}}$. Then use $k$ for whatever else is asked.
5. **Nested triangles** (a line parallel to one side): the small and the large triangles are similar. Write the **whole** side of the large triangle, not just the extra piece: if the small triangle's side is 4 and the extension is 6, the large side is 10.
6. **Proving similarity:** show two pairs of angles are equal (with reasons: vertically opposite, alternate, common angle), then say "so the triangles are similar (AA)".
7. **Maps and scale drawings** are similar figures with $k = $ the scale.
:::

::: example Missing length in similar triangles
Triangles $ABC$ and $PQR$ are similar with $AB = 6$, $BC = 9$, $PQ = 10$. Find $QR$.

$k = \dfrac{10}{6} = \dfrac53$. $QR = 9 \times \dfrac53 = 15$.
:::

::: example Nested triangles
In triangle $ABC$, $D$ is on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$ with $DE$ parallel to $BC$. $AD = 4$, $DB = 6$, $DE = 5$. Find $BC$.

Triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ are similar (common angle $A$; $\angle ADE = \angle ABC$, corresponding angles). $AB = 10$, so $k = \dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5$. $BC = 5 \times 2.5 = 12.5$.
:::

::: example Area scale factor
Two similar photos have widths 10 cm and 25 cm. The smaller has area 150 cm$^2$. Find the area of the larger.

$k = 2.5$; area $\times 2.5^2 = 6.25$: $150 \times 6.25 = 937.5$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example Volume scale factor
Two similar bottles are 12 cm and 18 cm tall. The smaller holds 400 ml. How much does the larger hold?

$k = 1.5$; $k^3 = 3.375$; $400 \times 3.375 = 1350$ ml.
:::

::: example Backwards from areas
Two similar solids have surface areas $36$ cm$^2$ and $100$ cm$^2$. The smaller has volume $54$ cm$^3$. Find the volume of the larger.

Area ratio $\dfrac{100}{36}$, so $k = \sqrt{\dfrac{100}{36}} = \dfrac{10}{6} = \dfrac53$. Volume $= 54 \times \left(\dfrac53\right)^3 = 54 \times \dfrac{125}{27} = 250$ cm$^3$.
:::

::: example Backwards from volumes
Two similar cones have volumes $250$ cm$^3$ and $2000$ cm$^3$. The larger has height 24 cm. Find the height of the smaller.

$k^3 = \dfrac{2000}{250} = 8 \Rightarrow k = 2$. Smaller height $= 12$ cm.
:::

::: example Proving similarity
$AB$ and $CD$ are parallel; $AD$ and $BC$ cross at $X$. Prove triangles $AXB$ and $DXC$ are similar, and find $XC$ if $AX = 6$, $XD = 9$, $BX = 4$.

$\angle AXB = \angle DXC$ (vertically opposite); $\angle XAB = \angle XDC$ (alternate angles, $AB \parallel CD$). Two angles equal, so similar (AA).

$k = \dfrac{9}{6} = 1.5$; $XC = 4 \times 1.5 = 6$.
:::

::: q
Two similar rectangles have widths 4 cm and 10 cm. The smaller has length 7 cm. Find the length of the larger.
---
$k = 2.5$; $17.5$ cm.
:::

::: q
Triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ are similar with $AB = 8$, $AC = 12$, $DE = 6$. Find $DF$.
---
$k = 0.75$; $DF = 9$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $PQR$, $S$ is on $PQ$, $T$ is on $PR$, $ST \parallel QR$. $PS = 3$, $SQ = 5$, $QR = 12$. Find $ST$.
---
$PQ = 8$, $k = \dfrac{3}{8}$; $ST = 4.5$.
:::

::: q
A model boat is made to a scale of $1 : 40$. The real boat's deck has area $80$ m$^2$. Find the model's deck area in cm$^2$.
---
$80 \div 1600 = 0.05$ m$^2 = 500$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
Two similar cylinders have radii 3 cm and 5 cm. The smaller has volume $90$ cm$^3$. Find the volume of the larger.
---
$\left(\dfrac53\right)^3 = \dfrac{125}{27}$; $90 \times \dfrac{125}{27} = 416.7$ cm$^3$.
:::

::: q
Two similar solids have volumes $64$ cm$^3$ and $216$ cm$^3$. Find the ratio of their surface areas.
---
$k = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{216}{64}} = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$; areas $1 : 2.25$, i.e. $4 : 9$.
:::

::: q
Two similar triangles have areas $20$ cm$^2$ and $45$ cm$^2$. The smaller has a side of 6 cm. Find the corresponding side of the larger.
---
$k = \sqrt{\dfrac{45}{20}} = 1.5$; $9$ cm.
:::

::: q
A sphere of radius 4 cm has surface area $A$. What is the surface area of a sphere of radius 6 cm, in terms of $A$?
---
$k = 1.5$, $k^2 = 2.25$: $2.25A$.
:::

::: q
A cone is cut parallel to its base halfway up its height. What fraction of the volume is the small cone at the top?
---
$k = \dfrac12$, $k^3 = \dfrac18$.
:::

::: q
Two similar jugs hold $1.2$ litres and $4.05$ litres. The larger is 27 cm tall. Find the height of the smaller.
---
$k^3 = \dfrac{4.05}{1.2} = 3.375 \Rightarrow k = 1.5$; $27 \div 1.5 = 18$ cm.
:::

::: q
$AB \parallel DE$, and $AE$ and $BD$ meet at $C$. $AC = 5$, $CE = 8$, $BC = 4$. Find $CD$ and state why the triangles are similar.
---
Vertically opposite angles at $C$; alternate angles: similar (AA). $k = \dfrac85$; $CD = 6.4$.
:::

::: watch
- **Area uses $k^2$, volume uses $k^3$** — and going backwards from an area needs a square root, from a volume a cube root.
- Nested triangles: use the **whole** side of the big triangle ($4 + 6 = 10$), not the extension.
- Match **corresponding** sides — the ones opposite equal angles. Write both triangles in the matching order ($ABC$ and $PQR$ with $A \leftrightarrow P$).
- Similarity needs **equal angles**, with reasons; congruence needs equal sides too. Don't confuse them.
- Model scales: $1 : 40$ in length is $1 : 1600$ in area and $1 : 64\,000$ in volume.
- Keep $k$ as a fraction ($\frac53$) until the end — decimals like $1.67$ introduce error.
:::
