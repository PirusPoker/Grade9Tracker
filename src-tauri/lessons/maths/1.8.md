## What it is

Every measurement is rounded, so every measurement hides a range. "A length of 12 cm to the nearest cm" could be anything from 11.5 cm up to (but not including) 12.5 cm. Those are its **lower bound** and **upper bound**:

$$\text{lower bound} = \text{value} - \tfrac12 \text{unit}, \qquad \text{upper bound} = \text{value} + \tfrac12 \text{unit}$$

where the "unit" is the size of the rounding (1 cm, 0.1 kg, 10 people, 5 g …). The error interval is written $11.5 \le L < 12.5$.

The Higher-tier questions then ask what happens to those bounds when you **calculate** with rounded numbers. A perimeter built from two rounded lengths could be as big as the sum of the upper bounds; a speed from a rounded distance and a rounded time is biggest when the distance is at its upper bound *and* the time at its **lower** bound.

**Rounding** itself: **decimal places** count digits after the point; **significant figures** count from the first non-zero digit. $0.004\underline{7}61$ to 2 s.f. is $0.0048$; $12\,\underline{3}47$ to 2 s.f. is $12\,000$. **Estimation** means rounding every number to 1 s.f. and calculating — the answer is only meant to be roughly right.

::: terms
- **Decimal places (d.p.)**: the number of digits after the decimal point.
- **Significant figures (s.f.)**: digits counted from the first non-zero digit.
- **Estimation**: rounding every number to 1 significant figure to get an approximate answer.
- **Upper bound**: the largest value a rounded number could have been (halfway up to the next value).
- **Lower bound**: the smallest value a rounded number could have been.
- **Error interval**: the range of possible values, e.g. 6.5 ≤ x < 7.5 for 7 to the nearest whole number.
- **Bounds in division**: the maximum of a ÷ b is upper bound of a ÷ lower bound of b.
:::

::: method
1. **Find the unit** the value was rounded to. "Nearest 10" → half-unit 5. "1 decimal place" → half-unit 0.05. "2 significant figures" → depends on the size: $3.4$ (2 s.f.) has half-unit $0.05$; $340$ (2 s.f.) has half-unit $5$.
2. **Bounds:** LB $= v - \frac12 u$, UB $= v + \frac12 u$. Write the error interval as $\text{LB} \le x < \text{UB}$.
3. **For a calculation**, ask "which bound of each input makes the answer biggest?" and "smallest?":
   - Add / multiply: biggest uses all UBs; smallest uses all LBs.
   - Subtract $a - b$: biggest is $\text{UB}(a) - \text{LB}(b)$; smallest is $\text{LB}(a) - \text{UB}(b)$.
   - Divide $a \div b$: biggest is $\text{UB}(a) \div \text{LB}(b)$; smallest is $\text{LB}(a) \div \text{UB}(b)$.
4. **"Suitable degree of accuracy":** work out the UB and LB of the answer; round both to the same number of figures until they *agree*; that agreed value is the answer, and the reason is "the bounds both round to it".
5. **Estimate:** round each number to 1 s.f., then calculate exactly with those. Show the rounded numbers.
6. **Truncation** (cut off, not rounded): the interval is $v \le x < v + u$ — the whole unit sits above.
:::

::: example Bounds of a single measurement
Write the error interval for (a) a mass of $3.7$ kg to 1 d.p., (b) a crowd of $24\,000$ to the nearest thousand, (c) a length of $0.060$ m to 2 s.f.

(a) Half-unit $0.05$: $3.65 \le m < 3.75$.

(b) Half-unit $500$: $23\,500 \le n < 24\,500$.

(c) Unit is $0.001$, half-unit $0.0005$: $0.0595 \le L < 0.0605$.
:::

::: example Perimeter and area
A rectangle measures $8.4$ cm by $5.2$ cm, each to the nearest mm. Find the upper bound of the perimeter and the lower bound of the area.

Bounds: $8.35 \le l < 8.45$, $5.15 \le w < 5.25$.

Perimeter UB $= 2(8.45 + 5.25) = 27.4$ cm.

Area LB $= 8.35 \times 5.15 = 43.0025$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example Division: speed
A car travels $150$ m (nearest 10 m) in $8.3$ s (nearest 0.1 s). Find the upper and lower bounds of its average speed.

$145 \le d < 155$; $8.25 \le t < 8.35$.

UB of speed $= \dfrac{155}{8.25} = 18.79$ m/s. LB $= \dfrac{145}{8.35} = 17.37$ m/s.
:::

::: example Suitable degree of accuracy
$V = \dfrac{m}{d}$ with $m = 250$ g (nearest g) and $d = 8.9$ g/cm$^3$ (1 d.p.). Find $V$ to a suitable degree of accuracy.

UB $= \dfrac{250.5}{8.85} = 28.305\ldots$; LB $= \dfrac{249.5}{8.95} = 27.877\ldots$

To 2 s.f. both are $28$. So $V = 28$ cm$^3$, because the upper and lower bounds both round to 28 to 2 s.f.
:::

::: example Estimation
Estimate $\dfrac{4.87 \times 21.3}{0.52}$.

$\approx \dfrac{5 \times 20}{0.5} = \dfrac{100}{0.5} = 200$.
:::

::: example Subtraction
Two rods measure $45$ cm and $28$ cm, each to the nearest cm. Find the greatest possible difference in their lengths.

$\text{UB}(45) - \text{LB}(28) = 45.5 - 27.5 = 18$ cm.
:::

$P = \dfrac{F}{A}$ with $F = 620$ N (2 s.f.) and $A = 0.35$ m$^2$ (2 s.f.). Find $P$ to a suitable degree of accuracy, explaining your choice.
---
UB $= \dfrac{625}{0.345} = 1811.6$; LB $= \dfrac{615}{0.355} = 1732.4$. To 2 s.f. both give $1800$. $P = 1800$ N (2 s.f.), since both bounds round to it.

$\approx \dfrac{10 \times 3}{0.2} = 150$.

::: q
The density of a block is $\dfrac{m}{V}$. $m = 1.2$ kg (2 s.f.) and $V = 480$ cm$^3$ (nearest 10). Find the upper bound of the density in g/cm$^3$.
---
$m$ UB $= 1250$ g; $V$ LB $= 475$. $\dfrac{1250}{475} = 2.63$ g/cm$^3$ (3 s.f.).
:::

::: watch
- **The upper bound is strict:** write $< $ on the right, $\le$ on the left. $11.5 \le L < 12.5$.
- Half the unit, not the whole unit. "Nearest 10" gives $\pm 5$; "1 d.p." gives $\pm 0.05$.
- For **division** and **subtraction**, the biggest answer uses the *opposite* bounds of the second number. Ask "what makes it biggest?" rather than reaching for UB everywhere.
- Don't round bounds early — keep them exact ($8.45$, not $8.5$).
- "Suitable degree of accuracy": you must show both bounds and say they agree when rounded. The number alone is not enough.
- Estimation: round to **1 s.f.** and show the rounded calculation; an exact answer from the calculator gets no marks for an estimate.
:::

::: test
A bag holds 1.5 kg of flour, correct to the nearest 0.1 kg. A recipe uses 250 g, correct to the nearest 10 g. Show that the bag can never make more than 6 complete recipes. (3)
---
Upper bound of flour $= 1550$ g (1). Lower bound of recipe $= 245$ g (1). $1550 \div 245 = 6.33$, so at most 6 complete recipes (1).

::: test

Find the upper bound of the time taken to fill the tank. Give your answer in minutes to 3 significant figures. (1 m$^3$ = 1000 litres) (5)
---
Upper bounds 1.25, 0.85, 0.55 m (1). Maximum volume $= 0.584375$ m$^3$ $= 584.375$ litres (1). Lower bound of rate $= 19.5$ litres/min (1). Time $= 584.375 \div 19.5$ (1) $= 30.0$ minutes (29.97) (1).
