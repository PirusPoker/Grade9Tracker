## What it is

For triangles **without** a right angle, label the angles $A$, $B$, $C$ and the sides opposite them $a$, $b$, $c$. Three formulae handle everything:

**Sine rule** (a side and its opposite angle, plus one more of either):
$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$$

**Cosine rule** (two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides):
$$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A \qquad \cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$$

**Area** (two sides and the angle between them):
$$\text{Area} = \tfrac12 ab\sin C$$

Because angles in these triangles can be **obtuse**, you also need $\sin$, $\cos$ and $\tan$ of angles beyond $90°$: $\sin(180° - \theta) = \sin\theta$ (so $\sin 130° = \sin 50°$), while $\cos(180° - \theta) = -\cos\theta$ (so $\cos 130° = -\cos 50°$ — negative). That is why the sine rule can give two possible angles and the cosine rule never does.

::: method
1. **Label** the triangle $A, B, C$ / $a, b, c$ with each side opposite its angle. Write what you know and what you want.
2. **Choose:** know a matching pair (side + opposite angle)? **Sine rule**. Know two sides and the included angle, or three sides? **Cosine rule**. Area with two sides and the included angle? **$\frac12 ab\sin C$**.
3. **Sine rule for a side:** put the unknown on top: $\dfrac{x}{\sin X} = \dfrac{\text{known side}}{\sin(\text{its angle})}$.
4. **Sine rule for an angle:** flip it, $\sin$ on top; then $\sin^{-1}$. If the angle could be obtuse (the question says so, or the side is the longest), the second possibility is $180° -$ your answer.
5. **Cosine rule for a side:** the angle in the formula is the one **between** the two known sides. Evaluate $b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ fully before square-rooting.
6. **Cosine rule for an angle:** rearranged form; the side opposite the wanted angle goes with the minus.
7. **Multi-step:** find a side with one rule, then an angle with another; keep full accuracy between steps. Area often comes last.
8. **Third angle:** once you have two, the third is $180° -$ their sum.
:::

::: example Sine rule for a side
In triangle $ABC$, $A = 48°$, $B = 67°$, $a = 9.2$ cm. Find $b$.

$\dfrac{b}{\sin 67°} = \dfrac{9.2}{\sin 48°} \Rightarrow b = \dfrac{9.2\sin 67°}{\sin 48°} = 11.4$ cm.
:::

::: example Sine rule for an angle
In triangle $PQR$, $p = 7$, $q = 10$, $P = 38°$. Find $Q$, given that it is acute.

$\dfrac{\sin Q}{10} = \dfrac{\sin 38°}{7} \Rightarrow \sin Q = 0.8795 \Rightarrow Q = 61.6°$.

(The obtuse alternative would be $118.4°$ — also geometrically possible, which is why the question said "acute".)
:::

::: example Cosine rule for a side
Two sides of a triangle are 8 cm and 11 cm with an angle of $52°$ between them. Find the third side.

$x^2 = 64 + 121 - 2 \times 8 \times 11 \cos 52° = 185 - 108.36 = 76.64 \Rightarrow x = 8.75$ cm.
:::

::: example Cosine rule for an angle
A triangle has sides 6, 9 and 13. Find its largest angle.

Largest angle opposite 13: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{36 + 81 - 169}{2 \times 6 \times 9} = \dfrac{-52}{108} \Rightarrow \theta = 118.8°$.
:::

::: example Area
Find the area of a triangle with sides 7 cm and 12 cm and included angle $115°$.

$\dfrac12 \times 7 \times 12 \times \sin 115° = 38.1$ cm$^2$. ($\sin 115° = \sin 65°$ — positive.)
:::

::: example A full problem
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 10$ cm, $BC = 14$ cm and angle $ABC = 40°$. Find $AC$, then angle $ACB$, then angle $BAC$, then the area.

Cosine rule (two sides and the included angle): $AC^2 = 100 + 196 - 2 \times 10 \times 14\cos 40° = 296 - 214.5 = 81.51$, so $AC = 9.03$ cm.

Sine rule for angle $C$ (opposite $AB$, the shortest side, so it must be acute): $\dfrac{\sin C}{10} = \dfrac{\sin 40°}{9.028} \Rightarrow \sin C = 0.7120 \Rightarrow C = 45.4°$.

Angle $BAC = 180° - 40° - 45.4° = 94.6°$ — obtuse, as expected for the angle opposite the longest side.

Area $= \dfrac12 \times 10 \times 14 \times \sin 40° = 45.0$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $B = 55°$, $C = 70°$, $b = 12$. Find $c$.
---
$c = \dfrac{12\sin 70°}{\sin 55°} = 13.8$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $XYZ$, $x = 8$, $y = 11$, $X = 35°$. Find angle $Y$ (acute).
---
$\sin Y = \dfrac{11\sin 35°}{8} = 0.7887 \Rightarrow Y = 52.1°$.
:::

::: q
Find the third side of a triangle with sides 5 cm and 9 cm and included angle $110°$.
---
$x^2 = 25 + 81 - 90\cos 110° = 106 + 30.78 = 136.78 \Rightarrow x = 11.7$ cm.
:::

::: q
A triangle has sides 7, 8 and 12. Find the angle opposite the side of length 12.
---
$\cos\theta = \dfrac{49 + 64 - 144}{112} = -0.2768 \Rightarrow \theta = 106.1°$.
:::

A triangle has sides 10, 13 and 15. Find its smallest angle.
---
Opposite 10: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{169 + 225 - 100}{390} = 0.7538 \Rightarrow \theta = 41.1°$.
:::

::: q
Find the area of a triangle with sides 9 cm and 14 cm and included angle $48°$.
---
$\dfrac12 \times 9 \times 14 \sin 48° = 46.8$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A triangle has area 30 cm$^2$ and two sides of 8 cm and 10 cm. Find the two possible angles between them.
---
$\sin\theta = \dfrac{60}{80} = 0.75$: $48.6°$ or $131.4°$.
:::

::: q
Write down the exact values of $\sin 150°$, $\cos 120°$ and $\cos 135°$.
---
$\dfrac12$; $-\dfrac12$; $-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$.
:::

::: q
Two ships leave port together. One sails 15 km on a bearing of $040°$, the other 22 km on a bearing of $110°$. How far apart are they?
---
Angle between $= 70°$: $d^2 = 225 + 484 - 660\cos 70° = 709 - 225.7 = 483.3 \Rightarrow d = 22.0$ km.
:::

::: q
In triangle $PQR$, $PQ = 9$, $QR = 12$ and angle $PQR = 62°$. Find $PR$ and angle $QRP$.
---
$PR^2 = 81 + 144 - 216\cos 62° = 225 - 101.4 = 123.6 \Rightarrow PR = 11.1$.

Angle $R$ is opposite $PQ = 9$, the shortest side, so it is acute: $\dfrac{\sin R}{9} = \dfrac{\sin 62°}{11.12} \Rightarrow \sin R = 0.7147 \Rightarrow R = 45.6°$. (Then $P = 72.4°$.)
:::

::: q
A field is a triangle with sides 120 m, 150 m and 200 m. Find its area.
---
Angle opposite 200: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{14400 + 22500 - 40000}{36000} = -0.0861$, $\theta = 94.9°$. Area $= \dfrac12 \times 120 \times 150 \times \sin 94.9° = 8970$ m$^2$ (3 s.f.).
:::

::: watch
- **Side opposite angle.** Mislabel one pair and the sine rule quietly gives a wrong answer.
- Sine rule for an angle: the calculator only gives the **acute** answer. Ask whether $180° -$ it is possible; if the question says "obtuse", use that one.
- Cosine rule: the angle is the one **between** the two sides you square. And $\cos$ of an obtuse angle is **negative**, which turns the minus into a plus.
- Do $b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ as one calculator line with the cos term in brackets; don't square-root until the end.
- Keep unrounded values between steps (use Ans). A side rounded to 9.0 makes the next angle wrong by a degree.
- The area formula needs the **included** angle — the one between the two sides used.
:::

::: test
In a triangle, the side of length 9 cm is opposite an angle of $80^\circ$. Find the angle opposite the side of length 7 cm, to 1 decimal place. (2)
---
$\sin A = \dfrac{7 \sin 80^\circ}{9}$ (1); $A = 50.0^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
A parallelogram has sides 8 cm and 11 cm and an angle of $55^\circ$. Find its area to 3 significant figures. (2)
---
Two triangles: $2 \times \dfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 11 \times \sin 55^\circ = 88 \sin 55^\circ$ (1) $= 72.1$ cm$^2$ (1).
:::

::: test
A triangle has area 24 cm$^2$ and two sides of 8 cm and 9 cm. The angle between them is obtuse. Find it, to 1 decimal place. (2)
---
$\sin\theta = \dfrac{48}{72} = \dfrac{2}{3}$, giving $41.8^\circ$ (1); the obtuse angle is $180 - 41.8 = 138.2^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
From a port, ship $A$ is 25 km away on a bearing of $050^\circ$ and ship $B$ is 40 km away on a bearing of $130^\circ$. Find the distance between the ships, to 3 significant figures. (3)
---
Angle at the port $= 130 - 50 = 80^\circ$ (1); $AB^2 = 25^2 + 40^2 - 2 \times 25 \times 40 \cos 80^\circ$ (1); $AB = 43.3$ km (1).
:::
