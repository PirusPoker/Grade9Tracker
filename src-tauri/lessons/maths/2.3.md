## What it is

A **formula** is an equation that links quantities: $v = u + at$, $A = \pi r^2$, $s = \dfrac{d}{t}$. You do two things with formulae: **substitute** numbers in to find one quantity, and **rearrange** (change the subject) so a different letter is on its own.

Changing the subject is solving an equation where the other letters are treated as if they were numbers. Every step you would use on $3x + 5 = 20$ works on $y = mx + c$ to get $x = \dfrac{y - c}{m}$.

The Higher-tier version has the new subject appearing **twice** (collect its terms on one side, factorise it out), or inside a **power** or **root** (square, or square-root, both sides).

You also need to **write** formulae from words or diagrams: a perimeter, a cost with a fixed charge plus a rate, the number of matches in a pattern.

::: method
1. **Substitute** with brackets round every value, especially negatives and fractions, then work through BIDMAS. Units in, units out.
2. **Change the subject — the subject appears once:** undo the operations in reverse order. Whatever was done last to the subject, undo first. Do the same thing to both sides at every step.
3. **Subject in a power:** get the power on its own, then take the root of both sides (and consider $\pm$ if the context allows negatives). $A = \pi r^2 \Rightarrow r = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}$.
4. **Subject under a root:** isolate the root, then square both sides.
5. **Subject in a denominator:** multiply both sides by the denominator first.
6. **Subject appears twice:** clear fractions, expand brackets, move every term containing the subject to one side and everything else to the other, **factorise** the subject out, divide by the bracket.
7. **Writing a formula:** define each letter, write the rule in words first ("total cost = fixed charge + rate × hours"), then swap in the letters.
:::

::: example Substitution
$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$. Find $v$ when $u = 3$, $a = -2$ and $s = -8$.

$v^2 = 9 + 2(-2)(-8) = 9 + 32 = 41$, so $v = \sqrt{41} = 6.40$ (3 s.f.).
:::

::: example Subject appearing once
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{3x - 2}{5}$.

$5y = 3x - 2 \Rightarrow 5y + 2 = 3x \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{5y + 2}{3}$.
:::

::: example Subject in a power and under a root
(a) Make $r$ the subject of $V = \dfrac43 \pi r^3$. (b) Make $a$ the subject of $t = 2\sqrt{\dfrac{a}{g}}$.

(a) $r^3 = \dfrac{3V}{4\pi} \Rightarrow r = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{3V}{4\pi}}$.

(b) $\dfrac t2 = \sqrt{\dfrac ag} \Rightarrow \dfrac{t^2}{4} = \dfrac{a}{g} \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{g t^2}{4}$.
:::

::: example Subject appearing twice
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}$.

$y(x - 3) = 2x + 1 \Rightarrow xy - 3y = 2x + 1$.

$x$ terms to the left: $xy - 2x = 3y + 1 \Rightarrow x(y - 2) = 3y + 1 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{3y + 1}{y - 2}$.
:::

::: example Subject in a denominator
Make $u$ the subject of $\dfrac1f = \dfrac1u + \dfrac1v$.

$\dfrac1u = \dfrac1f - \dfrac1v = \dfrac{v - f}{fv} \Rightarrow u = \dfrac{fv}{v - f}$.
:::

::: example Writing a formula
A plumber charges a £45 call-out fee plus £32 per hour. Write a formula for the cost £$C$ of a job lasting $h$ hours, and find how long a job costing £189 took.

$C = 45 + 32h$. $189 = 45 + 32h \Rightarrow h = 4.5$ hours.
:::

::: q
$s = ut + \dfrac12 at^2$. Find $s$ when $u = 5$, $a = -3$, $t = 4$.
---
$20 + \dfrac12(-3)(16) = 20 - 24 = -4$.
:::

::: q
Make $t$ the subject of $v = u + at$.
---
$t = \dfrac{v - u}{a}$.
:::

::: q
Make $x$ the subject of $y = 4(x + 3) - 7$.
---
$y + 7 = 4x + 12 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{y - 5}{4}$.
:::

::: q
Make $h$ the subject of $V = \pi r^2 h$, and $r$ the subject of $A = 4\pi r^2$.
---
$h = \dfrac{V}{\pi r^2}$; $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{4\pi}}$.
:::

::: q
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \sqrt{2x - 5}$.
---
$y^2 = 2x - 5 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{y^2 + 5}{2}$.
:::

::: q
Make $a$ the subject of $p = \dfrac{3}{a} + q$.
---
$p - q = \dfrac3a \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{3}{p - q}$.
:::

::: q
Make $x$ the subject of $ax + b = cx + d$.
---
$ax - cx = d - b \Rightarrow x(a - c) = d - b \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{d - b}{a - c}$.
:::

::: q
Make $m$ the subject of $k = \dfrac{m + 2}{m - 1}$.
---
$k(m - 1) = m + 2 \Rightarrow km - k = m + 2 \Rightarrow km - m = k + 2 \Rightarrow m = \dfrac{k + 2}{k - 1}$.
:::

::: q
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{3}{x^2} + 1$.
---
$y - 1 = \dfrac{3}{x^2} \Rightarrow x^2 = \dfrac{3}{y - 1} \Rightarrow x = \pm\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{y - 1}}$.
:::

::: q
A taxi charges £2.80 plus £1.65 per km. Write a formula for the fare £$F$ for $d$ km, and find $d$ when $F = 19.30$.
---
$F = 2.8 + 1.65d$; $16.5 = 1.65d \Rightarrow d = 10$ km.
:::

::: q
The $n$th pattern in a sequence of matchstick squares uses $3n + 1$ matches. Which pattern uses 100 matches?
---
$3n + 1 = 100 \Rightarrow n = 33$.
:::

::: watch
- **Undo in reverse order.** In $y = \dfrac{3x - 2}{5}$, the last thing done to $x$ was "÷5", so multiply by 5 first.
- Do the same to **both sides** — and to **every term** when multiplying through by something.
- When the subject appears twice, you **must factorise** it out; leaving $xy - 2x = \dots$ is not an answer.
- Squaring both sides squares the **whole** side: $\left(\dfrac{t}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{t^2}{4}$, not $\dfrac{t^2}{2}$.
- Substitute negatives in brackets: $2(-2)(-8) = +32$.
- Write the units and define the letters when you build a formula from words.
:::

::: test
$v = u + at$. Find $v$ when $u = 4$, $a = -2$ and $t = 3.5$. (1)
---
$v = 4 + (-2)(3.5) = 4 - 7 = -3$.
:::

::: test
Make $x$ the subject of $y = 3x - 7$. (1)
---
$x = \dfrac{y + 7}{3}$.
:::

::: test
Make $r$ the subject of $A = \pi r^2$. (2)
---
$r^2 = \dfrac{A}{\pi}$ (1), so $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}$ (1).
:::

::: test
Make $u$ the subject of $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$. (2)
---
$u^2 = v^2 - 2as$ (1), so $u = \sqrt{v^2 - 2as}$ (1).
:::

::: test
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}$. (3)
---
$y(x - 3) = 2x + 1$, so $xy - 3y = 2x + 1$ (1). Collect $x$: $xy - 2x = 3y + 1$, $x(y - 2) = 3y + 1$ (1). $x = \dfrac{3y + 1}{y - 2}$ (1).
:::

::: test
Make $a$ the subject of $\dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b} = \dfrac{1}{f}$. (3)
---
$\dfrac{1}{a} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{b} = \dfrac{b - f}{bf}$ (2). Invert: $a = \dfrac{bf}{b - f}$ (1).
:::

::: test
The surface area of a closed cylinder is $A = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$. Find $h$ when $A = 300$ and $r = 4$, to 3 significant figures. (2)
---
$h = \dfrac{A - 2\pi r^2}{2\pi r} = \dfrac{300 - 100.53\ldots}{25.13\ldots}$ (1) $= 7.94$ (1).
:::

::: test
Make $l$ the subject of $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$. (3)
---
$\dfrac{T}{2\pi} = \sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$ (1); square: $\dfrac{T^2}{4\pi^2} = \dfrac{l}{g}$ (1); $l = \dfrac{gT^2}{4\pi^2}$ (1).
:::
