## What it is

Nothing new in formulae — Pythagoras and SOH CAH TOA — but in three dimensions the right-angled triangle you need is **hidden inside a solid**, and the skill is finding it and drawing it flat.

The two standard questions:

- **The longest diagonal of a cuboid** (or a distance between two corners of any box): use Pythagoras twice — once on the base to get the base diagonal, once in the vertical triangle to get the space diagonal. Or in one go: $d = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$.
- **The angle between a line and a plane**: it is the angle between the line and its **projection** (shadow) on the plane. Drop a perpendicular from the top end of the line to the plane; the line, the perpendicular and the projection form a right-angled triangle, and the angle you want is at the bottom, where the line meets the plane.

Pyramids add one idea: the apex is above the centre of the base, so half the base diagonal (or half the base side, for a face) is the horizontal leg of the triangle.

::: method
1. **Identify the triangle.** Which three points? Is there a right angle among them? (Vertical edges meet the base at right angles; a base diagonal and a vertical edge meet at a right angle.)
2. **Draw it separately**, flat, with the right angle marked and the known lengths written on. Never try to read lengths off the 3D picture.
3. **Base first.** If the triangle's horizontal side is a base diagonal, find that with Pythagoras on the base.
4. **Then the vertical triangle.** Pythagoras for a length; $\tan$ for the angle (opposite $=$ height, adjacent $=$ projection), or $\sin$ / $\cos$ if the slant length is what you know.
5. **Angle between line and plane** = angle between the line and its projection: the angle at the foot of the line, with the height opposite it.
6. **Pyramids:** the apex is above the centre of the base; distance from centre to a corner is half the base diagonal; to the midpoint of a side is half the base side.
7. **Keep exact values** through the steps ($\sqrt{45}$ rather than $6.7$) and round only at the end.
:::

::: example Space diagonal of a cuboid
A cuboid measures 8 cm by 6 cm by 5 cm. Find the length of its longest diagonal.

Base diagonal: $\sqrt{64 + 36} = 10$. Space diagonal: $\sqrt{100 + 25} = \sqrt{125} = 11.2$ cm.

(Or directly: $\sqrt{64 + 36 + 25} = \sqrt{125}$.)
:::

::: example Angle between a diagonal and the base
For the same cuboid, find the angle the longest diagonal makes with the base.

Triangle: base diagonal 10 (adjacent), height 5 (opposite). $\tan\theta = \dfrac{5}{10} \Rightarrow \theta = 26.6°$.
:::

::: example Angle with a face
Cuboid $ABCDEFGH$ has $AB = 12$, $BC = 9$, $CG = 8$ (vertical). Find the angle between the diagonal $AG$ and the face $ABFE$ (the front face containing $AB$ and the vertical edges).

Projection of $AG$ onto face $ABFE$ is $AF$. $AF = \sqrt{144 + 64} = \sqrt{208}$. The leg perpendicular to the face is $FG = BC = 9$.

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{9}{\sqrt{208}} \Rightarrow \theta = 32.0°$.
:::

::: example A square-based pyramid
A pyramid has a square base of side 10 cm and vertex $V$ vertically above the centre $O$, with $VO = 12$ cm. Find the length of a slant edge $VA$ and the angle $VA$ makes with the base.

$OA$ = half the base diagonal $= \dfrac{\sqrt{200}}{2} = \sqrt{50} = 7.07$.

$VA = \sqrt{144 + 50} = \sqrt{194} = 13.9$ cm.

Angle with base: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{12}{\sqrt{50}} \Rightarrow \theta = 59.5°$.
:::

::: example Angle between a face and the base
For the same pyramid, find the angle between the triangular face $VBC$ and the base.

Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$: $OM = 5$ (half the base side) and $VM \perp BC$. In triangle $VOM$: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{12}{5} \Rightarrow \theta = 67.4°$.
:::

::: example A wedge
A wedge has a horizontal rectangular base $ABCD$ with $AB = 10$, $BC = 4$, and a vertical rectangular back face $DCFE$ of height 3 ($DE = CF = 3$). Find the length $AF$ and the angle $AF$ makes with the base.

$AC = \sqrt{100 + 16} = \sqrt{116}$. $AF = \sqrt{116 + 9} = \sqrt{125} = 11.2$.

Angle: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{116}} \Rightarrow \theta = 15.6°$.
:::

::: q
Find the longest diagonal of a cuboid 3 cm by 4 cm by 12 cm.
---
$\sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$ cm.
:::

::: q
A cube has side 6 cm. Find the length of a face diagonal and of a space diagonal, exactly.
---
$6\sqrt2$ cm; $6\sqrt3$ cm.
:::

::: q
A cuboid is 7 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm. Find the angle between its longest diagonal and the base.
---
Base diagonal $\sqrt{74}$; $\tan\theta = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{74}} \Rightarrow \theta = 24.9°$.
:::

::: q
A room is 5 m long, 4 m wide and 2.5 m high. Find the angle of elevation of the top far corner from the bottom near corner.
---
Floor diagonal $\sqrt{41}$; $\tan\theta = \dfrac{2.5}{\sqrt{41}} \Rightarrow \theta = 21.3°$.
:::

::: q
A square-based pyramid has base side 8 cm and slant edges 11 cm. Find its vertical height.
---
Half diagonal $= 4\sqrt2$; height $= \sqrt{121 - 32} = \sqrt{89} = 9.43$ cm.
:::

::: q
For the same pyramid, find the angle between a slant edge and the base.
---
$\cos\theta = \dfrac{4\sqrt2}{11} \Rightarrow \theta = 59.0°$.
:::

::: q
A cone has base radius 5 cm and slant height 13 cm. Find its height and the angle between the slant height and the base.
---
$h = 12$ cm; $\tan\theta = \dfrac{12}{5} \Rightarrow \theta = 67.4°$.
:::

::: q
Cuboid $ABCDEFGH$ has base $ABCD$ with $AB = 6$ and $AD = 8$, and vertical edges of length 10 ($E$ above $A$, $F$ above $B$). $M$ is the midpoint of the top edge $EF$. Find the length $CM$.
---
$M$ is directly above $N$, the midpoint of $AB$. In the base, $CN = \sqrt{3^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{73}$. Then $CM = \sqrt{73 + 10^2} = \sqrt{173} = 13.2$ (3 s.f.).
:::

::: q
A vertical mast $OT$ of height 20 m stands at $O$. Point $A$ is 15 m due east of $O$ and point $B$ is 15 m due north of $O$. Find $AT$, $AB$, and the angle of elevation of $T$ from $A$.
---
$AT = \sqrt{20^2 + 15^2} = 25$ m. $AB = \sqrt{15^2 + 15^2} = 15\sqrt2 = 21.2$ m. Elevation from $A$: $\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{20}{15}\right) = 53.1°$.
:::

::: q
A triangular prism has a horizontal rectangular base 12 cm by 5 cm and a ridge 4 cm directly above the centre line of the base, running the 12 cm length. Find the angle between a sloping face and the base.
---
Horizontal distance from the base edge to the ridge's foot is $2.5$; $\tan\theta = \dfrac{4}{2.5} \Rightarrow \theta = 58.0°$.
:::

::: watch
- **Draw the triangle flat.** Every 3D error comes from reading a length off a slanted picture.
- The angle between a line and a plane is measured to the **projection**, at the foot of the line, with the **height opposite** it.
- Two Pythagoras steps: base diagonal, then space diagonal. Or $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$ — but only for a cuboid.
- Pyramids: apex above the **centre**; to a corner is half the **diagonal**, to a side-midpoint is half the **side**. Choose the right one for the triangle you need.
- Keep surds through intermediate steps; round at the end.
- Label points before you start ($ABCD$ base, $EFGH$ top) so the triangle's vertices are unambiguous.
:::

::: test
A vertical mast is 20 m tall. From a point $S$ due south of its base the angle of elevation of the top is $30^\circ$; from a point $E$ due east of its base it is $25^\circ$. Find the distance $SE$, to 3 significant figures. (4)
---
$S$ is $\dfrac{20}{\tan 30^\circ} = 34.6$ m from the base (1); $E$ is $\dfrac{20}{\tan 25^\circ} = 42.9$ m from the base (1); $SE = \sqrt{34.64^2 + 42.89^2}$ (1) $= 55.1$ m (1).
:::
