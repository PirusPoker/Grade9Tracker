## What it is

A shape has a **line of symmetry** (mirror line) if folding along it makes the two halves match exactly. It has **rotational symmetry** of order $n$ if it looks the same $n$ times during one full turn — including the starting position. Every shape has order at least 1; "no rotational symmetry" means order 1.

The standard results:

| Shape | Lines of symmetry | Order of rotational symmetry |
|---|---|---|
| Square | 4 | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 | 2 |
| Rhombus | 2 | 2 |
| Parallelogram | 0 | 2 |
| Kite | 1 | 1 |
| Isosceles trapezium | 1 | 1 |
| Equilateral triangle | 3 | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 | 1 |
| Regular $n$-gon | $n$ | $n$ |
| Circle | infinitely many | infinite |

Symmetry questions also appear in reverse: shade squares on a grid so that a pattern has a given symmetry, or complete a shape given its mirror line.

::: method
1. **Lines of symmetry:** try folds through opposite vertices, through midpoints of opposite sides, and the diagonals. Count only the ones that actually match — a rectangle's diagonal is **not** a line of symmetry.
2. **Rotational symmetry:** imagine turning the shape about its centre; count how many positions (including the start) look identical in a full turn. A regular $n$-gon has order $n$; a shape with no repeat has order 1.
3. **Completing a pattern for a mirror line:** reflect each shaded square across the line — same distance the other side, perpendicular to the line. Diagonal mirror lines swap the row and column distances.
4. **Completing for rotational symmetry of order $n$:** rotate each shaded cell about the centre by $360°/n$ and shade where it lands; repeat.
5. **Minimum squares to shade:** find the cells whose images under the symmetry are not yet shaded; shade only those.
6. **Letters and logos:** treat them as shapes; the alphabet is a standard source (H has 2 lines and order 2; S has 0 lines and order 2; A has 1 line and order 1).
:::

::: example A regular shape
State the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry of a regular octagon.

8 lines (4 through opposite vertices, 4 through midpoints of opposite sides); order 8.
:::

::: example Parallelogram versus rhombus
Why does a parallelogram have no lines of symmetry, while a rhombus has two?

Folding a parallelogram along a diagonal does not match the halves (the acute and obtuse corners swap sides). A rhombus has equal sides, so both diagonals are mirror lines. Both have rotational symmetry of order 2 (a half turn maps each onto itself).
:::

::: example Completing a grid pattern
A $4 \times 4$ grid has cells $(1,1)$, $(2,1)$ and $(1,3)$ shaded (column, row). Shade the fewest extra cells so the pattern has the vertical line $x = 2.5$ as a line of symmetry.

Reflect each: $(1,1) \to (4,1)$, $(2,1) \to (3,1)$, $(1,3) \to (4,3)$. Three extra cells.
:::

::: example Rotational symmetry on a grid
On a $3 \times 3$ grid the corner $(1,1)$ is shaded. Shade the fewest extra cells to give rotational symmetry of order 4 about the centre.

Rotating $(1,1)$ by $90°$ about the centre gives $(3,1)$, $(3,3)$, $(1,3)$: shade the other three corners.
:::

::: example Letters
Give the symmetry of the capital letters H, N and T.

H: 2 lines, order 2. N: 0 lines, order 2. T: 1 line, order 1.
:::

::: q
How many lines of symmetry has a regular pentagon, and what is its order of rotational symmetry?
---
5 and 5.
:::

::: q
Write down the symmetry of a rectangle and of a square.
---
Rectangle: 2 lines, order 2. Square: 4 lines, order 4.
:::

::: q
Which quadrilateral has exactly one line of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 1?
---
A kite (or an isosceles trapezium).
:::

::: q
A shape has rotational symmetry of order 3 and no lines of symmetry. Sketch or describe one.
---
A three-bladed "propeller" / triskelion shape; the three arms are identical but hooked the same way, so no mirror line.
:::

::: q
A $4 \times 4$ grid has cells $(1,1)$ and $(2,3)$ shaded. Shade the fewest extra cells so that the diagonal from $(1,1)$ to $(4,4)$ is a line of symmetry.
---
Reflecting in that diagonal swaps coordinates: $(1,1) \to (1,1)$ (on the line, stays), $(2,3) \to (3,2)$. One extra cell.
:::

::: q
A $4 \times 4$ grid has cell $(1,2)$ shaded. Shade the fewest extra cells to give rotational symmetry of order 2 about the centre.
---
A half turn about the centre sends $(1,2)$ to $(4,3)$. One extra cell.
:::

::: q
Which capital letters have rotational symmetry of order 2 but no line of symmetry?
---
N, S, Z.
:::

::: q
A regular hexagon has one line of symmetry drawn through two opposite vertices. How many more lines of symmetry does it have, and how many of those pass through vertices?
---
5 more; 2 of them through vertices (3 in total through vertices, 3 through midpoints of sides).
:::

::: watch
- Order of rotational symmetry counts the **starting position** — the minimum is 1, never 0.
- A rectangle's **diagonals are not** lines of symmetry; only the lines through the midpoints of opposite sides are.
- A parallelogram has **no** lines of symmetry but order 2 — the shape everyone gets wrong.
- On a grid, reflect **cell by cell** and measure the distance to the mirror line; diagonal mirrors swap the two distances.
- "Fewest extra cells" — shade only the images that are missing; extra shading breaks the symmetry.
- Regular $n$-gon: $n$ lines and order $n$. Irregular shapes must be checked, not assumed.
:::
