## What it is

A **surd** is a root you cannot write as a whole number or fraction — $\sqrt2$, $\sqrt3$, $\sqrt{50}$ — kept as a root so the answer is **exact**. $\sqrt2 = 1.414\ldots$ never ends; $\sqrt2$ itself is exact.

Everything rests on one rule, which works for multiplying and dividing but **not** for adding:

$$\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab} \qquad \frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b} = \sqrt{\frac ab} \qquad \text{but } \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b} \ne \sqrt{a + b}$$

Two skills: **simplify** a surd by pulling out the biggest square factor ($\sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = 5\sqrt2$), and **rationalise** a fraction so there is no surd on the bottom. The second is what "give your answer in the form $a + b\sqrt2$" is really asking for.

::: terms
- **Surd**: a root that cannot be simplified to a whole number or fraction, such as √2; it is irrational.
- **Simplifying a surd**: take out square factors: √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2.
- **Multiplying surds**: √a × √b = √(ab), and √a × √a = a.
- **Rationalising the denominator**: removing a surd from the bottom of a fraction by multiplying top and bottom by a suitable surd.
- **Conjugate**: for a + √b, the conjugate is a − √b; their product (a² − b) has no surd.
:::

::: method
1. **Simplify:** find the largest square number dividing the surd. $\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = 6\sqrt2$. Miss the largest? Simplify again.
2. **Collect like surds** as if they were letters: $3\sqrt5 + 2\sqrt5 = 5\sqrt5$. Simplify first so like surds appear: $\sqrt{20} + \sqrt{45} = 2\sqrt5 + 3\sqrt5 = 5\sqrt5$.
3. **Multiply:** numbers with numbers, surds with surds, then simplify. $3\sqrt2 \times 4\sqrt6 = 12\sqrt{12} = 24\sqrt3$. Brackets expand exactly like algebra (FOIL); remember $\sqrt a \times \sqrt a = a$.
4. **Rationalise $\dfrac{k}{\sqrt a}$:** multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt a$. $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt3} = \dfrac{6\sqrt3}{3} = 2\sqrt3$.
5. **Rationalise $\dfrac{k}{a + \sqrt b}$:** multiply top and bottom by the **conjugate** $a - \sqrt b$ (flip the middle sign). The bottom becomes $a^2 - b$ — no surd.
6. **Exact answers in geometry:** keep $\sqrt{\ }$ in Pythagoras and trig results; simplify at the end.
:::

::: example Simplifying and collecting
Simplify $\sqrt{48} + \sqrt{27} - \sqrt{12}$.

$4\sqrt3 + 3\sqrt3 - 2\sqrt3 = 5\sqrt3$.
:::

::: example Multiplying and expanding
Expand and simplify $(2 + \sqrt3)(4 - \sqrt3)$ and $(\sqrt5 + 2)^2$.

$(2 + \sqrt3)(4 - \sqrt3) = 8 - 2\sqrt3 + 4\sqrt3 - 3 = 5 + 2\sqrt3$.

$(\sqrt5 + 2)^2 = 5 + 4\sqrt5 + 4 = 9 + 4\sqrt5$.
:::

::: example Rationalising a single surd
Write $\dfrac{10}{\sqrt5}$ and $\dfrac{4\sqrt2}{\sqrt6}$ in simplest surd form.

$\dfrac{10}{\sqrt5} = \dfrac{10\sqrt5}{5} = 2\sqrt5$.

$\dfrac{4\sqrt2}{\sqrt6} = \dfrac{4\sqrt{12}}{6} = \dfrac{4 \times 2\sqrt3}{6} = \dfrac{4\sqrt3}{3}$.
:::

::: example Rationalising with a conjugate
Write $\dfrac{6}{2 - \sqrt2}$ in the form $a + b\sqrt2$.

$\dfrac{6(2 + \sqrt2)}{(2 - \sqrt2)(2 + \sqrt2)} = \dfrac{12 + 6\sqrt2}{4 - 2} = 6 + 3\sqrt2$.
:::

::: example A surd in Pythagoras
A right-angled triangle has shorter sides $\sqrt{12}$ cm and $\sqrt{15}$ cm. Find the exact length of the hypotenuse.

$h^2 = 12 + 15 = 27$, so $h = \sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt3$ cm.
:::

::: example Area with surds
A rectangle has sides $(3 + \sqrt5)$ cm and $(3 - \sqrt5)$ cm. Show that its area is an integer, and find its perimeter.

Area $= (3 + \sqrt5)(3 - \sqrt5) = 9 - 5 = 4$ cm$^2$ — an integer.

Perimeter $= 2(3 + \sqrt5 + 3 - \sqrt5) = 12$ cm.
:::

:::

Expand and simplify $(2\sqrt3 - \sqrt5)^2$.
---
$12 - 4\sqrt{15} + 5 = 17 - 4\sqrt{15}$.
:::

::: q
Rationalise the denominator of $\dfrac{15}{\sqrt{10}}$.
---
$\dfrac{15\sqrt{10}}{10} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{10}}{2}$.
:::

::: q
Write $\dfrac{8}{3 + \sqrt7}$ in the form $a + b\sqrt7$.
---
$\dfrac{8(3 - \sqrt7)}{9 - 7} = 4(3 - \sqrt7) = 12 - 4\sqrt7$.
:::

::: q
Write $\dfrac{\sqrt3 + 1}{\sqrt3 - 1}$ in the form $a + b\sqrt3$.
---
$\dfrac{(\sqrt3 + 1)^2}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{4 + 2\sqrt3}{2} = 2 + \sqrt3$.
:::

::: q
A square has area $50$ cm$^2$. Find the exact length of its diagonal.
---
Side $= \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt2$; diagonal $= \sqrt{50 + 50} = \sqrt{100} = 10$ cm.
:::

::: q
Show that $\dfrac{\sqrt{18} + \sqrt{8}}{\sqrt2}$ is an integer.
---
$\dfrac{3\sqrt2 + 2\sqrt2}{\sqrt2} = \dfrac{5\sqrt2}{\sqrt2} = 5$. ∎
:::

::: q
Solve $x\sqrt2 = \sqrt{32} + 4$, giving $x$ in the form $a + b\sqrt2$.
---
$x = \dfrac{4\sqrt2 + 4}{\sqrt2} = 4 + \dfrac{4}{\sqrt2} = 4 + 2\sqrt2$.
:::

::: watch
- **$\sqrt{a + b} \ne \sqrt a + \sqrt b$.** $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, not $7$.
- Always give the **simplest** form: $\sqrt{50}$ loses the mark that $5\sqrt2$ gets. Check no square factor is left inside.
- The conjugate flips **only the middle sign**: $3 + \sqrt7 \to 3 - \sqrt7$.
- "Exact" or "in the form $a + b\sqrt c$" means no decimals anywhere.
- $\sqrt a \times \sqrt a = a$, not $\sqrt a$ and not $a^2$.
- When expanding $(\sqrt5 + 2)^2$, there **is** a middle term: $4\sqrt5$. Squaring term by term is wrong.
:::

Rationalise the denominator of $\dfrac{1 + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$, giving your answer in the form $a + b\sqrt{2}$. (3)

:::

::: test
A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse $(4 + \sqrt{3})$ cm and one shorter side $(2 + 2\sqrt{3})$ cm. Find the exact length of the third side, and the area of the triangle in the form $a + b\sqrt{3}$. (5)
---
$(4 + \sqrt{3})^2 = 19 + 8\sqrt{3}$ (1). $(2 + 2\sqrt{3})^2 = 16 + 8\sqrt{3}$ (1). Third side$^2 = 3$, so third side $= \sqrt{3}$ cm (1). Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times \sqrt{3} \times (2 + 2\sqrt{3}) = \frac{1}{2}(2\sqrt{3} + 6)$ (1) $= 3 + \sqrt{3}$ cm$^2$ (1).
:::
