## What it is

Take a graph $y = f(x)$ and change the equation slightly — the graph moves, flips or stretches in a predictable way. Those are **transformations of graphs**:

| Equation | What happens to the graph |
|---|---|
| $y = f(x) + a$ | shifts **up** by $a$ |
| $y = f(x - a)$ | shifts **right** by $a$ (the opposite of what it looks like) |
| $y = -f(x)$ | reflects in the **$x$-axis** |
| $y = f(-x)$ | reflects in the **$y$-axis** |
| $y = af(x)$ | stretches vertically by factor $a$ |
| $y = f(ax)$ | stretches horizontally by factor $\dfrac1a$ (squashes if $a > 1$) |

Rule of thumb: anything done **outside** the function acts on $y$ and does what it says; anything done **inside** the bracket acts on $x$ and does the *opposite*.

Two further graph skills: finding the **gradient of a curve** at a point by drawing a **tangent** there and measuring its slope (an estimate — the calculus in 3.4 makes it exact), and reading the **intersections** of a line and a curve as the solutions of the equation you get by setting them equal.

::: terms
- **y = f(x) + a**: a translation of the graph a units up.
- **y = f(x + a)**: a translation of the graph a units to the left.
- **y = −f(x)**: a reflection in the x-axis.
- **y = f(−x)**: a reflection in the y-axis.
- **Tangent**: a straight line that touches a curve at one point; its gradient is the gradient of the curve there.
- **Graphical solution**: the x-coordinates where two graphs intersect are the solutions of the equation formed by setting them equal.
:::

::: method
1. **Translations:** $f(x) + a$ moves every point up $a$; $f(x - a)$ moves every point right $a$. As a vector: $f(x - a) + b$ is a translation by $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$.
2. **Reflections:** $-f(x)$ flips top-to-bottom; $f(-x)$ flips left-to-right. Points on the mirror axis stay put.
3. **Stretches:** $af(x)$ multiplies every $y$ by $a$ (points on the $x$-axis stay); $f(ax)$ divides every $x$ by $a$ (points on the $y$-axis stay). $\sin 2x$ has period $180°$; $2\sin x$ has amplitude 2.
4. **Track a marked point.** If $(3, 5)$ is on $y = f(x)$, then on $y = f(x - 2) + 1$ it is at $(5, 6)$; on $y = -f(x)$ at $(3, -5)$; on $y = f(2x)$ at $(1.5, 5)$. Turning points and intercepts move the same way.
5. **Writing the transformed function:** identify the shift or stretch from the picture, then write it into $f$. Shift right 3 and up 2 → $y = f(x - 3) + 2$. Reflect in the $x$-axis → $y = -f(x)$.
6. **Gradient at a point on a curve:** draw the tangent (a straight line touching the curve there, balanced so it does not cut through), pick two points far apart on it, gradient $= \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$. In a distance–time graph that is speed; in a speed–time graph, acceleration.
7. **Intersections:** where $y = f(x)$ meets $y = g(x)$, $f(x) = g(x)$. The $x$-coordinates of the crossing points are the solutions; the number of crossings is the number of solutions.
:::

::: example Translating a marked point
The point $A(2, -3)$ lies on $y = f(x)$. Write down the coordinates of $A$ on (a) $y = f(x) + 5$, (b) $y = f(x + 4)$, (c) $y = f(x - 1) - 2$.

(a) $(2, 2)$. (b) Inside the bracket, opposite: left 4: $(-2, -3)$. (c) Right 1, down 2: $(3, -5)$.
:::

::: example Reflections and stretches
The curve $y = f(x)$ has a maximum at $(4, 6)$. Write the coordinates of the corresponding point on (a) $y = -f(x)$, (b) $y = f(-x)$, (c) $y = 3f(x)$, (d) $y = f(2x)$.

(a) $(4, -6)$ — now a minimum. (b) $(-4, 6)$. (c) $(4, 18)$. (d) $(2, 6)$.
:::

::: example Writing the equation of a transformed graph
The graph of $y = x^2$ is translated so that its vertex is at $(3, -2)$. Write its equation, and expand it.

Right 3, down 2: $y = (x - 3)^2 - 2 = x^2 - 6x + 7$.
:::

::: example Trig transformations
Sketch $y = 2\cos x$ and $y = \cos 2x$ for $0 \le x \le 360°$, stating the maximum value and period of each.

$2\cos x$: amplitude 2 (max 2, min $-2$), period $360°$ — same shape as $\cos x$, stretched vertically.

$\cos 2x$: max 1, period $180°$ — two full waves in $360°$.
:::

::: example Gradient from a tangent
On a distance–time graph, the tangent to the curve at $t = 4$ passes through $(2, 10)$ and $(6, 34)$. Estimate the speed at $t = 4$.

Gradient $= \dfrac{34 - 10}{6 - 2} = 6$ m/s.
:::

::: example Intersections as solutions
The graphs of $y = x^2 - 3$ and $y = 2x$ are drawn. Explain how to use them to solve $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$, and give the solutions.

$x^2 - 3 = 2x$ rearranges to $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$, so the solutions are the $x$-coordinates where the curve meets the line. $(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0$: they cross at $x = -1$ and $x = 3$.
:::

::: q
$(5, 2)$ lies on $y = f(x)$. Where is it on $y = f(x) - 4$, $y = f(x - 3)$ and $y = f(x + 2) + 1$?
---
$(5, -2)$; $(8, 2)$; $(3, 3)$.
:::

::: q
The minimum of $y = f(x)$ is at $(-1, -5)$. Give the turning point of $y = -f(x)$ and of $y = f(-x)$, and say whether each is a maximum or minimum.
---
$-f(x)$: $(-1, 5)$, maximum. $f(-x)$: $(1, -5)$, minimum.
:::

::: q
Describe the transformation that maps $y = x^2$ to $y = (x + 2)^2$, and to $y = x^2 + 2$.
---
Translation 2 to the **left**; translation 2 **up**.
:::

::: q
Describe the transformation from $y = \sin x$ to $y = \sin(x - 30°)$, and to $y = \sin 3x$.
---
Translation $30°$ to the right; horizontal stretch by factor $\frac13$ (period becomes $120°$).
:::

::: q
The graph of $y = x^3$ is reflected in the $x$-axis and then translated 2 up. Write the new equation.
---
$y = -x^3 + 2$.
:::

::: q
Write the equation of the graph obtained by translating $y = 2^x$ by $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$.
---
$y = 2^{x - 1} - 3$.
:::

::: q
$y = f(x)$ crosses the $x$-axis at $x = -2$ and $x = 4$. Where does $y = f(2x)$ cross it? And $y = 3f(x)$?
---
$f(2x)$: $x = -1$ and $2$. $3f(x)$: unchanged, $-2$ and $4$.
:::

::: q
The tangent to a curve at $x = 3$ passes through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 14)$. Estimate the gradient of the curve at $x = 3$.
---
$\dfrac{12}{4} = 3$.
:::

::: q
The graphs of $y = x^2 + 1$ and $y = 4x - 2$ are drawn. Which equation do their intersections solve, and how many solutions does it have?
---
$x^2 + 1 = 4x - 2 \Rightarrow x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$; $(x - 1)(x - 3) = 0$: two intersections, $x = 1$ and $x = 3$.
:::

::: q
$y = f(x)$ has a maximum at $(2, 7)$. Find the maximum of $y = 2f(x - 1) + 3$.
---
Right 1: $(3, 7)$; stretch $\times 2$: $(3, 14)$; up 3: $(3, 17)$.
:::

::: watch
- **Inside the bracket is backwards.** $f(x - 3)$ moves **right**; $f(x + 3)$ moves **left**; $f(2x)$ **squashes**.
- Outside is straightforward: $+a$ up, $-f$ flips vertically, $af$ stretches vertically.
- A reflection in the $x$-axis turns a maximum into a minimum; write the new coordinates *and* say which it is if asked.
- Describe a transformation fully: "translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$", not "moves along a bit".
- A tangent **touches**; if your line crosses the curve at the point you are measuring, it is not the tangent. Use two points far apart on it to reduce the error.
- Intersections give **$x$-values** as solutions; if asked for the points, give $y$ too.
:::
