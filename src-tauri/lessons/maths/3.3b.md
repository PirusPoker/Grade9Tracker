## What it is

Beyond straight lines, you must plot and recognise five families of curve, and use their shapes to answer questions without a calculator.

- **Quadratic** $y = ax^2 + bx + c$: a parabola — a U if $a > 0$, an upside-down U if $a < 0$. Symmetric about a vertical line through its turning point.
- **Cubic** $y = ax^3 + \dots$: an S-shape. $+x^3$ goes from bottom-left to top-right; $-x^3$ from top-left to bottom-right. Up to two turning points.
- **Reciprocal** $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$: two separate branches (in opposite quadrants), never touching the axes. The axes are **asymptotes** — lines the curve approaches but never reaches. $y = \dfrac{k}{x^2}$ has both branches above the axis.
- **Trigonometric** $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$: waves between $-1$ and $1$, repeating every $360°$. $\sin$ starts at 0, $\cos$ starts at 1. $y = \tan x$ repeats every $180°$ with vertical asymptotes at $90°$, $270°$, ….
- **Exponential** $y = a^x$ ($a > 1$): grows ever faster, passes through $(0, 1)$, never below the $x$-axis.

Plotting is a table of values, plotted and joined with a **smooth curve** (never straight segments). Recognising is matching an equation to a sketch — or a sketch to an equation — from the shape and the key points.

::: terms
- **Linear graph**: a straight line, from an equation like y = mx + c.
- **Quadratic graph**: a U-shaped (or ∩-shaped) curve called a parabola, from y = ax² + bx + c.
- **Cubic graph**: an S-shaped curve from an equation with x³.
- **Reciprocal graph**: y = k/x — two curves in opposite quadrants, never touching the axes.
- **Asymptote**: a line a curve gets closer and closer to but never meets.
- **Trigonometric graphs**: y = sin x and y = cos x repeat every 360° and lie between −1 and 1; y = tan x repeats every 180°.
:::

::: method
1. **Table of values:** choose the $x$ values the question gives; work each $y$ carefully (negatives in brackets); if a value looks out of line with its neighbours, recheck it before plotting.
2. **Plot** with small crosses; **join with a smooth curve**, drawing through each point in one movement. Do not join the two branches of a reciprocal graph.
3. **Use the graph:** to solve $f(x) = k$, draw the line $y = k$ and read the $x$ values where it meets the curve; to find where $y$ is at its least, read the lowest point.
4. **Recognise a quadratic:** $y$-intercept is $c$; roots where it factorises; turning point midway between the roots.
5. **Recognise a cubic:** the sign of the $x^3$ term fixes the ends; roots are where the factors are zero; a squared factor touches the axis.
6. **Trig graphs:** know the key points — $\sin$: $(0,0), (90,1), (180,0), (270,-1), (360,0)$; $\cos$: $(0,1), (90,0), (180,-1), (270,0), (360,1)$. Use symmetry to find the second solution of $\sin x = k$: $180° - x$; for $\cos x = k$: $360° - x$.
7. **Matching sketches to equations:** eliminate by shape (line / U / S / branches / wave), then by intercept and sign.
:::

::: example Plotting a quadratic
Draw $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$ for $-2 \le x \le 4$, and use it to solve $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$ and to find the minimum value of $y$.

Table: $x = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ gives $y = 5, 0, -3, -4, -3, 0, 5$.

Curve crosses the $x$-axis at $x = -1$ and $x = 3$: those are the solutions. Minimum $y = -4$ at $x = 1$.
:::

::: example Plotting a cubic
Complete a table for $y = x^3 - 4x$ from $x = -3$ to $3$ and sketch it.

$x = -3$: $-27 + 12 = -15$; $-2$: $0$; $-1$: $3$; $0$: $0$; $1$: $-3$; $2$: $0$; $3$: $15$.

Passes through $(-2, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$; up on the right. An S through the origin with a hump at $(-1, 3)$ and a dip at $(1, -3)$.
:::

::: example A reciprocal graph
Sketch $y = \dfrac{6}{x}$, and say what happens as $x$ gets very large, and as $x$ gets close to 0.

Points: $(1, 6), (2, 3), (3, 2), (6, 1)$ and their negatives $(-1, -6), \dots$. Two branches, top-right and bottom-left.

As $x \to \infty$, $y \to 0$ (the curve hugs the $x$-axis). As $x \to 0^+$, $y \to \infty$ (hugs the $y$-axis). The axes are asymptotes; $x = 0$ is not allowed.
:::

::: example Reading a trig graph
Use the graph of $y = \sin x$ to find all solutions of $\sin x = 0.5$ for $0 \le x \le 360°$.

$\sin 30° = 0.5$. By the symmetry of the wave about $90°$, the other solution is $180° - 30° = 150°$. Solutions: $30°, 150°$.
:::

::: example Matching graphs to equations
Four sketches: (A) a U through $(0, -4)$; (B) an S rising to the right through the origin; (C) two branches in the first and third quadrants; (D) a curve through $(0, 1)$ rising steeply to the right and flattening to the left. Match them to $y = 2^x$, $y = x^2 - 4$, $y = x^3 + x$, $y = \dfrac{3}{x}$.

A: $y = x^2 - 4$ (parabola, intercept $-4$). B: $y = x^3 + x$ (cubic through origin). C: $y = \dfrac3x$ (reciprocal, positive $k$). D: $y = 2^x$ (exponential through $(0, 1)$).
:::

::: example Solving an equation from a drawn curve
The graph of $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$ is drawn. Use it to solve $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 2$ and $x^2 - 2x - 5 = 0$.

First: draw $y = 2$; read the crossings: $x \approx -1.4$ and $x \approx 3.4$.

Second: $x^2 - 2x - 5 = 0$ is $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 2$ — the same line, the same readings. (Exact values $1 \pm \sqrt6 = -1.45, 3.45$.)
:::

::: q
Complete the table for $y = x^2 + x - 6$ for $x = -4$ to $3$, and write down the roots.
---
$6, 0, -4, -6, -6, -4, 0, 6$. Roots $x = -3$ and $x = 2$.
:::

::: q
Write down the coordinates of the turning point of $y = x^2 + x - 6$ using symmetry.
---
Midway between the roots: $x = -\frac12$; $y = \frac14 - \frac12 - 6 = -6.25$. $\left(-\frac12, -6.25\right)$.
:::

::: q
Complete a table for $y = x^3 - 3x + 1$ for $x = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2$.
---
$-1, 3, 1, -1, 3$.
:::

::: q
Sketch $y = -x^3$. Which way do its ends go?
---
From top-left to bottom-right, passing through the origin (a reflected S).
:::

::: q
Sketch $y = -\dfrac{4}{x}$. In which quadrants are its branches?
---
Second (top-left) and fourth (bottom-right); asymptotes are the axes.
:::

::: q
Use the graph of $y = \cos x$ to solve $\cos x = -0.5$ for $0 \le x \le 360°$.
---
$\cos 60° = 0.5$; $\cos$ is $-0.5$ at $180° - 60° = 120°$ and $180° + 60° = 240°$.
:::

::: q
Solve $\sin x = -0.8$ for $0 \le x \le 360°$, using the symmetry of the sine graph.
---
$\sin^{-1}(0.8) = 53.1°$. Negative values are in the second half: $180° + 53.1° = 233.1°$ and $360° - 53.1° = 306.9°$.
:::

::: q
What are the maximum and minimum values of $y = 3\sin x$, and the period of $y = \tan x$?
---
$3$ and $-3$; period $180°$.
:::

::: q
Which of these equations gives a graph that never goes below the $x$-axis: $y = x^2 - 1$, $y = 3^x$, $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$, $y = x^3$?
---
$y = 3^x$ only (always positive). ($x^2 - 1$ is negative between $-1$ and $1$.)
:::

::: q
The graph of $y = x^2 - 4x$ is drawn. What line would you add to solve $x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$, and what are the approximate solutions?
---
$x^2 - 4x = -1$: draw $y = -1$. Solutions $x \approx 0.3$ and $x \approx 3.7$ (exact $2 \pm \sqrt3$).
:::

::: watch
- **Smooth curves, not straight lines** between points — and a parabola has a rounded bottom, not a point.
- Check any table value that breaks the pattern: with $x^2$, $(-3)^2 = 9$, not $-9$.
- A reciprocal graph is **two** pieces. Never draw a line joining them through the origin.
- $\sin x = k$ has **two** solutions in a full turn ($x$ and $180° - x$); $\cos x = k$ has $x$ and $360° - x$. Read both off the graph.
- Solving from a drawn graph: rearrange so the drawn curve is on one side, then draw the **line** on the other side. Accuracy is to the grid — say "approximately".
- $y = x^2 - 4$ and $y = (x - 4)^2$ are very different graphs: one shifts down 4, the other shifts right 4.
:::

::: test
Describe the shape of the graph of $y = x^3 - 4x$ and state where it crosses the $x$-axis. (2)
---
A cubic: rises, falls, rises again (an S-shape through the origin) (1). $x(x - 2)(x + 2) = 0$: crosses at $x = -2, 0, 2$ (1).
:::

::: test
Write down the equations of the two asymptotes of $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$. (1)
---
$x = 0$ and $y = 0$ (the axes).
:::

::: test
Write down the coordinates of the maximum and minimum points of $y = \sin x$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$. (2)
---
Maximum $(90^\circ, 1)$ (1); minimum $(270^\circ, -1)$ (1).
:::

::: test
Where does the graph of $y = \cos x$ cross the $x$-axis for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$? (1)
---
At $x = 90^\circ$ and $x = 270^\circ$.
:::

::: test
Solve $\sin x = 0.5$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$. (2)
---
$x = 30^\circ$ (1) and, by the symmetry of the sine graph, $x = 180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
Solve $\cos x = -0.6$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$, to 1 decimal place. (2)
---
$\cos^{-1}(-0.6) = 126.9^\circ$ (1); the second solution is $360^\circ - 126.9^\circ = 233.1^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: test
Complete the table of values for $y = x^3 - 3x^2$ at $x = -1, 0, 1, 2, 3$. (2)
---
$-4, 0, -2, -4, 0$ (2; one mark for at least three correct).
:::

::: test
Explain why $y = \dfrac{2}{x}$ has no value at $x = 0$, and describe what happens to $y$ as $x$ becomes very large. (2)
---
Division by zero is undefined, so the graph has a break (an asymptote) at $x = 0$ (1). As $x \to \infty$, $y \to 0$ from above — the curve approaches the $x$-axis without touching it (1).
:::
