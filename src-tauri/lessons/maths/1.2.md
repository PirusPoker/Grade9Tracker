## What it is

A fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is $a$ parts out of $b$ equal parts — and also the division $a \div b$. Two fractions are **equivalent** if one is the other scaled top and bottom by the same number: $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{12}$.

A **mixed number** like $2\frac{3}{5}$ is $2 + \frac35$; as an **improper fraction** it is $\frac{13}{5}$. Convert to improper before multiplying or dividing; convert back to mixed for the final answer if the question used mixed numbers.

The four rules:

- **Add / subtract:** same denominator first, then add the tops. $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{8}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$.
- **Multiply:** tops times tops, bottoms times bottoms (cancel first if you can). $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{18}{30} = \frac{3}{5}$.
- **Divide:** flip the second fraction and multiply. $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{5} = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$.

$\dfrac{1}{b}$ is the **multiplicative inverse** (reciprocal) of $b$: $b \times \frac1b = 1$. That is *why* dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal.

You will be asked to do these **without a calculator** on Paper 1-style questions, so the mechanics must be automatic — but on 4MA1 both papers allow a calculator, so the fraction button is your check, not your method.

::: method
1. **Simplify** by dividing top and bottom by their HCF. $\frac{18}{30} \to \frac{3}{5}$.
2. **Mixed ↔ improper:** $2\frac35 = \frac{2 \times 5 + 3}{5} = \frac{13}{5}$; $\frac{17}{4} = 4$ remainder $1$, so $4\frac14$.
3. **Add / subtract mixed numbers:** either convert both to improper, or deal with the whole numbers and fractions separately (borrow one when the fraction part goes negative).
4. **Multiply / divide mixed numbers:** *always* convert to improper first. Cancel diagonally before multiplying to keep numbers small.
5. **Fraction of a quantity:** divide by the bottom, multiply by the top. $\frac{3}{8}$ of 56 $= 56 \div 8 \times 3 = 21$.
6. **One number as a fraction of another:** $\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}$, then simplify. Same units first.
7. **Ordering:** common denominator, or convert each to a decimal.
8. **Show your working** on non-calculator fraction questions: the common denominator line and the improper-fraction line are where the method marks are.
:::

::: example Adding mixed numbers
Work out $3\frac{2}{5} + 1\frac{3}{4}$.

Whole numbers: $3 + 1 = 4$. Fractions: $\frac25 + \frac34 = \frac{8}{20} + \frac{15}{20} = \frac{23}{20} = 1\frac{3}{20}$.

Total: $4 + 1\frac{3}{20} = 5\frac{3}{20}$.
:::

::: example Subtracting with borrowing
Work out $5\frac{1}{6} - 2\frac{2}{3}$.

Improper: $\frac{31}{6} - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{31}{6} - \frac{16}{6} = \frac{15}{6} = \frac{5}{2} = 2\frac12$.
:::

::: example Multiplying mixed numbers
Work out $2\frac{1}{4} \times 1\frac{3}{5}$.

$\frac{9}{4} \times \frac{8}{5}$. Cancel the 4 and the 8: $\frac{9}{1} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{18}{5} = 3\frac35$.
:::

::: example Dividing
Work out $3\frac{1}{2} \div 1\frac{1}{6}$.

$\frac{7}{2} \div \frac{7}{6} = \frac{7}{2} \times \frac{6}{7} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$.
:::

::: example Fraction of an amount, and the reverse
(a) Find $\frac{5}{8}$ of £240. (b) $\frac{3}{7}$ of a number is 45. Find the number.

(a) $240 \div 8 = 30$, $30 \times 5 = 150$. £150.

(b) One seventh is $45 \div 3 = 15$; the number is $15 \times 7 = 105$.
:::

::: example Ordering
Put $\frac{5}{8}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $0.64$ and $\frac{13}{20}$ in order, smallest first.

As decimals: $\frac58 = 0.625$, $\frac23 = 0.666\ldots$, $0.64$, $\frac{13}{20} = 0.65$.

Order: $\frac58, \ 0.64, \ \frac{13}{20}, \ \frac23$.
:::

Work out $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{5}{12}$.
---
$\frac{9}{24} + \frac{10}{24} = \frac{19}{24}$.

::: q
Work out $4\frac{2}{3} - 1\frac{5}{6}$.
---
$\frac{14}{3} - \frac{11}{6} = \frac{28}{6} - \frac{11}{6} = \frac{17}{6} = 2\frac56$.
:::

::: q
Work out $1\frac{3}{4} \times 2\frac{2}{7}$.
---
$\frac74 \times \frac{16}{7} = \frac{16}{4} = 4$.
:::

::: q
Work out $2\frac{2}{5} \div \frac{9}{10}$.
---
$\frac{12}{5} \times \frac{10}{9} = \frac{120}{45} = \frac{8}{3} = 2\frac23$.
:::

::: q
Find $\frac{7}{12}$ of 156.
---
$156 \div 12 = 13$; $13 \times 7 = 91$.
:::

::: q
In a class of 30, 18 are girls. Write the number of boys as a fraction of the class, in its simplest form.
---
$\frac{12}{30} = \frac{2}{5}$.
:::

::: q
$\frac{4}{9}$ of the people at a concert are under 18. There are 1240 people under 18. How many people are at the concert?
---
One ninth is $1240 \div 4 = 310$; total $310 \times 9 = 2790$.
:::

::: q
Write $\frac{7}{8}$, $\frac{5}{6}$, $\frac{11}{12}$ and $\frac{17}{24}$ in ascending order.
---
Over 24: $\frac{21}{24}, \frac{20}{24}, \frac{22}{24}, \frac{17}{24}$. Order: $\frac{17}{24}, \frac56, \frac78, \frac{11}{12}$.
:::

::: q
A tank is $\frac{2}{5}$ full. After 42 litres are added it is $\frac{3}{4}$ full. How much does the tank hold?
---
$\frac34 - \frac25 = \frac{15}{20} - \frac{8}{20} = \frac{7}{20}$ of the tank is 42 litres, so $\frac{1}{20}$ is 6 litres and the tank holds $6 \times 20 = 120$ litres.
:::

::: q
Work out $\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 \div \frac{4}{15}$, giving your answer as a mixed number.
---
$\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{15}{4} = \frac{15}{9} = \frac{5}{3} = 1\frac23$.
:::

::: watch
- **Convert mixed numbers to improper before multiplying or dividing.** $2\frac14 \times 1\frac35$ is not $2 \times 1 + \frac14 \times \frac35$.
- To divide, flip the **second** fraction only.
- Adding needs a **common denominator**; multiplying does not. Don't cross-multiply when adding.
- Give the final answer in its **simplest form**, and as a mixed number if the question used them.
- Write the working. On a calculator paper the answer alone may score, but the method line rescues you if you press a wrong key.
- "Of" means multiply: $\frac35$ of $40 = \frac35 \times 40$.
:::

Work out $2\frac{2}{3} + 1\frac{4}{5}$. Give the answer as a mixed number. (2)
---
$\frac{8}{3} + \frac{9}{5} = \frac{40}{15} + \frac{27}{15} = \frac{67}{15}$ (1) $= 4\frac{7}{15}$ (1).
:::

::: test
Work out $3\frac{1}{4} - 1\frac{5}{8}$. (2)
---
$\frac{13}{4} - \frac{13}{8} = \frac{26}{8} - \frac{13}{8}$ (1) $= \frac{13}{8} = 1\frac{5}{8}$ (1).
:::

::: test
Work out $2\frac{2}{5} \times 1\frac{2}{3}$. (2)
---
$\frac{12}{5} \times \frac{5}{3}$ (1) $= \frac{60}{15} = 4$ (1).
:::

::: test
Work out $4\frac{1}{2} \div 2\frac{1}{4}$. (2)
---
$\frac{9}{2} \div \frac{9}{4} = \frac{9}{2} \times \frac{4}{9}$ (1) $= 2$ (1).
:::

::: test
Write these in order, smallest first: $\frac{5}{8}$, 0.6, $\frac{13}{20}$, 62%. (2)
---
As decimals: 0.625, 0.6, 0.65, 0.62 (1). Order: 0.6, 62%, $\frac{5}{8}$, $\frac{13}{20}$ (1).
:::

::: test
In a class, $\frac{3}{8}$ of the students walk to school and $\frac{1}{3}$ cycle. The remaining 14 students take the bus. How many students are in the class? (3)
---
Fraction on the bus $= 1 - \frac{3}{8} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{24 - 9 - 8}{24} = \frac{7}{24}$ (2). $\frac{7}{24}$ of the class is 14, so the class is $14 \div 7 \times 24 = 48$ (1).
:::

::: test
Find the fraction exactly halfway between $\frac{2}{5}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$. (2)
---
$\frac{2}{5} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{8}{20} + \frac{15}{20} = \frac{23}{20}$ (1); halfway is half the sum: $\frac{23}{40}$ (1).
:::

::: test
A tank is $\frac{2}{3}$ full. After 30 litres are removed it is $\frac{5}{12}$ full. Find the capacity of the tank. (3)
---
$\frac{2}{3} - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{8}{12} - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$ (2). $\frac{1}{4}$ of the tank is 30 litres, so the capacity is 120 litres (1).
:::
