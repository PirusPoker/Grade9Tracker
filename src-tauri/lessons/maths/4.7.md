## What it is

In a "give a reason" question the marks are split: some for the angle, some for the **reason** — a standard geometrical statement, written in the standard words. The reasoning marks are lost far more often than the angles.

The bank of accepted reasons (learn the wording):

- Angles on a straight line add to $180°$. Angles at a point add to $360°$. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- Alternate angles are equal. Corresponding angles are equal. Co-interior (allied) angles add to $180°$.
- Angles in a triangle add to $180°$. Exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the interior opposite angles. Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
- Angles in a quadrilateral add to $360°$. Interior angles of a polygon add to $(n - 2) \times 180°$. Exterior angles of a polygon add to $360°$.
- Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180°$.
- Angle in a semicircle is $90°$. Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. Angles in the same segment are equal. Tangent is perpendicular to the radius. Tangents from a point are equal. Alternate segment theorem.
- Congruent triangles: SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS. Similar triangles: corresponding angles equal (AAA).

**Proof** questions ask you to show something is *always* true: two angles are equal, a line bisects another, a quadrilateral is a parallelogram. The method is the same chain of reasons, ending with a sentence that states the conclusion.

::: terms
- **Geometric proof**: a chain of statements, each justified by a known fact, that shows a result must be true.
- **Reason**: the standard fact that justifies a step, e.g. "alternate angles are equal".
- **Given**: the information stated in the question that a proof starts from.
- **Congruence proof**: showing two triangles are congruent using SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS, then using equal sides or angles.
:::

::: method
1. **One line per step:** the angle (or the fact), then the reason in brackets. "$\angle ABC = 68°$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal)."
2. **Only use facts that are given or that you have proved** — never "it looks equal". If a line looks parallel but the question does not say so, it is not.
3. **Name angles with three letters** when there could be confusion: $\angle ABC$ is the angle at $B$.
4. **Proof structure:** state what you are proving; mark the diagram; chain the reasons; finish with "therefore …", restating the claim.
5. **Proving congruence:** list three matching facts with their reasons, then name the case (SAS etc.). Then use "corresponding sides/angles of congruent triangles are equal" for the final step.
6. **Proving a shape is a parallelogram / rhombus etc.:** show the defining property (both pairs of opposite sides parallel or equal; all four sides equal; …) with reasons.
7. **Algebraic geometry proofs:** let an unknown angle be $x$, express others in terms of $x$, and show the required relation holds for every $x$.
:::

::: example A two-step reason chain
$AB$ is parallel to $CD$. A transversal meets $AB$ at $E$ and $CD$ at $F$. Angle $AEF = 70°$, where $A$ and $C$ are on the same side of the transversal. Find angle $EFD$ and angle $EFC$, giving a reason for each.

$\angle EFD = 70°$ (alternate angles are equal).

$\angle EFC = 180° - 70° = 110°$ (angles on a straight line add to $180°$; equivalently, co-interior angles add to $180°$).
:::

::: example A clean reason chain
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = AC$ and angle $BAC = 40°$. $BC$ is extended to $D$. Find angle $ACD$, giving reasons for each step.

$\angle ABC = \angle ACB$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal).

$\angle ACB = (180° - 40°) \div 2 = 70°$ (angles in a triangle add to $180°$).

$\angle ACD = 180° - 70° = 110°$ (angles on a straight line add to $180°$).
:::

::: example Proving two angles are equal
$O$ is the centre of a circle; $A$, $B$, $C$ lie on the circle. Prove that angle $OAB +$ angle $OCB =$ angle $ABC$.

$OA = OB$ (radii), so $\angle OAB = \angle OBA$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle). $OB = OC$, so $\angle OCB = \angle OBC$.

$\angle ABC = \angle OBA + \angle OBC = \angle OAB + \angle OCB$. ∎
:::

::: example Proving congruence, then using it
$ABCD$ is a kite with $AB = AD$ and $CB = CD$. Prove that $AC$ bisects angle $BAD$.

In triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$: $AB = AD$ (given), $CB = CD$ (given), $AC$ is common. So the triangles are congruent (SSS).

Hence $\angle BAC = \angle DAC$ (corresponding angles of congruent triangles), i.e. $AC$ bisects angle $BAD$. ∎
:::

::: example An algebraic proof
$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral. $AB$ is extended to $E$. Prove that the exterior angle $CBE$ equals the interior opposite angle $ADC$.

Let $\angle ADC = x$. Then $\angle ABC = 180° - x$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180°$). $\angle CBE = 180° - (180° - x) = x$ (angles on a straight line add to $180°$). So $\angle CBE = \angle ADC$. ∎
:::

::: q
$PQ$ and $RS$ are parallel; a transversal makes an angle of $63°$ at $P$ (inside the parallels, on the left). State, with reasons, the angle at $R$ on the left inside the parallels, and the one on the right.
---
Left (co-interior): $117°$ — co-interior angles add to $180°$. Right (alternate): $63°$ — alternate angles are equal.
:::

::: q
In triangle $XYZ$, $XY = XZ$ and angle $YXZ = 50°$. Find angle $XYZ$ with a reason.
---
$65°$ — base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal, and angles in a triangle add to $180°$.
:::

::: q
$ABCD$ is a parallelogram with angle $DAB = 72°$. Find angle $BCD$ and angle $ABC$, with reasons.
---
$BCD = 72°$ (opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal); $ABC = 108°$ (co-interior angles between the parallel sides add to $180°$).
:::

::: q
Prove that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
---
Let $AB = AC$ and let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Triangles $ABM$ and $ACM$: $AB = AC$, $BM = CM$, $AM$ common — congruent (SSS). So $\angle ABM = \angle ACM$. ∎
:::

::: q
$AB$ is a diameter of a circle and $C$ is on the circle. Prove that angle $ACB = 90°$ using isosceles triangles.
---
Let $O$ be the centre; $OA = OB = OC$. Let $\angle OAC = x$, $\angle OBC = y$; then $\angle OCA = x$ and $\angle OCB = y$ (isosceles). Triangle $ABC$: $2x + 2y = 180$, so $x + y = 90$, i.e. $\angle ACB = 90°$. ∎
:::

::: q
Two tangents from $P$ touch a circle centre $O$ at $A$ and $B$. Prove that $OP$ bisects angle $APB$.
---
Triangles $OAP$, $OBP$: $OA = OB$ (radii), $OP$ common, $\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90°$ (tangent perpendicular to radius) — congruent (RHS). So $\angle APO = \angle BPO$. ∎
:::

::: q
Prove that the exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
---
Let the interior angles be $a, b, c$ with the exterior angle $e$ next to $c$. $a + b + c = 180$ (angles in a triangle) and $c + e = 180$ (straight line). Subtract: $e = a + b$. ∎
:::

::: q
$ABCD$ is a quadrilateral with $AB = CD$ and $AB$ parallel to $CD$. Prove it is a parallelogram.
---
Draw diagonal $AC$. $\angle BAC = \angle DCA$ (alternate angles), $AB = CD$ (given), $AC$ common: triangles $ABC$, $CDA$ congruent (SAS). So $\angle BCA = \angle DAC$, which are alternate angles, hence $BC \parallel AD$. Both pairs of opposite sides parallel: a parallelogram. ∎
:::

::: q
What is wrong with this reasoning: "Angle $x = 45°$ because the triangle looks right-angled and isosceles"?
---
Nothing in the question stated a right angle or equal sides; appearance is not a reason. Only given facts and proved results may be used.
:::

::: watch
- **Standard wording, every step.** "Z angles" and "the angle rule" do not score; "alternate angles are equal" does.
- Give a reason for **each** step, not one at the end. Two angles found means two reasons.
- Never use what the diagram merely *looks* like. Parallel needs the arrows or the statement; equal needs the marks.
- Three-letter angle names: $\angle ABC$, with the vertex in the middle.
- Proofs end with a **conclusion sentence** restating what was to be shown.
- Congruence: quote the three facts *and* the case (SSS/SAS/ASA/RHS); then "corresponding angles of congruent triangles are equal" for anything you deduce.
:::

::: test
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal and one angle is $74^\circ$. Write down the size of the corresponding angle and give the reason. (1)
---
$74^\circ$, because corresponding angles on parallel lines are equal.
:::

::: test
Write the exact sentence you would give as the reason when two co-interior angles add to $180^\circ$. (1)
---
Co-interior angles between parallel lines add up to $180^\circ$ (or: are supplementary).
:::

::: test
Angle $x$ lies on a straight line next to an angle of $112^\circ$. Find $x$ and give the reason. (1)
---
$x = 68^\circ$, because angles on a straight line add up to $180^\circ$.
:::

::: test
In triangle $ABC$, angle $A = 50^\circ$ and angle $B = 60^\circ$. A line through $C$ is parallel to $AB$. Find the angle between $CA$ and this line, giving reasons. (2)
---
$50^\circ$ (1), because it is alternate to angle $A$ and alternate angles on parallel lines are equal (1).
:::

::: test
$AB$ is parallel to $CD$, and $AC$ is a straight line joining them. Angle $BAC = 3x + 10$ and angle $ACD = 5x - 30$. Find $x$, giving the reason. (2)
---
The angles are alternate, so equal: $3x + 10 = 5x - 30$ (1); $x = 20$ (1).
:::

::: test
Three angles of a quadrilateral are $85^\circ$, $95^\circ$ and $100^\circ$. Find the fourth angle and give the reason. (1)
---
$80^\circ$, because the angles of a quadrilateral add up to $360^\circ$.
:::

::: test
Prove that an exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the two interior opposite angles. (2)
---
Let the interior angles be $a$, $b$, $c$ with the exterior angle $e$ next to $c$. $a + b + c = 180$ (angles in a triangle) and $c + e = 180$ (angles on a straight line) (1). So $e = 180 - c = a + b$ (1).
:::

::: test
$PQRS$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle $P = 3y$ and angle $R = y + 40$. Find $y$, giving the reason in a full sentence. (2)
---
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$, so $3y + y + 40 = 180$ (1); $y = 35$ (1).
:::
