## What it is

The parts of a circle by name: **radius**, **diameter**, **circumference**, **chord** (a line joining two points on the circle), **arc**, **sector** (a pizza slice — two radii and an arc), **segment** (the region cut off by a chord), **tangent** (a line touching the circle at one point).

The **circle theorems** — each one is a reason you must quote word for word:

1. The angle in a **semicircle** is $90°$.
2. The angle at the **centre** is twice the angle at the circumference (standing on the same arc).
3. Angles in the **same segment** are equal (same arc, same side).
4. Opposite angles of a **cyclic quadrilateral** add to $180°$.
5. A **tangent** is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
6. **Two tangents** from an external point are equal in length.
7. The perpendicular from the centre to a **chord** bisects the chord.
8. **Alternate segment theorem:** the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment (the angle at the circumference on the other side of the chord).

Plus the **intersecting chords** results: if chords $AB$ and $CD$ meet at $X$ (inside the circle), $AX \cdot XB = CX \cdot XD$. If they meet outside the circle at $X$, the same product rule holds with the external lengths: $XA \cdot XB = XC \cdot XD$; and for a tangent from $X$ touching at $T$, $XT^2 = XA \cdot XB$.

::: method
1. **Mark the centre and radii.** Two radii make an isosceles triangle — that is often the hidden step.
2. **Look for the standard pictures:** a diameter → $90°$; an angle at the centre and one at the edge on the same arc → double; two angles on the same chord → equal; four points on the circle → opposite angles add to $180°$; a tangent → right angle with the radius; a tangent and a chord → alternate segment.
3. **Chain the reasons.** Each step: the angle, then the theorem in its standard wording. "Angle $ABC = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle)."
4. **Tangent kite:** two tangents from a point with the two radii make a kite with two right angles; the angle at the centre is $180° -$ the angle between the tangents.
5. **Alternate segment:** the angle between tangent and chord at the point of contact equals the angle *opposite* — the one subtended by that chord at the far side of the circle.
6. **Intersecting chords:** label the four segment lengths, write the product equation, solve (possibly a quadratic).
7. **Proofs:** draw in the radii, use the isosceles base angles, and let the angle sum in the triangle or at the centre do the work — the exam proof of theorem 2 is exactly this.
:::

::: example Angle at the centre
O is the centre. Points A, B, C are on the circle with angle $AOC = 136°$ (the reflex arc not containing B). Find angle $ABC$.

Angle at the circumference $= \dfrac{136°}{2} = 68°$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference).
:::

::: example Cyclic quadrilateral and same segment
ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle $ABC = 104°$ and angle $ACB = 33°$. Find angle $ADC$ and angle $ADB$.

$ADC = 180° - 104° = 76°$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180°$).

$ADB = ACB = 33°$ (angles in the same segment, both standing on chord AB).
:::

::: example Tangent and radius
PT is a tangent to a circle centre O, touching at T. Angle $TPO = 34°$. Find angle $TOP$.

Angle $OTP = 90°$ (tangent perpendicular to radius). $TOP = 180 - 90 - 34 = 56°$.
:::

::: example Two tangents
Tangents from P touch the circle (centre O) at A and B, and angle $APB = 48°$. Find angle $AOB$ and angle $OAB$.

$OAP = OBP = 90°$, so in quadrilateral $OAPB$, $AOB = 360 - 180 - 48 = 132°$.

$OA = OB$ (radii), so triangle $OAB$ is isosceles: $OAB = \dfrac{180 - 132}{2} = 24°$.
:::

::: example Alternate segment
A tangent at T meets chord TA. Angle between the tangent and TA is $57°$; B is a point on the circle on the far side of TA. Find angle $TBA$.

$TBA = 57°$ (alternate segment theorem).
:::

::: example Intersecting chords
Chords AB and CD cross at X inside a circle. $AX = 4$, $XB = 9$, $CX = 6$. Find $XD$.

$AX \cdot XB = CX \cdot XD \Rightarrow 36 = 6 \cdot XD \Rightarrow XD = 6$.
:::

::: example Proving the angle at the centre
Prove that the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

Let A, B, C be on the circle, centre O, and draw the diameter from B through O to a point D on the circle. In triangle $OAB$, $OA = OB$ (radii), so $OAB = OBA = x$; the exterior angle $AOD = 2x$. Similarly in triangle $OBC$, $COD = 2y$ where $OBC = y$. So $AOC = 2x + 2y = 2(x + y) = 2 \times ABC$. ∎
:::

::: q
AB is a diameter and C is on the circle. Angle $CAB = 28°$. Find angle $ABC$.
---
$ACB = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle), so $ABC = 62°$.
:::

::: q
O is the centre; angle $AOB = 110°$. Find the angle $ACB$ at a point C on the major arc, and the angle $ADB$ at a point D on the minor arc.
---
$ACB = 55°$; $ADB = 180 - 55 = 125°$ (cyclic quadrilateral ACBD).
:::

::: q
In cyclic quadrilateral PQRS, angle $P = 3x$ and angle $R = x + 40$. Find angle $P$.
---
$4x + 40 = 180 \Rightarrow x = 35$; $P = 105°$.
:::

::: q
A tangent touches the circle at T; O is the centre. A point P on the tangent has $PT = 12$ cm and $OP = 13$ cm. Find the radius.
---
Right angle at T: $r = \sqrt{169 - 144} = 5$ cm.
:::

::: q
Two tangents from P touch at A and B; angle $AOB = 100°$. Find angle $APB$.
---
$360 - 90 - 90 - 100 = 80°$.
:::

::: q
Chord AB has length 16 cm in a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the distance from the centre to the chord.
---
The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord: $\sqrt{100 - 64} = 6$ cm.
:::

::: q
A tangent at T makes an angle of $65°$ with chord TS. Find the angle TRS, where R is on the far side of TS, and the angle in the other segment (at a point Q on the same side as the $65°$).
---
$TRS = 65°$ (alternate segment); $TQS = 115°$ (cyclic quadrilateral).
:::

::: q
Chords AB and CD meet at X inside the circle with $AX = 3$, $XB = 8$, and $CX = XD$. Find $CX$.
---
$CX^2 = 24 \Rightarrow CX = 2\sqrt6 = 4.90$ cm.
:::

::: q
From an external point X, a line cuts the circle at A then B with $XA = 4$ and $AB = 5$, and a tangent from X touches at T. Find $XT$.
---
$XT^2 = XA \cdot XB = 4 \times 9 = 36$, $XT = 6$.
:::

::: q
O is the centre. A, B, C lie on the circle with angle $OAB = 35°$ and angle $OCB = 20°$. Find angle $ABC$ and hence angle $AOC$.
---
Isosceles triangles: $OBA = 35°$, $OBC = 20°$, so $ABC = 55°$; $AOC = 110°$ (angle at the centre).
:::

::: q
Prove that the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180°$.
---
Join the centre to two opposite vertices B and D. The angle at the centre on one side is $2 \times$ angle $A$, on the other side $2 \times$ angle $C$; together they make $360°$, so $2A + 2C = 360$, $A + C = 180°$. ∎
:::

::: watch
- **Quote the theorem in full**, every time: "angle in a semicircle is 90°", "opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°". Reasons are marked separately from the angles.
- Angle at the centre is **twice** the angle at the circumference — and both must stand on the **same arc**. Check which arc.
- A radius meets a tangent at **$90°$** — draw the radius to the point of contact if it is not there.
- Two radii make an **isosceles** triangle. It is the most-missed step in centre questions.
- The alternate segment angle is the one on the **far** side of the chord from the tangent angle.
- Intersecting chords: multiply the two pieces of **each** chord; don't add.
:::
