## What it is

Data is collected, put into a table, drawn as a chart, and read back off. The charts on this course:

- **Pictogram**: a symbol stands for a fixed number; half a symbol is half the number. Always read the key.
- **Bar chart**: bars of equal width, height $=$ frequency, gaps between bars for categories. A **dual bar chart** compares two groups side by side; a **composite** (stacked) bar chart stacks them.
- **Pie chart**: angle of each sector $\propto$ frequency. Angle $= \dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}} \times 360°$. Going backwards: frequency $= \dfrac{\text{angle}}{360} \times$ total.
- **Frequency table** and **tally chart**: counting. A **two-way table** counts by two categories at once (boys/girls against walk/bus/car), and the totals row and column let you fill in gaps.
- **Line graph / time series**: points joined, for something changing over time.

Interpreting means reading numbers off correctly, comparing (which is bigger, by how much, what fraction), and spotting when a chart misleads (a $y$-axis that does not start at zero, pictogram symbols of different sizes, a pie chart used to compare two groups of different sizes).

::: method
1. **Read the key and the scale first.** In a pictogram find what one symbol is worth; on a bar chart find what one small grid line is worth.
2. **Pie chart from a table:** total the frequencies; angle for each $= \dfrac{f}{\text{total}} \times 360$; check the angles add to $360°$; draw with a protractor and label each sector.
3. **Frequency from a pie chart:** measure or read the angle; frequency $= \dfrac{\text{angle}}{360} \times$ total. If you are told one sector's frequency and angle, find the total first: total $= f \times \dfrac{360}{\text{angle}}$.
4. **Two-way table:** fill in the totals row and column; every missing cell is a subtraction. Then answer the question (a probability, a fraction, "how many girls walked").
5. **Comparing two pie charts:** they show *proportions*. Different totals mean the same angle can be different numbers of people — say so.
6. **Misleading charts:** name the specific feature (truncated axis, uneven scale, 3D distortion, missing labels) and say what impression it gives.
:::

::: example Pie chart angles
Sixty students chose a sport: football 24, hockey 15, tennis 12, other 9. Find the angles for a pie chart.

Football $\dfrac{24}{60} \times 360 = 144°$; hockey $90°$; tennis $72°$; other $54°$. Check: $144 + 90 + 72 + 54 = 360$ ✓.
:::

::: example Reading a pie chart backwards
In a pie chart of 720 voters, the sector for Party A has angle $95°$. How many voted for Party A?

$\dfrac{95}{360} \times 720 = 190$.
:::

::: example Finding the total from one sector
In a pie chart, the "bus" sector has angle $72°$ and represents 18 people. How many people does the chart represent in total?

$72°$ is $\dfrac{72}{360} = \dfrac15$ of the circle, so 18 people is one fifth of the total: total $= 18 \times 5 = 90$.
:::

::: example Two-way table
100 students were asked whether they play an instrument. 45 are girls; 28 boys play an instrument; 60 students in total play an instrument. Complete the table and find how many girls do not play.

Boys $= 55$; boys not playing $= 55 - 28 = 27$; girls playing $= 60 - 28 = 32$; girls not playing $= 45 - 32 = 13$.
:::

::: example A pictogram
A pictogram uses one circle for 8 cars. Monday shows $3\frac12$ circles and Tuesday shows 2 and a quarter circles. How many more cars on Monday?

Monday $28$, Tuesday $18$: 10 more.
:::

::: example Misleading chart
A bar chart of two companies' profits has a vertical axis starting at £40m. Company X's bar is twice the height of Company Y's. Explain why the chart is misleading.

The axis does not start at zero, so the bar heights exaggerate the difference — X's profit is not twice Y's; if the bars reach £48m and £44m, the real difference is about 9%, not 100%.
:::

::: q
A class of 36 voted: red 15, blue 9, green 12. Find the pie chart angles.
---
$150°$, $90°$, $120°$.
:::

::: q
In a pie chart of 480 people, the "walk" sector is $105°$. How many walk?
---
$140$.
:::

::: q
A pie chart sector of $40°$ represents 14 people. How many people does the whole chart represent?
---
$14 \times 9 = 126$.
:::

::: q
A two-way table: 80 people; 44 adults; 30 children prefer tea; 50 people in total prefer tea (the rest coffee). How many adults prefer coffee?
---
Children $36$; adults preferring tea $50 - 30 = 20$; adults preferring coffee $44 - 20 = 24$.
:::

::: q
In a pictogram one symbol represents 20 books. A row shows 4 symbols and three-quarters of a symbol. How many books?
---
$4.75 \times 20 = 95$ books.
:::

::: q
A dual bar chart shows boys' and girls' favourite subjects. Boys: Maths 12, Science 9, Art 4. Girls: Maths 8, Science 10, Art 7. Which subject has the biggest difference between boys and girls, and what fraction of the girls chose Art?
---
Maths (difference 4); $\dfrac{7}{25}$.
:::

::: q
Two pie charts show the sports played at two schools of 300 and 900 students. Football is $120°$ at the first and $90°$ at the second. Which school has more footballers?
---
First: $100$; second: $225$. The second, despite the smaller angle.
:::

::: q
A line graph shows a shop's monthly sales. Explain what a horizontal section and a steep downward section mean.
---
Horizontal: sales stayed the same month to month. Steep downward: sales fell quickly.
:::

::: q
Give two features that could make a bar chart misleading.
---
A vertical axis not starting at zero; unequal bar widths or an uneven scale; 3D bars; missing axis labels or units.
:::

::: q
A frequency table has classes $0\text{–}10$, $10\text{–}20$, $20\text{–}30$ with frequencies 5, 12, 8. What is the modal class and what fraction of the data is $20$ or more?
---
$10\text{–}20$; $\dfrac{8}{25}$.
:::

::: watch
- **Pie chart angles must add to 360°** — check before drawing. Label sectors, not just angles.
- A pie chart shows **proportions**, not amounts: comparing two pie charts needs the totals.
- Two-way tables: fill the **totals** first; every gap is then one subtraction.
- Pictograms: read the **key**; part-symbols are proportional parts of the key value.
- Bar charts for categories have **gaps** between bars; equal widths.
- "Explain why misleading": name the feature **and** the false impression it gives.
:::
