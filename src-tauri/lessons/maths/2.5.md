## What it is

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when one is a constant multiple of the other: $y = kx$. Double $x$, $y$ doubles. The graph is a straight line through the origin. Written $y \propto x$ ("$y$ is proportional to $x$").

They are in **inverse proportion** when their product is constant: $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$. Double $x$, $y$ halves. The graph is a reciprocal curve that never touches either axis. Written $y \propto \dfrac1x$.

Proportion can involve powers and roots too: "$y$ is proportional to the square of $x$" is $y = kx^2$; "$y$ is inversely proportional to the square root of $x$" is $y = \dfrac{k}{\sqrt x}$. Physics is full of these — the area of a circle $\propto r^2$, the time for a pendulum swing $\propto \sqrt{l}$, the brightness of a light $\propto \dfrac{1}{d^2}$.

The **constant of proportionality** $k$ is found from one pair of values you are given; after that the formula answers every other question.

::: terms
- **Direct proportion**: y = kx — as x doubles, y doubles; the graph is a straight line through the origin.
- **Inverse proportion**: y = k/x — as x doubles, y halves.
- **Constant of proportionality (k)**: the fixed number linking the two quantities, found from a known pair of values.
- **Proportional symbol (∝)**: y ∝ x means y is directly proportional to x.
- **Proportional to a power or root**: y ∝ x², y ∝ x³ or y ∝ √x — write y = kx², etc.
:::

::: method
1. **Translate the words into a formula with $k$.** "Proportional to" → $y = k \times (\dots)$; "inversely proportional to" → $y = \dfrac{k}{(\dots)}$. Include the power or root exactly as stated.
2. **Substitute the given pair** of values to find $k$.
3. **Write the formula** with the number for $k$ — that line is usually a mark on its own.
4. **Use it:** substitute the new $x$ to find $y$, or the new $y$ and solve for $x$ (which may need a square root or a cube root).
5. **Recognise from a table or graph:** direct → $\dfrac yx$ constant, line through origin; inverse → $xy$ constant, reciprocal curve; $y \propto x^2$ → $\dfrac{y}{x^2}$ constant, parabola through origin.
6. **"What happens to $y$ when $x$ is tripled?"** For $y = kx^2$: $y$ becomes $k(3x)^2 = 9kx^2$, so 9 times bigger. For $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$: divided by 3.
:::

::: example Direct proportion
$y$ is directly proportional to $x$. When $x = 6$, $y = 15$. Find $y$ when $x = 14$, and $x$ when $y = 40$.

$y = kx$: $15 = 6k \Rightarrow k = 2.5$. $y = 2.5x$.

$x = 14$: $y = 35$. $y = 40$: $x = 16$.
:::

::: example Proportional to a square
The energy stored in a spring, $E$ joules, is proportional to the square of its extension $x$ cm. When $x = 4$, $E = 24$. Find $E$ when $x = 10$, and $x$ when $E = 54$.

$E = kx^2$: $24 = 16k \Rightarrow k = 1.5$. $E = 1.5x^2$.

$x = 10$: $E = 150$. $E = 54$: $x^2 = 36 \Rightarrow x = 6$.
:::

::: example Inverse proportion
The time $T$ hours to complete a job is inversely proportional to the number of workers $n$. 6 workers take 10 hours. How long do 15 workers take? How many workers are needed to finish in 4 hours?

$T = \dfrac kn$: $10 = \dfrac k6 \Rightarrow k = 60$. $T = \dfrac{60}{n}$.

$n = 15$: $T = 4$ hours. $T = 4$: $n = 15$. (For 4 hours, 15 workers.)
:::

::: example Inversely proportional to a square
The force $F$ between two magnets is inversely proportional to the square of the distance $d$ between them. When $d = 2$, $F = 45$. Find $F$ when $d = 3$, and $d$ when $F = 5$.

$F = \dfrac{k}{d^2}$: $45 = \dfrac k4 \Rightarrow k = 180$. $F = \dfrac{180}{d^2}$.

$d = 3$: $F = 20$. $F = 5$: $d^2 = 36 \Rightarrow d = 6$.
:::

::: example Square root
$y \propto \sqrt x$ and $y = 12$ when $x = 9$. Find $y$ when $x = 25$.

$y = k\sqrt x$: $12 = 3k \Rightarrow k = 4$. $y = 4\sqrt{25} = 20$.
:::

::: example Scaling without a value
$p$ is inversely proportional to the cube of $q$. What happens to $p$ when $q$ is doubled?

$p = \dfrac{k}{q^3}$. With $2q$: $\dfrac{k}{8q^3}$ — $p$ is divided by 8.
:::

::: q
$y \propto x$ and $y = 21$ when $x = 6$. Find $y$ when $x = 10$.
---
$k = 3.5$; $y = 35$.
:::

::: q
$y$ is proportional to $x^2$. $y = 50$ when $x = 5$. Find $y$ when $x = 8$ and $x$ when $y = 18$.
---
$k = 2$; $y = 128$; $x^2 = 9 \Rightarrow x = 3$.
:::

::: q
$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$. $y = 8$ when $x = 5$. Find $y$ when $x = 20$ and $x$ when $y = 2.5$.
---
$k = 40$; $y = 2$; $x = 16$.
:::

::: q
$y \propto \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ and $y = 2$ when $x = 6$. Find $y$ when $x = 4$.
---
$k = 72$; $y = \dfrac{72}{16} = 4.5$.
:::

::: q
The cost $C$ of a circular table is proportional to the square of its diameter $d$. A table of diameter 80 cm costs £96. Find the cost of a 120 cm table.
---
$k = \dfrac{96}{6400} = 0.015$; $C = 0.015 \times 14400 = \pounds 216$.
:::

::: q
The number of days $D$ that food lasts is inversely proportional to the number of people $n$. Food for 8 people lasts 15 days. How long does it last 12 people?
---
$k = 120$; $D = 10$ days.
:::

::: q
$T \propto \sqrt{l}$. $T = 3$ when $l = 2.25$. Find $T$ when $l = 16$.
---
$k = 2$; $T = 8$.
:::

::: q
$y$ is proportional to the cube of $x$. When $x = 2$, $y = 40$. Find $x$ when $y = 1080$.
---
$k = 5$; $x^3 = 216 \Rightarrow x = 6$.
:::

::: q
Which of these tables shows direct proportion, and which inverse? Table A: $x = 2, 4, 8$; $y = 6, 12, 24$. Table B: $x = 2, 4, 8$; $y = 12, 6, 3$.
---
A: $\dfrac yx = 3$ throughout — direct. B: $xy = 24$ throughout — inverse.
:::

::: q
$y \propto x^2$. What happens to $y$ when $x$ is halved? And if instead $y \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt x}$ and $x$ is multiplied by 9?
---
$y$ is divided by 4. $y$ is divided by 3.
:::

::: watch
- **Write the formula with $k$ first**, then find $k$, then rewrite the formula with the number. Skipping to "$y = 35$" loses method marks even when right.
- "Inversely proportional" means $\dfrac{k}{x}$, **not** $-kx$ or $k - x$.
- Powers and roots go on the $x$ exactly as the words say: "proportional to the square of $x$" is $kx^2$, not $(kx)^2$.
- Solving for $x$ from $x^2 = 36$: in a real context take the positive root, and say so.
- Direct proportion graphs go through the **origin**; a straight line with an intercept is not proportion.
- Check the direction: more workers, less time — if your answer went the other way, you set up direct instead of inverse.
:::

::: test
$d$ is directly proportional to $t^2$, and $d = 20$ when $t = 2$. (a) Find $d$ when $t = 5$. (b) Find the positive value of $t$ when $d = 180$. (4)
---
$d = kt^2$, $20 = 4k$, $k = 5$ (1). (a) $d = 5 \times 25 = 125$ (1). (b) $180 = 5t^2$, $t^2 = 36$ (1); $t = 6$ (1).
:::

::: test
Light intensity $I$ is inversely proportional to the square of the distance $d$ from the source. $I = 45$ when $d = 2$. Find $I$ when $d = 3$. (3)
---
$I = \dfrac{k}{d^2}$, $45 = \dfrac{k}{4}$ (1), $k = 180$ (1). $I = \dfrac{180}{9} = 20$ (1).
:::

::: test
The frequency $f$ of a vibrating string is inversely proportional to its length $L$. $f = 256$ Hz when $L = 0.75$ m. Find $f$ when $L = 0.6$ m. (3)
---
$f = \dfrac{k}{L}$, $k = 256 \times 0.75 = 192$ (2). $f = 192 \div 0.6 = 320$ Hz (1).
:::

::: test
$y$ is directly proportional to $\sqrt{x}$, and $x$ is inversely proportional to $z^2$. When $z = 2$, $x = 9$ and $y = 12$. Find a formula for $y$ in terms of $z$. (5)
---
$y = k\sqrt{x}$: $12 = 3k$, $k = 4$ (1). $x = \dfrac{c}{z^2}$: $9 = \dfrac{c}{4}$, $c = 36$ (1). $y = 4\sqrt{\dfrac{36}{z^2}}$ (1) $= 4 \times \dfrac{6}{z}$ (1) $= \dfrac{24}{z}$ (1).
:::
