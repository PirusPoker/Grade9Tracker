## What it is

The gradient of a curve changes from point to point. **Differentiation** gives a formula for it: from $y$ you get $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, the **gradient function**, and substituting an $x$ value gives the exact gradient there — no tangent-drawing needed.

The one rule on this course:

$$y = ax^n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dy}{dx} = nax^{n-1}$$

Multiply by the power, then reduce the power by one. A constant on its own differentiates to $0$; terms added together are differentiated one at a time. So $y = 3x^4 - 5x^2 + 2x - 7$ gives $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12x^3 - 10x + 2$.

Where the gradient is **zero** the curve is flat: a **stationary point** — a **maximum** (peak) or a **minimum** (trough). To tell which, look at the gradient just either side (or use $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$: negative at a maximum, positive at a minimum).

$\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ is also a **rate of change**. If $s$ is distance and $t$ time, $\dfrac{ds}{dt}$ is velocity and $\dfrac{dv}{dt}$ is acceleration — that is **kinematics**.

::: method
1. **Write every term as $ax^n$**: $\dfrac{3}{x^2} = 3x^{-2}$, $\sqrt x = x^{1/2}$, $\dfrac{x^3 + x}{x} = x^2 + 1$. Expand any brackets first.
2. **Differentiate term by term:** power down in front, power minus one. Negative and fractional powers follow the same rule.
3. **Gradient at a point:** substitute the $x$ value into $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
4. **Stationary points:** solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$; substitute each $x$ into $y$ (not into the derivative) for the coordinates.
5. **Nature:** find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ (differentiate again) and substitute: $< 0$ maximum, $> 0$ minimum. Or test the gradient a little to the left and right.
6. **Tangent at a point:** gradient from $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, then $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
7. **Kinematics:** $v = \dfrac{ds}{dt}$, $a = \dfrac{dv}{dt}$. "At rest" → $v = 0$. "Maximum height / greatest distance" → $v = 0$ then substitute back into $s$.
8. **Increasing / decreasing:** $\dfrac{dy}{dx} > 0$ means $y$ is increasing there; $< 0$ decreasing.
:::

::: example Differentiating
Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for (a) $y = 4x^3 - 6x^2 + x - 9$, (b) $y = \dfrac{2}{x} + 3\sqrt x$, (c) $y = (x + 2)(x - 5)$.

(a) $12x^2 - 12x + 1$.

(b) $y = 2x^{-1} + 3x^{1/2}$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2x^{-2} + \dfrac32 x^{-1/2} = -\dfrac{2}{x^2} + \dfrac{3}{2\sqrt x}$.

(c) Expand: $y = x^2 - 3x - 10$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 3$.
:::

::: example Gradient at a point, and a tangent
Find the gradient of $y = x^3 - 2x$ at $x = 2$, and the equation of the tangent there.

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 2 = 10$ at $x = 2$. $y = 8 - 4 = 4$.

Tangent: $y - 4 = 10(x - 2) \Rightarrow y = 10x - 16$.
:::

::: example Stationary points and their nature
Find the stationary points of $y = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x + 5$ and determine their nature.

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2 - 6x - 12 = 6(x^2 - x - 2) = 6(x - 2)(x + 1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2$ or $x = -1$.

$x = 2$: $y = 16 - 12 - 24 + 5 = -15$. $x = -1$: $y = -2 - 3 + 12 + 5 = 12$.

$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 12x - 6$: at $x = 2$, $18 > 0$ minimum $(2, -15)$; at $x = -1$, $-18 < 0$ maximum $(-1, 12)$.
:::

::: example A practical maximum
A farmer has 60 m of fencing to make a rectangular pen against a wall (no fence needed on the wall side). Find the maximum area.

Width $x$ (two sides), length $60 - 2x$. $A = x(60 - 2x) = 60x - 2x^2$.

$\dfrac{dA}{dx} = 60 - 4x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 15$. $\dfrac{d^2A}{dx^2} = -4 < 0$: maximum. $A = 15 \times 30 = 450$ m$^2$.
:::

::: example Kinematics
A particle moves so that its distance from $O$ after $t$ seconds is $s = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$ metres. Find its velocity and acceleration at $t = 1$, and the times when it is at rest.

$v = \dfrac{ds}{dt} = 3t^2 - 12t + 9$; at $t = 1$: $0$ m/s. $a = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = 6t - 12$; at $t = 1$: $-6$ m/s$^2$.

At rest: $3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 0 \Rightarrow t^2 - 4t + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow t = 1$ or $t = 3$.
:::

::: example Maximum height
A ball is thrown so that its height is $h = 20t - 5t^2$ metres after $t$ seconds. Find the greatest height reached.

$\dfrac{dh}{dt} = 20 - 10t = 0 \Rightarrow t = 2$. $h = 40 - 20 = 20$ m. ($\dfrac{d^2h}{dt^2} = -10 < 0$ confirms a maximum.)
:::

::: q
Differentiate $y = 5x^4 - 2x^3 + 7x - 1$.
---
$20x^3 - 6x^2 + 7$.
:::

::: q
Differentiate $y = \dfrac{4}{x^2} - \sqrt x + 3$.
---
$y = 4x^{-2} - x^{1/2} + 3$: $-8x^{-3} - \dfrac12 x^{-1/2} = -\dfrac{8}{x^3} - \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt x}$.
:::

::: q
Differentiate $y = (2x - 1)(x + 3)$.
---
$y = 2x^2 + 5x - 3$: $4x + 5$.
:::

::: q
Find the gradient of $y = x^2 - 4x + 1$ at $x = 3$, and the value of $x$ where the gradient is $6$.
---
$2x - 4 = 2$ at $x = 3$; $2x - 4 = 6 \Rightarrow x = 5$.
:::

::: q
Find the equation of the tangent to $y = x^3 + x$ at the point $(1, 2)$.
---
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 + 1 = 4$: $y - 2 = 4(x - 1) \Rightarrow y = 4x - 2$.
:::

::: q
Find the stationary point of $y = x^2 - 6x + 11$ and state its nature.
---
$2x - 6 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3$, $y = 2$. $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 2 > 0$: minimum at $(3, 2)$.
:::

::: q
Find the coordinates of the stationary points of $y = x^3 - 12x + 1$ and determine which is the maximum.
---
$3x^2 - 12 = 0 \Rightarrow x = \pm 2$. $(2, -15)$ and $(-2, 17)$. $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 6x$: negative at $x = -2$, so $(-2, 17)$ is the maximum.
:::

::: q
Find the range of values of $x$ for which $y = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x$ is decreasing.
---
$3x^2 - 6x - 9 < 0 \Rightarrow x^2 - 2x - 3 < 0 \Rightarrow (x - 3)(x + 1) < 0$: $-1 < x < 3$.
:::

::: q
A box with a square base of side $x$ and no lid has volume $V = 32x - \dfrac{x^3}{2}$ (for the fixed amount of card available). Find the value of $x$ that gives the greatest volume.
---
$\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 32 - \dfrac{3x^2}{2} = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 = \dfrac{64}{3} \Rightarrow x = 4.62$ (3 s.f.). $\dfrac{d^2V}{dx^2} = -3x < 0$: maximum.
:::

::: q
A particle has displacement $s = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t$ metres at time $t$ seconds. Find when it is at rest and its acceleration at those times.
---
$v = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 = 6(t - 1)(t - 2) = 0$: $t = 1, 2$. $a = 12t - 18$: $-6$ m/s$^2$ at $t = 1$, $6$ m/s$^2$ at $t = 2$.
:::

::: q
A stone's height is $h = 30t - 5t^2$. Find its velocity after 1 second, the time it reaches its greatest height, and that height.
---
$v = 30 - 10t = 20$ m/s at $t = 1$; $v = 0$ at $t = 3$; $h = 90 - 45 = 45$ m.
:::

::: q
The curve $y = ax^2 + bx$ has gradient 7 at $x = 1$ and a stationary point at $x = -\dfrac12$. Find $a$ and $b$.
---
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2ax + b$: $2a + b = 7$ and $-a + b = 0 \Rightarrow b = a \Rightarrow 3a = 7$: $a = b = \dfrac73$.
:::

::: watch
- **Rewrite roots and fractions as powers first.** $\dfrac{4}{x^2}$ is $4x^{-2}$, and differentiates to $-8x^{-3}$.
- The derivative of a constant is $0$; the derivative of $7x$ is $7$.
- Stationary point coordinates: substitute into $y$ for the $y$-value, **not** into $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ (which is 0 there).
- Justify maximum/minimum with the second derivative or a gradient check — "it looks like a max" scores nothing.
- Kinematics: "at rest" is $v = 0$; "greatest height" is $v = 0$ then back into $h$. Give units.
- Expand brackets before differentiating; there is no product rule on this course.
:::

::: test
A rectangle has perimeter 40 cm and one side of length $x$ cm. Show that its area is $A = 20x - x^2$, and use calculus to find the maximum area. (3)
---
The other side is $20 - x$, so $A = x(20 - x) = 20x - x^2$ (1). $\dfrac{dA}{dx} = 20 - 2x = 0$ gives $x = 10$ (1); $A = 100$ cm$^2$, a maximum since $\dfrac{d^2A}{dx^2} = -2 < 0$ (1).
