## What it is

Solids by name: cube, cuboid, prism (same cross-section all the way through), cylinder (a circular prism), pyramid, cone, sphere, hemisphere. Count their **faces** (flat surfaces), **edges** (where faces meet) and **vertices** (corners): a cuboid has 6, 12, 8.

**Volume** — the space inside:

| Solid | Volume |
|---|---|
| Prism (any) | area of cross-section $\times$ length |
| Cuboid | $l \times w \times h$ |
| Cylinder | $\pi r^2 h$ |
| Pyramid | $\frac13 \times$ base area $\times$ height |
| Cone | $\frac13 \pi r^2 h$ |
| Sphere | $\frac43 \pi r^3$ |

**Surface area** — the total area of all the faces. For a cuboid, add the six rectangles (three pairs). For a cylinder: two circles plus the curved surface, a rectangle of width $2\pi r$ and height $h$, so $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$. Cone: curved surface $\pi r l$ ($l$ the slant height) plus the base $\pi r^2$. Sphere: $4\pi r^2$.

Cone and sphere formulae are on the formula sheet; prisms and cylinders are not.

Volume units convert by the cube of the length factor: $1$ m$^3 = 1\,000\,000$ cm$^3$; $1$ litre $= 1000$ cm$^3$.

::: terms
- **Face, edge, vertex**: a flat surface, a line where two faces meet, and a corner of a solid.
- **Prism**: a solid with the same cross-section all the way through.
- **Volume of a prism**: area of cross-section × length.
- **Volume of a cylinder**: V = πr²h, where r is the radius and h the height.
- **Curved surface area of a cylinder**: 2πrh — the area of the rectangle that wraps around it.
- **Volume of a cone**: ⅓πr²h; curved surface area πrl, where l is the slant height.
- **Volume of a sphere**: ⁴⁄₃πr³; surface area 4πr².
- **Volume of a pyramid**: ⅓ × base area × height.
- **Surface area**: the total area of all the faces of a solid.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the solid** and write down its formula before substituting.
2. **Prisms:** find the cross-section area first (triangle, trapezium, L-shape, circle), then multiply by the length. Check which dimension is the length — the one perpendicular to the cross-section.
3. **Surface area:** list every face with its area, then add. Draw the net if it helps. For curved surfaces use the formulae; for a hemisphere, half the sphere's curved surface plus the flat circle.
4. **Cones:** the slant height $l$ and vertical height $h$ are linked by Pythagoras, $l^2 = r^2 + h^2$ — questions give one and need the other.
5. **Composite solids:** add or subtract volumes; for surface area, count only the exposed faces (a hemisphere on a cylinder hides one circle).
6. **Working backwards:** set the formula equal to the given volume and solve — a cube root for a sphere's radius, a square root for a cylinder's.
7. **Units:** all lengths in the same unit before calculating; convert litres ↔ cm$^3$ ↔ m$^3$ at the end if needed.
:::

::: example A triangular prism
A prism has a triangular cross-section with base 6 cm and height 4 cm, and is 15 cm long. Find its volume and surface area (the triangle's third sides are 5 cm each).

Volume $= \dfrac12 \times 6 \times 4 \times 15 = 180$ cm$^3$.

Surface: two triangles $2 \times 12 = 24$; three rectangles $15 \times 6 + 2 \times 15 \times 5 = 90 + 150 = 240$. Total $264$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example A cylinder
A cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.

$V = \pi \times 16 \times 10 = 160\pi = 503$ cm$^3$.

$S = 2\pi \times 16 + 2\pi \times 4 \times 10 = 32\pi + 80\pi = 112\pi = 352$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example A cone
A cone has radius 6 cm and vertical height 8 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.

$V = \dfrac13 \pi \times 36 \times 8 = 96\pi = 302$ cm$^3$.

Slant height $l = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10$. Curved surface $= \pi \times 6 \times 10 = 60\pi$; base $= 36\pi$. Total $96\pi = 302$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example A sphere, backwards
A sphere has volume $2000$ cm$^3$. Find its radius and surface area.

$\dfrac43\pi r^3 = 2000 \Rightarrow r^3 = \dfrac{1500}{\pi} = 477.5 \Rightarrow r = 7.82$ cm.

$S = 4\pi r^2 = 4\pi \times 61.1 = 768$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example A composite solid
A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm with a hemisphere of radius 3 cm on top. Find its volume and surface area.

$V = \pi \times 9 \times 10 + \dfrac12 \times \dfrac43 \pi \times 27 = 90\pi + 18\pi = 108\pi = 339$ cm$^3$.

Surface: base circle $9\pi$, curved cylinder $2\pi \times 3 \times 10 = 60\pi$, hemisphere $\dfrac12 \times 4\pi \times 9 = 18\pi$. Total $87\pi = 273$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: example Litres
A cuboid tank is 1.2 m by 0.8 m by 0.5 m. How many litres does it hold?

$0.48$ m$^3 = 480\,000$ cm$^3 = 480$ litres.
:::

Find the volume of a cuboid 12 cm by 7 cm by 4 cm, and its surface area.
---
$336$ cm$^3$; $2(84 + 48 + 28) = 320$ cm$^2$.

A prism has a trapezium cross-section with parallel sides 5 cm and 9 cm, 4 cm apart, and is 20 cm long. Find its volume.
---
Cross-section $28$ cm$^2$; volume $560$ cm$^3$.

A cylinder has diameter 10 cm and height 12 cm. Find its volume and curved surface area.
---
$V = \pi \times 25 \times 12 = 300\pi = 942$ cm$^3$; curved $= 2\pi \times 5 \times 12 = 120\pi = 377$ cm$^2$.

::: q
A square-based pyramid has base side 6 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume.
---
$\dfrac13 \times 36 \times 10 = 120$ cm$^3$.
:::

::: q
A cone has radius 5 cm and slant height 13 cm. Find its volume.
---
$h = 12$; $V = \dfrac13 \pi \times 25 \times 12 = 100\pi = 314$ cm$^3$.
:::

::: q
Find the volume and surface area of a sphere of radius 6 cm, exactly.
---
$288\pi$ cm$^3$; $144\pi$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A hemisphere has radius 4 cm. Find its total surface area, including the flat face.
---
$2\pi \times 16 + \pi \times 16 = 48\pi = 151$ cm$^2$.
:::

::: q
A cylinder has volume $500$ cm$^3$ and height 8 cm. Find its radius.
---
$r^2 = \dfrac{500}{8\pi} = 19.89 \Rightarrow r = 4.46$ cm.
:::

::: q
Convert $2.5$ m$^3$ to litres, and $750$ cm$^3$ to litres.
---
$2500$ litres; $0.75$ litres.
:::

::: q
A metal cube of side 6 cm is melted and recast as a cylinder of radius 3 cm. Find the cylinder's height.
---
$216 = 9\pi h \Rightarrow h = 7.64$ cm.
:::

::: q
How many faces, edges and vertices has a triangular prism? A square-based pyramid?
---
Prism: 5, 9, 6. Pyramid: 5, 8, 5.
:::

::: watch
- **Prism volume = cross-section area × length** — find the cross-section area first, and make sure the "length" is the dimension perpendicular to it.
- Surface area is the sum of **every face**, including the ends. Missing one end of a prism or a cylinder is the classic loss.
- Cone: $l$ (slant) for surface area, $h$ (vertical) for volume. Pythagoras links them.
- Halve a diameter before using $r$.
- Volume converts by the **cube** of the length factor; $1$ litre $= 1000$ cm$^3$.
- Exact answers keep $\pi$: $160\pi$, not $503$.
:::

::: test
