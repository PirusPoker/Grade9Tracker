## What it is

### What red-shift is

**There is an observed increase in the wavelength of light from most distant galaxies. The further away the galaxies, the faster they are moving away from us, and the bigger the observed increase in wavelength. This effect is called red-shift.**

**How we observe it.** When light from a star or galaxy is split into a spectrum, it contains dark **absorption lines** at particular wavelengths, caused by elements such as hydrogen and helium in the star's outer layers. Each element always produces the same pattern of lines. In light from distant galaxies, the **whole pattern is shifted towards the red end** (longer wavelength, lower frequency) compared with the same lines measured in a laboratory. The pattern is the same — it has just moved.

**Explaining red-shift qualitatively (explain this):**
1. A galaxy that is moving **away (receding)** from us emits light waves.
2. Between emitting one wave crest and the next, the galaxy has moved further away, so each crest has further to travel.
3. So the crests arrive **further apart**: the observed **wavelength is longer** and the frequency lower.
4. The light is shifted towards the **red** end of the spectrum.
5. The **faster** the galaxy is receding, the **bigger** the increase in wavelength (the bigger the red-shift).

(This is like the drop in pitch of a siren moving away from you. A galaxy moving **towards** us would show a **blue-shift**; only a few nearby galaxies, such as Andromeda, do.)

**Mini-example.** A hydrogen absorption line measured in the laboratory at 656 nm is observed at 669 nm in light from a distant galaxy. Increase in wavelength $= 669 - 656 = 13$ nm. A galaxy twice as far away would be expected to show roughly twice the increase, about 26 nm.

### Red-shift as evidence of an expanding universe

Observations (first by Edwin Hubble in 1929) show:
- **Almost all** distant galaxies show red-shift, so almost all are **moving away from us**.
- **The further away a galaxy is, the bigger its red-shift**, so the **faster it is moving away**. A graph of a galaxy's speed against its distance is roughly a **straight line through the origin**: speed is roughly **proportional** to distance.

**Why this is evidence that the universe is expanding (explain this):**
- If **space itself is expanding**, every galaxy moves away from every other galaxy, and the space between distant galaxies grows faster than the space between near ones — exactly the pattern observed.
- Think of dots on a balloon being blown up: every dot moves away from every other dot, and dots further apart separate faster. No dot is "the centre".
- So the change of each galaxy's speed with distance is evidence of an **expanding universe**, not of us being at a special central point.

**Mini-example (proportionality).** A galaxy 100 million light years away is receding at 2200 km/s. If speed is proportional to distance, a galaxy 300 million light years away (3 times as far) should be receding at about $3 \times 2200 = 6600$ km/s.

### The Big Bang theory

**The observed red-shift provides evidence that space itself (the universe) is expanding and supports the Big Bang theory.**

**The Big Bang theory suggests that the universe began from a very small region that was extremely hot and dense.** Since then it has been expanding and cooling (for about 13.8 billion years).

**How red-shift supports the Big Bang (explain this):**
1. Red-shift shows galaxies are moving apart now, and the further away they are the faster they move.
2. If the universe is expanding now, then **in the past** the galaxies must have been **closer together**.
3. Running the expansion backwards, everything must once have been in a **very small, extremely hot and dense region** — the starting point described by the Big Bang theory.

### How scientists arrive at theories such as the Big Bang

Scientists use **observations** to develop and test **theories**:
- They collect data (e.g. measuring the red-shift and distance of many galaxies) using better and better telescopes.
- They look for **patterns** (red-shift increases with distance).
- They suggest a **theory** that explains the pattern (an expanding universe that began in a Big Bang).
- The theory makes **predictions** that can be tested with **new observations**; if new evidence agrees, confidence in the theory grows. If not, the theory must be changed or replaced.
- Other scientists check the data and ideas through **peer review**.

Historically there were rival theories. The **Steady State** theory said the universe has always existed and new matter is created as it expands. Red-shift alone could fit both, so further evidence was needed to decide between them; over time the evidence came to support the Big Bang theory. (Other evidence, such as the cosmic microwave background radiation, is not required by the AQA specification.)

### An accelerating universe, dark mass and dark energy

**Since 1998, observations of supernovae suggest that distant galaxies are receding ever faster** — the expansion of the universe is **accelerating**. Scientists expected gravity to slow the expansion down, so this was a surprise.

**There is still much about the universe that is not understood**, for example:
- **Dark mass (dark matter)**: galaxies rotate so fast that the visible stars and gas do not have enough mass (gravity) to hold them together. There must be extra mass that we cannot see because it does not emit electromagnetic radiation. Nobody yet knows what it is.
- **Dark energy**: something must be causing the expansion of the universe to **accelerate**. This unknown cause is called dark energy. Its nature is not understood.

Together, dark mass and dark energy are thought to make up about 95% of the universe — ordinary matter (stars, planets, gas, us) is only about 5%. This shows that scientific theories are **tentative**: they explain current evidence but can be changed as new observations are made.

::: terms
- **Red-shift**: the observed increase in wavelength of light from a galaxy that is moving away from us.
- **Absorption spectrum**: a spectrum with dark lines at wavelengths absorbed by elements, used to measure red-shift.
- **Receding**: moving away from the observer; almost all distant galaxies are receding from us.
- **Expanding universe**: the idea, supported by red-shift, that space itself is stretching so galaxies move apart.
- **Big Bang theory**: the theory that the universe began from a very small region that was extremely hot and dense, and has expanded since.
- **Dark mass**: unseen mass, also called dark matter, needed to explain how galaxies hold together, which does not emit radiation.
- **Dark energy**: the unknown cause of the accelerating expansion of the universe observed since 1998.
- **Steady State theory**: a rival theory in which the universe has always existed, with new matter created as it expands.
:::

- **Interpret** data and graphs: a straight line through the origin on a speed–distance graph means speed is proportional to distance; read values and use proportion.

**Maths**: wavelength increase = observed wavelength − laboratory wavelength; use proportionality (double the distance → double the speed and the red-shift). Keep units consistent (nm, km/s, light years).

::: watch
- **"Red-shift means the light turns red"**: the wavelength **increases** (shifts towards the red end); the galaxy does not look red.
- **"Galaxies are moving away from the centre where we are"**: **space itself** is expanding — every galaxy moves away from every other one.
- **Forgetting the distance link**: the key evidence is that **further galaxies have bigger red-shift** and so move **faster**.
- **Big Bang described as an explosion in space**: it was a very small, **extremely hot and dense region** that has **expanded**.
- **Dark mass vs dark energy**: dark mass explains how **galaxies hold together**; dark energy explains the **accelerating expansion**.
- **Steps missing in "how does red-shift support the Big Bang"**: go from moving apart now → closer in the past → small, hot, dense start.
:::
