## What it is

**Costs** are what a firm pays for its factors of production. Six terms, all examinable with calculations:

| Cost | Definition | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed cost (FC)** | costs that do **not** change with output — paid even at zero output | rent, insurance, salaries, loan interest |
| **Variable cost (VC)** | costs that **change** with output | raw materials, energy, piece-rate wages |
| **Total cost (TC)** | all costs of producing a given output | $TC = FC + VC$ |
| **Average fixed cost (AFC)** | fixed cost per unit | $AFC = FC \div Q$ |
| **Average variable cost (AVC)** | variable cost per unit | $AVC = VC \div Q$ |
| **Average total cost (ATC)** | total cost per unit | $ATC = TC \div Q = AFC + AVC$ |

- **AFC always falls** as output rises: the same fixed cost is spread over more units.
- **ATC is U-shaped**: it falls while AFC falls quickly and economies of scale are gained, then rises as diseconomies set in.
- The gap between ATC and AVC is AFC, so it narrows as output grows.

**Revenue** is what the firm receives from sales:

- **Total revenue (TR)** $=$ price $\times$ quantity sold.
- **Average revenue (AR)** $= TR \div Q$ — which is just the **price**, if every unit sells at the same price.

**Profit** $=$ TR $-$ TC. A loss is negative profit. Given TR, TC and output, you can find profit per unit as $AR - ATC$.

**Sales and revenue** are different: sales are the *number* of units; revenue is the *money*. Cutting price can raise sales but cut revenue (inelastic demand — see 2.6).

**Objectives of firms** — what they are trying to do:

- **Profit maximisation** — the usual assumption: shareholders want the largest possible return; profit funds investment.
- **Survival** — in a recession or for a new firm, staying in business comes first, even at a loss.
- **Growth / sales maximisation** — a larger market share brings economies of scale, market power and status; managers' pay is often tied to size.
- **Social welfare / ethical objectives** — charities, cooperatives, public-sector firms and firms with green or fair-trade aims.
- Also: **customer satisfaction**, **quality**, keeping the owner's independence.

Objectives change over time: a new firm aims at survival, then growth, then profit; a large firm under pressure from shareholders returns to profit.

Key definitions to learn word for word:

- **Fixed costs**: costs that do not change with the level of output.
- **Variable costs**: costs that change directly with the level of output.
- **Average total cost**: total cost divided by output.
- **Total revenue**: price multiplied by quantity sold.
- **Average revenue**: total revenue divided by quantity sold.
- **Profit**: total revenue minus total cost.

::: method
1. **Fixed or variable?** Ask: does it change when one more unit is made? Rent — no → fixed. Materials — yes → variable. Salaries fixed; piece-rate wages variable.
2. **Calculations in order**: TC = FC + VC; then divide each by Q for the averages; check ATC = AFC + AVC.
3. **Revenue**: TR = P × Q. AR = P. Profit = TR − TC. Show the working line by line — method marks are given.
4. **Change in output**: FC stays the same in total; AFC falls; VC and TC rise. State which move and which do not.
5. **Objectives**: name it, explain *why* a firm might hold it, and give the kind of firm — then note objectives can change with circumstances (recession → survival).
6. **Sales vs revenue**: if asked about the effect of a price cut on revenue, bring in PED.
:::

::: example Calculate the six costs
A firm has fixed costs of 4,000 dollars a month. Each unit costs 6 dollars in materials and labour. It produces 500 units. Calculate TC, AFC, AVC and ATC. (4)

VC $= 6 \times 500 = 3{,}000$; TC $= 4{,}000 + 3{,}000 = 7{,}000$ dollars (1). AFC $= 4{,}000 \div 500 = 8$ dollars (1). AVC $= 3{,}000 \div 500 = 6$ dollars (1). ATC $= 7{,}000 \div 500 = 14$ dollars, which equals $8 + 6$ (1).
:::

::: example Revenue and profit
The same firm sells all 500 units at 20 dollars each. Calculate total revenue, average revenue and profit. (3)

TR $= 20 \times 500 = 10{,}000$ dollars (1). AR $= 10{,}000 \div 500 = 20$ dollars — the price (1). Profit $= 10{,}000 - 7{,}000 = 3{,}000$ dollars, or $(20 - 14) \times 500$ (1).
:::

::: example Explain — why AFC falls
Explain why average fixed cost falls as output rises. (2)

Fixed cost is the same total whatever the output (1), so as output rises it is spread over more units and the fixed cost per unit falls (1).
:::

::: example Analyse — objectives
Analyse why a firm might choose growth rather than profit maximisation as its objective. (6)

A larger firm gains economies of scale — bulk buying, specialised machinery — which lower its average cost, so growth now can lead to higher profits later (2). A bigger market share gives market power over prices and suppliers and makes the firm harder for rivals or takeover bidders to challenge; managers may also prefer growth because their salaries and status rise with the size of the firm (2). Growth may, however, mean low prices and heavy spending that cut profit in the short run, so shareholders wanting dividends may resist; the objective the firm picks depends on who controls it and on how competitive its market is (2).
:::

::: q
Define "variable cost" and give an example. (2)
---
A cost that changes directly with output (1), e.g. raw materials (1).
:::

::: q
Classify as fixed or variable: factory rent; electricity used by machines; the manager's salary; packaging. (4)
---
Rent — fixed; electricity — variable; salary — fixed; packaging — variable.
:::

::: q
Fixed costs are 12,000; variable costs are 18,000; output is 3,000 units. Calculate TC and ATC. (2)
---
TC $= 30{,}000$ (1); ATC $= 30{,}000 \div 3{,}000 = 10$ per unit (1).
:::

::: q
Using the figures above, calculate AFC and AVC. (2)
---
AFC $= 12{,}000 \div 3{,}000 = 4$; AVC $= 18{,}000 \div 3{,}000 = 6$.
:::

::: q
A firm sells 800 units at 15 each. Its total cost is 9,600. Calculate total revenue and profit. (2)
---
TR $= 12{,}000$ (1); profit $= 12{,}000 - 9{,}600 = 2{,}400$ (1).
:::

::: q
Explain what happens to total fixed cost and average fixed cost when output doubles. (2)
---
Total fixed cost is unchanged (1); average fixed cost halves, as the same cost is spread over twice the units (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a firm might aim for survival rather than profit. (4)
---
A new firm has few customers and high start-up costs, so it accepts low or no profit to establish itself (2). In a recession demand falls and a firm may sell at a loss to keep cash coming in and keep its workers, rather than close (2).
:::

::: q
Explain the difference between sales and revenue. (2)
---
Sales are the number of units sold (1); revenue is the money received, price times sales (1) — a price cut can raise sales but reduce revenue.
:::

::: q
Explain why the average total cost curve is U-shaped. (4)
---
At low output, AFC falls quickly as fixed costs are spread and economies of scale are gained, so ATC falls (2). At high output diseconomies of scale — management and communication problems — raise cost per unit, so ATC rises (2).
:::

::: q
Discuss whether all firms should aim to maximise profit. (8)
---
For: profit rewards the owners and shareholders who risked their capital, funds investment and growth, and forces the firm to keep costs low and produce what consumers want; without profit a private firm cannot survive long (3). Against: some firms have other aims — charities and cooperatives aim at social welfare, public-sector bodies at service; new firms need survival and growth first; pursuing maximum profit can mean cutting quality, paying low wages or polluting, which damages reputation and long-run sales (3). Judgement: most private firms need profit, but not necessarily the maximum in every year; the best objective depends on the firm's ownership, age and market, and many balance profit against growth and reputation (2).
:::

::: watch
- **ATC = AFC + AVC.** Use it to check your arithmetic.
- **AFC always falls** with output; total fixed cost does **not** change.
- **AR = price** when all units sell at one price. Do not overthink it.
- **Profit = TR − TC**, and show the working; a bare number without working can lose method marks.
- Objectives: give the **reason** and the **type of firm**, and note that objectives **change** with circumstances.
:::
