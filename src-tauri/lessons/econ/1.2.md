## What it is

Everything that is produced — a loaf of bread, a hospital operation, a video game — needs inputs. Economists group every input into four **factors of production**, and each factor earns a **reward** for the person who supplies it.

| Factor | What it is | Examples | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Land** | All natural resources — the gifts of nature | fields, forests, oil, fish, minerals, the sea | **Rent** |
| **Labour** | Human effort, physical and mental, used in production | a nurse, a coder, a bricklayer | **Wages** |
| **Capital** | Human-made goods used to produce other goods and services | machines, factories, lorries, tools, software | **Interest** |
| **Enterprise** | The willingness to take risks and to organise the other three factors | an entrepreneur who starts a business | **Profit** |

Two traps. **Capital** in economics means *physical* goods used in production, not money — money only becomes capital when it is spent on a machine. And **enterprise** is a separate factor from labour: the entrepreneur bears the risk of losing their money and takes the decisions; the workers do not.

The **quantity** and **quality** of each factor changes over time, and that is what makes an economy able to produce more (see the PPC in 1.4):

- **Land** — *quantity*: discovery of new oil or minerals, land reclamation from the sea, deforestation, rising sea levels (a fall). *Quality*: fertilisers, irrigation, drainage; pollution or soil erosion (a fall).
- **Labour** — *quantity*: birth rate, net migration, retirement age, school-leaving age, the proportion of people who choose to work (the participation rate). *Quality*: education, training, health care, experience.
- **Capital** — *quantity*: **investment** (spending on new capital goods) minus **depreciation** (capital wearing out). *Quality*: new technology — a modern machine produces more per hour than an old one.
- **Enterprise** — *quantity*: business education, government grants and lower taxes on profit, immigration of entrepreneurs, a culture that rewards risk-taking. *Quality*: experience, management training.

Key definitions to learn word for word:

- **Factors of production**: the resources used to produce goods and services — land, labour, capital and enterprise.
- **Capital**: human-made goods used in the production of other goods and services.
- **Enterprise**: the factor that takes the risks and organises the other factors of production.
- **Investment**: spending by firms on capital goods.

::: method
1. **Name the factor and its reward together.** Land–rent, labour–wages, capital–interest, enterprise–profit. A "define" on any factor earns its second mark by naming the reward or giving a correct example.
2. **Classify by asking three questions.** Is it natural? → land. Is it human effort? → labour. Is it a made thing used to make other things? → capital. Is it the risk-taker who organises? → enterprise.
3. **Separate quantity from quality.** Quantity means *more* of the factor (more workers); quality means *better* (more skilled workers). A 4-mark "explain two ways" question usually wants one of each, or two clearly different causes.
4. **Link a change in a factor to output.** "Training raises the quality of labour, so each worker produces more per hour, so total output rises." The chain factor → productivity → output is what analyse questions reward.
5. **Watch for the money trap.** If a question says "the firm borrowed 1 million dollars", that is not capital until it is spent on machinery or buildings. Money is a means of buying capital.
:::

::: example Define
Define "capital" as a factor of production. (2)

Capital is human-made goods used to produce other goods and services (1), such as machinery, factories and tools; its reward is interest (1).
:::

::: example Classify
A farm uses fields, two tractors, four workers and is run by its owner who has borrowed money to expand. Identify the factor of production in each case. (4)

Fields — land, a natural resource (1). Tractors — capital, human-made goods used in production (1). Workers — labour, human effort (1). The owner — enterprise, because she takes the risk of borrowing and organises the other factors (1). (The borrowed money itself is not a factor; it becomes capital when spent on the tractors.)
:::

::: example Explain — quantity and quality of labour
Explain two ways the quality of a country's labour force could be increased. (4)

More spending on education and training raises workers' skills, so each worker can carry out more complex tasks and produce more output per hour (2). Better healthcare means workers take fewer sick days and can work productively for longer, which also raises the output each worker contributes (2).
:::

::: example Analyse — investment
Analyse how an increase in investment might affect a country's output. (6)

Investment means firms spend more on capital goods such as machinery and factories, so the *quantity* of capital in the economy rises and there is more equipment for workers to use, increasing what can be produced (2). New capital usually embodies newer technology, so the *quality* of capital rises too: a modern machine produces more per hour than the old one it replaces, raising productivity (2). With more and better capital the economy's maximum possible output rises — shown by an outward shift of its production possibility curve — so long as there are enough skilled workers to operate the new equipment (2).
:::

::: q
State the four factors of production and the reward each earns. (4)
---
Land — rent; labour — wages; capital — interest; enterprise — profit.
:::

::: q
Define "enterprise". (2)
---
The factor of production that takes the risk of starting a business (1) and organises the other three factors of production (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why money is not usually counted as capital by economists. (2)
---
Capital means human-made goods used in production, such as machines (1). Money produces nothing by itself; it only becomes capital once spent on such goods (1).
:::

::: q
Explain two ways the quantity of land available to an economy could increase. (4)
---
Discovery of new natural resources, e.g. a new oil field, adds to the natural resources available (2). Land reclamation, draining marshes or building out into the sea, creates land that can be used for farming or building (2).
:::

::: q
Explain the difference between an increase in the quantity of labour and an increase in its quality. (4)
---
Quantity is the number of workers or hours available, raised by immigration, a higher birth rate or a later retirement age (2). Quality is how productive each worker is, raised by education, training and better health (2).
:::

::: q
Identify the factor of production for each: (a) a fishing boat, (b) a shoal of fish, (c) a fisherman, (d) the person who set up the fishing company. (4)
---
(a) capital, (b) land, (c) labour, (d) enterprise.
:::

::: q
Explain how a government could increase the amount of enterprise in an economy. (4)
---
It could cut taxes on profits so the reward for taking risks is higher, encouraging more people to start businesses (2). It could offer grants, cheap loans or business training, lowering the cost and risk of starting a firm (2).
:::

::: q
Analyse how an ageing population might affect a country's factors of production. (6)
---
As more people retire the quantity of labour falls, so fewer workers are available and output may fall (2). Older workers may have more experience, which can raise the quality of labour, but a smaller workforce pays less tax while pensions and healthcare spending rise (2). Firms may respond by investing in more capital to replace scarce workers, raising the quantity of capital; whether output rises depends on whether the extra capital outweighs the loss of labour (2).
:::

::: watch
- The four rewards are **rent, wages, interest, profit** — matched to the right factor. Swapping interest and profit is the most common slip.
- **Capital is not money.** Machines, factories, tools, vehicles. A question about a loan is about money; capital is what the loan buys.
- **Enterprise is not labour.** The entrepreneur takes the *risk*; the manager who is paid a salary is labour.
- "Quantity" and "quality" are different marks. Say which one you mean and give a cause for each.
- Land includes everything natural — the sea, minerals underground, forests — not just fields.
:::
