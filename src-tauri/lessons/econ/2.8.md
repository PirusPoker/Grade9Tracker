## What it is

An **economic system** is the way a country answers the three questions — what, how and for whom. In a **market economic system** the answers come from the **price mechanism**, with **private ownership** of resources and **no government involvement** beyond the law. Consumers decide what is made by what they buy ("consumer sovereignty"); firms compete for their money; prices allocate resources.

No country is a pure market economy — the closest examples (Hong Kong, Singapore, the USA) still have governments providing defence, courts and some services. The pure version is a model used to see the strengths and weaknesses of leaving everything to markets.

**Advantages of the market system:**

- **Consumer sovereignty** — resources go to what people actually want, because firms only profit by selling what buyers choose.
- **Efficiency** — competition forces firms to cut costs and keep prices low; wasteful firms lose customers and close.
- **Incentives** — profit rewards risk-taking, innovation and hard work, so new products appear and the economy grows.
- **Choice** — many competing producers offer variety.
- **Automatic** — resources are reallocated by prices without an expensive bureaucracy of planners.

**Disadvantages of the market system:**

- **Inequality** — resources go to those who can pay. The poor may not afford healthcare or education; the rich can buy luxuries while others go without necessities.
- **Public goods** are not provided — no firm can charge for street lighting or defence, since non-payers cannot be excluded.
- **Merit goods** are under-consumed and **demerit goods** over-consumed — people ignore the full benefits of education or the harm of tobacco.
- **Externalities** are ignored — firms pollute because the cost falls on others, not on them.
- **Monopolies** can form, raising prices and cutting output.
- **Instability** — booms and slumps, unemployment; no government to stabilise demand.
- **Information problems** — consumers may be misled by advertising; safety and quality may be poor without regulation.

These disadvantages are the **market failures** of topic 2.9 — and the reason every real economy is mixed (2.10).

::: terms
- **Economic system**: the way an economy answers the three economic questions of what, how and for whom to produce.
- **Market economic system**: an economy in which resources are allocated through the price mechanism, with private ownership and no government intervention.
- **Private sector**: the part of the economy owned and run by individuals and firms.
- **Consumer sovereignty**: the idea that consumers' spending decisions determine what is produced.
- **Profit motive**: the incentive for firms to produce what earns the most profit.
- **Competition**: rivalry between firms for customers, which encourages efficiency and choice.
:::

::: method
1. **Define the system with three features**: price mechanism allocates resources; private ownership; little or no government.
2. **"Advantages" questions** — pick the strongest three and develop each: consumer sovereignty → what people want gets made; competition → efficiency and low prices; profit incentive → innovation.
3. **"Disadvantages" questions** — inequality, public goods, merit/demerit goods, externalities, monopoly. Each is a specific reason the market gives the *wrong* answer to what/how/for whom.
4. **"Discuss whether…" (8 marks)** — two or three developed advantages, two or three developed disadvantages, then a judgement: markets are efficient but need a government to correct the failures, which is why real economies are mixed.
5. **Use the three questions** as a framework: how does the market answer each, and where does each answer go wrong?
:::

::: example Define
Define "market economic system". (2)

An economic system in which resources are allocated by the price mechanism through the decisions of consumers and firms (1), with private ownership of resources and no government intervention (1).
:::

::: example Explain — an advantage
Explain how a market economy achieves efficiency. (4)

Firms compete with each other for consumers' spending, so any firm with higher costs and prices than its rivals loses customers and profit (2). This forces firms to use the cheapest methods of production and to keep prices low; inefficient firms go out of business and their resources move to more efficient producers (2).
:::

::: example Explain — a disadvantage
Explain why a market economy may not provide street lighting. (4)

Street lighting is a public good: once provided, nobody can be stopped from benefiting from it, whether they paid or not (2). Because people can free-ride, no consumer would pay voluntarily and a private firm could not earn revenue, so it would not be produced even though society wants it (2).
:::

::: example Discuss
Discuss whether a country would benefit from moving towards a market economic system. (8)

In favour: prices and profits would direct resources to what consumers actually want, replacing planners' guesses; competition would push firms to cut costs and innovate, raising efficiency and growth; individuals would have more choice and the incentive of profit would encourage enterprise (4). Against: the market ignores the poor, who cannot pay for healthcare or education, so inequality would rise; public goods would go unprovided, merit goods under-consumed and pollution ignored; large firms could become monopolies and the economy could suffer unemployment in slumps (3). Judgement: the country would likely gain efficiency and growth, but only if the government kept a role in providing public and merit goods and regulating monopolies and pollution — a mixed rather than a pure market system (1).
:::

::: example Explain — consumer sovereignty
Explain how consumer sovereignty decides what is produced in a market economic system. (4)

When consumers want more of a product, they spend more on it, so demand rises and its price rises (1). The higher price makes producing it more profitable (1), so firms, driven by the profit motive, move resources into making it (1); products consumers stop buying fall in price and profit, and resources move out of them — so consumers' spending, not the government, decides what is produced (1).
:::

::: q
State three features of a market economic system. (3)
---
Resources allocated by the price mechanism; private ownership of resources; no (or minimal) government intervention.
:::

::: q
Define "consumer sovereignty". (2)
---
The idea that consumers, through what they choose to buy (1), determine what firms produce (1).
:::

::: q
Explain how the profit motive benefits consumers in a market economy. (4)
---
Firms earn profit only by selling what consumers want, so they research and produce goods that buyers value (2). To raise profit they innovate and cut costs, giving consumers new products and lower prices (2).
:::

::: q
Explain why inequality is likely in a market economy. (4)
---
Goods go to those willing and able to pay (1). Income depends on the resources a person owns — skills, capital, land — and these are unevenly spread (1). Those with few resources earn little and cannot buy even necessities, while the wealthy buy luxuries (2).
:::

::: q
Explain why a market economy may produce too many demerit goods. (4)
---
Demerit goods such as tobacco are harmful, but consumers underestimate the harm or ignore it (1). Firms supply whatever is profitable, and demand is strong (1), so the market produces more than is good for society; no one in the market takes account of the costs to third parties (2).
:::

::: q
Give two reasons why no real economy is a pure market economy. (2)
---
Governments always provide some public goods such as defence and law; and intervene to correct market failures such as pollution and inequality.
:::

::: q
Explain the role of competition in a market economy. (4)
---
Competition between firms for customers forces them to keep prices low and quality high (2). Firms that fail to compete lose sales and close, so resources move to efficient firms; competition also pushes innovation (2).
:::

::: q
Discuss whether the disadvantages of a market economy outweigh its advantages. (8)
---
Advantages: consumer sovereignty means resources follow what people want; competition gives efficiency, low prices and innovation; profit rewards enterprise, driving growth; choice is wide (3). Disadvantages: inequality — the poor may lack necessities; public goods not provided; merit goods under-consumed and demerit goods over-consumed; externalities such as pollution ignored; monopolies may exploit consumers; unemployment in slumps (3). Judgement: the advantages are large for ordinary goods, but the disadvantages are serious for healthcare, education and the environment, so the best outcome combines markets with government intervention where they fail — which is why countries choose a mixed system (2).
:::

::: watch
- Market system = **price mechanism + private ownership + no government**. Say all three.
- Disadvantages are the **market failures** — name the type (public good, merit good, externality, monopoly, inequality) and explain *why* the market gets it wrong.
- In a "discuss", **develop** points — "efficiency" alone is a word; "competition forces firms to cut costs because…" is a mark.
- Remember **no pure examples** exist; use "moving towards a market system".
- Do not confuse **market economy** with **mixed economy** — mixed has a substantial public sector.
:::

Firms must attract customers, so they cut prices (1); improve quality and choice (1); and innovate to stay ahead (1).

---
Consumers determine what is produced (1) through their spending choices, which firms follow to make profit (1).

Analyse how a market economy might respond to a rise in demand for renewable energy. (4)
---
Higher demand raises prices and profits in renewables (1); firms invest and new ones enter (1); resources move from fossil fuels (1); but without intervention, external benefits may still leave provision below the ideal (1).
:::

Income depends only on what a person's factors can sell for; those with no skills or assets earn little (1); there is no government redistribution through taxes and benefits (1).
