## What it is

Prices change because a demand curve or a supply curve **shifts**. Four cases cover every exam question:

| Shift | Price | Quantity | Typical cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand **increases** (D right) | **rises** | rises | incomes up, substitute dearer, fashion |
| Demand **decreases** (D left) | **falls** | falls | health scare, complement dearer |
| Supply **increases** (S right) | **falls** | rises | good harvest, new technology, subsidy |
| Supply **decreases** (S left) | **rises** | falls | higher costs, tax, bad weather |

The pattern to memorise: a **demand** shift moves price and quantity the **same** way; a **supply** shift moves them in **opposite** directions.

The mechanism, in words, for a demand increase: demand shifts right → at the old price P1 there is now a **shortage** → price rises → supply **extends** along S and demand **contracts** along D2 → new equilibrium at P2, Q2 with a higher price and quantity.

Once price has changed, the **effect on sales** follows the law of demand: a higher price means fewer units sold (demand contracts), a lower price means more sold (demand extends). *How much* sales change depends on price elasticity of demand (2.6) — but the direction is always as above.

Both curves can shift at once. Then one of price or quantity is certain and the other is uncertain: if demand and supply both increase, quantity definitely rises but price could go either way, depending on which shift is larger.

::: method
1. **Decide which curve shifts** — is the cause about *buyers* (income, tastes, other goods' prices, population) or about *sellers* (costs, technology, tax, weather)? Then which direction.
2. **Draw the shift** on a full D-and-S diagram: original equilibrium P1, Q1; new curve; new equilibrium P2, Q2, all labelled, with dotted lines.
3. **Narrate the adjustment**: shortage or surplus at the old price → price moves → extension along one curve and contraction along the other → new equilibrium.
4. **State the outcome for price and quantity** explicitly: "price rises from P1 to P2 and quantity rises from Q1 to Q2."
5. **For "effect on sales" questions**, note the price change first, then apply the law of demand, and — for an evaluation — mention that the size of the change depends on elasticity.
6. **Two shifts at once**: state what is certain and what depends on the relative size of the shifts.
:::

::: example Demand increases
Explain, using a diagram, the effect of a rise in incomes on the market for restaurant meals. (4)

Restaurant meals are a normal good, so higher incomes increase demand — the demand curve shifts right from D1 to D2 (1). At the original price there is now a shortage, so restaurants raise prices (1). Supply extends and demand contracts along D2 until a new equilibrium is reached at a higher price P2 and a higher quantity Q2 (2).
:::

::: example Supply decreases
Explain, using a diagram, the effect of a drought on the market for rice. (4)

The drought ruins harvests, so supply decreases and the supply curve shifts left from S1 to S2 (1). At the original price there is a shortage of rice, so price rises (1). Demand contracts along the demand curve and supply extends along S2 until the new equilibrium at a higher price and a lower quantity (2).
:::

::: example Supply increases — the effect on sales
New technology cuts the cost of producing solar panels. Analyse the effect on their price and sales. (6)

Lower costs increase supply: the supply curve shifts right, creating a surplus at the old price (2). Producers cut prices to clear the surplus; as price falls demand extends, so more panels are bought — sales rise and price settles at a new lower equilibrium (2). How far sales rise depends on demand: if buyers are very responsive to price (elastic demand), the fall in price brings a large rise in sales; if not, sales rise only a little even though price has fallen substantially (2).
:::

::: example Both curves shift
Demand for electric cars increases at the same time as new factories increase their supply. Explain the effect on equilibrium price and quantity. (4)

Both shifts raise quantity, so equilibrium quantity definitely increases (2). The demand shift pushes price up while the supply shift pushes it down, so the effect on price is uncertain — it depends on which shift is larger; if supply rises by more, price falls (2).
:::

::: q
State the effect on equilibrium price and quantity of a decrease in demand. (2)
---
Price falls (1) and quantity falls (1).
:::

::: q
State the effect on equilibrium price and quantity of an increase in supply. (2)
---
Price falls (1) and quantity rises (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a rise in the price of cocoa beans raises the price of chocolate. (4)
---
Cocoa is a raw material, so the cost of producing chocolate rises (1) and supply decreases — the curve shifts left (1). At the old price there is a shortage, so price is bid up; demand contracts until the new equilibrium at a higher price and lower quantity (2).
:::

::: q
Explain, using a diagram, the effect of a successful advertising campaign on the market for a brand of trainers. (4)
---
Advertising changes tastes, increasing demand: D shifts right (1). At the old price a shortage appears, so price rises (1); supply extends and the market settles at a higher price and higher quantity (2).
:::

::: q
Explain how a fall in price affects the sales of a product, and what determines the size of the effect. (4)
---
A lower price extends demand: more consumers can afford the product and some switch from substitutes, so sales rise (2). The size of the rise depends on the price elasticity of demand — sales rise proportionately more if demand is elastic (2).
:::

::: q
The demand for and supply of wheat both fall. Explain the effect on price and quantity. (4)
---
Both shifts reduce quantity, so equilibrium quantity certainly falls (2). Lower demand pushes price down while lower supply pushes it up, so the effect on price depends on which shift is larger (2).
:::

::: q
Analyse the effect on the market for face masks of a sudden outbreak of a respiratory disease. (6)
---
Demand for masks increases sharply as people try to protect themselves; the curve shifts right, creating a large shortage at the existing price (2). Sellers raise prices, which rations the limited masks and gives producers an incentive to expand output — supply extends and, over time, new firms enter, shifting supply right (2). In the short run price rises a lot because supply cannot respond quickly; as new production arrives the supply shift pushes price back down, so the long-run price rise is smaller than the initial one (2).
:::

::: q
Explain why a fall in the price of a good does not always mean demand for it has fallen. (2)
---
Price falls when supply increases, with demand unchanged (1); the lower price then extends demand — quantity bought actually rises (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Demand shift**: price and quantity move the **same** way. **Supply shift**: **opposite** ways. Learn the four-row table.
- Always give **both** price and quantity in the answer.
- After the shift, the adjustment is a **movement along** the other curve — use "extends"/"contracts".
- Diagrams need **P1, P2, Q1, Q2** and the direction of the shift labelled.
- With two shifts, say which variable is **certain** and which **depends on the relative size**.
:::

::: test
Explain, using a diagram, the effect on the market for ice cream of a hot summer. (4)
---
Demand increases, shifting the demand curve right (1); at the old price there is a shortage (1); price rises (1); supply extends and equilibrium quantity is higher (1).
:::

::: test
Explain the effect on the market for laptops of a fall in the price of computer chips. (3)
---
Lower costs increase supply — curve shifts right (1); equilibrium price falls (1); equilibrium quantity rises (1).
:::

::: test
The price of new houses rises sharply. Explain two possible causes. (4)
---
An increase in demand, e.g. lower interest rates making mortgages cheaper (2); a decrease in supply, e.g. a shortage of building land or higher building costs (2).
:::

::: test
Explain the effect on the market for bus travel of a rise in the price of petrol. (3)
---
Car travel becomes more expensive, so demand for the substitute, bus travel, rises (1); the demand curve shifts right (1); fare and passenger numbers both rise (1).
:::

::: test
Demand for a good increases while supply decreases. Explain the effect on price and quantity. (3)
---
Price definitely rises (1); the effect on quantity depends on the relative sizes of the shifts (1); it could rise, fall or stay the same (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why a bad harvest can raise farmers' total revenue. (3)
---
Supply falls, so price rises (1); demand for food is price inelastic (1); the percentage rise in price exceeds the percentage fall in quantity, so revenue rises (1).
:::

::: test
Analyse the effect on the market for smartphones of a new, cheaper competitor entering the market. (4)
---
Market supply increases (1); price falls (1); quantity bought rises (1); existing firms may lose sales or cut their prices to compete (1).
:::

::: test
Explain the effect on the market for taxis of a government subsidy to bus companies. (2)
---
Bus fares fall, so some passengers switch from taxis (1); demand for taxis decreases, lowering fares and journeys (1).
:::
