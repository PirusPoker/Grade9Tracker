### Production stages

A film goes through **development** (idea, rights, screenplay and finance, including **product-placement** deals), **pre-production** (casting, crew, locations, sets), **production** (shooting), **post-production** (editing, sound, visual effects), and **distribution** (marketing, release to cinemas, then home and streaming). **Exhibition** is showing the film in cinemas. Personnel matter too. Phoebe Waller-Bridge worked on the script, which was reported as a way to strengthen the female characters, and Billie Eilish's theme song gave the film **star appeal** with a young global music audience. The song went on to win an Oscar.

### Ownership, conglomerates and vertical integration

A **conglomerate** is a large corporation that owns businesses across different media and sometimes other industries. **Universal Pictures** is part of Comcast, which also owns US broadcasters and Sky in the UK. **Amazon** combines retail, streaming (Prime Video), cloud computing and, since buying MGM, a Hollywood studio with the Bond catalogue.

**Vertical integration** means one company owns several **stages of the supply chain**: production, distribution and exhibition. The advantages:
- **cost control and economies of scale**: fewer fees paid to other firms, and shared staff, studios and equipment;
- **guaranteed distribution and exhibition**: a studio that also owns a streaming service or cinemas knows its films will reach an audience;
- **control of release dates**, so its own blockbusters don't compete with each other;
- **ownership of intellectual property** across spin-offs, merchandise, games and TV;
- **data and marketing power** across all its platforms.

Streaming has revived vertical integration. Amazon now produces films, distributes them on Prime Video, and sells the Bond DVDs and merchandise, while Disney and Netflix produce and stream their own content. **Horizontal integration** is owning several companies at the **same** stage, e.g. a studio buying rival studios, as Amazon did with MGM.

### Convergence and cross-media promotion

**Convergence** means the coming together of media **technologies** (one device plays films, trailers, games and music) and of media **industries** (film companies working with music, games, fashion and car brands). A Bond release is a **cross-media event**:

- **The 007 website** acts as the franchise's hub. It offers news and features about the films, trailers and clips (often via YouTube), background on characters, cars and gadgets, links to the **official store** for merchandise, sign-up for updates, information on **experiences and partners**, and links to all the **social media** accounts (Instagram, X, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok). It keeps the whole franchise alive between films and pushes users across platforms.
- **Trailers and teasers** premiered online and on social media. The first teaser poster was released on **James Bond Day** (5 October, the anniversary of the first Bond film), which is a fan-focused marketing date.
- **The theme song** by Billie Eilish was released as a single, reaching music-streaming audiences months before the film opened.
- **Brand partnerships** (**synergy**): Aston Martin cars, Omega watches, Triumph motorbikes and Nokia phones appear in the film and in the partners' own advertising campaigns, which both helps to fund the film and multiplies the marketing.
- **Formats and platforms**: IMAX cinema, then DVD, Blu-ray, digital download and streaming (now Prime Video), plus TV rights.
- **Games, books and exhibitions** extend the brand into other media.

### Regulation: the BBFC

The **BBFC** (British Board of Film Classification) gives age ratings to films shown in UK cinemas and to physical and many online releases, using its published guidelines on violence, threat, language, sex and drugs. Local councils legally license cinemas, and they follow BBFC ratings. Producers often **aim for a rating**: a **12A** lets under-12s see the film with an adult, which widens a blockbuster's audience. Bond keeps its violence largely bloodless for this reason. Challenges include streaming services that release films straight online, and international releases that face different rules in each country.

### Global audiences

Bond is a **global** brand with an established **fan base** built over six decades, and it also targets a **mainstream mass audience** through spectacle, stars and a family-friendly rating. The franchise balances **nostalgia** for loyal fans (the DB5, the gun-barrel logo, the theme) with updates for a contemporary audience, such as more active female characters and a black female 00 agent. The global locations, international stars and worldwide release help the film sell in every market.

::: terms
- **Franchise**: a series of related media products built on the same brand or characters, such as the James Bond films.
- **Blockbuster**: a very high-budget film designed for a global mass audience and heavy marketing.
- **Conglomerate**: a large company that owns many businesses across different media or industries, such as Comcast or Amazon.
- **Vertical integration**: ownership by one company of different stages of production, distribution and exhibition.
- **Horizontal integration**: ownership by one company of several businesses at the same stage, such as a studio buying another studio.
- **Distribution**: the stage that gets a film to audiences, including marketing, release planning and selling rights to cinemas and platforms.
- **Exhibition**: showing a film to audiences, in cinemas or through home and streaming services.
- **Convergence**: the merging of media technologies and industries, so one product is promoted and consumed across many platforms.
- **Synergy**: two or more companies or products working together for mutual promotional benefit, such as a film and its brand partners.
- **Product placement**: payment by a brand to have its product shown in a film or programme.
- **BBFC**: the British Board of Film Classification, which gives age ratings to films released in the UK.
:::

::: example Briefly explain: vertical integration
Briefly explain the advantages of vertical integration for the film industry. (4)

Vertical integration means one company owns more than one stage of the supply chain, such as production, distribution and exhibition (1). Keeping these stages in-house cuts costs, because the company does not pay other firms to distribute or show its films and can share studios, staff and technology across many projects (1). It also guarantees that its films reach audiences and lets it control release dates, for example a company like Disney can place its own films on its own streaming service and avoid clashing with its other blockbusters (1). Owning every stage also means it keeps the intellectual property and the income from spin-offs, merchandise and streaming, as Amazon now does with the Bond catalogue after buying MGM (1).
:::

::: example Briefly explain: franchises
Briefly explain why film companies invest in franchises. (4)

A franchise has an established brand and fan base, so audiences already know what to expect, which lowers the financial risk of a very expensive film (1). Familiar elements such as the Bond theme, the gun-barrel logo and the Aston Martin can be used in marketing to attract both loyal fans and a global mass audience (1). Each new film renews interest in older ones, boosting streaming, DVD and merchandise sales across the whole catalogue (1). Franchises also attract brand partners willing to pay for product placement, which helps fund budgets of hundreds of millions of dollars (1).
:::

::: example Extended: convergence
Explain how the film industry uses convergence to promote films. Refer to the 007 website to support your points. (12)

Convergence is the merging of media technologies and industries, and a Bond release is promoted across many connected platforms rather than just by a poster and a trailer (1).

The **007 website** is the hub of this strategy. It gathers news, features and trailers for every film, so fans can move from No Time to Die to the whole franchise history, and this keeps interest alive between releases (2). Its links to Instagram, X, Facebook, YouTube and TikTok push users on to social platforms where content can be shared, reaching a global and younger audience beyond cinema-goers (2).

The site also shows **industrial convergence and synergy**. Pages on the cars, watches and gadgets link Bond with brand partners such as Aston Martin and Omega, whose own advertising promotes the film. The partners gain glamour and the film gains marketing money and exposure (2). The official store sells merchandise, turning the website into a source of income and extending the brand into fans' everyday lives (1).

Music and other media extend the campaign further. Billie Eilish's theme song was released well before the film and reached listeners on streaming services, and her star image attracted a young audience (2). Finally, **technological convergence** means the website, trailers and later the film itself can be accessed on phones, tablets, smart TVs and streaming services, now including Prime Video after Amazon bought MGM, so the franchise can be consumed anywhere (1). Convergence therefore lets the producers reach both loyal fans and a mass global audience and spread a huge budget's risk across many revenue streams (1).

This would reach the top band (10–12): accurate knowledge of convergence and detailed reference to the 007 website, with confident terminology.
:::

::: example Briefly explain: the pandemic
Briefly explain how the Covid-19 pandemic affected the release of No Time to Die. (4)

The film was due in cinemas in April 2020, but cinemas worldwide closed, so the release was postponed several times until autumn 2021 (1). Because the budget was so large, the producers needed a full global cinema release to earn back their investment, so they chose to wait rather than release it straight to streaming (1). The delays added costs, such as re-planned marketing campaigns and brand-partner promotions that had to be rescheduled (1). When it finally opened, the industry watched it as a test of whether audiences would return to cinemas in large numbers after the lockdowns (1).
:::
