## What it is

Justin Bieber's ***Intentions*** (2020, featuring the rapper **Quavo**) is the alternative to *Superheroes* for your **second** contemporary music video. The spec sets the **video** for **media language, representation and contexts**. His official website, **justinbiebermusic.com**, and his social media are studied for **all five areas**, so industry and audience points about Bieber come through the website. The video appears in **Q3 (20 marks)** with your first video (*Good as Hell* or *The Man*). In 2024 Q3 asked "How far do these two products challenge the conventions of music videos?" and in 2025 "How far do music videos represent social issues?"; *Intentions* was one of the options both times.

**Background.**
- *Intentions* was the second single from Bieber's fifth album, ***Changes*** (February 2020), released on **Def Jam** (part of **Universal Music Group**). The video premiered on his YouTube channel on the same day as the song.
- It was directed by **Michael D. Ratner** (OBB Media), who also made Bieber's YouTube docuseries *Justin Bieber: Seasons*.
- It was filmed at **Alexandria House**, a non-profit shelter in **Los Angeles** for women and children in crisis, and at a university library. The set version is the **short version**; a longer version adds interview voice-overs and the founder of the shelter explaining its mission.
- Bieber set up the **"Intentions Fund"** for the women and children of Alexandria House, donating 200,000 US dollars, and the video ends with an appeal to viewers to donate. Donations to the shelter rose in the days after release.

### What happens

The video follows **three women** connected to Alexandria House, each introduced with an on-screen name caption in a graffiti-style font before Bieber appears:
- **Bhari**, a student with a criminal-justice textbook, travelling for hours by bus and studying late, who wants to help others;
- **Marcy**, a young woman seen doing her make-up in the mirror and studying in the library;
- **Angela**, who helps homeless people on the streets of LA and campaigns on community radio.

Bieber and Quavo arrive at the shelter to cheers and hugs. They act as **helpers**: Bhari is given a **car**, Marcy and her friends **backpacks of school supplies**, and Angela **studio/air time**. Children dance on tables; residents dance in the street; everyone gathers for a group photo; a **thank-you** message and the fund's details close the video.

### Media language

- **A documentary hybrid.** Real people, real locations, name captions, handheld and observational shots and genuine emotional reactions give it a **documentary** style. It is partly **concept** and **narrative**, and only partly **performance**: Bieber and Quavo sing some sections in **direct address**, but much of the song works as a **soundtrack** to the women's stories.
- **Challenges conventions.** The women are the **stars**: they appear first, and Bieber does not appear until well into the video. Dance is **spontaneous and communal**, not choreographed; the settings are **not glamorous**; and the purpose is **charity**. It also keeps conventions: star close-ups, Bieber **centred** in a red hoodie singing to camera, and Quavo rapping at the centre of a group.
- **Narrative (Propp and Todorov).** Each woman is a **hero** with a goal ("intention"). Bieber and Quavo are **donors/helpers** who give them the means to succeed, though you could argue the video casts the stars as **rescuers** and so makes them the heroes. Todorov: struggle → help → a new equilibrium of hope.
- **Gesture codes.** **Hugs, smiles, high-fives** and hands on shoulders signify **support and community**; the high-five motif marks each woman's achievements; tears when the gifts arrive **demand an emotional response**.
- **Mise-en-scène and props.** The textbook and the library signify **education** as the route out of hardship; the bus journey at night shows the effort needed; the **"welcome" banner** and children's artwork make the shelter a home; the car, backpacks and radio studio are the "magical agents" of the story.
- **Typography.** The graffiti-style font for the title and names connotes the urban setting and the **hip-hop genre**, a signal to Quavo's fans.
- **Ending.** A posed "family" photo with Bieber at the centre and a thank-you message restate the purpose and include the stars in the community.

### Representation

- **Women.** Represented as **active, determined and ambitious** through study, independent travel and helping others. They are **not objectified** or glamorised, which counters a strong convention of pop and hip-hop videos. However, the shelter frames them as **victims in need** (of safety, of help from famous men), and the mirror scene may reinforce the idea of young women focused on appearance, or may show their insecurity. You can argue both ways.
- **Ethnicity and community.** An **ethnically diverse** community of women and children, many black and Latina, represented positively as supportive and strong, which challenges the **misrepresentation** of homeless and poor communities as problems.
- **Men.** Bieber and Quavo are **caring and emotionally open** (hugging the women and each other, hugging children), challenging hyper-masculine hip-hop stereotypes and suggesting **racial harmony** between a white and a black star.
- **Bieber's self-representation.** After a troubled public image in the mid-2010s, the video shows a **mature, caring, faith-driven** Bieber. **Critical readings** ask whether this is genuine philanthropy or **image rebuilding and promotion**. Critics noticed that he wears a hoodie from his own **Drew House** clothing brand, and the lyrics mention not needing a sponsor. Strong answers **weigh** charity against self-promotion.
- **Issues represented**: homelessness, domestic violence (implied by the shelter), access to **education**, poverty and community activism.

### Contexts

- **Celebrity philanthropy and "giving" videos.** It resembles Drake's *God's Plan* (2018), in which the rapper gives away money, and YouTubers' giveaway videos, reflecting a trend in which generosity is filmed and shared, sometimes criticised as performative.
- **2020.** Bieber posted support for **Black Lives Matter** and International Women's Day on Instagram; he and Quavo later performed an acoustic version for a Global Citizen fundraising event responding to the pandemic. The video reflects wider campaigns for **racial and gender equality**.
- **Genre and industry context.** Collaborations between pop and hip-hop stars (Quavo's biggest US hits have been collaborations with Bieber) widen audiences.

### justinbiebermusic.com, social media, industry and audiences

- **Industry.** Discovered through **YouTube** videos his mother posted, signed as a teenager and launched by the label's star-building machine, Bieber is a product of the platform era. He is on **Def Jam/Universal**; his management company became part of HYBE, the Korean company behind BTS, in 2021, showing **global conglomerate** ownership. **Convergence**: YouTube docuseries, YouTube channel, streaming, Drew House fashion brand, tours.
- **Website (credit any version).** A home page led by the current release, styled imagery, links to stream and watch, tour dates, a merchandise store, mailing list, and social links. Record your own dated examples.
- **Social media.** Instagram (one of the most followed accounts in the world), X, YouTube (for years the most-subscribed artist channel on YouTube Music), TikTok. For *Intentions* he posted a "big announcement" teaser the day before and a short trailer, then promoted the premiere. Fans (**"Beliebers"**) trend hashtags and share content.
- **Audiences.** He began with a **teenage girl** fanbase, which has grown up with him (now mostly 18–30), and his hip-hop collaborations widen his appeal. Uses and gratifications: *personal identity and catharsis* (the emotional stories), *entertainment* (dance, celebration), *information/education* (homelessness), *social interaction* (sharing, commenting, donating).

::: terms
- **Documentary style**: filming real people and places in an observational way, with captions and genuine reactions.
- **Philanthropy**: giving money or time to help others, especially by the wealthy or famous.
- **Performative generosity**: charity carried out publicly mainly so that it can be seen and praised.
- **Image rebuilding**: using media to repair a public figure's reputation after negative coverage.
- **Donor**: in Propp's theory, a character who gives the hero something that helps them succeed.
- **Gesture codes**: meanings created by body language, such as hugs and high-fives.
- **Non-profit organisation**: an organisation, such as a charity, that uses its income for its cause, not for profit.
- **Collaboration**: two artists working together on a track, often to combine and widen their audiences.
- **Beliebers**: the name for Justin Bieber's fans.
:::

::: example Documentary conventions
Explore how Intentions uses documentary conventions to create meaning. (8)

The video introduces real women from Alexandria House with on-screen name captions, a documentary convention that tells us these are real people, not actors (2). Observational shots of Bhari studying on a long bus journey at night show the real effort behind her goal, making her story authentic and moving (2). Reactions to Bieber and Quavo's gifts are filmed as they happen, with tears and hugs, which demands an emotional response from the audience (2). The closing thank-you and details of the Intentions Fund turn the video into an appeal, so the documentary style serves the purpose of raising awareness and money (2).
:::

::: example Charity or promotion?
How far is Intentions a charity video rather than a promotional one? (10)

In many ways it is a charity video. The women are the stars, introduced before Bieber appears, and their goals (study, activism, helping homeless people) drive the narrative (2). It raised awareness of Alexandria House, ended with an appeal to donate, and donations rose after release (2). Yet it is still a music video promoting a single from Changes. Bieber is framed centrally in a red hoodie singing to camera, and the stars appear as donors whose gifts transform the women's lives, which makes them the rescuers and the heroes (2). He wears his own Drew House brand, which critics saw as self-promotion (1). The video also helps rebuild his image as a mature, caring man after years of negative press (1). My judgement is that it does both at once: the charity is real, but the video's structure makes generosity part of the Bieber brand, reflecting a wider trend of filmed celebrity giving (2).
:::

::: example Representation of men
Explain how Intentions represents men. (4)

Bieber and Quavo are shown as caring and emotionally open, hugging the women, the children and each other (2), which challenges the tough, hyper-masculine image common in hip-hop videos. However, they are positioned as powerful benefactors whose gifts rescue the women, which can reinforce the idea of men as providers (2).
