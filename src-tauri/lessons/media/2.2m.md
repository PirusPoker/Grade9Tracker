## What it is

Duran Duran's ***Rio*** (1982) is one of the two options for the **music video from the past** (the other is TLC's *Waterfalls*). It lets you show how music videos, and their representations, **reflect the time in which they were made**. The question most likely to use it is **Q4 (10 marks)**, e.g. "How do music videos reflect the time in which they were made? Refer to Rio or Waterfalls" (2024). You can also use it in Q3 as a point of comparison to show how conventions have changed.

**The band and the song.**
- Duran Duran formed in **Birmingham** in 1978 (founders John Taylor and Nick Rhodes); by 1982 the line-up was Simon Le Bon (vocals), Nick Rhodes (keyboards), John Taylor (bass), Andy Taylor (guitar) and Roger Taylor (drums). They were a **new wave / synth-pop** band linked to the **New Romantic** scene, with a stylish, fashion-conscious image.
- *Rio* was the title track of their second album (**May 1982**) and was released as a single later that year, reaching the **UK top ten**. They were signed to **EMI**.
- The video was directed by **Russell Mulcahy** and shot on **film** in **Antigua**, in the Caribbean, on and around a racing **yacht**. It is one of the most famous videos of the early MTV era.

### What happens

There is **little plot**. The five band members, in pastel **designer suits**, sail a yacht across turquoise water, sing on deck, and compete for the attention of a glamorous woman, "Rio".
- It opens with **point-of-view shots** through a telescope or binoculars: **extreme close-ups** of a smiling woman looking into the lens. Fragmented images **reassemble** to reveal her sunbathing on a jetty, while a band member lies watching her through binoculars, focusing on her body.
- Rio walks out of the sea in a shot that recalls **Ursula Andress in the Bond film *Dr. No* (1962)**. The drummer approaches, is nipped by a crab, and she kicks him into the water.
- A **pink telephone** arrives on a **silver platter** to Rio on a raft and to the band on deck; when the singer tries to charm her, she yanks the cord and **pulls him into the sea**, then laughs.
- A **black-and-white dream sequence** shows a band member storming the beach with a rifle, only to end up beside the sunbathing Rio, whose champagne glass fills magically from above.
- In a final close-up Rio **winks at the camera**; the yacht heads home.

### Media language

- **Type.** A **concept/performance hybrid**: the band sing (lip-sync) to camera on the yacht, but the video is built around a mood (**glamour, escape, fun**) and loose comic vignettes rather than a story. It also borrows from the **spy film** and the **travel advert**.
- **Setting and iconography.** Turquoise sea, white beaches, a speeding yacht, champagne and cocktails, telephones delivered on silver trays by unseen hands. Together these signify a **playboy lifestyle** of **wealth, leisure and excess**.
- **Costume.** Brightly coloured, loose-fitting **designer suits** worn casually on a boat: stylish, playful, expensive, part of the band's **New Romantic** image.
- **Camerawork.** **POV shots** through binoculars construct a **voyeuristic** gaze; **extreme close-ups** of Rio's face and body; **aerial and tracking shots** of the yacht at speed give scale and energy; the band is framed as a group of friends having fun.
- **Editing.** Fast cutting to the beat, the fragmented image that reassembles, the switch to **black and white** for the dream sequence, and slow-motion shots of the yacht. For 1982 these were ambitious and cinematic.
- **Technology.** Shooting on **film** (not videotape) in an exotic location gave a **polished, cinematic** look that made the band stand out on television.
- **Intertextuality.** The *Dr. No* beach entrance links the band to **James Bond** glamour; the spy-style gadgets and dream sequence add to the parody.
- **Tone.** Knowing and **comic**: the band are repeatedly defeated by Rio, which undercuts their playboy pose.

### Representation

- **Men.** The band are represented as **young, rich, stylish playboys**, "well-travelled" and carefree, living the dream lifestyle of the early 1980s. They are also **playful and slightly ridiculous**, repeatedly rejected, which softens the image and appeals to fans.
- **Women: the male gaze.** Rio is presented first as an **object to be looked at**: the audience shares the band's binocular view of her body, and the camera keeps returning to her from many angles. With her tanned skin and body paint she is also made **exotic**, a fantasy figure associated with a faraway place. This fits **feminist** criticism of the **male gaze** in music videos.
- **But she answers back.** Rio kicks, pulls and laughs at the men and winks at the camera at the end, so she has a personality and **power** over them. She can be read as both **object and active subject**; the video both **upholds and subverts** the idea of the passive female.
- **Class and place.** A world of **privilege and leisure**, with servants unseen; the Caribbean is used as a **backdrop** for Western fantasy, and its local people are absent, an example of selective representation.

### Industry

- **Record label and investment.** EMI funded an **expensive shoot abroad** because a striking video was now an essential **marketing** tool. It was an investment in making the band stars in the **United States**, the world's largest music market.
- **MTV synergy.** **MTV** launched in the US in **1981** and needed videos to fill 24 hours of airtime; Duran Duran wanted exposure. Their glossy videos were played heavily, and MTV helped make them stars there, which in turn gave MTV glamorous content. This was **mutual promotion**.
- **Star image.** The video builds a **brand**: fashion, glamour and good looks. It was consistent across record sleeves, magazines, posters and TV.
- **UK distribution.** In Britain videos were shown on TV programmes such as *Top of the Pops*, and fans read about bands in pop magazines.

### Audiences

- **Then:** mainly **young fans**, especially teenage girls who followed the band as **pin-ups** through magazines and posters; viewers watching MTV or TV chart shows at scheduled times. The video offered **escapism**: several minutes of sunshine and luxury.
- **Now:** watched on **YouTube**, with nostalgia by older fans and as a period piece by new viewers; modern audiences may read the gaze on Rio far more critically.
- **Uses and gratifications:** *entertainment/escapism*; *personal identity* (fans copying the band's fashion); *social interaction* (talking about the video and the band).

### Contexts: how Rio reflects its time

- **Early MTV and the video boom**: videos as a new, essential marketing form; big budgets; film directors; exotic locations.
- **1980s consumerism and aspiration**: the Thatcher and Reagan years celebrated wealth and enterprise; the image of champagne, yachts and designer suits fits the rise of the **yuppie** and a culture of **excess**, even if the video also mocks it.
- **Fashion and youth culture**: the **New Romantic** movement's emphasis on style, dressing up and glamour.
- **Gender**: the **male gaze** was routine in early-1980s videos, but the continuing influence of **second-wave feminism** can be seen in Rio's refusal to be passive.
- **Ambition in America**: the band have said the "Rio" of the song stood for **America**, the market they wanted to conquer.
- **Film culture**: the Bond reference shows the influence of 1960s cinematic glamour.

::: terms
- **New Romantic**: an early-1980s British music and fashion movement known for flamboyant, stylish clothes and synth-pop.
- **MTV**: a US cable channel launched in 1981 that played music videos around the clock.
- **Concept video**: a music video built around an idea, mood or visual style rather than a clear story.
- **Point-of-view shot**: a shot showing what a character sees, as if through their eyes.
- **Voyeurism**: the pleasure of looking at someone who is unaware or presented as an object to be watched.
- **Male gaze**: a feminist term for media that positions the viewer as a heterosexual man looking at women as objects.
- **Escapism**: using media to escape from everyday life into a more exciting or glamorous world.
- **Yuppie**: a 1980s term for a young, ambitious, well-paid urban professional associated with conspicuous spending.
- **Synergy**: two or more media products or companies promoting each other, as MTV and the band did.
:::

::: method
1. For Q4 "reflect the time", organise by **context**: MTV and the video boom → 1980s wealth and consumerism → fashion/New Romantics → gender and the male gaze → the band's American ambitions.
2. For **each context**, give a **specific detail** of the video (yacht, champagne, silver-platter phones, designer suits, binocular POV, the *Dr. No* entrance, Rio's wink) and explain the link.
3. Use **terms**: concept video, POV shot, male gaze, intertextuality, escapism, synergy.
4. Show awareness of **change**: how a 2020 audience or video treats the same things differently (compare with *Good as Hell* or *The Man*).
5. Four or five **developed, accurate** points reach the top band.
:::

::: example Reflecting its time
How do music videos reflect the time in which they were made? Refer to Rio (Duran Duran, 1982) to support your points. (10)

Rio reflects the arrival of MTV in 1981, which needed videos to fill its schedule; the band's glossy, filmed videos were ideal and were played heavily, helping them break America (1). This explains the expensive shoot on a yacht in Antigua, filmed like a movie, because the video was now the main advert for the single (1). The iconography of champagne, cocktails and telephones delivered on silver platters constructs a playboy lifestyle that reflects the 1980s celebration of wealth and consumerism in the Thatcher and Reagan years (1), although the comic defeats of the band suggest it is also gently mocking that excess (1). The pastel designer suits reflect the New Romantic fashion culture, in which a band's style was as important as its sound (1). The opening binocular point-of-view shots of Rio's body show how routine the male gaze was in early-1980s videos (1), yet Rio kicking the drummer into the sea and pulling the singer in by the phone cord gives her power, which could reflect the influence of second-wave feminism (1). The beach entrance that recalls Ursula Andress in Dr. No links the band to Bond glamour, showing the pull of 1960s film culture (1). The band have said "Rio" stood for America, reflecting their ambition to succeed in the US market (1). Today a viewer on YouTube may read the gaze on Rio far more critically than audiences did in 1982, showing how interpretation changes over time (1).
:::

::: example Representation of women
Explain how Rio represents women. (6)

Rio is first presented through the band's binocular point of view, with close-ups of her body, so the audience is positioned to look at her as an object, which fits the male gaze (2). She is made exotic through her tanned skin, body paint and Caribbean setting, which makes her a fantasy figure (2). However, she rejects the men's advances, kicking one into the sea and pulling another in by the phone cord, and winks at the camera at the end, so she is also an active character who controls the situation (2).
:::

::: example Industry: MTV
Explain how MTV and Duran Duran benefited each other. (4)

MTV, launched in 1981, needed a constant supply of videos, and Duran Duran's expensive, cinematic videos were exactly the eye-catching content it wanted (2). In return, heavy play on MTV gave the band huge exposure in the US market, helping them become international stars: an example of synergy (2).
:::
