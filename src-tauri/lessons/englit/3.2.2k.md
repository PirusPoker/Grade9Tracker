## What it is

**War Photographer** (Carol Ann Duffy, 1985) watches a photographer in his darkroom developing pictures from a war zone, alone, in "Rural England". The poem's subject is the **distance between suffering and the people who consume images of it** — and the photographer, who stands between the two, pays for his detachment. The final stanza turns on the reader: the accusation is ours.

**The four stanzas.**

| Stanza | What happens | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The darkroom: "spools of suffering set out in ordered rows"; the red light like a church; the photographer "a priest preparing to intone a Mass". The place names: "Belfast. Beirut. Phnom Penh." Then: "All flesh is grass." | Religious imagery — the darkroom is a chapel, the photographer a priest, the developing a rite. The list of cities is a liturgy of war zones; "All flesh is grass" is Isaiah — the priest's text on mortality. "Ordered rows" is control imposed on chaos. |
| **2** | "He has a job to do." His hands "did not tremble then / though seem to now" — steady in the war, shaking at home. "Rural England" is contrasted with places "where the fields explode beneath the feet / of running children in a nightmare heat". | The delayed tremor — detachment worked in the field and fails in safety. "Ordinary pain" here is weather; there, fields explode (landmines). The contrast is geographical and moral. |
| **3** | A "half-formed ghost" appears in the developing tray — a dead man; the photographer remembers "the cries / of this man's wife", how "he sought approval / without words to do what someone must" and how "the blood stained into foreign dust". | The image develops as the memory does; "ghost" makes the photograph a haunting. "Someone must" — the justification for the job, and its guilt: he asked permission to photograph a dying man. |
| **4** | "A hundred agonies in black-and-white / from which his editor will pick out five or six / for Sunday's supplement." The reader's "eyeballs prick / with tears between the bath and pre-lunch beers". He flies out again "impassively"; "they do not care". | Arithmetic of indifference: a hundred reduced to five or six; a Sunday's tears lasting the length of a bath. The final line is the verdict on the readers — and Duffy's readers are those readers. |

**The photographer's detachment and its cost.** He needs not to feel in order to work — "He has a job to do"; his hands "did not tremble then" — and the poem shows the bill arriving afterwards, in the darkroom, in the tremor, in the ghost. He is the poem's centre of guilt: he photographed a man dying with the wife's cries in his ears, and he tells himself "someone must". Duffy neither condemns nor excuses him; she shows the price of being the person who looks so that others can glance.

**Religious imagery of the darkroom.** "The only light is red and softly glows, / as though this were a church and he / a priest preparing to intone a Mass." The developing is a sacrament; the photographs are the dead being raised ("a half-formed ghost"); "All flesh is grass" is scripture. The effect is to make his work a moral duty — and to raise the question of what the congregation (the readers) do with the sacrament. They look "between the bath and pre-lunch beers".

**Rural England against the war zones.** "Rural England. Home again / to ordinary pain which simple weather can dispel" — the alliterating softness of a country where pain is weather. Against it: "fields explode beneath the feet / of running children" — the same word, *fields*, meaning a landmine. Duffy makes the contrast in one noun.

**The reader as the real target.** The poem spends three stanzas on the photographer and its last on his audience: the editor who picks "five or six", the reader whose eyes "prick with tears" for the duration of a Sunday morning. The final stanza's flat "they do not care" is aimed past the photographer at the person holding the supplement — who is also the person holding the poem. An answer that stops at the photographer's suffering has missed the accusation.

**Form.** Four regular six-line stanzas, rhyming ABBCDD — control, order, the "ordered rows" of the spools; against it, enjambment that lets memory spill ("the cries / of this man's wife"). The regularity is the photographer's professional composure; the content is what it holds down.

**Context in a sentence.** Duffy was thinking of the photographers Don McCullin and Philip Jones Griffiths, whose war pictures appeared in Sunday colour supplements; the poem asks what such images do to those who take them and those who consume them.

::: method
1. **Darkroom as church**: quote the priest simile and say what it makes the job — and what it asks of the congregation.
2. **Then/now**: "did not tremble then / though seem to now" — detachment working abroad, failing at home.
3. **The ghost stanza**: the photograph and the memory developing together; "someone must".
4. **The last stanza turns on the reader** — the arithmetic (a hundred → five or six), the bath and beers, "they do not care".
5. **Form as composure**: regular stanzas holding disordered content.
:::

::: example The darkroom
How does Duffy present the photographer's work in stanza 1? Build the paragraph.

Point: Duffy makes the darkroom a church so that the photographer's work becomes a rite and his detachment a vocation.

Evidence: "as though this were a church and he / a priest preparing to intone a Mass"; the list "Belfast. Beirut. Phnom Penh."; "All flesh is grass."

Analysis: the red light, the solitude and the ritual of developing are sacramental; the cities, full-stopped, are a litany; and "All flesh is grass" (Isaiah) is the priest's text on mortality. "Spools of suffering set out in ordered rows" — the horror is filed. The order is what allows him to function.

Purpose: if the work is a Mass, the readers are the congregation — and Duffy will show, in the final stanza, how briefly they kneel.
:::

::: example Then and now
Analyse "his hands, which did not tremble then / though seem to now".

In the war zone the hands were steady: the photographer's professional detachment let him frame a dying man while his wife screamed. In "Rural England", in safety, the hands shake.

The line break — "then / though seem to now" — separates the two states as the poem separates the two places. Detachment is not the absence of feeling but its postponement; the tremor is the feeling arriving late.

Duffy shows the cost of being the one who looks: he cannot feel it there, because the job requires him not to, and he cannot stop feeling it here. The reader, by contrast, will feel it for the length of a bath.
:::

::: example The ghost
How does Duffy use the image developing in stanza 3?

"Something is happening. A stranger's features / faintly start to twist before his eyes, / a half-formed ghost." The photograph appears in the tray as the memory appears in his mind — the two develop at once.

"Ghost" turns the picture into a haunting; the man is dead, and the chemical process raises him. The memory that comes with him is sound — "the cries / of this man's wife" — which the photograph cannot hold and the photographer cannot lose.

"He sought approval / without words to do what someone must": he asked, by a look, for permission to photograph a dying man, and answered his own guilt with necessity. Duffy leaves "someone must" unargued — the justification and the wound are the same sentence.
:::

::: example The accusation
Explain how the final stanza turns on the reader.

"A hundred agonies in black-and-white / from which his editor will pick out five or six / for Sunday's supplement." The arithmetic is the indifference: a hundred deaths become a spread, chosen for layout.

The reader's response is timed: "eyeballs prick / with tears between the bath and pre-lunch beers" — grief slotted between two comforts, over before the drink. "Prick" is small and physical; the tears are a reflex, not a change.

Then "they do not care", and the photographer flies out again "impassively". Duffy's readers in 1985 were the readers of Sunday supplements; her readers now are anyone who scrolls past a war. The photographer's guilt was earned by looking; ours is earned by looking away.
:::

::: watch
- The last stanza's target is the **reader** — an answer that stops at the photographer's pain has missed it.
- **Darkroom as church** needs the consequence: readers as a congregation who barely kneel.
- **Then/now** tremor: detachment postponed, not absent.
- "Fields" — one word, two worlds: **use it**.
- The arithmetic — a hundred to five or six — is the **indifference**; quote the numbers.
:::
