## What it is

The poetry question names **one poem**, prints it, and asks you to compare it with **one other of your choice** from the fifteen. The choice is made in the exam under pressure, so make it now: for every poem, know two or three partners and the ground on which they meet. A well-planned pair saves five minutes of panic and produces a comparison built on a real shared idea rather than a forced one.

**Grouping by theme.** Most poems belong to more than one group — that is what makes them pairable.

| Theme | Poems | The shared question |
|---|---|---|
| **Power of humans** (rulers, institutions, control) | Ozymandias, London, My Last Duchess, Checking Out Me History, Tissue | How power is exercised, and how it fails or is exposed |
| **Power of nature** | Ozymandias (the sand), Extract from The Prelude, Storm on the Island, Exposure, Kamikaze (the sea) | Nature bigger than human confidence; nature as attacker or as pull |
| **Effects of conflict on those who fight** | Charge of the Light Brigade, Exposure, Bayonet Charge, Remains, Kamikaze | Duty, instinct, futility, the individual inside the machine |
| **Effects of conflict on those left behind** | Poppies, War Photographer, Remains (the home half), Kamikaze (the family) | Grief, witness, guilt at a distance |
| **Reality of war vs glory** | Charge (both), Exposure, Bayonet Charge, War Photographer | What the propaganda says and what the poem shows |
| **Identity and memory** | The Émigrée, Checking Out Me History, Kamikaze, Poppies, Tissue | Who you are when a place, a history or a person is taken |
| **Loss and grief** | Poppies, Kamikaze, Remains, War Photographer, The Émigrée | What remains after the loss — memory, guilt, silence |
| **Guilt** | Remains, War Photographer, Kamikaze (the daughter), My Last Duchess (the absence of it) | Living with what one did or witnessed |
| **Pride, patriotism and duty** | Charge, Bayonet Charge, Kamikaze, Exposure, Ozymandias | Ideals tested and dropped |
| **Place and displacement** | The Émigrée, Kamikaze, London, Exposure ("Rural England") | Home as memory, exile, or the place shut against you |

**Grouping by form** — a comparison of methods earns AO2, so know who shares a form:

| Form | Poems |
|---|---|
| **Dramatic monologue / single speaker addressing someone** | My Last Duchess (Duke to envoy), London (speaker walking), Remains (soldier's testimony), Poppies (mother to son), Storm on the Island (islander to visitor), Checking Out Me History (speaker to "dem") |
| **Sonnet** | Ozymandias (broken) |
| **Blank verse** | Extract from The Prelude, Storm on the Island |
| **Regular rhymed stanzas** | London (ABAB quatrains), Charge (dactylic, refrain), Exposure (pararhyme), War Photographer (ABBCDD), My Last Duchess (couplets) |
| **Free verse** | Bayonet Charge, Poppies, Tissue, The Émigrée (loose), Kamikaze, Remains (loose quatrains) |
| **Second-hand / framed narration** | Ozymandias (traveller), Kamikaze (daughter), War Photographer (third person), Bayonet Charge (third person) |
| **Refrain** | Charge ("the six hundred"), Exposure ("But nothing happens"), The Émigrée ("sunlight"), Checking Out Me History ("Dem tell me") |
| **Circular structure** | Exposure, Ozymandias (sand at both ends of time), Remains (the image returning) |

**Pre-planned pairs** — for each named poem, two you could sensibly choose and the ground:

- **Ozymandias** → *My Last Duchess* (power, art, pride; a monument vs a portrait) · *Tissue* (permanence vs fragility) · *London* (power over people)
- **London** → *Checking Out Me History* (institutions controlling people; the speaker's voice) · *Ozymandias* (power exposed) · *Tissue* (structures)
- **The Prelude** → *Storm on the Island* (confidence to fear; nature's scale; blank verse) · *Exposure* (nature as antagonist) · *Kamikaze* (nature's pull)
- **My Last Duchess** → *Ozymandias* (ego, art, control) · *Checking Out Me History* (one voice controlling another's story)
- **Charge of the Light Brigade** → *Exposure* (glory vs futility; outside vs inside) · *Bayonet Charge* (the charge from within) · *Remains* (obeying orders, aftermath)
- **Exposure** → *Charge* · *Bayonet Charge* (soldier's experience; nature and war) · *Storm on the Island* (weather as enemy)
- **Storm on the Island** → *The Prelude* · *Exposure* · *Ozymandias* (human structures vs nature)
- **Bayonet Charge** → *Exposure* · *Charge* · *Remains* (instinct and its aftermath)
- **Remains** → *War Photographer* (guilt, memory, witness) · *Poppies* (aftermath at home) · *Bayonet Charge* · *Kamikaze* (living with survival)
- **Poppies** → *War Photographer* (those who watch) · *Kamikaze* (family and loss) · *Remains*
- **War Photographer** → *Remains* · *Poppies* · *Exposure* (England vs the front)
- **Tissue** → *Ozymandias* · *London* · *The Émigrée* (light; borders)
- **The Émigrée** → *Kamikaze* (memory, belonging, family) · *Checking Out Me History* (identity vs an imposed version) · *Tissue*
- **Checking Out Me History** → *The Émigrée* · *London* · *My Last Duchess*
- **Kamikaze** → *Poppies* · *The Émigrée* · *Exposure* (duty vs home) · *Remains*

**How to choose in the exam.** Read the question's focus word (power, nature, memory, conflict's effects, identity). Pick the partner where the *focus* is central, not incidental — do not pair Ozymandias with Poppies on "power" because you like Poppies. Prefer a partner that **shares a method** (both monologues, both refrains) *and* offers a **contrast** (Tennyson honours obedience; Owen shows its cost) — comparison needs likeness and difference.

::: terms
- **Comparison**: discussing two poems together, showing how they are similar and different.
- **Anthology question**: Paper 2 Section B, which prints one Power and Conflict poem and asks you to compare it with another of your choice.
- **Comparative connectives**: words that link poems — "similarly", "whereas", "in contrast", "likewise".
- **Theme**: a central idea explored in a poem — power, conflict, memory, identity.
- **Choosing a partner poem**: pick one that allows both clear links and useful differences in method.
:::

::: method
1. **Learn three groups per poem** — it is in the "power of nature" group *and* the "power of humans" group *and* the "form: sonnet" group.
2. **For each poem, fix two partners and the ground** now, in a sentence each: "Ozymandias with My Last Duchess: both about men who tried to own through art."
3. **Choose by the question's focus**, not by which poem you know best.
4. **Likeness and difference**: pick partners that share a method and differ in attitude.
5. **Rehearse the first sentence** of the comparison for your top five pairs.
:::

::: example Choosing a partner
The named poem is Exposure and the question is about the power of nature. Choose and justify.

Storm on the Island: both make weather the attacker with military language — Owen's "merciless iced east winds that knive us", Heaney's "salvo" and "bombarded" — and both end in a fear of something that cannot be fought ("nothing happens", "a huge nothing").

The difference: Heaney's islanders are at home and prepared; Owen's soldiers are abroad and abandoned. Heaney's storm passes; Owen's is the war.

Alternative if the question were about the effects of conflict: Bayonet Charge or the Charge of the Light Brigade instead — the partner changes with the focus word.
:::

::: example A pair built on form
Name two poems that share a form and say what the shared form lets you compare.

My Last Duchess and Remains are both monologues in which the speaker reveals more than he intends. The Duke intends refinement and reveals murder; the soldier intends an anecdote — "On another occasion" — and reveals trauma.

The comparison writes itself as method: both poets use a single, self-revealing voice; the Duke's is controlled couplets, the soldier's is loose colloquial quatrains. What each speaker cannot say — the Duke's guilt (absent), the soldier's guilt (everywhere) — is the contrast.

Pairing by form gives AO2 marks for free, because the "how" is shared and the "why" differs.
:::

::: example A pair built on contrast
Choose a pair where the attitudes oppose.

The Charge of the Light Brigade and Exposure: both about soldiers obeying orders in a war where the order was the enemy's best weapon. Tennyson, at home and Laureate, honours the obedience — "Theirs but to do and die"; Owen, in the trench, shows what it costs — "We turn back to our dying".

The likeness is the subject — duty, and men who cannot "reason why"; the difference is the writer's position and purpose. One writes to make a nation proud of a blunder; the other writes to tell the nation the truth.

A comparison of this pair can move between them in every paragraph — same theme, opposite verdicts — which is exactly what the mark scheme wants.
:::

::: example A pair built on a shared image
Find a shared image across two poems and make it a comparison.

Light: The Émigrée's "sunlight" (memory that cannot be corrected) and Tissue's light "shining through" (truth that comes when structures are thin). Both poets make light the thing that survives — a city, a monolith — and both end with the speaker as the light's evidence or vessel: "my shadow falls as evidence of sunlight"; "turned into your skin".

The difference: Rumens's light is personal memory holding out against a state; Dharker's is a proposal for how everything could be built.

An image shared across two poems is the cleanest ground for comparison, because you can analyse the same word twice and get two meanings.
:::

::: q
Name three poems in which nature is more powerful than people.
---
Extract from The Prelude, Storm on the Island, Exposure (also Ozymandias's sand, Kamikaze's sea).
:::

::: q
Name three poems about the effects of conflict on those who did not fight.
---
Poppies, War Photographer, Kamikaze (the family); Remains in its second half.
:::

::: q
Which poems are dramatic monologues?
---
My Last Duchess, London, Remains, Poppies, Storm on the Island, Checking Out Me History.
:::

::: q
Which poems use a refrain?
---
Charge of the Light Brigade ("the six hundred"), Exposure ("But nothing happens"), The Émigrée ("sunlight"), Checking Out Me History ("Dem tell me").
:::

::: q
Name two sensible partners for Ozymandias.
---
My Last Duchess (power, art, pride) and Tissue (permanence vs fragility) — or London.
:::

::: q
Name two sensible partners for Kamikaze.
---
Poppies (family and loss) and The Émigrée (memory and belonging) — or Exposure (duty vs home).
:::

::: q
Which two poems are in blank verse?
---
Extract from The Prelude and Storm on the Island.
:::

::: q
Which poems are told at second hand or through a frame?
---
Ozymandias (a traveller), Kamikaze (a daughter), War Photographer and Bayonet Charge (third person).
:::

::: q
What decides which partner poem to choose?
---
The question's focus word — pick the poem where that focus is central, and that shares a method while differing in attitude.
:::

::: q
Why plan pairs before the exam?
---
The choice is made under time pressure; pre-planned pairs with a known ground save minutes and avoid forced comparisons.
:::

::: watch
- Every poem belongs to **more than one group** — learn three per poem.
- Choose by the **focus word**, not by your favourite poem.
- Best pairs share a **method** and differ in **attitude**.
- Know the **form groups** — monologue, refrain, blank verse, free verse — for AO2.
- Rehearse the **first sentence** of your top five pairings.
:::

---

:::

::: test
Write the first sentence of a comparison of Exposure and Bayonet Charge on the reality of war. (2)
---
Both poets strip war of glory by showing the soldier's body rather than his cause (1); Owen's men freeze while nothing happens, Hughes's man runs while thought is abandoned (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why the best comparison partners share a method but differ in attitude. (2)
---
A shared method (monologue, refrain, personification) gives real ground for AO2 comparison (1); a different attitude gives the essay an argument rather than a list of similarities (1).
:::
