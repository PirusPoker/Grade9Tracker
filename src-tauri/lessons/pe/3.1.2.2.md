## What it is

Planes and axes are a way of describing **which direction** a body or body part moves and **what it turns around**.
- A **plane** is an imaginary **flat sheet** passing through the body, splitting it into two halves. Movement happens **in** a plane.
- An **axis** is an imaginary **line** passing through the body. The body or limb rotates **about** (around) an axis.

Every movement happens **in one plane and about one axis**, and they always come in the same three pairs. The axis always runs **at right angles** to its plane, like the axle through a wheel.

### The three planes

| Plane | How it divides the body | Movements that happen in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Sagittal plane** | into **left and right** halves (a sheet running from front to back, through the nose and belly button) | **flexion and extension**: forwards and backwards movements |
| **Frontal plane** | into **front and back** halves (a sheet running from side to side, through the ears and shoulders) | **abduction and adduction**: sideways movements |
| **Transverse plane** | into **top and bottom** halves (a horizontal sheet through the waist) | **rotation**: turning movements |

### The three axes

| Axis | Which way it runs | Picture it as |
|---|---|---|
| **Transverse axis** | from **side to side** (left to right) through the body | a pole through both hips, like the bar a gymnast swings around |
| **Sagittal axis** | from **front to back** through the body | a pole through the belly button and out of the back |
| **Longitudinal axis** | from **head to toe** (top to bottom) | a pole running down through the top of the head, like a spinning top |

### The pairs, and the actions to learn

The pairs **cross over**. They do not match by name, except that "transverse" appears in two different pairs:

| Plane | Axis | Movement type | Spec examples | Other examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sagittal** | **Transverse** | flexion / extension | **front somersault**, **forward roll**, **running action** | biceps curl, squat, kicking a ball, sit-up, a tuck jump, a chest pass |
| **Frontal** | **Sagittal** | abduction / adduction | **cartwheel** | star jump, lateral raise of the arms, side bends, a goalkeeper diving sideways |
| **Transverse** | **Longitudinal** | rotation | **360° twist** (e.g. an ice-skating spin), **discus thrower rotating** in the circle | pirouette in dance, full twist on a trampoline, hammer throw, a golfer's hip and trunk turn |

A quick check for any movement: ask **"which way is it moving?"**
- **Forwards or backwards** (like a wheel rolling forwards): sagittal plane, transverse axis.
- **Sideways** (like a door swinging open towards the side): frontal plane, sagittal axis.
- **Turning on the spot** (like a spinning top): transverse plane, longitudinal axis.

**Working through the spec actions:**
- **Front somersault / forward roll**: the whole body rotates forwards, head over heels. It moves in the **sagittal plane**, rotating about a **transverse axis** running through the hips from side to side.
- **Running action**: the legs swing forwards and back (hip and knee flexion and extension), and the arms swing forwards and back at the shoulder. **Sagittal plane, transverse axis.**
- **Cartwheel**: the body rotates sideways, hands then feet. **Frontal plane, sagittal axis** running through the belly button.
- **360° twist / ice-skating spin**: the body turns on the spot. **Transverse plane, longitudinal axis** running head to toe.
- **Discus thrower rotating in the circle**: the thrower turns round before release. **Transverse plane, longitudinal axis.**

**Applying to unfamiliar actions.** The exam often picks an action you have not seen listed. For a **star jump**, the arms and legs move out to the side and back in, which is abduction and adduction at the shoulder and hip. So it is the **frontal plane** and **sagittal axis**. For a **biceps curl**, the elbow flexes and extends forwards and back, which is the **sagittal plane** and **transverse axis**. A question may name one joint ("at the hip"), so analyse only that joint's movement.

::: terms
- **Plane of movement**: an imaginary flat surface that divides the body into two halves, in which movement takes place.
- **Axis of movement**: an imaginary line through the body about which the body or a body part rotates.
- **Sagittal plane**: a plane dividing the body into left and right halves; flexion and extension occur in it.
- **Frontal plane**: a plane dividing the body into front and back halves; abduction and adduction occur in it.
- **Transverse plane**: a plane dividing the body into top and bottom halves; rotation occurs in it.
- **Transverse axis**: an axis running from side to side through the body, paired with the sagittal plane.
- **Sagittal axis**: an axis running from front to back through the body, paired with the frontal plane.
- **Longitudinal axis**: an axis running from head to toe through the body, paired with the transverse plane.
:::

::: method
1. **Identify the movement type** in the action, or at the named joint: flexion/extension, abduction/adduction or rotation.
2. **Choose the pair**: flexion/extension = sagittal plane + transverse axis; abduction/adduction = frontal plane + sagittal axis; rotation = transverse plane + longitudinal axis.
3. **Write the plane first, then the axis**, and label each ("plane: frontal; axis: sagittal"), so they cannot be read the wrong way round.
4. **If asked to explain**, describe how the plane divides the body and the direction the axis runs, then link it to what the performer does.
5. **Longer questions**: break a routine or skill into its parts, give the pair for each part, and explain how the movement in that plane helps the performance.
:::

::: example Explain the pairing
Explain why a cartwheel takes place in the frontal plane. (2)

The frontal plane divides the body into front and back halves (1); in a cartwheel the body rotates sideways, with the arms and legs moving away from and towards the midline, so the movement stays within that sideways plane (1).
:::

::: example Analyse a routine
A trampolinist performs a routine including a tuck jump, a full twist and a front somersault. Analyse the planes and axes used in each skill. (6)

In the tuck jump, the hips and knees flex to bring the knees to the chest and then extend. These are flexion and extension, so the movement is in the sagittal plane about a transverse axis (AO1/AO2). In the full twist, the trampolinist spins on the spot while in the air, which is rotation, so it is the transverse plane about the longitudinal axis running head to toe (AO2). Keeping the body long and straight keeps it close to that axis, so the twist is fast and controlled and the landing faces the right way, which the judges reward (AO3). The front somersault is a forward rotation of the whole body, head over heels, in the sagittal plane about the transverse axis through the hips (AO2). Tucking tightly brings the body closer to that axis, so the rotation is quicker and the trampolinist completes it with time to open out and land on their feet (AO3). Using all three skills shows control in more than one plane, which increases the variety of the routine (AO3).

Level 3 (5–6): all three pairs correct and applied, with analysis of how the body position affects the quality of the skill.
:::

::: watch
- **The pairs cross over.** Sagittal **plane** goes with the transverse **axis**; frontal **plane** with the sagittal **axis**; transverse **plane** with the longitudinal **axis**.
- Many students give the plane and axis the **wrong way round**. Label them clearly: "Plane: … Axis: …".
- "Transverse" appears twice, as a plane (rotation) and as an axis (somersaults). Read which one the question wants.
- A **cartwheel** is frontal/sagittal, not sagittal/transverse. It is a sideways rotation, not a forward one.
- If the question names **one joint**, analyse only that joint's movement (the hip in a star jump is abduction/adduction, so frontal/sagittal).
:::

::: test
Analyse the planes and axes of movement used by a discus thrower from the start of the throw to release. (6)
---
AO1 (1): knowledge of the three plane–axis pairs and the movement type in each.
AO2 (2): application: the thrower turns in the circle — transverse plane, longitudinal axis; the hips and trunk rotate ahead of the throwing arm — transverse/longitudinal; the knees flex and extend to drive upward — sagittal plane, transverse axis; the throwing arm is swung out and around at the shoulder in a horizontal sweep.
AO3 (3): analysis: rotating about the longitudinal axis builds up speed over the turn so the discus is released faster and travels further; keeping the body upright and balanced over that axis stops the thrower falling out of the circle (a foul); the leg drive in the sagittal plane adds height and power at release; combining the planes efficiently determines the distance; a reasoned conclusion on which movement matters most.
Levels: 5–6 — accurate pairs; clearly applied to the phases of the throw; thorough analysis of the effect on distance. 3–4 — mostly accurate pairs; some application; analysis limited. 1–2 — some correct terms but little application or analysis; pairs confused. 0 — nothing worthy of credit.
