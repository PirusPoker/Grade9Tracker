## What it is

This lesson covers the first bullet of Part three, **The experiences of Germans under the Nazis: economic changes**. You need the **benefits and drawbacks** of Nazi economic policy, **employment**, **public works programmes**, **rearmament** and **self-sufficiency**, and the **impact of the Second World War on the economy and the German people**, including **bombing, rationing, labour shortages and refugees**.

The big idea: the Nazis promised "work and bread" and delivered work, but the economy was built for war, not for the people. Workers had jobs and some leisure perks, but lost their unions, worked longer and bought less than they hoped. After 1939 the war economy brought rationing, then total war, bombing and flight.

### 1. Reducing unemployment, 1933–39

Unemployment was about **six million** when Hitler took power. By the late 1930s official figures showed only a few hundred thousand, and by 1939 there was a **labour shortage**. How?

| Method | What it involved |
|---|---|
| **Public works** | Building **autobahns** (motorways), thousands of kilometres by 1939, plus public buildings, housing and land drainage. Labour-intensive methods used many men |
| **The Reich Labour Service (RAD)** | Young men did manual work on public projects; from **1935** six months' service was **compulsory for men aged 18–25**. Low pay, uniforms, camps and drill |
| **Rearmament** | Huge spending on tanks, aircraft and warships from 1933, rising sharply after 1936, created jobs in steel, coal, chemicals and engineering |
| **Conscription (from 1935)** | Military service took hundreds of thousands of young men out of the job market; the army grew to over a million by 1939 |
| **Removing people from the figures** | "**Invisible unemployment**": Jews dismissed from jobs, women encouraged to leave work, and men in the RAD or the army were **not counted** as unemployed |

So the fall was real but **exaggerated**, and much of it came from preparation for war.

### 2. Schacht, Göring and self-sufficiency

- **Hjalmar Schacht** (President of the Reichsbank from 1933, Economics Minister 1934–37) managed early recovery. His **New Plan (1934)** controlled imports and made trade deals, especially with countries in south-eastern Europe, in which Germany swapped manufactured goods for raw materials. He found ways to fund rearmament without obvious inflation.
- **Hermann Göring's Four Year Plan (1936)** aimed to make Germany ready for war within four years. Its aim was **autarky** (self-sufficiency): producing everything Germany needed so it could not be blockaded as in 1914–18.
  - Increase production of raw materials and invent **ersatz** (substitute) goods such as **synthetic oil from coal** and **artificial rubber**.
  - Control prices, wages and investment, and prioritise **guns over butter**.
- **Results:** rearmament surged, but autarky **failed**: by 1939 Germany still relied on imports for much of its oil, rubber and other raw materials. Schacht, who warned about the cost, resigned as Economics Minister in 1937. One reason Hitler wanted conquest was to seize the resources Germany could not produce.

### 3. Workers: benefits and drawbacks

Trade unions were abolished in May 1933 and replaced by the **German Labour Front (DAF)** under **Robert Ley**, which controlled workers and employers.

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| **Jobs**: steady work after the Depression | **No unions**; strikes illegal; no right to bargain |
| **Strength through Joy (KdF)**: cheap holidays, cruises, theatre trips, concerts and sports for workers | **Wages** were frozen at low levels while **hours rose**; many worked longer weeks by the late 1930s |
| **Beauty of Labour**: campaigns to improve factories: canteens, lighting, washrooms | Workers often did the improvements themselves, unpaid |
| **The Volkswagen ("people's car") scheme**: workers paid weekly into a savings scheme for a car | **No worker received a car**: the factory switched to military vehicles when war came, and the money was not returned |
| Some **real wage** recovery from the depths of 1932 for those in armaments work | Food prices rose; shortages of butter, meat and imported goods as resources went to rearmament |

**Other groups:** **big business** gained (no unions, rearmament contracts) but lost freedom to decide prices and investment; **small businesses** were squeezed by big firms and controls; **farmers** got security of land (Reich Food Estate, inheritance laws) but strict controls and a drift of workers to the towns.

### 4. The impact of the Second World War on the economy and people

| Aspect | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Rationing** | Introduced at the very start of the war in **1939** for food, then clothing; rations were cut as the war went on; a **black market** grew |
| **Total war** | Early victories meant the economy was not fully mobilised until **1942–43**. **Albert Speer**, Armaments Minister from **1942**, increased arms production sharply. After the defeat at **Stalingrad (early 1943)** Goebbels declared **"total war"**: non-essential businesses closed, more women and older men drafted into work |
| **Labour shortages** | Millions of men were in the forces. Nazi ideology slowed the recruitment of women, but from 1943 women had to register for work. The gap was filled by **forced and slave labour**: millions of foreign workers and prisoners of war, and concentration camp prisoners, worked in German factories and farms in brutal conditions |
| **Bombing** | British and American air raids intensified from 1942. The raids on **Hamburg (1943)** and **Dresden (February 1945)** each killed tens of thousands of people in firestorms. Hundreds of thousands of civilians died across Germany; millions were made homeless; factories and transport were disrupted |
| **Refugees** | Bombed-out families moved to the countryside; children were evacuated. In 1944–45 **millions of Germans fled west** as the Soviet army advanced through eastern Germany |

**Overall:** the war economy produced enormous quantities of weapons late in the war, but the German people's living standards collapsed. By 1945 cities were in ruins, food was scarce and millions were homeless or displaced.

::: terms
- **Public works**: state-funded building projects such as the autobahns, used to create jobs.
- **Reich Labour Service (RAD)**: the organisation giving young men manual work; compulsory for six months for men aged 18–25 from 1935.
- **Invisible unemployment**: unemployed people left out of official figures, such as Jews, women pushed out of work and men in the RAD or army.
- **Rearmament**: rebuilding Germany's armed forces in breach of the Treaty of Versailles, which created jobs in heavy industry.
- **Autarky**: economic self-sufficiency, the aim of the Four Year Plan, so that Germany could fight a war without imports.
- **Four Year Plan**: Göring's plan of 1936 to prepare the economy for war within four years.
- **Ersatz goods**: substitute products such as synthetic oil or rubber made in Germany to replace imports.
- **German Labour Front (DAF)**: the Nazi organisation under Robert Ley that replaced trade unions and controlled workers.
- **Strength through Joy (KdF)**: the DAF scheme offering workers cheap holidays, outings and leisure activities.
- **Beauty of Labour**: the DAF campaign to improve working conditions in factories.
- **Total war**: the full mobilisation of the economy and people for war, declared by Goebbels in 1943.
:::

::: method
**Paper 1 Section A at a glance.** About an hour, 40 marks: Q1 differ (4), Q2 why differ (4), Q3 more convincing (8), Q4 describe two (4), Q5 in what ways (8), Q6 which bullet (12). Interpretations on whether workers were better off are a favourite: AQA used one in 2023.

1. **Q1 (4): content only.** E.g. one interpretation says workers were controlled and exploited, the other that their lives improved.
2. **Q2 (4): provenance + context.** A Hitler Youth member who never knew pre-1933 working life; a foreign visitor judging by democratic standards; a DAF official writing propaganda; a historian with statistics on wages and hours.
3. **Q3 (8): test both views.** Jobs, KdF, Beauty of Labour **against** frozen wages, longer hours, no unions, the Volkswagen that never came, invisible unemployment. A top answer argues experience depended on which industry and which period.
4. **Q4 (4): two features with precise detail.**
5. **Q5 (8): effects on workers or people; Level 4 shows how they differed** by group (armaments workers versus consumer-goods workers, men versus women) or changed after 1939.
6. **Q6 (12): explain both bullets, then judge.** For "peacetime economy or war economy", note the war's effects (bombing, rationing, refugees) were more severe but grew out of the peacetime priority on rearmament.
:::

::: example Q1 How interpretations differ (4)
Interpretation A, written for this practice question, is in the style of an American journalist who lived in Berlin, writing in 1939: "The German worker has bread but no voice. He cannot strike, he cannot change his job without permission, and his so-called leisure is organised by the same men who set his wages." Interpretation B, also written for practice, is in the style of a former factory worker interviewed in 1985: "After years on the dole, I had work every day. We went on a KdF trip to the Baltic, my first holiday ever. Of course we grumbled, but life was better than in 1932." How does Interpretation B differ from Interpretation A about the lives of German workers under the Nazis? (4)

A says workers had **lost their freedom**: no right to strike or change jobs, and even their leisure controlled by the state (1–2). B says workers' lives had **improved**: they had regular work and holidays they had never had before (+1). So A judges by freedom and control, B by jobs and living standards compared with 1932 (+1). Level 2, 4/4.
:::

::: example Q3 Which is more convincing (8)
Using Interpretations A and B above: which interpretation gives the more convincing opinion about the lives of German workers under the Nazis? Explain your answer based on your contextual knowledge and what it says in Interpretations A and B. (8)

**A is convincing about control.** Trade unions were abolished on 2 May 1933 and replaced by Ley's DAF; strikes were illegal and wages frozen while hours rose. KdF was run by the DAF, so leisure was organised by the state. The Volkswagen scheme took workers' savings but delivered no cars.

**B is convincing about jobs.** Unemployment fell from six million to a labour shortage by 1939, through autobahns, the RAD, rearmament and conscription. KdF really did offer cheap holidays and outings many workers had never had.

**Judgement.** Both are convincing about different things, and experience varied. Armaments workers did best, while consumer-goods workers saw little gain, and the fall in unemployment was partly invisible unemployment. B is more convincing for how many workers *felt* compared with 1932; A is more convincing about the price, which was the loss of every independent right. (Level 4.)
:::

::: example Q4 Describe two (4)
Describe two methods the Nazis used to reduce unemployment. (4)

**Public works.** The Nazis paid for large building projects such as the autobahns (1). These used labour-intensive methods, so they employed many thousands of men (1).

**The Reich Labour Service.** From 1935 men aged 18–25 had to do six months' manual work in the RAD (1), on projects such as draining land and planting forests, for very low pay, which took them off the unemployment figures (1).
:::

::: example Q5 In what ways (8)
In what ways were the lives of the German people affected by the Second World War? Explain your answer. (8)

**Rationing and shortages.** Food rationing began in 1939 and was later extended to clothing; rations were cut as the war went on, and a black market grew.

**Work.** As men went to the front, labour shortages grew. After Stalingrad, Goebbels declared total war in 1943: non-essential businesses closed and women had to register for work. Millions of forced foreign workers were brought in.

**Bombing and refugees.** Allied raids destroyed cities: the Hamburg raids of 1943 and Dresden in February 1945 killed tens of thousands each, and millions were made homeless. In 1944–45 millions of Germans fled from the east as the Soviet army advanced.

**Complexity (Level 4).** The impact changed over time: in 1939–41, victories and plunder from occupied countries kept life fairly normal, but from 1942–43 bombing, total war and defeat brought hardship to almost everyone. It also differed by place, as big cities suffered far more from bombing than villages.
:::

::: example Q6 Which had the greater impact (12)
Which of the following had the greater impact on the German people: the Nazi economy in peacetime, 1933–39; the war economy, 1939–45? Explain your answer with reference to both bullet points. (12)

**Peacetime.** Unemployment fell from six million to a labour shortage, through public works, the RAD, rearmament and conscription, so most families had work. But unions were abolished, wages frozen and hours lengthened; KdF and Beauty of Labour softened this. Rearmament and the Four Year Plan (1936) meant guns before butter, with shortages of some foods.

**War.** Rationing from 1939; total war from 1943 under Speer and Goebbels; millions of forced labourers; bombing of Hamburg (1943) and Dresden (1945) that killed tens of thousands; millions of refugees fleeing west in 1944–45; by 1945 cities in ruins.

**Judgement.** The war economy had the greater impact, because it touched every family, often fatally, while peacetime changes were felt mainly at work. But they were connected: the peacetime economy was designed for war from 1936, so the hardships of 1939–45 were the result of choices made in the 1930s. (Level 4.)
:::

::: q

---
Unemployed people who did not appear in official figures (1), e.g. Jews dismissed from their jobs and women encouraged to leave work (1); men in the RAD or the army were also not counted (1).
:::

::: q
Explain two ways the DAF tried to win workers' support. (4)
---
Strength through Joy offered cheap holidays, cruises, theatre and sport (1), giving workers leisure they had never had (1). Beauty of Labour improved factory conditions such as canteens and washrooms (1); the Volkswagen savings scheme promised workers a car (1).
:::

::: q
Why did autarky fail? (3)
---
Germany lacked many raw materials such as oil and rubber (1); ersatz goods were expensive and could not be produced in enough quantity (1); rearmament needed huge imports, so by 1939 Germany still relied on imports for much of its raw materials (1).
:::

::: q
Give two effects of Allied bombing on German civilians. (2)
---
Any two (1 each): hundreds of thousands killed (e.g. tens of thousands each in Hamburg 1943 and Dresden 1945); millions made homeless; evacuation of children and families; disruption of transport, power and food supplies; fear and exhaustion.
:::
