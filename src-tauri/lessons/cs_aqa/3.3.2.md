## What it is

You must be able to convert whole numbers from **0 to 255** in both directions between **binary**, **decimal** and **hexadecimal**: six conversions in all. The limits are fixed by the spec: at most **255** in decimal, **1111 1111** in binary and **FF** in hex. Every method below rests on the column values from 3.3.1:

| Binary columns | 128 | 64 | 32 | 16 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Hex columns | 16 | 1 |
|---|---|---|

and the hex digits A = 10, B = 11, C = 12, D = 13, E = 14, F = 15.

It is worth learning the 4-bit patterns, because hex conversions use them constantly:

| Decimal | Binary | Hex | | Decimal | Binary | Hex |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0000 | 0 | | 8 | 1000 | 8 |
| 1 | 0001 | 1 | | 9 | 1001 | 9 |
| 2 | 0010 | 2 | | 10 | 1010 | A |
| 3 | 0011 | 3 | | 11 | 1011 | B |
| 4 | 0100 | 4 | | 12 | 1100 | C |
| 5 | 0101 | 5 | | 13 | 1101 | D |
| 6 | 0110 | 6 | | 14 | 1110 | E |
| 7 | 0111 | 7 | | 15 | 1111 | F |

**1. Binary to decimal: add the column values where there is a 1.**
Convert 1011 0110:

| 128 | 64 | 32 | 16 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

128 + 32 + 16 + 4 + 2 = **182**.

**2. Decimal to binary: subtract the biggest column value that fits, working left to right.**
Convert 157:
- 157 ≥ 128, so write 1 and leave 29.
- 29 < 64, write 0. 29 < 32, write 0.
- 29 ≥ 16, write 1, leaving 13. 13 ≥ 8, write 1, leaving 5. 5 ≥ 4, write 1, leaving 1.
- 1 < 2, write 0. 1 ≥ 1, write 1, leaving 0.

Answer: **1001 1101**. Check by adding back: 128 + 16 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 157.

An alternative is **repeated division by 2**, writing down remainders and reading them **from the bottom up**. For 45: 45 ÷ 2 = 22 r 1; 22 ÷ 2 = 11 r 0; 11 ÷ 2 = 5 r 1; 5 ÷ 2 = 2 r 1; 2 ÷ 2 = 1 r 0; 1 ÷ 2 = 0 r 1. Reading upwards gives 101101, written as 8 bits: **0010 1101**. Either method is fine; the subtraction method is usually quicker for 8 bits.

Always give an 8-bit answer with **leading zeros** when the question asks for 8 bits: 45 is 0010 1101, not 101101.

**3. Binary to hexadecimal: split into nibbles, convert each nibble.**
Convert 1011 1001:
- Left nibble 1011 = 8 + 2 + 1 = 11 = **B**.
- Right nibble 1001 = 8 + 1 = 9 = **9**.

Answer: **B9**. If the binary has fewer than 8 bits, pad with zeros **on the left** first: 11010 becomes 0001 1010, which is 1A.

**4. Hexadecimal to binary: convert each digit to 4 bits.**
Convert 3C:
- 3 = **0011** (four bits, including the leading zeros).
- C = 12 = **1100**.

Answer: **0011 1100**. Each hex digit must become exactly four bits, or the place values of everything to its left shift and the answer is wrong.

**5. Hexadecimal to decimal: multiply the left digit by 16 and add the right digit.**
Convert A7: A = 10, so 10 × 16 = 160; add 7: **167**.
Convert FF: 15 × 16 + 15 = **255**, the largest value you will be given.

**6. Decimal to hexadecimal: divide by 16; the whole-number part is the left digit and the remainder is the right digit.**
Convert 200: 200 ÷ 16 = 12 remainder 8 (because 12 × 16 = 192, and 200 − 192 = 8). 12 = C, so the answer is **C8**. In AQA pseudo-code terms, the left digit is `200 DIV 16` and the right digit is `200 MOD 16`.

Alternatively, go **via binary**: 200 = 1100 1000, which splits into C and 8: C8 again. Going via binary is a good way to check your answer.

**Useful shortcuts** that save time and catch errors:
- **All ones**: 1111 1111 = 255 = FF; 0000 1111 = 15 = 0F; 1111 0000 = 240 = F0.
- **One 1 followed by zeros** is a power of two: 1000 0000 = 128 = 80 in hex; 0100 0000 = 64 = 40; 0001 0000 = 16 = 10 in hex.
- **One less than a power of two** is all ones below it: 127 = 0111 1111 = 7F; 63 = 0011 1111 = 3F.
- **Hex counting** carries after F, not after 9: …, 08, 09, 0A, 0B, …, 0F, 10, 11, …, 1F, 20. So 10 in hex is sixteen, and 20 in hex is thirty-two.
- **The left nibble alone** tells you roughly how big a number is: the left hex digit is the number of complete sixteens, so 9F is a bit under 10 × 16 = 160.
- A hex number with a **letter on the left** (A–F) is at least 160; a binary number starting **1** is at least 128.

**One value, three ways.** Seeing the same number in every base makes the links obvious:

| Decimal | Binary | Hex | How the hex links to the binary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0000 1010 | 0A | 0000 → 0, 1010 → A |
| 47 | 0010 1111 | 2F | 0010 → 2, 1111 → F |
| 128 | 1000 0000 | 80 | 1000 → 8, 0000 → 0 |
| 199 | 1100 0111 | C7 | 1100 → C, 0111 → 7 |
| 255 | 1111 1111 | FF | 1111 → F, 1111 → F |

Notice that the hex digit for each nibble is the decimal value of that nibble (0 to 15), written as a single character.

**Showing working.** Conversion questions often say "show your working". A mark is usually given for a correct method even if there is an arithmetic slip, and an unexplained wrong answer gets nothing. Good working is short: the column headings with the bits under them, the sum of column values, the nibble split, or the division with its remainder.

**Checking.** Convert your answer back the other way. Large binary numbers have a 1 in the 128 column; any value over 127 must start with a 1. Any value over 15 in hex needs two digits. Odd numbers end in 1 in binary.

::: terms
- **Conversion**: rewriting a number in a different base without changing its value.
- **Place value**: the amount a column is worth, such as 128 for the left-hand column of an 8-bit binary number.
- **Nibble**: a group of four bits, which converts to exactly one hexadecimal digit.
- **Leading zeros**: zeros written on the left of a number to make it a fixed length, e.g. 0000 0101 for 5 in 8 bits.
- **Repeated division**: converting decimal to binary by dividing by 2 and reading the remainders from bottom to top.
- **DIV 16**: integer division by 16, which gives the left hex digit of a number up to 255.
- **MOD 16**: the remainder after dividing by 16, which gives the right hex digit of a number up to 255.
- **Hexadecimal digit**: one of 0 to 9 and A to F, standing for the values 0 to 15.
:::

::: method
1. **Write the column headings** first: 128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1 for binary, 16 and 1 for hex.
2. **Binary → decimal:** add the headings above each 1.
3. **Decimal → binary:** from the left, write 1 and subtract wherever the heading fits; otherwise write 0. Give all 8 bits.
4. **Binary → hex:** pad to 8 bits, split into two nibbles, convert each (8 4 2 1 headings) to a hex digit.
5. **Hex → binary:** each hex digit becomes exactly four bits, with leading zeros.
6. **Hex → decimal:** left digit × 16 + right digit (A–F as 10–15).
7. **Decimal → hex:** divide by 16; quotient = left digit, remainder = right digit; or go via binary.
8. **Check** by converting back, and show your working.
:::

::: example Binary to decimal and hex
Convert 1110 0101 to (a) decimal and (b) hexadecimal. Show your working. (4)

(a) 128 + 64 + 32 + 4 + 1 (1) = 229 (1).

(b) 1110 = 14 = E and 0101 = 5 (1), so E5 (1).
:::

::: example Decimal to binary
Convert 93 to an 8-bit binary number. Show your working. (2)

93 − 64 = 29; 29 − 16 = 13; 13 − 8 = 5; 5 − 4 = 1; 1 − 1 = 0, so there are 1s in the 64, 16, 8, 4 and 1 columns (1).

0101 1101 (1).
:::

::: example Hex to binary and decimal
Convert the hexadecimal number 9F to (a) binary and (b) decimal. (3)

(a) 9 = 1001 and F = 1111, so 1001 1111 (1).

(b) 9 × 16 = 144 (1); 144 + 15 = 159 (1).
:::

::: example Decimal to hex
Convert 171 to hexadecimal. Show your working. (2)

171 ÷ 16 = 10 remainder 11 (1); 10 = A and 11 = B, so AB (1).
:::

::: example Comparing values in different bases
Put these in order, smallest first: 0110 1000, 6A, 105. (2)

Convert to decimal: 0110 1000 = 64 + 32 + 8 = 104; 6A = 6 × 16 + 10 = 106 (1).

Order: 0110 1000, 105, 6A (1).
:::

::: example Every direction from one number
Convert 0011 1100 to decimal and to hexadecimal, then convert the hexadecimal answer back to decimal to check. (3)

0011 1100 = 32 + 16 + 8 + 4 = 60 (1).

Nibbles 0011 = 3 and 1100 = C, so 3C (1).

Check: 3 × 16 + 12 = 48 + 12 = 60, which matches (1).
:::

::: watch
- Give **all 8 bits** when 8 bits are asked for: 7 is 0000 0111.
- Each hex digit is **exactly four bits**: 3 is 0011, not 11.
- Split binary into nibbles from the **right**; pad on the **left**.
- Hex letters are values **10 to 15**: A = 10, not 1.
- In decimal → hex, the **remainder** is the right digit, and a remainder of 10–15 must be written as a letter.
- Show working: column headings, the sum, the nibble split or the division. An unexplained wrong answer scores zero.
:::

::: test
A student converts the hexadecimal number 3B to binary and writes 111011. Explain the mistake and give the correct 8-bit answer. (2)
---
The 3 has been written as 11 instead of four bits (0011) / leading zeros are missing from the first nibble (1); 0011 1011 (1).
:::

::: test
A student converts 0110 1010 to hexadecimal and gets 610. Explain the mistake and give the correct answer. (2)
---
The second nibble, 1010, is 10, which must be written as the single hex digit A, not as 10 (1); 6A (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how to convert a decimal number between 16 and 255 into hexadecimal without converting to binary first, using 236 as an example. (3)
---
Divide the number by 16; the whole-number result is the left hex digit (1). The remainder is the right hex digit (1). 236 ÷ 16 = 14 remainder 12, which gives EC (1).
:::

::: test
Convert 0101 0111 to decimal, then convert your answer to hexadecimal by dividing by 16. Show that this gives the same result as converting 0101 0111 directly to hexadecimal. (3)
---
64 + 16 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 87 (1). 87 ÷ 16 = 5 remainder 7, so 57 (1). Nibbles 0101 = 5 and 0111 = 7 also give 57 (1).
:::
