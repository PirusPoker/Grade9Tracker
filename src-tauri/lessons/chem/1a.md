## What it is

Everything is made of **particles** (atoms, molecules or ions) in constant motion. The **particle model** explains the three states of matter by how the particles are **arranged**, how they **move** and how much **energy** they have.

| | **Solid** | **Liquid** | **Gas** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrangement | **regular** lattice, particles **touching**, close-packed | **random**, still mostly **touching**, close together | **random**, **far apart** |
| Movement | **vibrate about fixed positions** — cannot move past each other | **move around and slide past each other** | **move rapidly and randomly** in all directions |
| Energy | least | more | most |
| Forces between particles | strong | fairly strong | almost none |
| Shape / volume | fixed shape and volume | takes the shape of the container, fixed volume | fills the container; easily compressed (space between particles) |

**Changes of state** — physical changes; the particles themselves do not change, only their arrangement and energy:

- **Melting** (solid → liquid): heating gives particles more energy; they vibrate faster until they have enough energy to **break free from their fixed positions** and move around, though they stay close. Happens at the **melting point**.
- **Boiling** (liquid → gas): particles gain enough energy to **overcome the forces of attraction** between them completely and move far apart. Happens at the **boiling point** throughout the liquid (evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature).
- **Freezing** (liquid → solid) and **condensing** (gas → liquid): the reverse — particles lose energy, slow down and the forces pull them into a fixed arrangement.
- **Sublimation** (solid → gas directly, e.g. iodine, solid CO₂ "dry ice"): particles gain enough energy to leave the lattice straight into the gas state.

During a change of state the **temperature stays constant** — the energy is used to overcome forces between particles, not to speed them up (the flat sections on a heating curve).

**Diffusion** is the **net movement of particles from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration** as a result of their **random motion**, until they are evenly spread. It happens in gases and liquids but not solids (the particles cannot move past each other). **Rate of diffusion increases with**: **higher temperature** (particles move faster) and **lower particle mass** (lighter particles move faster at the same temperature). Gases diffuse far faster than liquids because the particles are further apart and moving faster.

**Evidence for the particle model — experiments to describe:**

- **Bromine in air**: a gas jar of brown bromine vapour opened under a jar of air — the brown colour spreads slowly into the upper jar and both end up pale brown. Bromine particles move randomly and spread out among the air particles.
- **Ammonia and hydrogen chloride**: cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia at one end of a horizontal glass tube and in concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other. A white ring of **ammonium chloride** forms where the gases meet — **closer to the HCl end**, because ammonia (Mr 17) is lighter than hydrogen chloride (Mr 36.5) and so diffuses faster. Shows that gases diffuse, that lighter particles diffuse faster, and that the diffusion takes time (the particles collide with air molecules).
- **Potassium manganate(VII) in water**: a purple crystal at the bottom of a beaker slowly colours all the water purple, without stirring — diffusion in a liquid, slower than in a gas.
- **Dilution**: a few crystals colour a large volume, showing there are very many tiny particles.

::: method
1. **State questions: three things per state** — arrangement, movement, energy (and forces). Give all three for full marks; "solids are hard" scores nothing.
2. **Changes of state**: heating → particles gain energy → vibrate/move faster → overcome forces → new arrangement. Say what the particles do, not what the substance looks like.
3. **Flat parts of a heating curve**: temperature constant because energy is breaking forces between particles.
4. **Diffusion definition**: high → low concentration, random motion, net movement. Then the two rate factors with reasons (speed of particles; mass).
5. **Experiments**: describe what is seen, then explain each observation with particles — and for NH₃/HCl, why the ring is nearer the HCl end.
:::

::: example Describe a state
Describe the arrangement, movement and energy of the particles in a liquid. (3)

The particles are close together and touching, but arranged randomly (1). They move around and slide past each other (1). They have more energy than particles in a solid but less than in a gas (1).
:::

::: example Explain a change of state
Explain, in terms of particles, what happens when a solid is heated until it melts. (3)

Heating gives the particles more energy, so they vibrate faster about their fixed positions (1). At the melting point they have enough energy to overcome some of the forces holding them in the lattice (1), so they break free of their fixed positions and can move past each other, though they stay close together — the solid becomes a liquid (1).
:::

::: example Diffusion experiment
Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia solution and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid are placed at opposite ends of a sealed glass tube. Describe and explain what is observed. (4)

After a few minutes a white ring of solid ammonium chloride forms inside the tube (1). It forms where the ammonia and hydrogen chloride gases meet, showing both gases have diffused along the tube by the random movement of their particles (1). The ring forms nearer the hydrochloric acid end (1), because ammonia molecules are lighter than hydrogen chloride molecules and so move faster and diffuse further in the same time (1).
:::

::: example Rate of diffusion
Explain why a gas diffuses faster at a higher temperature. (2)

At a higher temperature the particles have more energy and move faster (1), so they spread out and mix with the other gas more quickly (1).
:::

::: q
Describe the arrangement and movement of particles in a solid. (2)
---
Regular lattice, particles touching (1); they vibrate about fixed positions and cannot move past each other (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why gases can be compressed but liquids cannot. (2)
---
Gas particles are far apart with empty space between them, so they can be pushed closer (1); liquid particles are already touching, so there is no space to remove (1).
:::

::: q
Name the change of state from solid directly to gas, and give an example. (2)
---
Sublimation (1); iodine or solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why the temperature of a substance stays constant while it is boiling, even though it is being heated. (2)
---
The energy supplied is used to overcome the forces of attraction between the particles (1) so they can separate into a gas, rather than to increase the particles' speed, which would raise the temperature (1).
:::

::: q
Define diffusion. (2)
---
The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration (1), resulting from their random motion (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why diffusion does not happen in solids. (1)
---
The particles are held in fixed positions and cannot move past each other.
:::

::: q
A purple crystal of potassium manganate(VII) is placed in a beaker of still water. Describe and explain what happens over an hour. (3)
---
The purple colour spreads slowly from the crystal until all the water is evenly purple (1); the crystal dissolves and its particles move randomly, diffusing from high concentration near the crystal to low concentration elsewhere (1); it is slow because liquid particles are close together and move less freely than gas particles (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why bromine vapour spreads more slowly into a jar of air than into a vacuum. (2)
---
In air the bromine molecules collide with air molecules, which changes their direction and slows their progress (1); in a vacuum they move unhindered (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why hydrogen diffuses faster than oxygen at the same temperature. (2)
---
Hydrogen molecules have a much smaller mass (1), so at the same temperature they move faster and spread out more quickly (1).
:::

::: watch
- Every state or change-of-state answer is about **what the particles do**: arrangement, movement, energy, forces.
- Changes of state are **physical** — the particles are unchanged; only their arrangement and energy change.
- **Temperature is constant** during melting/boiling: energy goes into overcoming forces.
- Diffusion needs "**net** movement", "**high to low** concentration", "**random** motion".
- NH₃/HCl ring is **nearer the HCl end** because **ammonia is lighter and diffuses faster**.
:::

::: test
Describe what happens to the particles when a liquid is heated until it boils. (3)
---
The particles gain energy and move faster (1); the forces between them are overcome (1); they separate and move freely and randomly as a gas, far apart (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why a gas fills its container but a liquid takes the shape of only the bottom of it. (2)
---
Gas particles move freely in all directions with negligible forces between them, spreading to fill the space (1); liquid particles are held close together by forces of attraction, so they flow but stay together (1).
:::

::: test
Name the changes of state: (a) liquid to solid; (b) gas to liquid; (c) solid to gas without melting. (3)
---
(a) freezing (1); (b) condensing (1); (c) sublimation (1).
:::

::: test
A gas jar of air is placed upside down on a gas jar of brown bromine vapour. After a while both jars are pale brown. Explain, in terms of particles. (3)
---
Bromine and air particles move randomly (1); they spread out and mix through the gaps between each other (1) until evenly spread — diffusion; it happens even though bromine is denser than air (1).
:::

::: test
Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia solution is placed at one end of a tube and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other. A white ring forms nearer the acid end. Explain. (3)
---
Ammonia (Mr 17) and hydrogen chloride (Mr 36.5) gases diffuse along the tube (1); they react where they meet to form the white solid ammonium chloride (1); ammonia particles are lighter so move faster and travel further (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why diffusion is faster at higher temperatures. (1)
---
Particles have more kinetic energy and move faster, so they spread out more quickly.
:::

::: test
A student adds a drop of ink to still water without stirring. Describe what is seen over an hour and explain it. (2)
---
The colour slowly spreads until the water is evenly coloured (1); the ink and water particles move randomly and mix — diffusion (1).
:::

::: test
Sketch or describe the heating curve for a solid heated until it becomes a gas, labelling what happens at the flat sections. (2)
---
Temperature rises, then a flat section while it melts, rises again, then a flat section while it boils, then rises (1); the flat sections are where energy is used to overcome forces between particles rather than raise the temperature (1).
:::
