## What it is

Most metals are found in the Earth's crust as **ores** — rocks containing enough of a metal **compound** (usually the oxide or sulfide) to be worth extracting. Only the least reactive metals (gold, sometimes silver and copper) are found **native** as the element. Extraction always means **reducing** the metal ion to the metal — the ion must gain electrons — and how hard that is depends on the metal's position in the **reactivity series**:

| Position in reactivity series | Extraction method | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Above carbon** (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) | **Electrolysis** of the molten compound — expensive because of the energy needed to melt the ore and drive the current | aluminium from bauxite |
| **Below carbon, above hydrogen or near it** (Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu) | **Heating with carbon** — carbon is more reactive, so it takes the oxygen: **reduction with carbon** in a blast furnace or similar | iron from haematite, zinc from zinc oxide |
| **Very unreactive** (Ag, Au) | Found **native** — no chemical extraction needed | gold nuggets |

**Iron — reduction with carbon.** Iron ore is mainly **haematite, Fe₂O₃**. In the blast furnace, coke (carbon) burns to carbon dioxide, which reacts with more carbon to form **carbon monoxide**, the reducing agent that takes oxygen from the iron oxide:

- C + O₂ → CO₂ (exothermic — heats the furnace)
- CO₂ + C → 2CO
- **Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂** (iron(III) oxide is *reduced*; the carbon monoxide is *oxidised*)

Overall the simpler equation **2Fe₂O₃ + 3C → 4Fe + 3CO₂** is also accepted. Limestone is added to remove sandy impurities as slag.

**Aluminium — electrolysis.** Aluminium is above carbon, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide. **Bauxite** is purified to aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, which is **dissolved in molten cryolite** — this **lowers the melting point** from over 2000 °C to about 950 °C, saving energy, and improves conductivity. Electrolysis with **graphite (carbon) electrodes**:

- Cathode: **Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al** — molten aluminium collects at the bottom and is tapped off.
- Anode: **2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻** — the oxygen produced reacts with the hot carbon anodes (C + O₂ → CO₂), so the anodes **burn away and must be replaced regularly**.
- Overall: 2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂.

The process uses huge amounts of electricity, which is why aluminium is expensive and why **recycling** aluminium (which needs about 5% of the energy) matters.

**Uses of metals** are linked to their properties:

| Metal | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Aluminium** | low density, does not corrode (protective oxide layer), good conductor, malleable | aircraft bodies, drinks cans, overhead power cables (with a steel core), window frames |
| **Copper** | excellent electrical conductor, ductile, unreactive, good thermal conductor | electrical wiring, water pipes, saucepan bases |
| **Iron** (cast/pure) | strong but brittle when cast; pure iron soft | engine blocks, manhole covers — usually converted to steel |
| **Steel** (iron + carbon) | harder and stronger than iron | see alloys below |

**Alloys** are **mixtures of a metal with other elements** (usually other metals, or carbon in steel). They are harder and stronger than the pure metal because the **atoms of the added element are a different size**, which **disrupts the regular layers** of atoms in the metal lattice, so the **layers cannot slide over each other** as easily. Pure metals are soft *because* the identical atoms form neat layers that slide.

| Alloy | Composition | Properties and uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-carbon (mild) steel** | iron + ~0.25% carbon | soft, easily shaped — car bodies, wire, nails |
| **High-carbon steel** | iron + ~1–1.5% carbon | hard but brittle — cutting tools, knives, springs |
| **Stainless steel** | iron + chromium + nickel | resists corrosion — cutlery, sinks, surgical instruments |
| **Duralumin** | aluminium + copper (+ magnesium) | stronger than aluminium but still light — aircraft |
| **Brass / bronze** | copper + zinc / copper + tin | hard-wearing — instruments, coins, statues |

::: method
1. **Extraction method = position vs carbon**: above carbon → electrolysis (expensive); below carbon → heat with carbon (reduction). Say *why* — "carbon is more reactive than iron, so it removes the oxygen" or "aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide".
2. **Iron equations**: know Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ and identify which is reduced (loses oxygen) and which is oxidised (gains oxygen).
3. **Aluminium**: cryolite lowers the melting point → half-equations → anodes burn away in the oxygen → replaced. Four ideas, four marks.
4. **Uses → properties**: always pair a use with the property that makes it suitable ("copper for wiring because it is an excellent conductor and ductile").
5. **Alloys are harder** — give the *atomic* explanation: different-sized atoms disrupt the layers so they cannot slide.
:::

::: example Choosing a method
Explain why iron is extracted from its oxide by heating with carbon, but aluminium is extracted by electrolysis. (3)

Carbon is more reactive than iron, so when heated it removes the oxygen from iron oxide, reducing it to iron (1). Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot take the oxygen from aluminium oxide (1). Electrolysis is needed to force electrons onto the aluminium ions, which is more expensive because of the energy used (1).
:::

::: example Blast furnace
Write an equation for the reduction of iron(III) oxide by carbon monoxide and explain which substance is oxidised. (3)

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (2). Carbon monoxide gains oxygen to form carbon dioxide, so it is oxidised; it acts as the reducing agent, and the iron(III) oxide loses oxygen and is reduced (1).
:::

::: example Aluminium electrolysis
Aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite and electrolysed with carbon electrodes. Explain the purpose of the cryolite, write the half-equation at the cathode, and explain why the anodes need replacing. (4)

Cryolite lowers the melting point of the aluminium oxide, so less energy is needed to keep the electrolyte molten (1). Cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al (1). Oxygen is produced at the anode (2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻) (1); the hot carbon anodes react with this oxygen to form carbon dioxide, so they burn away and must be replaced (1).
:::

::: example Alloys
Explain why steel is harder than pure iron. (3)

Pure iron has atoms of the same size arranged in regular layers (1), which can slide over each other easily, making the metal soft (1). In steel, carbon atoms of a different size sit between the iron atoms and disrupt the regular arrangement, so the layers cannot slide as easily and the alloy is harder (1).
:::

::: q
What is an ore? (1)
---
A rock that contains enough of a metal compound to make extracting the metal worthwhile.
:::

::: q
Why is gold found as the element rather than as a compound? (1)
---
Gold is very unreactive, so it does not combine with other elements in the Earth's crust.
:::

::: q
Name the main ore of iron and give its formula. (2)
---
Haematite (1); Fe₂O₃ (1).
:::

::: q
Why could zinc be extracted by heating its oxide with carbon, but not magnesium? (2)
---
Zinc is below carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon can reduce zinc oxide (1); magnesium is above carbon, so carbon cannot remove oxygen from magnesium oxide (1).
:::

::: q
Write the balanced equation for the reduction of zinc oxide by carbon. (1)
---
ZnO + C → Zn + CO (or 2ZnO + C → 2Zn + CO₂).
:::

::: q
Write the anode half-equation in the electrolysis of aluminium oxide. (1)
---
2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻.
:::

::: q
Give two reasons why extracting aluminium is expensive. (2)
---
Large amounts of electricity are needed for the electrolysis (1); the carbon anodes burn away and must be replaced regularly (or the ore must be kept molten) (1).
:::

::: q
Give one use of aluminium and the property that makes it suitable. (2)
---
Aircraft bodies (1) — low density/lightweight and resistant to corrosion (1).
:::

::: q
Why is copper used for electrical wiring? (2)
---
It is an excellent conductor of electricity (1) and ductile, so it can be drawn into wires (1).
:::

::: q
Define an alloy and give one example. (2)
---
A mixture of a metal with one or more other elements (1); e.g. steel (iron and carbon), brass (copper and zinc) (1).
:::

::: watch
- The rule is position **relative to carbon**, not relative to hydrogen: above carbon → electrolysis, below → heat with carbon.
- In the blast furnace the reducing agent is **carbon monoxide** (accept carbon) — iron oxide is **reduced** because it **loses oxygen**.
- Cryolite **lowers the melting point** of Al₂O₃ — it is not a catalyst and it is not electrolysed itself.
- Anodes burn away because **oxygen reacts with the hot carbon**; the cathode does not.
- Alloys: **different-sized atoms disrupt the layers so they cannot slide** — "stronger bonds" is wrong.
:::
