## What it is

**Clean, dry air** is a mixture: about **78% nitrogen**, **21% oxygen**, **0.9% argon** and **0.04% carbon dioxide**, plus traces of other noble gases (water vapour varies and is excluded from "dry" air).

**Finding the percentage of oxygen in air experimentally:**

- **Copper method**: a known volume of air (say 100 cm³) is passed backwards and forwards over **heated copper** in a silica tube between two gas syringes. The copper reacts with the oxygen — 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO — turning from pink-brown to black. The volume falls until all the oxygen is used; when cooled, the remaining volume (about 79 cm³) shows that about 21% was oxygen. Excess copper must be used and the gas passed repeatedly until the volume stops changing.
- **Iron method**: iron wool (or iron filings) in the closed end of an inverted measuring cylinder standing in water. Over several days the iron **rusts**, using up oxygen; water rises to replace the oxygen removed. Rise in water level ÷ original air volume × 100 ≈ 21%.
- **Phosphorus method**: phosphorus burning under a bell jar over water uses the oxygen; water rises by about a fifth of the volume.

**Combustion of elements in oxygen** — burning is reaction with oxygen to form an oxide:

| Element | Observation | Equation | Product in water |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Magnesium** | burns with a **bright white flame**, leaves a **white powder** | 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO | magnesium oxide slightly soluble → **alkaline** solution (metal oxides are basic) |
| **Hydrogen** | burns with a pale blue flame, squeaky pop | 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O | water is neutral |
| **Sulfur** | burns with a **blue flame**, producing a choking gas | S + O₂ → SO₂ | sulfur dioxide dissolves → **acidic** solution (non-metal oxides are acidic) |
| Carbon | glows red/orange | C + O₂ → CO₂ | carbon dioxide → weakly acidic |

**Carbon dioxide** — formed by: the **combustion** of carbon and of carbon-containing fuels (hydrocarbons: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O); the **thermal decomposition of metal carbonates** on heating (CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂; copper carbonate green → black CuO + CO₂); the reaction of carbonates with acids; respiration. Properties: colourless, odourless, denser than air, slightly soluble to give a weakly acidic solution (carbonic acid); does not support combustion, so used in fire extinguishers; test — turns limewater milky.

**The greenhouse effect**: short-wave radiation from the sun passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface; the surface emits long-wave **infrared** radiation; **greenhouse gases** — carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour — **absorb this infrared and re-emit it** in all directions, including back to the surface, so heat is trapped and the atmosphere is warmer than it would otherwise be. The natural greenhouse effect keeps the planet habitable, but **burning fossil fuels and deforestation** have raised CO₂ from about 0.028% to over 0.04%, enhancing the effect and causing **global warming** and climate change (sea-level rise, extreme weather). Reduced by using renewable energy, carbon capture, planting trees, efficiency.

::: terms
- **Composition of dry air**: about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.9% argon and 0.04% carbon dioxide.
- **Combustion**: a reaction in which a substance burns in oxygen, releasing energy.
- **Thermal decomposition**: breaking down a substance by heating it, e.g. CuCO₃ → CuO + CO₂.
- **Greenhouse gas**: a gas, such as carbon dioxide, that absorbs heat radiated from the Earth and keeps the atmosphere warm.
- **Limewater test**: carbon dioxide turns limewater milky (cloudy).
:::

::: method
1. **Percentages**: 78 N₂, 21 O₂, 0.9 Ar, 0.04 CO₂ — and which way round nitrogen and oxygen go.
2. **Oxygen experiments**: name the reactant (copper/iron), what it does to the oxygen, how the volume change is measured, and the calculation. Copper must be in excess and heated until no further change.
3. **Combustion**: flame colour → product → equation → acidic or basic when dissolved (metal oxide basic, non-metal oxide acidic).
4. **CO₂ formation**: give the equation for the route asked — combustion or carbonate decomposition.
5. **Greenhouse effect as a sequence**: sunlight in → surface warms → infrared out → absorbed by CO₂/CH₄ → re-emitted → warmer. Then the human cause and consequence.
:::

::: example Oxygen in air
Describe an experiment to find the percentage of oxygen in air using copper. (4)

Trap 100 cm³ of air in a gas syringe connected through a silica tube containing excess copper to a second syringe (1). Heat the copper strongly and pass the air backwards and forwards over it; the copper reacts with the oxygen forming black copper oxide (1). Continue until the volume stops decreasing, then let the apparatus cool and read the final volume (1). Volume of oxygen = 100 − final volume ≈ 21 cm³, so air is about 21% oxygen (1).
:::

::: example Combustion
Describe what is seen when sulfur burns in oxygen, write the equation, and state the effect of dissolving the product in water. (3)

Sulfur burns with a blue flame producing a colourless, choking gas (1). S + O₂ → SO₂ (1). Sulfur dioxide dissolves to form an acidic solution — it turns universal indicator red (1).
:::

::: example Carbon dioxide from a carbonate
Describe how carbon dioxide can be made by heating a metal carbonate, and how you would test for it. (3)

Heating copper(II) carbonate causes thermal decomposition: CuCO₃ → CuO + CO₂; the green powder turns black (2). Bubble the gas through limewater — it turns milky (1).
:::

::: example Greenhouse effect
Explain how carbon dioxide in the atmosphere causes the greenhouse effect. (3)

Radiation from the sun passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface, which then gives off infrared radiation (1). Carbon dioxide molecules absorb this infrared radiation (1) and re-emit it in all directions, including back towards the surface, so heat is trapped and the atmosphere warms (1).
:::

::: q
Give the approximate percentages of nitrogen, oxygen, argon and carbon dioxide in dry air. (2)
---
Nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21% (1); argon 0.9%, carbon dioxide 0.04% (1).

::: q
In the iron-wool experiment, why does the water level rise inside the measuring cylinder? (2)
---
The iron rusts, reacting with and removing the oxygen from the trapped air (1); the pressure inside falls, so water is pushed up to replace the lost volume (1).
:::

::: q
Why must the copper be in excess in the oxygen experiment? (1)
---
So that all the oxygen is used up — otherwise the volume decrease would be too small.
:::

::: q
Describe the flame and product when magnesium burns in air. (2)
---
Bright white flame (1); white powder, magnesium oxide (1).
:::

::: q
Write the equation for hydrogen burning in oxygen. (1)
---
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
:::

::: q
Magnesium oxide and sulfur dioxide are each added to water with universal indicator. Give the colours and explain. (2)
---
Magnesium oxide: blue/purple — a metal oxide is basic (1). Sulfur dioxide: red — a non-metal oxide is acidic (1).
:::

::: q
Write the equation for the thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate. (1)
---
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂.
:::

::: q
Name two greenhouse gases other than carbon dioxide. (2)
---
Methane; water vapour.
:::

::: q
Give two human activities that increase atmospheric carbon dioxide. (2)
---
Burning fossil fuels; deforestation.
:::

::: q
Why is carbon dioxide used in fire extinguishers? (2)
---
It is denser than air, so it blankets the fire (1), and it does not support combustion, so it cuts off the oxygen (1).

::: watch
- **78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen** — not the other way round.
- Oxygen experiments: **excess** reactant, **repeat until no change**, **cool before reading**.
- Metal oxides → **basic** (alkaline solution); non-metal oxides → **acidic**.
- Greenhouse effect is about **infrared absorbed and re-emitted** — not "trapping sunlight" or the ozone layer.
- Carbonates decompose on heating to **metal oxide + CO₂**; test with **limewater → milky**.
:::

::: test
100 cm³ of air is passed back and forth over heated copper until the volume stops changing. The final volume is 79 cm³. Calculate the percentage of oxygen in the air sample and explain why the volume decreased. (3)
---
Volume decrease = 100 − 79 = 21 cm³; percentage = 21 ÷ 100 × 100 = 21% (2). The oxygen reacted with the hot copper to form solid copper(II) oxide, so it was removed from the gas (1).

::: test
Explain why the result of the experiment above might be lower than 21%. (2)
---
Not all of the oxygen reacted — e.g. not enough copper or not passed over enough times (1); or the gas was measured while still hot, so its volume was larger (1).
:::
