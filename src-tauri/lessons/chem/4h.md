## What it is

**Carboxylic acids** are a homologous series with the **carboxyl functional group, –COOH** (a carbon double-bonded to one oxygen and single-bonded to an –OH). General formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH**. Named with **-oic acid**:

| Acid | Formula | Structural formula | Found in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Methanoic acid** | **HCOOH** | HCOOH | ant stings, nettles |
| **Ethanoic acid** | **CH₃COOH** | CH₃COOH | vinegar |
| **Propanoic acid** | **C₂H₅COOH** | CH₃CH₂COOH | food preservative |
| **Butanoic acid** | **C₃H₇COOH** | CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH | rancid butter |

In a displayed formula the –COOH group shows the **C=O** double bond and the **O–H** bond separately. Ethanoic acid is made by **oxidising ethanol** (4g) with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) or by bacteria in air.

**Acidic properties.** Carboxylic acids are **weak acids**: in water only a small fraction of the molecules **ionise** to release **H⁺**: CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺. Solutions have pH about 3 and react like any acid (2g), but **more slowly** than hydrochloric acid of the same concentration because there are fewer H⁺ ions present at any moment. The salts are **ethanoates** (CH₃COO⁻):

- **Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen**: 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂ (magnesium ethanoate; gentle fizzing)
- **Acid + base → salt + water**: CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O (sodium ethanoate)
- **Acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO₂**: 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ (fizzing; limewater milky)

**Esters.** A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol to form an **ester** and **water** — **esterification**, a reversible reaction that is **catalysed by concentrated sulfuric acid** and needs warming:

**ethanol + ethanoic acid ⇌ ethyl ethanoate + water**
**CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O**

The ester **functional group is –COO–** (the carbon is bonded to a C=O and to an –O– that leads to the alcohol's carbon chain). Esters are named in **two words**: the first from the **alcohol** (ethyl, methyl, propyl), the second from the **acid** (ethanoate, methanoate, propanoate):

| Alcohol | Acid | Ester |
|---|---|---|
| ethanol | ethanoic acid | **ethyl ethanoate** |
| methanol | ethanoic acid | **methyl ethanoate** |
| ethanol | methanoic acid | **ethyl methanoate** |
| propanol | ethanoic acid | propyl ethanoate |
| ethanol | propanoic acid | ethyl propanoate |

**Making ethyl ethanoate in the laboratory**: warm a mixture of ethanoic acid and ethanol with a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid (catalyst) in a boiling tube in a water bath, then pour the mixture into sodium carbonate solution to neutralise the leftover acid; the ester forms an insoluble layer on top with a sweet, fruity ("pear drops"/nail varnish) smell. Esters are **volatile** liquids that are **insoluble in water**, with **sweet smells**, used as **flavourings** and in **perfumes** and as solvents (nail-varnish remover).

Making an ester is a **condensation** reaction — two molecules join and a **small molecule (water) is eliminated**: the –OH from the acid and the H from the alcohol's –OH combine to form water. The same idea underlies condensation polymerisation (4i).

::: method
1. **Functional group –COOH**; name with **-oic acid**; count carbons *including* the carboxyl carbon (CH₃COOH has 2 → ethanoic).
2. **Weak acid**: partially ionised; write the ⇌ equation; fewer H⁺ → slower reactions, higher pH than a strong acid of the same concentration.
3. **Reactions of acids** apply — salt names end in **-anoate**.
4. **Ester name**: alcohol part first (**-yl**), acid part second (**-anoate**). Equation: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, **concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst**.
5. **Properties/uses of esters**: sweet smell, volatile, insoluble in water → flavourings, perfumes, solvents.
:::

::: example Naming and formula
Give the name and structural formula of the carboxylic acid with three carbon atoms, and identify its functional group. (3)

Propanoic acid (1), CH₃CH₂COOH (1). The functional group is the carboxyl group, –COOH (1).
:::

::: example Weak acid
Ethanoic acid and hydrochloric acid solutions of the same concentration are added to magnesium. Explain why the ethanoic acid fizzes more slowly. (3)

Ethanoic acid is a weak acid — only a small fraction of its molecules ionise in water, CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ (1), whereas hydrochloric acid is fully ionised (1). The ethanoic acid solution therefore has a lower concentration of hydrogen ions, so collisions with the magnesium that produce hydrogen are less frequent (1).
:::

::: example Esterification
Write an equation for the reaction between ethanoic acid and ethanol, name the product, and give the conditions. (4)

CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O (2). The organic product is the ester ethyl ethanoate (1). The mixture is warmed with a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid as a catalyst (1).
:::

::: example Naming esters
Name the esters formed from (a) methanol and propanoic acid, (b) propanol and methanoic acid. (2)

(a) Methyl propanoate (1). (b) Propyl methanoate (1) — the alcohol gives the first word, the acid the second.
:::

::: q
Give the functional group of the carboxylic acids and the name of the two-carbon acid. (2)
---
–COOH (1); ethanoic acid (1).
:::

::: q
Give the formula of butanoic acid. (1)
---
C₃H₇COOH (CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH).
:::

::: q
How is ethanoic acid made from ethanol? (1)
---
By oxidation — with acidified potassium dichromate(VI), or by bacteria in the air (vinegar).
:::

::: q
Why is ethanoic acid described as a weak acid? (1)
---
Only a small proportion of its molecules ionise in water to release H⁺ ions.
:::

::: q
Name the salt formed when ethanoic acid reacts with sodium hydroxide, and give its formula. (2)
---
Sodium ethanoate (1); CH₃COONa (1).
:::

::: q
What is observed when sodium carbonate is added to ethanoic acid, and what gas is produced? (2)
---
Fizzing/effervescence (1); carbon dioxide (1).
:::

::: q
What is the functional group of an ester? (1)
---
–COO– (the ester link).
:::

::: q
Name the catalyst used in esterification. (1)
---
Concentrated sulfuric acid.
:::

::: q
Name the ester CH₃COOCH₃ and the two compounds it is made from. (2)
---
Methyl ethanoate (1); methanol and ethanoic acid (1).
:::

::: q
Give two uses of esters and the property that makes them suitable. (2)
---
Flavourings and perfumes (1) — they are volatile and have sweet, fruity smells (1).
:::

::: watch
- Count the **carboxyl carbon** when naming: CH₃COOH is **ethanoic**, not methanoic.
- Weak acid = **partially ionised**; it is not the same as dilute.
- Ester naming: **alcohol first (-yl), acid second (-anoate)** — "ethyl ethanoate" from ethanol and ethanoic acid.
- Esterification needs **concentrated sulfuric acid** as catalyst and is **reversible** (⇌).
- Esters are **insoluble in water** and form a **layer on top**; smell is **sweet/fruity**.
:::
