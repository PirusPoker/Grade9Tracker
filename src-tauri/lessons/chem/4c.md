## What it is

Hydrocarbons make good **fuels** because they burn **exothermically**, releasing a lot of energy per gram. What is produced depends on how much oxygen is available.

**Complete combustion** — plenty of oxygen: the carbon is fully oxidised to **carbon dioxide** and the hydrogen to **water**; a clean **blue flame**, maximum energy released.

- Methane: **CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O**
- Propane: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
- Octane (petrol): 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O
- Ethanol (not a hydrocarbon, but a fuel): C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O

Balancing tip: balance C first, then H, then count the O on the right and halve it for O₂ — double everything if you get a half.

**Incomplete combustion** — limited (insufficient) oxygen: the hydrogen still forms water, but the carbon forms **carbon monoxide, CO** and/or **carbon (soot)** instead of CO₂; a **yellow, smoky flame** and **less energy** released. Occurs in faulty boilers, badly ventilated gas fires, car engines, and long-chain fuels.

- 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O
- CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O

**Carbon monoxide is a poisonous, colourless, odourless gas** (so you cannot tell it is there — hence CO detectors). It **binds to haemoglobin** in red blood cells **more strongly than oxygen does**, forming carboxyhaemoglobin, so **less oxygen is carried** around the body: drowsiness, headaches, unconsciousness and death. Soot blackens buildings, dirties appliances and causes breathing problems.

**Sulfur dioxide and acid rain.** Fossil fuels (especially coal and heavy oil fractions) contain **sulfur impurities**; when burned these form **sulfur dioxide**: S + O₂ → SO₂. It dissolves in water in clouds to form sulfurous/sulfuric acid, giving **acid rain**, which **damages limestone buildings and statues** (CaCO₃ reacts with acid), **corrodes metal** structures, **kills fish** by acidifying lakes, and **damages trees** and crops. Prevented by removing sulfur from fuels before use (desulfurisation) or using **flue-gas desulfurisation** — scrubbing power-station gases with calcium oxide/hydroxide or limestone (CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃).

**Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) in engines.** Inside a car engine the temperature and pressure are so high that the **nitrogen and oxygen from the air react** — normally unreactive nitrogen can combine at these temperatures: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO, then 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂. Nitrogen oxides also cause **acid rain** (nitric acid) and **photochemical smog**, and irritate the lungs. A **catalytic converter** (platinum/rhodium) converts CO and NO into harmless CO₂ and N₂: 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.

**Hydrogen as a fuel** — 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: the only product is **water**, so no CO₂ or CO; it releases a lot of energy per gram. Drawbacks: it is a gas, so it is hard to store and transport safely (explosive, needs high-pressure tanks); producing it by electrolysis uses electricity, which may itself come from fossil fuels; and there are few filling stations.

::: terms
- **Fuel**: a substance that releases heat energy when burned.
- **Complete combustion**: burning in plenty of oxygen, producing carbon dioxide and water.
- **Incomplete combustion**: burning in too little oxygen, producing carbon monoxide or carbon (soot) and water.
- **Carbon monoxide**: a colourless, odourless, poisonous gas that combines with haemoglobin and reduces the oxygen the blood can carry.
- **Nitrogen oxides**: formed when nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at the high temperatures in car engines.
- **Sulfur dioxide**: formed when sulfur impurities in fuels burn.
- **Acid rain**: rain made acidic by sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides dissolving in it; it damages buildings, trees and aquatic life.
:::

::: method
1. **Complete combustion equation**: products always CO₂ + H₂O; balance C, H, then O. Practise with CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ + (3n+1)/2 O₂.
2. **Incomplete**: same fuel, **less oxygen**; products CO and/or C plus water. Say "insufficient oxygen" and name the products.
3. **Why CO is dangerous**: colourless, odourless, **binds to haemoglobin** so blood carries less oxygen. Two ideas, two marks.
4. **Pollutant → source → problem**: SO₂ from sulfur impurities → acid rain; NOₓ from N₂ + O₂ at high engine temperatures → acid rain/smog; CO₂ → global warming; particulates → health.
5. **Hydrogen**: advantage (only water produced) *and* disadvantages (storage, production, availability).
:::

::: example Combustion equations
Write balanced equations for the complete combustion of propane and for the incomplete combustion of propane to form carbon monoxide. (4)

C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O (2). With insufficient oxygen: 2C₃H₈ + 7O₂ → 6CO + 8H₂O (2).
:::

::: example Carbon monoxide
A gas fire in a poorly ventilated room produces carbon monoxide. Explain how it forms and why it is dangerous. (4)

With a limited supply of oxygen the methane undergoes incomplete combustion, so the carbon is only partly oxidised to carbon monoxide instead of carbon dioxide (1). Carbon monoxide is colourless and odourless, so it is not noticed (1). It binds to haemoglobin in red blood cells more strongly than oxygen does (1), so less oxygen is carried to the body's cells, causing drowsiness, unconsciousness and possibly death (1).
:::

::: example Nitrogen oxides
Explain why nitrogen oxides are produced in a car engine even though the petrol contains no nitrogen. (3)

Air drawn into the engine contains nitrogen and oxygen (1). The temperature inside the engine is high enough for nitrogen and oxygen to react together, which they do not do at normal temperatures (1): N₂ + O₂ → 2NO (1).
:::

::: example Acid rain
Explain how burning coal can lead to acid rain and describe two effects of acid rain. (4)

Coal contains sulfur impurities which burn to form sulfur dioxide, S + O₂ → SO₂ (1). Sulfur dioxide dissolves in rainwater to form acidic solutions (sulfurous/sulfuric acid), which falls as acid rain (1). It reacts with and erodes limestone buildings and statues (1) and acidifies lakes, killing fish, and damages trees (1).
:::

::: q
Name the products of the complete combustion of a hydrocarbon. (1)
---
Carbon dioxide and water.
:::

::: q
Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of butane, C₄H₁₀. (2)
---
2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O.
:::

::: q
Under what conditions does incomplete combustion occur, and which two carbon-containing products may form? (2)
---
When the supply of oxygen is limited (1); carbon monoxide and carbon/soot (1).
:::

::: q
Why does a yellow flame indicate a problem in a gas appliance? (1)
---
It shows incomplete combustion is happening, so carbon monoxide may be produced.
:::

::: q
Explain why carbon monoxide is described as toxic. (2)
---
It binds to haemoglobin in the blood (1), preventing it from carrying oxygen to the cells (1).
:::

::: q
Which impurity in fossil fuels leads to acid rain, and what gas does it form? (2)
---
Sulfur (1); sulfur dioxide (1).
:::

::: q
Write the equation for the formation of nitrogen monoxide in an engine. (1)
---
N₂ + O₂ → 2NO.
:::

::: q
How does a catalytic converter reduce pollution from a car? (2)
---
It converts carbon monoxide and nitrogen oxides into carbon dioxide and nitrogen (1) — 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ (1).
:::

::: q
Give one advantage and one disadvantage of hydrogen as a fuel. (2)
---
Advantage: only water is produced, no carbon dioxide (1). Disadvantage: difficult to store and transport safely / its production may use fossil-fuel electricity (1).
:::

::: q
Why does burning fossil fuels contribute to global warming? (1)
---
It releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, into the atmosphere.
:::

::: watch
- Incomplete combustion is caused by **insufficient oxygen** — say the reason, not just the products.
- Carbon monoxide **binds to haemoglobin** — "it is poisonous" alone is not an explanation.
- **Sulfur dioxide** comes from **sulfur impurities in the fuel**; **nitrogen oxides** come from **air reacting at high temperature** — different sources.
- CO₂ → **global warming**; SO₂/NOₓ → **acid rain**. Do not swap them.
- Balance combustion equations **C, then H, then O** — double the equation if O₂ comes out as a half.
:::
