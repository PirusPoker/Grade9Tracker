## What it is

**Organic chemistry** is the chemistry of carbon compounds. Carbon forms four covalent bonds and bonds to itself in chains and rings, so there are millions of compounds. Key terms:

- **Hydrocarbon** — a compound containing **hydrogen and carbon only** (methane CH₄, ethene C₂H₄). Ethanol C₂H₅OH is *not* a hydrocarbon.
- **Homologous series** — a family of compounds with the **same functional group** and the **same general formula**, where each member differs from the next by **CH₂**; they show a **gradual trend in physical properties** (boiling point rises with chain length) and **similar chemical reactions**. Alkanes, alkenes, alcohols and carboxylic acids are each a homologous series.
- **Functional group** — the atom or group of atoms **responsible for the characteristic reactions** of the compound: C=C in alkenes, –OH in alcohols, –COOH in carboxylic acids.
- **Saturated** — only single C–C bonds (alkanes); **unsaturated** — contains at least one C=C double bond (alkenes).
- **Isomers** — compounds with the **same molecular formula** but **different structural formulae** (different arrangement of atoms). Butane and 2-methylpropane are both C₄H₁₀ but have genuinely different chains, so they are different compounds with different boiling points.

**Five ways to write a formula** (for butane):

| Formula type | What it shows | Butane |
|---|---|---|
| **Empirical** | simplest whole-number ratio of atoms | C₂H₅ |
| **Molecular** | actual number of atoms of each element in a molecule | C₄H₁₀ |
| **General** | formula for the whole homologous series, *n* = number of carbons | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ |
| **Structural** | how the atoms are grouped, carbon by carbon | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| **Displayed** | every atom and every bond drawn out | see diagram |

**Naming (IUPAC rules)** — the name has a **prefix** for the number of carbons in the longest chain and a **suffix** for the functional group:

| Carbons | Prefix | Alkane (-ane) | Alkene (-ene) | Alcohol (-ol) | Carboxylic acid (-oic acid) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | meth- | methane CH₄ | — | methanol CH₃OH | methanoic acid HCOOH |
| 2 | eth- | ethane C₂H₆ | ethene C₂H₄ | ethanol C₂H₅OH | ethanoic acid CH₃COOH |
| 3 | prop- | propane C₃H₈ | propene C₃H₆ | propanol C₃H₇OH | propanoic acid C₂H₅COOH |
| 4 | but- | butane C₄H₁₀ | butene C₄H₈ | butanol C₄H₉OH | butanoic acid C₃H₇COOH |

Rules for branched or positional isomers: (1) find the **longest continuous carbon chain** — that gives the stem; (2) number the chain from the end that gives the functional group or branch the **lowest number**; (3) name branches as **methyl** (CH₃), **ethyl** (C₂H₅) with their position, e.g. **2-methylpropane**, **2-methylbutane**; (4) give the position of a double bond or –OH: **but-1-ene** (CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃) and **but-2-ene** (CH₃CH=CHCH₃), **propan-1-ol** and **propan-2-ol**. Multiple identical branches use di-, tri- (2,2-dimethylpropane).

**Isomers of C₄H₁₀**: butane (straight chain) and 2-methylpropane (branched). **Isomers of C₅H₁₂**: pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane. **Isomers of C₄H₈** (alkenes): but-1-ene, but-2-ene, 2-methylpropene. Be careful: drawing the same chain bent, or putting the branch on the *end* carbon (which just lengthens the chain), does **not** give a new isomer — "1-methylpropane" is simply butane.

::: method
1. **Definitions word-perfect**: hydrocarbon (H and C *only*); homologous series (same functional group, same general formula, differ by CH₂, gradual trend in physical properties, similar chemical properties); isomers (same molecular formula, different structural formula).
2. **Converting formulae**: count atoms in the displayed formula → molecular; divide by the highest common factor → empirical; group by carbon → structural.
3. **Naming**: longest chain → stem; suffix from the functional group; number from the end that gives the lowest locant; list branches alphabetically with positions.
4. **Drawing isomers**: start with the straight chain, then shorten it by one and attach the spare carbon as a methyl branch on an *inner* carbon; check every carbon has four bonds and count the atoms again.
5. **Displayed formulae** must show **every** bond, including C–H and O–H — drawing "OH" as a group loses the mark.
:::

::: example Definitions
Define the term homologous series and give two characteristics of its members. (3)

A homologous series is a family of compounds with the same general formula and the same functional group, in which each member differs from the next by CH₂ (1). Members have similar chemical properties because they share the functional group (1) and show a gradual trend in physical properties such as boiling point as the chain gets longer (1).
:::

::: example Formula types
Give the molecular, empirical and structural formulae of hexane. (3)

Alkanes have the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, so with n = 6 the molecular formula is C₆H₁₄ (1). Dividing by 2 gives the empirical formula C₃H₇ (1). Structural formula: CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ (1).
:::

::: example Naming
Name the compound CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ and the compound CH₃CH=CHCH₃. (2)

The longest chain is four carbons with a methyl branch on carbon 2: 2-methylbutane (1). The four-carbon alkene has its double bond between carbons 2 and 3: but-2-ene (1).
:::

::: example Isomers
Draw and name the structural isomers of C₅H₁₂. (3)

Pentane: CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃, a straight five-carbon chain (1). 2-methylbutane: CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃, a four-carbon chain with a methyl branch on carbon 2 (1). 2,2-dimethylpropane: C(CH₃)₄, a three-carbon chain with two methyl branches on the middle carbon (1). Each has the same molecular formula but a different structure.
:::

::: q
What is a hydrocarbon? (1)
---
A compound containing only hydrogen and carbon atoms.
:::

::: q
Which of these is not a hydrocarbon: C₂H₆, C₂H₄, C₂H₅OH, C₃H₈? Explain. (1)
---
C₂H₅OH (ethanol) — it contains oxygen as well as hydrogen and carbon.
:::

::: q
What is a functional group? (1)
---
The atom or group of atoms in a molecule that is responsible for its characteristic chemical reactions.
:::

::: q
Give the general formula of the alkanes and the alkenes. (2)
---
Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (1); alkenes CₙH₂ₙ (1).
:::

::: q
By how many atoms does each member of a homologous series differ from the next? (1)
---
One carbon and two hydrogens — a CH₂ unit.
:::

::: q
Define structural isomers. (1)
---
Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.
:::

::: q
Give the empirical formula of butene, C₄H₈. (1)
---
CH₂ (divide both subscripts by 4).
:::

::: q
Name CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ and CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃. (2)
---
But-1-ene (1); butan-2-ol (1).
:::

::: q
A student draws CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ and then draws the same four carbons in a zigzag and calls them isomers. Why is this wrong? (1)
---
Both drawings are the same continuous four-carbon chain (butane) — bending the chain does not change the structure, so they are the same compound.
:::

::: q
Explain why the boiling points of the alkanes increase with chain length. (2)
---
Longer molecules have more electrons and a larger surface area, so the intermolecular forces between them are stronger (1) and more energy is needed to separate them (1).
:::

::: watch
- Hydrocarbon = **hydrogen and carbon only** — alcohols and acids are not hydrocarbons.
- A homologous series definition needs the **general formula**, **CH₂ difference** and **functional group** ideas.
- Isomers must be **genuinely different structures** — the same chain drawn bent, or a branch on an end carbon, is the same molecule.
- Number the chain from the end giving the **lowest** locant: but-1-ene, not but-3-ene; 2-methylbutane, not 3-methylbutane.
- Displayed formulae show **every bond**, including in –OH and –COOH.
:::
