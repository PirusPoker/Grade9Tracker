## What it is

**Alkenes** are a homologous series of **unsaturated hydrocarbons**: each molecule contains one **carbon–carbon double bond, C=C**, which is the **functional group**. General formula **CₙH₂ₙ** — two fewer hydrogens than the alkane with the same number of carbons, because the double bond uses up two bonding positions.

| Alkene | Molecular formula | Structural formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethene** | **C₂H₄** | CH₂=CH₂ |
| **Propene** | **C₃H₆** | CH₂=CHCH₃ |
| **Butene** | **C₄H₈** | CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ (but-1-ene) or CH₃CH=CHCH₃ (but-2-ene) |
| Pentene | C₅H₁₀ | CH₂=CHCH₂CH₂CH₃ |

There is no "methene" — a double bond needs two carbons. Alkenes are made by **cracking** (4d) and are the starting materials for **polymers** and **ethanol**. Like alkanes they burn (C₂H₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O), but with smokier flames.

**Addition reactions.** The double bond is a region of high electron density and is much more reactive than a single bond. Alkenes react by **addition**: the double bond **opens** and a new atom or group **adds to each of the two carbons**, giving a **single saturated product** — nothing is lost.

| Reagent | Conditions | Product | Equation (ethene) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bromine** (bromine water or Br₂ vapour) | room temperature, no light needed | **dibromoalkane** | C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (1,2-dibromoethane) |
| **Hydrogen** | **nickel catalyst, ~150 °C** (hydrogenation) | **alkane** | C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ |
| **Steam** (water) | **phosphoric acid catalyst, 300 °C, 60–70 atm** (hydration) | **alcohol** | C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH |

Hydrogenation is used to turn liquid vegetable oils (unsaturated) into solid spreads. Hydration of ethene is the industrial route to ethanol (4g).

**Test for unsaturation — bromine water.** Shake the hydrocarbon with **bromine water**: an alkene turns it from **orange to colourless** (the bromine is used up in the addition reaction); an alkane produces **no change — stays orange**. Never write "clear" — clear means transparent, and orange bromine water is already clear; the mark is for **colourless**.

**Alkane vs alkene** — same test distinguishes the two families: propane (saturated) vs propene (unsaturated). Alkenes also burn with a more yellow, smoky flame because of their higher carbon-to-hydrogen ratio.

::: method
1. **Formulae**: CₙH₂ₙ; check the structural formula has exactly one C=C and every carbon has four bonds (a C in a double bond has only **two** other bonds).
2. **Unsaturated** = contains a **C=C double bond**; say "carbon–carbon double bond", not just "double bond".
3. **Addition equations**: reactant + small molecule → single product; the double bond becomes single and the two new atoms sit on **adjacent** carbons. Name the product: 1,2-dibromoethane, ethane, ethanol.
4. **Conditions**: hydrogen — nickel catalyst; steam — phosphoric acid catalyst, high temperature and pressure; bromine — none.
5. **Bromine water test**: "orange to colourless" for an alkene; "stays orange" for an alkane.
:::

::: example Definition
Explain why propene is described as an unsaturated hydrocarbon, and give its molecular formula. (2)

Propene contains a carbon–carbon double bond, so it does not hold the maximum possible number of hydrogen atoms (1). Its molecular formula is C₃H₆ (1).
:::

::: example Bromine
Ethene is bubbled through bromine water. Describe what is seen, write the equation and name the product and the type of reaction. (4)

The bromine water changes from orange to colourless (1). C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (1). The product is 1,2-dibromoethane (1). It is an addition reaction — the double bond opens and a bromine atom adds to each carbon (1).
:::

::: example Hydrogen and steam
Write equations, with conditions, for the reactions of ethene with (a) hydrogen and (b) steam. (4)

(a) C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆, using a nickel catalyst at about 150 °C — the product is ethane (2). (b) C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH, with a phosphoric acid catalyst at about 300 °C and 60–70 atmospheres — the product is ethanol (2).
:::

::: example Isomers of butene
Draw the structural formulae of two straight-chain isomers of butene and explain why they are isomers. (3)

But-1-ene: CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ (1). But-2-ene: CH₃CH=CHCH₃ (1). Both have the molecular formula C₄H₈, but the double bond is in a different position, so they have different structural formulae (1).
:::

CₙH₂ₙ.

What is the functional group of the alkenes? (1)

The carbon–carbon double bond, C=C.

Ethene C₂H₄; propene C₃H₆; butene C₄H₈.

Why is there no alkene with one carbon atom? (1)

A carbon–carbon double bond needs two carbon atoms.

C₃H₆ + Br₂ → C₃H₆Br₂ (1); 1,2-dibromopropane (1).

::: q
What catalyst is used for the addition of hydrogen to an alkene? (1)
---
Nickel.
:::

Name the product formed when hydrogen is added to propene. (1)
---
Propane.
:::

::: q
Describe the test and result for an unsaturated hydrocarbon. (2)
---
Shake with bromine water (1); it turns from orange to colourless (1).
:::

::: q
Why does bromine water not change colour with hexane? (1)
---
Hexane is saturated — it has no C=C double bond for bromine to add across.
:::

::: q
Why are addition reactions called "addition"? (1)
---
Two molecules join to form a single product, with nothing removed — the atoms are added across the double bond.
:::

::: watch
- Alkenes: **CₙH₂ₙ**, functional group **C=C**, **unsaturated** — mention the *carbon–carbon* double bond.
- Bromine water goes **orange → colourless**, not "clear" and not "white".
- Addition gives **one product**; substitution (alkanes) gives **two**.
- Hydrogen needs a **nickel catalyst**; steam needs **phosphoric acid**, high temperature and pressure.
- The Br atoms end up on **neighbouring carbons**: 1,2-dibromoethane.
:::
