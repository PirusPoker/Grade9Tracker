## What it is

An **atom** has a tiny, dense **nucleus** of **protons** and **neutrons**, surrounded by **electrons** in **shells** (energy levels).

| Particle | Relative mass | Relative charge | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | 1 | +1 | nucleus |
| **Neutron** | 1 | 0 | nucleus |
| **Electron** | 1/1836 (negligible) | −1 | shells around the nucleus |

Atoms are **neutral**: number of electrons = number of protons.

- **Atomic number** (Z) = number of **protons** — this defines the element. (Also the number of electrons in a neutral atom.)
- **Mass number** (A) = **protons + neutrons**.
- Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.
- Notation: mass number top-left, atomic number bottom-left: ²³₁₁Na has 11 protons, 11 electrons, 12 neutrons.

**Isotopes** are atoms of the **same element** (same number of protons) with **different numbers of neutrons**, so different mass numbers. Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 both have 17 protons; they have 18 and 20 neutrons. Isotopes have the **same chemical properties** (same electrons) but different masses.

**Relative atomic mass (Ar)** is the **weighted mean mass** of the atoms of an element (its isotopes in their natural abundances) compared with 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom. Because it is a weighted mean it is often not a whole number:

$$A_r = \frac{\sum (\text{isotope mass} \times \text{\% abundance})}{100}$$

Chlorine: 75% Cl-35, 25% Cl-37 → $\dfrac{35 \times 75 + 37 \times 25}{100} = 35.5$.

**Electronic configuration** — electrons fill shells from the inside: the **first shell holds 2**, the **second 8**, the **third 8** (for the first 20 elements). Write it as numbers separated by dots: sodium (11 electrons) = **2.8.1**; chlorine (17) = **2.8.7**; calcium (20) = **2.8.8.2**. Drawn as concentric circles with dots or crosses.

**The periodic table** is arranged in order of **atomic number**. Elements in the same **group** (column) have the **same number of outer-shell electrons** — the group number (Group 1 has 1, Group 7 has 7; Group 0 has full outer shells). Elements in the same **period** (row) have the same number of shells. So the electronic configuration can be read from the table: 2.8.1 → Group 1, Period 3.

**Why elements in a group react similarly**: chemical reactions involve the **outer electrons**, and elements in a group have the same number of them — so they lose, gain or share electrons in the same way and form ions with the same charge (Group 1 → +1, Group 7 → −1). **Noble gases (Group 0)** are unreactive because they have **full outer shells** — a stable arrangement — so they have no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons.

Metals are on the left and centre (lose electrons to form positive ions); non-metals on the right (gain or share electrons). The dividing line runs stepwise from boron to astatine.

::: method
1. **Counting particles**: protons = atomic number; electrons = protons (neutral atom); neutrons = mass − atomic number. For ions, adjust electrons only.
2. **Isotope definition**: same protons, different neutrons — say both halves. Same chemical properties because the electrons are the same.
3. **Ar calculation**: multiply each mass by its abundance, add, divide by 100 (or by the total abundance if not percentages). Give the answer to one decimal place. Sanity-check: the answer lies between the isotope masses, nearer the more abundant.
4. **Configurations**: 2, then 8, then 8, then 2 — dots between. Outer number = group; number of shells = period.
5. **Group similarity**: same outer electrons → same reactions; noble gases: full outer shell → stable → unreactive.
:::

::: example Particles
An atom of potassium is ³⁹₁₉K. State the number of protons, neutrons and electrons, and write its electronic configuration. (3)

19 protons, 19 electrons (1); 39 − 19 = 20 neutrons (1); configuration 2.8.8.1 (1).
:::

::: example Isotopes
Define "isotopes" and explain why isotopes of an element have the same chemical properties. (3)

Isotopes are atoms of the same element, with the same number of protons, but different numbers of neutrons (2). Chemical properties depend on the electrons, and isotopes have the same number and arrangement of electrons (1).
:::

::: example Relative atomic mass
Boron exists as 20% boron-10 and 80% boron-11. Calculate its relative atomic mass. (2)

$\dfrac{10 \times 20 + 11 \times 80}{100} = \dfrac{200 + 880}{100} = 10.8$ (2).
:::

::: example Group reactivity
Explain why lithium, sodium and potassium react in a similar way, and why neon does not react. (3)

Lithium, sodium and potassium are all in Group 1, so each has one electron in its outer shell (1), which is easily lost in reactions to form a +1 ion — so they react in the same way (1). Neon has a full outer shell of eight electrons, a stable arrangement, so it has no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons (1).
:::

::: q
State the relative mass and charge of a proton, a neutron and an electron. (3)
---
Proton: 1, +1; neutron: 1, 0; electron: negligible (1/1836), −1.
:::

::: q
Define atomic number and mass number. (2)
---
Atomic number — the number of protons in the nucleus (1); mass number — the total number of protons and neutrons (1).
:::

::: q
An atom has 12 protons and 13 neutrons. Give its mass number, its element and its electronic configuration. (3)
---
Mass number 25 (1); magnesium (atomic number 12) (1); 2.8.2 (1).
:::

::: q
Why is the relative atomic mass of chlorine 35.5 and not a whole number? (2)
---
It is the weighted mean of its isotopes (1): 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37 average to 35.5 (1).
:::

::: q
Copper is 69% Cu-63 and 31% Cu-65. Calculate its Ar to one decimal place. (2)
---
$\dfrac{63 \times 69 + 65 \times 31}{100} = \dfrac{4{,}347 + 2{,}015}{100} = 63.6$.
:::

::: q
Write the electronic configurations of oxygen (8), aluminium (13) and argon (18). (3)
---
2.6; 2.8.3; 2.8.8.
:::

::: q
An element has the configuration 2.8.7. Give its group and period, and state whether it is a metal. (3)
---
Group 7 (1); Period 3 (1); a non-metal — it gains an electron to form a −1 ion (1).
:::

::: q
How is the periodic table ordered, and what do elements in the same period have in common? (2)
---
By increasing atomic number (1); the same number of electron shells (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why atoms are electrically neutral. (1)
---
They have equal numbers of protons (+) and electrons (−).
:::

::: q
A magnesium ion is Mg²⁺. How many electrons does it have? (1)
---
10 (12 − 2).
:::

::: watch
- **Ar from isotopes is a weighted mean**: mass × abundance, summed, ÷ 100 — not a simple average.
- Isotopes: **same protons, different neutrons** — both halves of the definition.
- Configurations **2.8.8.2** — third shell stops at 8 for the first 20 elements.
- **Outer electrons = group number**; shells = period. Reactions are about **outer electrons**.
- Noble gases: **full outer shell → stable → unreactive.**
:::
