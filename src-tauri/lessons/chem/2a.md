## What it is

**Group 1** — lithium, sodium, potassium (rubidium, caesium, francium) — the **alkali metals**. They are **soft** (cut with a knife), have **low densities** (lithium, sodium and potassium float on water) and **low melting points** for metals, and are so reactive they are stored under oil. Each has **one electron in its outer shell**, which it loses easily to form a **+1 ion** — which is why they react similarly.

**Reactions with water** — all produce a **metal hydroxide** (an alkali, so the solution turns universal indicator purple) and **hydrogen gas**:

- 2Li + 2H₂O → 2LiOH + H₂
- 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂
- 2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂

| Metal | Observations with water |
|---|---|
| **Lithium** | floats, fizzes steadily, moves around slowly, gradually disappears |
| **Sodium** | floats, fizzes vigorously, melts into a shiny ball, moves quickly, disappears fast |
| **Potassium** | floats, fizzes violently, melts, moves very fast, the hydrogen ignites with a **lilac flame**, may spit |

All three: fizzing (hydrogen), the metal disappears, the solution becomes alkaline. The reaction is exothermic, which is why sodium and potassium melt.

**Similar reactions, explained**: all Group 1 atoms have **one outer electron**; in reactions each loses it to form an ion with a full outer shell: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻. Because they lose the same number of electrons they form the same type of compounds (M₂O, MOH, MCl) and react in the same way.

**Trend — reactivity increases down the group** (lithium < sodium < potassium): going down, the atoms get **larger** — the outer electron is in a shell **further from the nucleus**, and is **shielded** by more inner shells, so the **attraction between the nucleus and the outer electron is weaker**; the electron is **lost more easily**, so the metal reacts faster. (The nucleus has more protons lower down, but the shielding and distance effects outweigh it.)

**Predicting other Group 1 elements**: rubidium and caesium, below potassium, react even more violently with water (exploding), are softer, have even lower melting points, and form the same compounds (RbOH, Cs₂O). Francium would be the most reactive of all.

Other reactions: with oxygen they burn to form oxides (4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O — sodium burns with a yellow flame, potassium lilac); with chlorine they form chlorides (2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl). Compounds are white ionic solids, soluble in water.

::: terms
- **Alkali metals**: the Group 1 elements — lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, caesium; soft metals with one outer electron.
- **Alkali metal + water**: metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen, e.g. 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂.
- **Alkali metal + oxygen**: the metals tarnish quickly in air, forming oxides, e.g. 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O — which is why they are stored under oil.
- **Metal hydroxide**: the alkaline product of a Group 1 metal reacting with water, e.g. sodium hydroxide, which turns universal indicator purple.
- **Reactivity trend in Group 1**: reactivity increases down the group, because the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so it is lost more easily.
:::

::: method
1. **Observations**: three per metal, escalating — floats/fizzes/moves for lithium; add melts and faster for sodium; add lilac flame and violent for potassium.
2. **Equations**: balanced, with the alkali hydroxide and hydrogen; state symbols (s), (l), (aq), (g) if asked.
3. **Similarity = same outer electron count (one)** → lose one electron → +1 ion. Say it explicitly.
4. **Trend = atom size and shielding**: outer electron further from the nucleus, more shielding, weaker attraction, lost more easily. Give the *reason*, not just "more reactive".
5. **Prediction questions**: extrapolate the trend — more violent, lower melting point, softer — and write the analogous compound formula.
:::

::: example Observations and equation
Describe what you would see when a small piece of sodium is added to water, and write a balanced equation. (4)

The sodium floats and fizzes vigorously as hydrogen is given off (1); it melts into a silvery ball and moves rapidly across the surface (1); it gets smaller and disappears, and the solution turns purple with universal indicator, showing an alkali has formed (1). 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ (1).
:::

::: example Similarity
Explain why lithium, sodium and potassium react with water in a similar way. (2)

They are all in Group 1, so each has one electron in its outer shell (1). In the reaction each atom loses that one electron to form a +1 ion, so they all form hydroxides and hydrogen in the same way (1).
:::

::: example Trend
Explain why potassium is more reactive than sodium. (3)

A potassium atom is larger, so its outer electron is further from the nucleus (1) and is shielded by more inner shells of electrons (1). The attraction between the nucleus and the outer electron is therefore weaker, so the electron is lost more easily and potassium reacts faster (1).
:::

::: example Prediction
Rubidium is below potassium in Group 1. Predict how it reacts with water and write an equation for the reaction. (3)

It reacts even more violently than potassium — it may explode, and the hydrogen ignites immediately (1). It forms rubidium hydroxide and hydrogen (1): 2Rb + 2H₂O → 2RbOH + H₂ (1).
:::

::: q
Give three physical properties of the alkali metals. (3)
---
Soft; low density (float on water); low melting points for metals (also shiny when freshly cut, good conductors).
:::

::: q
Why are Group 1 metals stored under oil? (1)
---
They react rapidly with oxygen and water vapour in the air.
:::

::: q
Name the two products when lithium reacts with water. (2)
---
Lithium hydroxide (1) and hydrogen (1).
:::

::: q
What colour flame is seen when potassium reacts with water, and what is burning? (2)
---
Lilac (1); the hydrogen produced ignites (1).
:::

::: q
Write the equation for potassium reacting with water, with state symbols. (2)
---
2K(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g).
:::

::: q
Why does the solution become alkaline after a Group 1 metal reacts with water? (1)
---
A metal hydroxide forms, which dissolves to give hydroxide ions (OH⁻).
:::

::: q
State the trend in reactivity down Group 1. (1)
---
Reactivity increases down the group.
:::

::: q
Predict the formula of caesium oxide and caesium chloride. (2)
---
Cs₂O (1); CsCl (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a Group 1 atom forms a +1 ion rather than a −7 ion. (1)
---
Losing one electron gives a full outer shell far more easily than gaining seven.
:::

::: watch
- Trend reason: **further from the nucleus + more shielding → weaker attraction → electron lost more easily**. Not just "bigger".
- Observations escalate: **floats, fizzes, melts, lilac flame** — match them to the right metal.
- Products are **hydroxide + hydrogen** — never the oxide with water.
- Similarity is about the **one outer electron** and the **+1 ion**.
- Group 1 reactivity **increases** down; Group 7 **decreases** — keep them apart.
:::
