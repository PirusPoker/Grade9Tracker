## What it is

**Paper 3 (Reading, 1GN1/3H)** is worth **50 marks, 25%** of the GCSE and lasts **60 minutes**. No dictionary. All questions are in English and **all answers are in English**.

| Section | What you do | Marks | Time to spend |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Reading comprehension | eight questions on German texts, about 3 to 7 marks each | 40 | about 42 minutes (a minute a mark) |
| B Translation into English | one paragraph of five German sentences, rising in difficulty | 10 | about 10 minutes, as the paper advises |

That leaves 8 minutes to check. The texts cover all six thematic contexts: letters and emails, blogs, forum posts, adverts, brochures, guides, school posters, diaries, magazine articles. Some are cultural (transport in Austria, school in Switzerland, a festival in Köln).

**The question types and their traps.**

| Type | What it looks like | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| Gap-fill from a box | English sentences with gaps, a box of English words with **more words than gaps** | near-synonyms (*peaceful / boring*); a box word that is in the text but belongs to a different sentence |
| Complete the sentence, cross A, B or C | *(iii) The family ticket lasts …* | all three options are mentioned or nearly mentioned |
| Cross the **two** or **three** correct statements | six statements, cross exactly the number asked | half-true statements: right activity, wrong day; *nur*, *alle*, *nie* changing the scope |
| Table in English | *What she likes about her job (1 detail)* | a second, wrong detail can lose the mark |
| Short answers in English | *What did Elias do yesterday?* ("You do not need to write in full sentences") | answering a different question, or copying German |
| Inference, A, B or C | *Which of these is the best translation of the word …?* | the word is **not on the vocabulary list**; work it out |

**Glossed words and place names.** A word outside the list may be printed with an asterisk and its meaning underneath, and a little-known place name is explained (e.g. "a city in Germany"). Read the gloss first; it is there to help.

**Inference: two marks per paper.** Two single words from outside the vocabulary list are tested, one mark each. Work in three steps:
1. **Context:** what must the word mean for the sentence to make sense? (*Mein altes Handy war kaputt, also musste ich es ersetzen*: something you do to a broken phone: replace.)
2. **Word parts:** Pearson's own word-building rules. *Un-* gives the opposite (*unbekannt*); *-ung* makes a noun from a verb (*wandern → Wanderung*, a hike); *-er* makes a person from a verb (*anfangen → Anfänger*, a beginner); *-heit/-keit* [H] gives -ness or -ty (*Krankheit*); *-los* [H] means without (*arbeitslos*); *-chen/-lein* [H] means little (*Städtchen*); *Lieblings-* favourite; *Haupt-* main; *-s* on days and times means "on …s" (*montags*). Compounds take their meaning from the last part (*Fahrradweg*, a cycle path).
3. **Check:** put each option into the sentence and keep the one that fits the whole text.

**What Higher texts add.** The simple past (*fuhr, sah, kam, blieb, gab, war, hatte*); the conditional (*würde, hätte, wäre*); the genitive, for recognition only (*wegen des Wetters*, *das Auto meines Vaters*); Higher connectives (*nachdem, sobald, während, falls, da, damit, weder … noch, sondern*); relative clauses (*der Film, den ich gesehen habe*); *dessen/deren*; *da-* and *wo-* compounds (*dagegen*: against it).

**Precision is the grade 9 skill.** Most wrong answers come from a small word: negatives (*kein, nie, nicht mehr, noch nicht*), quantities (*alle, viele, manche, wenige, nur*), qualifiers (*fast, ziemlich, zu, sehr*), and time (*früher, jetzt, bald, schon, seit*). *Halb zwei* is 1.30.

**Section B: translation into English (10).** Marked as a whole, not word by word:

| Marks | What the examiner sees |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | meaning fully and appropriately transferred; any slips do not affect clarity |
| 6–8 | meaning mostly transferred; occasional errors affect clarity (a wrong or missing word, clumsy order) |
| 3–5 | parts transferred; some errors stop the message (missing phrases, wrong tenses) |
| 1–2 | only scattered parts transferred |

Pearson's exemplar scripts show that a close synonym is fine ("tasty" for *lecker*). What drags a script down is **adding** meaning ("must" that is not there), **wrong small words** (prepositions, quantities), **missing words** and **German left in the English**.

**Translation rules.**
- **Every word counts:** *oft, immer, schon, noch, vielleicht, leider, ziemlich, regelmäßig, fast* must all reach the English.
- **Tense:** perfect → English past (*ich habe gekocht* = I cooked); simple past → past; *seit* + present → "have been …ing for" (*Ich wohne seit zwei Jahren hier* = I have been living here for two years); *werden* + infinitive → will; *würde* → would.
- **Word order:** rebuild the sentence in English order; the German verb at the end comes forward.
- **False friends:** *bekommen* get; *also* so; *wer* who; *will* wants (from *wollen*); *Handy* mobile phone; *meinen* think; *Gymnasium* grammar school.
- **Natural English:** *Ich spiele gern Tennis* = I like playing tennis; *Es gibt* = there is / there are; *man* = you or people; *zu Hause* = at home.

::: terms
- **Section A**: eight comprehension questions, 40 marks, all answered in English.
- **Section B**: one five-sentence paragraph translated into English, 10 marks, marked holistically in five bands.
- **Gap-fill box**: a list of English words with more words than gaps; choose by meaning and by grammar.
- **Multiple response**: "Put a cross next to each one of the two / three correct statements"; cross exactly that number.
- **Inference item**: one mark for the best translation of a word outside the vocabulary list, worked out from context and word parts.
- **Gloss**: an asterisked word explained under the text, or a place name explained, because it is not on the list.
- **Derived word**: a listed word with one prefix or suffix added, such as un-, -ung, -er, -heit, -keit, -los, -chen.
- **Holistic marking**: the translation gets one mark for how fully and clearly the meaning comes across, not a mark per word.
- **False friend**: a German word that looks like an English one but means something else, e.g. bekommen means get.
- **Seit + present**: a German present tense that becomes English "have been …ing for / since".
- **Genitive**: a Higher reading-only case showing "of": wegen des Regens, the car of my father.
:::

::: method
1. **Read the title and question first**, underline what it asks (who, when, what kind of detail), then read the text.
2. **Find the part of the text** that answers each item; questions follow the order of the text.
3. **Before you commit,** check negatives, quantities, qualifiers and time words in that sentence.
4. **Gap-fill:** cross out each box word once it is used; check the chosen word fits both meaning and grammar.
5. **Statements:** test each one against the text and write T, F or "not mentioned" beside it; cross only the number asked.
6. **Tables and short answers:** one short English phrase with the exact detail asked; no German, no extra details.
7. **Inference:** context, word parts, then try each option in the sentence.
8. **Translation (about 10 minutes):** translate sentence by sentence, then tick off every German word against your English, check tenses and read it through as English.
:::

::: example Gap-fill with near-synonyms
Ben writes: *Mein Lieblingsessen ist Fisch, aber ich esse ihn nur am Wochenende. Unter der Woche koche ich mit meinem Vater, meistens Gemüse. Ich finde Kochen entspannend, aber mein Vater findet es anstrengend.* Box: **every day / only at weekends / his mother / his father / relaxing / boring / tiring**. (a) Ben eats fish ...... (b) During the week he cooks with ...... (c) Ben finds cooking ...... (3)

(a) only at weekends (1; *nur am Wochenende*; "every day" ignores *nur*). (b) his father (1). (c) relaxing (1; "tiring" is his father's view). *Entspannend* and *anstrengend* are not on the list; Pearson would gloss them, but the *aber* contrast already tells you the father's view is the opposite.
:::

::: example Three correct statements: testing each one
*In unserer Schule beginnt der Unterricht um acht Uhr, und um halb zwei ist die Schule schon aus. Nachmittags gibt es Klubs, zum Beispiel Theater oder Kochen.* Statements: A Lessons start at eight. B School ends at 2.30. C There are clubs in the afternoon. (2)

A true (1). B false: *halb zwei* is 1.30. C true (1). Writing T or F beside every statement before crossing stops you choosing B because it "sounds right".
:::

::: example Inference from context and word parts
*Mein altes Handy war kaputt, also musste ich es ersetzen. Nach einer langen Wanderung in den Bergen war ich total müde.* (a) Which of these is the best translation of the word *ersetzen*? A replace; B repair; C sell. (b) What is a *Wanderung*? (2)

(a) **A** (1). The phone was broken, so you had to do something to it; *also* (so) links the two ideas. Repair is possible in theory, but *alt* (old) and *ersetzen* containing *setzen* (put) point to putting a new one in its place. (b) a hike / walk (1): *wandern* + *-ung*.
:::

::: example Translation into English, with the band decided
Translate into English: *Meine Schule ist ziemlich groß. Ich habe montags immer zwei Stunden Mathe. Gestern hatten wir einen Test, aber er war nicht schwer. Ich hoffe, dass ich eine gute Note bekommen habe. Nächstes Jahr werde ich wahrscheinlich in der Oberstufe Mathe lernen.* (10)

**Model (9–10).** My school is quite big. I always have two hours of maths on Mondays. Yesterday we had a test, but it wasn't difficult. I hope that I got a good mark. Next year I will probably study maths in the sixth form.
**A 6–8 version.** "My school is big. I have two lessons of maths on Monday. Yesterday we had a test, but it was not hard. I hope that I will get a good mark. Next year I will study maths in the sixth form." It loses *ziemlich*, *immer*, *wahrscheinlich* and the plural sense of *montags*, and turns the perfect *bekommen habe* into a future: several small errors that affect clarity.
:::

::: q
Give the English meaning of each word: (a) *bekommen* (b) *also* (c) *wer* (3)
---
(a) to get / receive (1; not "become"). (b) so / therefore (1). (c) who (1; not "where", which is *wo*).
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *Seit zwei Jahren lerne ich Deutsch.* (2)
---
I have been learning German (1) for two years (1). "Since two years I learn German" scores 0 for the tense.
:::

::: q
What is the difference between *nicht mehr* and *noch nicht*? (2)
---
*Nicht mehr* = no longer / not any more (1). *Noch nicht* = not yet (1).
:::

::: q
[Higher, recognition] Translate into English: *Das Auto meines Vaters ist wegen des Regens schmutzig.* (2)
---
My father's car (1) is dirty because of the rain (1). Both *meines Vaters* and *des Regens* are genitive.
:::

::: q
What do these Higher derived words mean? (a) *arbeitslos* (b) *Krankheit* (2)
---
(a) unemployed / out of work (1; *-los* = without). (b) illness (1; *krank* + *-heit*).
:::

::: q
A question says "Put a cross next to each one of the three correct statements". You are sure of two and unsure between two others. What should you do? (1)
---
Cross exactly three: the two you are sure of plus the better of the other two after checking it against the text again (1). Four crosses can cost marks.
:::

::: watch
- **Answering in German**: Section A answers must be in English; a copied German word scores nothing.
- **Ignoring small words**: *nur, nie, fast, schon, noch nicht, nicht mehr* decide many answers.
- **Wrong number of crosses**: two means two, three means three.
- **Over-long table answers**: a second, wrong detail can lose a correct first one.
- **False friends**: *bekommen* (get), *will* (wants), *also* (so).
- **Tense drift in the translation**: the perfect is English past; *seit* + present is "have been".
- **Gaps and German in the translation**: write something sensible for every phrase; a German word left in drags the band down.
- **Inference by sound alone**: an unlisted word that looks like English may not mean the English; check it in the sentence.
:::
