## What it is

A **determiner** is the small word in front of a noun that says which one, whose or how many: *der, ein, kein, dieser, jeder, welcher, mein, viele*. In German it carries the **case**, so it changes with the noun's job in the sentence. Get the determiner right and the noun phrase is right; get it wrong and every sentence costs accuracy.

Where it matters:
- **Paper 4 (Writing and Q3 translation):** *mit meinem Vater, letzten Sommer, jeden Tag, kein Kino* turn up in every task. The accuracy grids reward "generally accurate" language, and case endings are where most German errors are.
- **Paper 3 (Reading) and Paper 2 (Listening):** *kein* reverses a statement, *jeder* means "every", *nächste* points to the future, and at Higher a genitive tells you **whose** something is.
- **Paper 1 (Speaking):** role plays need *Welcher Bus ...?*, *Ich möchte einen ...*, *Haben Sie keinen ...?*.

**What Edexcel lists.** Everything is examinable at both tiers except the right-hand column, which is Higher and **receptive only**: you must understand it in reading and listening, but you never have to produce it.

**1. What each case does.**
- **Nominative**: the subject, the person or thing doing the verb. *Der Lehrer ist nett.* Also after *sein* and *werden*: *Das ist mein Bruder.*
- **Accusative**: the direct object, the thing the verb acts on. *Ich habe einen Bruder. Ich kaufe den Rock.* Also after *es gibt* (*Es gibt einen Park*), after *für, durch, ohne, bis* and for time phrases (section 6).
- **Dative**: the indirect object, the person something is given, shown or told **to**. *Ich gebe dem Lehrer das Heft.* Also after *mit, von, zu, bei, aus, nach, seit*, and after *in, an, auf* for position (G5). A few verbs take a dative object: *Ich helfe meiner Mutter. Das Kleid gefällt mir.*

**2. The articles.**

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | der / ein | die / eine | das / ein | die / keine |
| accusative | **den / einen** | die / eine | das / ein | die / keine |
| dative | **dem / einem** | **der / einer** | **dem / einem** | **den / keinen** (+ noun -n) |

Only the masculine changes in the accusative. In the dative everything changes: *-em* for masculine and neuter, *-er* for feminine, *-en* for the plural, and the plural noun adds -n (G1a). *Ein* has no plural: "I have brothers" is simply *Ich habe Brüder*.

**3. kein (not a, no, not any).** *Kein* negates a noun that would have *ein* or no article, and takes the same endings as *ein*, with a plural *keine*. *Ich habe keinen Bruder. Es gibt keine Bibliothek. In meinem Dorf gibt es kein Kino und keine Geschäfte.* Use *nicht* for a verb or a noun with *der/die/das*: *Ich mag den Film nicht* (G2e). Pearson lists *kein* in the nominative and accusative; the dative (*mit keinem Freund*) is not required, credited if correct.

**4. The der-words: dieser, jeder, letzter, nächster, welcher.** They copy the endings of *der, die, das*.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | dies**er** | dies**e** | dies**es** | dies**e** |
| accusative | dies**en** | dies**e** | dies**es** | dies**e** |
| dative | dies**em** | dies**er** | dies**em** | dies**en** |

*Dieser Film ist spannend. Ich finde diese Jacke zu teuer. Welchen Bus nimmst du? Mit welchem Zug fährst du? Jedes Jahr fahren wir nach Österreich.* *Jeder* is singular only; for "all" in the plural use *alle*. *Letzter* and *nächster* are used mostly in time phrases (section 6).

**5. The possessives.** my *mein*, your *dein*, his *sein*, her *ihr*, its *sein*, our *unser*, your (plural) *euer*, your (formal) *Ihr*, their *ihr*. They take the endings of *ein*, and the plural endings of *kein*.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | mein | mein**e** | mein | mein**e** |
| accusative | mein**en** | mein**e** | mein | mein**e** |
| dative | mein**em** | mein**er** | mein**em** | mein**en** |

*Euer* drops its middle e when an ending is added: *euer Haus*, but *eure Schule*, *mit eurem Lehrer*. Formal *Ihr* has a capital letter: *Ist das Ihr Koffer?* Choose the word by the owner and the ending by the noun: *his sister* = *seine Schwester*; *her brother* = *ihr Bruder*; *with their parents* = *mit ihren Eltern*.

**6. Time phrases go in the accusative.** With no preposition, a time phrase takes accusative endings: *letzten Sommer, letzte Woche, letztes Jahr*; *jeden Tag, jede Woche, jedes Wochenende*; *nächsten Montag, nächste Woche, nächstes Jahr*; *diesen Samstag*. (With *am, im, in der* the time phrase is dative: *am Montag, im Sommer, in der Woche*.) Masculine is the one to watch: *letzten Sommer*, never *letzter Sommer*.

**7. Quantifiers.** With an **uncountable singular** noun use *viel* (a lot of, much) and *wenig* (little, not much), with no ending: *viel Zeit, viel Geld, wenig Obst, wenig Verkehr*. With a **plural** use *viele, wenige, alle, einige*: *viele Leute, wenige Geschäfte, alle Schüler, einige Freunde*. In the dative plural they end in -en: *mit vielen Leuten, mit allen Schülern, mit einigen Freunden*.

**8. [Higher, recognition only] The genitive.** It shows **whose** something is ("of the", "of my") and follows *trotz* (despite) and *wegen* (because of). You will meet it in reading and listening; in your own writing use *von* + dative instead (*der Hund von meiner Tante*).

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| genitive | **des** / eines / meines + noun -(e)s | **der** / einer / meiner | **des** / eines / meines + noun -(e)s | **der** / meiner |

*Das ist der Hund meiner Tante* (my aunt's dog). *Das Auto meines Vaters ist alt* (my father's car). *Trotz des Wetters sind wir gewandert* (despite the weather). *Wegen des Regens bleiben wir zu Hause* (because of the rain). Names simply add -s, as in English but with no apostrophe: *Annas Bruder*. The trap: the **owner comes second** in German. *Der Hund meiner Tante* is the aunt's dog, so the dog belongs to the aunt.

**What makes grade 9.** Dative endings right every time (*mit meinem Vater, mit meiner Mutter, mit meinen Eltern*), *his* and *her* never confused, time phrases in the accusative, and genitives read correctly in Paper 3.

::: terms
- **Nominative**: the case of the subject (and after sein): der Mann, ein Mann, dieser Mann.
- **Accusative**: the case of the direct object, of es gibt and of time phrases: den Mann, einen Mann, letzten Montag.
- **Dative**: the case of the indirect object and after mit, von, zu: dem Mann, der Frau, den Kindern.
- **Indirect object**: the person something is given, shown or told to: Ich zeige meiner Freundin die Stadt.
- **kein**: not a, no, not any; it takes ein-endings: keinen Bruder, keine Zeit, kein Kino, keine Geschäfte.
- **der-word**: dieser, jeder, letzter, nächster, welcher, which copy the endings of der, die, das.
- **Possessive**: mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr, ihr, which copy the endings of ein and kein.
- **Quantifier**: viel and wenig with uncountable singular nouns; viele, wenige, alle, einige with plurals.
- **Genitive**: [H, recognition] the case of the owner, "of the": der Hund meiner Tante, trotz des Wetters.
- **Time phrase**: a phrase of time without a preposition, put in the accusative: jeden Tag, nächste Woche.
:::

::: method
1. **Find the noun and its gender** (and whether it is plural).
2. **Find its job:** doer (nominative), done-to (accusative), given-to or after a dative word (dative). A time phrase with no preposition is accusative.
3. **Choose the family of endings:** der-endings for *der, dieser, jeder, welcher, letzter, nächster*; ein-endings for *ein, kein* and the possessives.
4. **Possessive?** Pick the word from the **owner** (*sein* his, *ihr* her or their), then the ending from the **noun**.
5. **Quantity?** Uncountable singular: *viel, wenig*. Plural: *viele, wenige, alle, einige*.
6. **Dative plural:** determiner in *-en* and noun in -n.
7. **[H] In reading, a genitive** (*des, der, meines, meiner* after a noun): the second noun is the owner.
:::

::: example Choosing the case from the job
Translate into German: *The teacher gives the boy a book.* (3)

*Der Lehrer (1) gibt dem Jungen (1) ein Buch (1).*

*Der Lehrer* is the subject: nominative. *The boy* is who it is given to: dative masculine *dem Jungen* (weak noun, Higher; *dem Kind* would avoid it). *A book* is the direct object: accusative neuter, which looks the same as the nominative, *ein Buch*. G1c covers the order of two objects.
:::

::: example Possessives: owner first, then ending
Translate into German: *My sister lives with her boyfriend in Vienna, and his parents live in Munich.* (4)

*Meine Schwester (1) wohnt mit ihrem Freund (1) in Wien, und seine Eltern (1) wohnen in München (1).*

*Ihrem*: the owner is she, so *ihr*; *mit* + masculine dative *-em*. *Seine Eltern*: the owner is he (the boyfriend), so *sein*; plural nominative *-e*. If it were her (female) friend: *mit ihrer Freundin*. The owner picks the word; the noun picks the ending.
:::

::: example Time phrases and quantifiers
Translate into German: *Last summer I had little time, but next year I will visit many cities.* (4)

*Letzten Sommer (1) hatte ich wenig Zeit (1), aber nächstes Jahr (1) werde ich viele Städte besuchen (1).*

*Letzten Sommer* and *nächstes Jahr* are accusative time phrases at the front, so the verb comes next (*hatte ich, werde ich*). *Wenig Zeit*: uncountable singular, no ending. *Viele Städte*: plural. *Letzter Sommer* or *viel Städte* would each cost an accuracy point.
:::

::: example [Higher] Reading a genitive
Read: *Das Fahrrad meines Bruders ist kaputt, also fahre ich mit dem Rad meiner Mutter. Trotz des Regens fahre ich jeden Tag zur Schule.* Answer in English: (a) Whose bike is broken? (b) Whose bike does the writer ride? (c) What does the writer do despite the rain? (3)

(a) The writer's brother's (1). (b) The writer's mother's (1). (c) Cycles to school every day (1).

*Meines Bruders* and *meiner Mutter* are genitives: the second noun owns the first. *Trotz des Regens* = despite the rain. You only need to recognise these; to write it yourself, use *das Fahrrad von meinem Bruder*.
:::

::: watch
- **Dative after mit**: *mit meinem Vater, mit meiner Mutter, mit meinen Eltern*. A bare *mit mein* is wrong every time.
- **His or her**: *sein* = his and its; *ihr* = her and their. "Her brother" is *ihr Bruder*, not *sein Bruder*.
- **Time phrases**: *letzten Sommer, nächsten Montag, jeden Tag*; the masculine *-en* is the ending people miss.
- **Es gibt + accusative**: *Es gibt einen Park, es gibt keinen Bahnhof*.
- **viel or viele**: *viel* + singular (*viel Zeit*), *viele* + plural (*viele Leute*).
- **euer loses its e**: *eure Schule, mit eurem Lehrer*.
- **[H] Genitive in reading**: the owner comes after: *der Hund meiner Tante* = my aunt's dog.
:::
