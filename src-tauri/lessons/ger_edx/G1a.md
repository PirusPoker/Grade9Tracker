## What it is

Every German noun has a **gender** (*der, die, das*), a **plural** and a **case**, and all three show in the words around it. Edexcel's grammar list (Appendix 2 of the 1GN1 spec) opens with nouns: how they are built from other words, how they become feminine and plural, and, at Higher, how verbs and adjectives turn into nouns.

Where it costs or earns marks:
- **Paper 4, Q3 (translation into German, 10 marks):** "my friends", "two brothers", "with the children". Each wrong plural or missing dative -n is an accuracy error, and the accuracy mark (out of 4) drops quickly.
- **Paper 3 (Reading):** the article and the ending tell you whether a text means one person or several: *der Lehrer* (the teacher) or *die Lehrer* (the teachers). A/B/C distractors are often built on exactly that.
- **Paper 2 (Dictation):** a plural ending is a sound you can hear (*Freunde*, *Freunden*), so write it. Lower-case nouns are not penalised; a missing ending is a missing sound.
- **Papers 1 and 4 (Speaking and Writing):** the Higher grids reward complex language, and *Das Gute ist, dass ...* or *etwas Neues* are quick ways to show it.

**What Edexcel lists.** Everything below is examinable; the right-hand column is Higher only.

| Foundation + Higher | Higher only |
|---|---|
| compound nouns: linking -e, -s/-es, -n/-en, -ens, -er; dropping -e/-en; gender from the last word | -(e)n plurals of some masculine people nouns and weak masculine nouns (*die Jungen*) |
| feminine person nouns with -in (*Freundin, Lehrerin*) | plural adjectival nouns in the nominative and accusative (*die Reichen*) |
| the plural patterns, and -n in the dative plural (*den Brüdern*) | *das Gute*; *etwas Gutes, viel Neues, wenig Interessantes, alles Gute* |
| infinitives as nouns (*das Schwimmen*); languages as nouns (*das Englisch*) | |

Nouns that break these patterns appear as separate items in the vocabulary list (*der Arzt, die Ärztin*; *die Mutter, die Mütter*): learn them as words. A form that is neither listed nor made by one of these rules is not examined.

**1. Compound nouns.** German joins words to make new nouns. The **last word decides the gender** and takes the plural ending; the words in front only narrow the meaning.

| Join | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| nothing added | das Haus + die Tür → **die** Haustür | front door |
| -e | der Tag + das Buch → **das** Tagebuch | diary |
| -s / -es | die Arbeit + die Stunde → **die** Arbeitsstunde; der Tag + die Zeitung → **die** Tageszeitung | working hour; daily newspaper |
| -n / -en | die Tasche + das Geld → **das** Taschengeld; die Frau + der Fußball → **der** Frauenfußball | pocket money; women's football |
| -ens | das Herz + der Wunsch → **der** Herzenswunsch | heart's desire |
| -er | das Kind + das Zimmer → **das** Kinderzimmer | child's bedroom |
| drop -e / -en | die Schule + das Buch → **das** Schulbuch; wohnen + das Zimmer → **das** Wohnzimmer | school book; living room |

In reading, split a long word **from the end**: *Geburtstagsgeschenk* = *Geburtstag + s + Geschenk*, a birthday present, and it is neuter because *das Geschenk* is. Compounding is on the grammar list, so expect compounds of listed words in texts without a gloss. The plural belongs to the last word too: *das Schulbuch → die Schulbücher*.

**2. Feminine person nouns.** Add **-in** to the male noun: *der Freund → die Freundin*, *der Lehrer → die Lehrerin*, *der Schauspieler → die Schauspielerin*, *der Polizist → die Polizistin*, *der Nachbar → die Nachbarin*. The plural adds **-nen**: *die Lehrerinnen, die Freundinnen*. A few add an umlaut as well (*der Arzt → die Ärztin*); those are listed as separate words.

**3. Plurals.** In the plural the article is always *die* in the nominative and accusative, whatever the gender. These are Edexcel's patterns:

| Pattern | Which nouns | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| -e | most masculine and neuter nouns | der Monat → die Monate; das Jahr → die Jahre; der Freund → die Freunde; das Spiel → die Spiele |
| ¨-e | some masculines, and some one-syllable feminines | der Platz → die Plätze; der Zug → die Züge; die Stadt → die Städte; die Angst → die Ängste; die Nacht → die Nächte |
| no change | many nouns ending -el, -en, -er (and -chen) | der Artikel → die Artikel; der Kuchen → die Kuchen; das Theater → die Theater; der Lehrer → die Lehrer; das Mädchen → die Mädchen |
| -er / ¨-er | some masculine and neuter nouns | der Mann → die Männer; das Licht → die Lichter; das Buch → die Bücher; das Kind → die Kinder; das Haus → die Häuser |
| -n / -en | most feminine and some neuter nouns | die Frage → die Fragen; die Zahl → die Zahlen; die Schule → die Schulen; das Auge → die Augen; das Bett → die Betten |
| -nen | feminine nouns in -in | die Lehrerin → die Lehrerinnen |
| -s | borrowed words | das Hotel → die Hotels; das Handy → die Handys; das Auto → die Autos; das Kino → die Kinos |
| -se | nouns ending -nis | das Verständnis → die Verständnisse; das Ergebnis → die Ergebnisse [H word] |

A few -el/-en/-er nouns add only an umlaut: *der Bruder → die Brüder*, *der Vater → die Väter*, *der Garten → die Gärten*. The patterns tell you what is likely; for the nouns you use in your own writing, learn the plural with the noun, and if you are unsure of a plural, choose a noun you are sure of.

**4. The dative plural adds -n.** Every plural noun in the dative ends in **-n**: *mit meinen Freunden*, *mit den Kindern*, *von meinen Brüdern*, *mit zwei Hunden*, *in den Städten*. Two exceptions: if the plural already ends in -n, nothing more is added (*mit den Frauen*, *in den Ferien*, *mit den Lehrerinnen*), and -s plurals never add it (*in den Hotels*, *mit den Handys*). You need the dative after *mit, von, zu, bei, aus, nach, seit*, after *in, an, auf* for position, and for the indirect object (G1b and G5).

**5. Infinitives and languages as nouns.** Any infinitive can become a **neuter noun** with a capital letter: *schwimmen → das Schwimmen* (swimming), *wandern → das Wandern* (hiking), *lesen → das Lesen* (reading). Use it where English has an "-ing" word as the subject: *Das Wandern ist gesund.* The article is often dropped: *Schwimmen macht mir Spaß.* Both are correct.
A language is the adjective with a capital letter and no ending: *englisch → (das) Englisch*. *Englisch ist mein Lieblingsfach. Mein Deutsch ist nicht perfekt. Ich lerne seit drei Jahren Spanisch.*

**6. [Higher] Weak masculine nouns.** Some masculine nouns for people add **-(e)n** for the plural: *der Junge → die Jungen*, *der Mensch → die Menschen*, *der Herr → die Herren*, *der Nachbar → die Nachbarn*, *der Student → die Studenten*, *der Polizist → die Polizisten*, *der Kunde → die Kunden* [H word]. You will also meet the -(e)n in the singular after *den* and *dem*: *Ich kenne den Jungen*; *mit unserem Nachbarn*; *Herrn Weber*. Pearson lists only the plural, so learn that; the singular forms are worth recognising and are credited when you use them correctly.

**7. [Higher] Adjectives used as nouns.** Give an adjective a capital letter and it becomes a noun.
- **Groups of people (plural, nominative and accusative):** *reich → die Reichen* (the rich), *arm → die Armen* (the poor). Pearson's list already has *der/die Jugendliche* (young person) and *[H] der/die Erwachsene* (adult), and they work the same way: *die Jugendlichen, die Erwachsenen*. With no article the ending is -e: *viele Jugendliche, einige Erwachsene*. *Viele Erwachsene verstehen die Jugendlichen nicht.*
- **An abstract idea (neuter singular):** *das Gute* (the good thing), *das Schlechte* (the bad thing), *das Interessante*. The phrase to learn is **Das Gute an ... ist, dass ...**: *Das Gute an meiner Schule ist, dass die Lehrer sehr nett sind.* (*dass* sends the verb to the end.)
- **After etwas, viel, wenig:** add **-es**: *etwas Neues* (something new), *viel Gutes* (a lot of good), *wenig Interessantes* (not much of interest). After **alles** add **-e**: *alles Gute* (all the best), *alles Neue*. *In meiner Stadt gibt es wenig Interessantes für junge Leute. Im Urlaub möchte ich etwas Neues ausprobieren. Alles Gute zum Geburtstag!* (*Nichts Neues* works the same way; *nichts* is not in this rule, so it is not required, credited if correct.)

**What makes grade 9.** Plurals that are right first time in a translation, the dative plural -n every time (*mit meinen Freunden*), and one or two Higher touches placed naturally: *Das Gute ist, dass ...*, *etwas Neues*, *die Jugendlichen*.

::: terms
- **Compound noun**: a noun made from two or more words; the last word gives the gender and takes the plural: das Schulbuch, die Schulbücher.
- **Linking letter**: the -e, -s/-es, -n/-en, -ens or -er that joins the parts of a compound: Arbeitsstunde, Taschengeld, Kinderzimmer.
- **Feminine -in**: the ending that turns a male person noun into a female one: der Lehrer, die Lehrerin; plural die Lehrerinnen.
- **Plural pattern**: one of Edexcel's ways of making a plural: -e, ¨-e, no change, -er, -n/-en, -nen, -s, -se.
- **Dative plural -n**: every plural noun in the dative ends in -n unless it already does or its plural is -s: mit den Kindern, in den Hotels.
- **Nominalised infinitive**: an infinitive used as a neuter noun with a capital letter: das Schwimmen, das Wandern.
- **Weak masculine noun**: [H] a masculine person noun with an -(e)n plural (and -(e)n after den/dem): der Junge, die Jungen.
- **Adjectival noun**: [H] an adjective used as a noun with a capital letter and an adjective ending: die Reichen, die Erwachsenen, das Gute.
- **etwas Neues**: [H] etwas, viel or wenig + capitalised adjective + -es: something new, a lot of good, not much of interest.
- **alles Gute**: [H] alles + capitalised adjective + -e: all the best; also on the vocabulary list as a set phrase.
:::

::: method
1. **Find the gender.** For a compound, look only at the last word: *die Tür* makes *die Haustür*.
2. **Singular or plural?** In reading, check the article and the verb: *die Lehrer sind* (plural) against *der Lehrer ist* (singular).
3. **Make the plural** from the pattern you have learned for that noun; for your own writing, prefer nouns whose plural you know.
4. **Check the case.** If the plural is in the dative (after *mit, von, zu, bei, aus, nach*, or *in* for position), add -n unless it already ends in -n or -s.
5. **Female person?** Add -in, plural -innen, unless the list gives a separate word (*Ärztin*).
6. **English "-ing" as a subject?** Use the infinitive as a neuter noun: *(Das) Schwimmen ist ...*
7. **[H] "something new", "the good thing"?** *etwas* + capital + -es; *das* + capital + -e. People in general: *die Reichen, die Jugendlichen*.
8. **Capitals:** every noun, including *das Wandern, das Englisch, etwas Neues*, starts with a capital letter.
:::

::: example Working out compounds in a reading text
Give the gender and the English meaning of each word: (a) *Geburtstagsgeschenk* (b) *Kinderbuch* (c) *Sportlehrerin*. (3)

(a) *das* (from *das Geschenk*), a birthday present (1). (b) *das* (from *das Buch*), a children's book (1). (c) *die* (from *die Lehrerin*), a (female) PE teacher (1).

Split from the end: the last word gives the gender and the core meaning, and the front word says what kind. The *-s-* in *Geburtstags-* and the *-er-* in *Kinder-* are only linking letters.
:::

::: example Plurals and the dative plural in a translation
Translate into German: *I go to the cinema with my friends and my two brothers.* (4)

*Ich gehe mit meinen Freunden (1) und meinen zwei Brüdern (1) ins Kino (1).* Verb second, *ins Kino* for movement (1).

*Mit* takes the dative. *Freund* makes *Freunde* (pattern -e), and the dative plural adds -n: *Freunden*. *Bruder* makes *Brüder* (umlaut only), and the dative adds -n: *Brüdern*. *Mit meinen Freunde* or *mit meinen zwei Brüder* each lose an accuracy point.
:::

::: example One or several? Reading the article
Translate into English: *Die Lehrerinnen an meiner Schule sind nett, aber der Lehrer für Mathe ist nicht sehr freundlich. Die Stunden sind lang, und die Klassen sind groß.* (4)

The (female) teachers at my school are nice (1), but the maths teacher is not very friendly (1). The lessons are long (1) and the classes are big (1).

*Lehrerinnen* (-nen) is plural, female; *der Lehrer ... ist* is one male teacher. *Stunden* and *Klassen* are plurals (-n), confirmed by *sind*. Writing "the teacher" for *die Lehrerinnen* changes the meaning.
:::

::: example [Higher] Das Gute and etwas Neues
Translate into German: *The good thing about the holidays is that I can try something new.* (4)

*Das Gute an den Ferien (1) ist, dass (1) ich etwas Neues (1) ausprobieren kann (1).*

*Das Gute* is the adjective *gut* as a neuter noun. *An* + dative plural: *Ferien* already ends in -n. *Etwas* + *Neues* with a capital and -es. After *dass*, the modal goes last, after the infinitive: *ausprobieren kann*.
:::

::: watch
- **Dative plural -n**: *mit meinen Freunden, mit den Kindern*. *Mit meinen Freunde* is one of the most common noun errors in Higher writing.
- **No extra -n on -s plurals**: *in den Hotels, mit den Autos*.
- **The front word does not decide the gender**: *die Haustür* (not *das*), *das Schulbuch* (not *die*).
- **Plural verb**: a plural noun needs a plural verb: *Die Lehrer sind nett*, not *ist*.
- **Umlaut plurals**: *Brüder, Städte, Bücher, Häuser*. In dictation and writing a missing umlaut is a spelling error that can change the meaning (*Bruder* is one brother).
- **Capitals on nominalised words**: *das Schwimmen, etwas Neues, die Jugendlichen*.
- **[H] etwas + -es, alles + -e**: *etwas Gutes* but *alles Gute*.
:::

::: test
Translate into German.

*My parents have two cars. The teachers at my school are friendly, but the lessons are very long. I often play football with the boys from my street. Last weekend I went to the cinema with my friends. The good thing about my town is that there is something new every week.* (10)
