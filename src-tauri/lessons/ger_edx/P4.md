## What it is

**Paper 4 (Writing, 1GN1/4H)** is worth **50 marks, 25%** of the GCSE and lasts **1 hour 20 minutes**. No dictionary. The rubric is in English; you write in German.

| Question | Task | Words | Marks | Time plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | choose (a) or (b): an informal piece, "Write to someone you know about …", **four bullet points** | 80–90 | 18 (response 13 + accuracy 5) | about 20 min |
| 2 | choose (a) or (b): a formal piece such as a blog or article, **four bullet points**, one of them "the pros and cons of …" | 130–150 | 22 (response 17 + accuracy 5) | 35–40 min |
| 3 | translate an English paragraph of five sentences, rising in difficulty, into German | about 60–70 English words | 10 (meaning 6 + accuracy 4) | about 10 min |

That leaves about 10 minutes to check. The word counts are **guidance**: there is no penalty for writing more or fewer, but writing far more adds errors, not marks.

**The bullets follow a pattern.** Usually the first is present (what you do or like), the second an opinion with reasons (or the pros and cons in Q2), the third a **past** event (*the last time …, recently, last week*) and the fourth the **future** (*next time, after your exams, in the summer*). Bullets can be answered in any order, but each must clearly be there.

**Choosing (a) or (b).** Spend one minute: pick the option where you can write a **past** and a **future** bullet confidently and where the opinion bullet gives you reasons you can say in German. The topic you like best is not always the one you can write best.

**Question 1 grids (18).**

| Response to stimulus (13) | Linguistic knowledge and accuracy (5) |
|---|---|
| 11–13: all four bullets, frequent development, comprehensible | 5: some variety and extended sentences, occasional complex language, three time frames generally successful, generally accurate (an occasional major error) |
| 8–10: three or more bullets, some development | 3–4: occasional variety, at least two time frames with some success, mostly minor errors |
| 5–7: two or more, occasional brief development | 1–2: limited variety, short simple sentences, frequent errors |
| 1–4: one or more, little detail | |

**Question 2 grids (22).**

| Response to stimulus (17) | Linguistic knowledge and accuracy (5) |
|---|---|
| 14–17: all four bullets, **consistent** development, easily comprehensible | 4–5: wide variety, frequent extended sentences and **frequent** complex language, three time frames **consistently** successful, any errors minor |
| 11–13: all four, frequent development | 2–3: some variety and complex language, at least two time frames generally successful, mostly minor errors |
| 8–10: three or more, some development | 1: occasional variety, occasional success with time frames, frequent errors |
| 5–7: two or more; 1–4: one or more | |

A missing bullet caps you: with three bullets, the best response mark is 10 in both questions, however good the German.

**Development** means an extra clause or sentence on a bullet: a reason, an example, a contrast, a consequence. *Ich spiele Tennis* addresses a bullet; *Ich spiele zweimal pro Woche Tennis, weil es mich fit hält, aber im Winter ist es oft zu kalt* develops it.

**Complex language**, as Pearson defines it: longer sentences with *und, aber, oder* and with subordinating conjunctions (*weil, wenn, dass, obwohl*; Higher *nachdem, sobald, während, damit, da, falls*); relative clauses (*der Film, den ich gesehen habe*); infinitive clauses (*um … zu, ohne … zu, statt … zu*); varied pronouns (*ihn, ihr, uns, sich*); several conjugated verbs in one sentence; word order unlike English (*Gestern bin ich …*, verb at the end).

**Time frames** can be shown by any tense: a present with *morgen* counts as future. Aim for each tense at least twice, and add the conditional (*Wenn ich Zeit hätte, würde ich …*) and the simple past of *sein, haben* and *es gibt* (*war, hatte, gab*) for variety.

**Minor or major?** Pearson's line:
- **Minor (meaning still clear):** wrong adjective ending (*ein schön Tag*), wrong gender (*mein Schule*), wrong case after a preposition (*in der Stadt* for movement), word order (*In Zukunft ich werde …*, *weil es ist ruhig*), a missing umlaut, a small spelling slip, *eine Lehrer*.
- **Major (meaning breaks down):** a wrong verb form (*wir spielst*), a wrong tense for the time (*Gestern werde ich …*), an infinitive instead of a conjugated verb (*Mia spielen*), a missing verb, an English word.

**Question 3: translation into German (10).**

| Meaning (6) | Accuracy (4) |
|---|---|
| 5–6: consistently transferred, any unclear bit is rare | 4: consistently accurate, any errors minor |
| 3–4: most transferred, an occasional message unclear or incomplete | 3: generally accurate, major errors rare |
| 1–2: some parts transferred, the message sometimes breaks down | 2: sometimes accurate, some major errors; 1: limited |

Pearson's exemplars show that leaving out one small word (*manchmal*) can still score 5 for meaning, while an unfinished last sentence or an English word left in drags both grids down. **Never leave a gap**: a half-translated sentence earns meaning marks; a blank earns none.

**What separates grade 9.** All four bullets developed; past, present and future each used several times, plus a conditional; a range of complex structures spread through the answer, not crammed into one sentence; and accuracy where it is easiest to lose it: verb second, verb last after *weil/dass/wenn*, *sein* with movement verbs, the case after every preposition, and adjective endings.

::: terms
- **Bullet point**: one of the four compulsory content points in Questions 1 and 2; each must be addressed.
- **Response to stimulus**: the AO2 grid for coverage of the bullets, development and how easy the answer is to understand.
- **Linguistic knowledge and accuracy**: the AO3 grid for variety, complex language, time frames and accuracy.
- **Development**: an extra clause or sentence that adds a reason, example, contrast or consequence to a bullet.
- **Complex language**: subordinate, relative and infinitive clauses, several verbs in one sentence and German word order.
- **Time frame**: past, present or future, shown by any tense; a present tense with a future time word counts as future.
- **Minor error**: an error that leaves the meaning clear, such as a wrong ending, gender, case or word order.
- **Major error**: an error that stops the meaning, such as a wrong verb form or tense, an infinitive for a verb, or an English word.
- **Pros and cons bullet**: the Question 2 bullet asking for advantages and disadvantages; give at least one of each with a reason.
- **Word guidance**: 80–90 and 130–150 words are guides; writing more or fewer is not penalised in itself.
:::

::: method
1. **Choose** (a) or (b) in one minute: pick the option where you can do the past and future bullets well.
2. **Plan** two or three lines per bullet in note form: the tense, a reason and one complex structure for each.
3. **Write each bullet in order** in its own paragraph, so the examiner can see all four; develop each with *weil*, *aber*, *zum Beispiel* or a consequence.
4. **Spread the time frames:** at least two past and two future verbs in Q1, more in Q2, plus one conditional.
5. **Q2 pros and cons:** *Ein Vorteil ist, dass …; Ein Nachteil ist aber, dass …*, each with a reason; end with your own view.
6. **Q3 translation:** translate sentence by sentence; check every English word is covered; put tense and word order right before polishing endings.
7. **Check (10 minutes):** verb position in every clause, *haben/sein*, cases after prepositions, adjective endings, noun capitals, umlauts.
:::

::: example Model answer, Question 1 (80–90 words)
**Task.** Write to someone you know about your school. You must include the following points: what your school is like; your favourite subject with reasons; a school trip you went on; what you will do after your exams. Write your answer in German. You should aim to write between 80 and 90 words. (18)

*Hallo Mila! Meine Schule ist ziemlich groß, aber die Lehrer sind meistens freundlich. Mein Lieblingsfach ist Deutsch, weil ich gern neue Wörter lerne und weil unsere Lehrerin so lustig ist. Letzten Monat sind wir mit der ganzen Klasse nach Wien gefahren. Wir haben ein Museum besucht, das ich wirklich interessant fand, und abends haben wir viel gelacht. Nach den Prüfungen werde ich wahrscheinlich in die Oberstufe gehen, um Deutsch und Mathe zu lernen. Was machst du nächstes Jahr? Bis bald! Ben* (81 words)

Response 13: every bullet is addressed and developed (*aber …, weil … und weil …, das …, um … zu …*). Accuracy 5: three time frames (*ist, lerne* / *sind … gefahren, haben … besucht, fand* / *werde … gehen*), a relative clause, *um … zu*, two *weil* clauses, accurate cases (*mit der ganzen Klasse, in die Oberstufe*).
:::

::: example Model answer, Question 2 (130–150 words)
**Task.** Write a blog about healthy living. You must include the following points: what you do to stay healthy; the pros and cons of team sports; something you did recently to relax; how you will live more healthily in the future. Write your answer in German. You should aim to write between 130 and 150 words. (22)

*Für mich ist Gesundheit sehr wichtig. Ich gehe dreimal pro Woche schwimmen, und ich versuche, jeden Tag Obst und Gemüse zu essen. Außerdem trinke ich viel Wasser statt Cola.*
*Mannschaftssport hat viele Vorteile. Man bleibt fit, und man lernt, mit anderen Leuten zusammenzuarbeiten. Ein Nachteil ist aber, dass es oft viel Druck gibt. Mein Bruder spielt in einem Fußballverein, und er ist immer traurig, wenn seine Mannschaft verliert.*
*Letztes Wochenende war ich sehr müde, weil ich viele Prüfungen hatte. Deshalb bin ich mit meiner Freundin im Park spazieren gegangen, und danach haben wir zusammen einen Film gesehen. Am Abend habe ich mich viel besser gefühlt.*
*In Zukunft werde ich früher ins Bett gehen, weil ich zu wenig schlafe. Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich auch einen Tanzkurs machen, damit ich mich mehr bewege.* (134 words)

Response 17: four paragraphs, one per bullet, each developed; the pros and cons each have a reason and an example. Accuracy 5: infinitive clauses (*versuche … zu essen*, *lernt … zusammenzuarbeiten*), *dass, wenn, weil, damit*, *war/hatte*, perfect with *sein* and *haben*, a reflexive perfect, *werden*, the conditional, *statt*, *deshalb* + inversion.
:::

::: example What holds a mid-band answer down
Same Question 1 task. *Meine Schule ist gut. Die Lehrer sind nett. Ich mag Mathe. Mathe ist interessant. Ich werde in die Oberstufe gehen. Ich mag mein Schule sehr.* Estimate the marks and say what would raise them. (18)

Response about 8 out of 13: three bullets (the school trip is missing) and little development. Accuracy about 3 out of 5: two time frames, short simple sentences, a minor error (*mein Schule*). To raise it: add the past bullet (*Letztes Jahr sind wir nach Köln gefahren*), a reason for every opinion (*weil der Lehrer alles gut erklärt*), and join sentences with *aber, weil, dass*.
:::

::: example Translation into German (Question 3)
Translate into German: *My sister is older than me. She works in a hospital in Leeds. We often go to the cinema together on Sundays. Last weekend we saw a film that was very funny. Next year she wants to travel to Austria because she has never been there.* (10)

*Meine Schwester ist älter als ich. Sie arbeitet in einem Krankenhaus in Leeds. Sonntags gehen wir oft zusammen ins Kino. Letztes Wochenende haben wir einen Film gesehen, der sehr lustig war. Nächstes Jahr will sie nach Österreich reisen, weil sie noch nie dort gewesen ist.*
Points to check: *als ich* (nominative after *als*); *in einem Krankenhaus* (dative); *sonntags* or *am Sonntag* + verb second; *ins Kino* (movement); perfect *haben … gesehen*; relative *der* for *Film* with *war* at the end; *will* (wants) from *wollen*; *nach* for countries; *weil* sends *ist* to the end. *Weil sie noch nie in Österreich war* is equally good.
:::

::: q
A Question 1 answer has 115 words. Is it penalised for length? (1)
---
No (1). The word count is guidance only; but every extra sentence is another chance of error, and time lost for Question 2.
:::

::: q
Classify each error as minor or major. (a) *Ich habe ein rot Fahrrad.* (b) *Morgen habe ich Fußball gespielt.* (2)
---
(a) minor: adjective ending, meaning clear (1). (b) major: past tense with a future time word (1).
:::

::: q
Rewrite starting with the words given, changing the word order: *Ich spiele am Wochenende Tennis.* → *Am Wochenende …* (1)
---
*Am Wochenende spiele ich Tennis.* (1) The verb stays second, so the subject moves after it.
:::

::: q
Translate into German: *There are many shops in my town.* (2)
---
*Es gibt viele Geschäfte in meiner Stadt.* / *In meiner Stadt gibt es viele Geschäfte.* Meaning (1); accuracy: *es gibt*, plural *Geschäfte*, dative *meiner* (1). *Läden* is also fine.
:::

::: q
In a typical Question 1, which bullet needs the past tense, and give one German sentence that would answer a bullet "the last time you went to a concert". (2)
---
The third bullet, about a past event (1). E.g. *Letzten Samstag bin ich mit meinem Cousin in ein Konzert in Manchester gegangen.* (1)
:::

::: q
Add development to this sentence in two different ways: *Ich lese gern.* (2)
---
1 for a reason (*…, weil ich dabei gut entspannen kann*); 1 for an example or contrast (*Im Moment lese ich einen spannenden Roman, aber ich habe selten Zeit dafür*). Any accurate, relevant extension scores.
:::

::: watch
- **A missing bullet**: three bullets cap the response mark at 10 in both questions.
- **Bullets with no development**: one flat sentence per bullet keeps Q2 in 8–10 at best.
- **One time frame dominating**: the accuracy grid wants three, used successfully and repeatedly.
- **Weil and dass**: the verb goes to the end. *Weil es ist ruhig* is an error every examiner looks for.
- **Haben or sein**: *ich bin gefahren, gegangen, geflogen*.
- **Writing far too much**: more words means more errors and less time for checking.
- **Gaps in the translation**: always write something; a blank scores nothing.
- **English left in**: one English word in a translation is a major error.
:::
