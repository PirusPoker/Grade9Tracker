## What it is

**Derivational morphology** means building new words from words you already know by adding a **prefix** (at the front) or a **suffix** (at the end): *Film → Lieblingsfilm* (favourite film), *glücklich → unglücklich* (unhappy), *lösen → Lösung* (solution), *krank → Krankheit* (illness). Edexcel lists exactly which prefixes and suffixes you must recognise. The rules work in both directions: if *Arbeit* is on the vocabulary list, Pearson may use *arbeitslos* in a reading text without glossing it, and if *Besucher* is on the list, it may use *besuchen*.

Where it matters:
- **Paper 3 (Reading):** this is mainly a reading skill. The spec says derived forms of listed words "may usually only appear in the Reading exam". A text can contain *Hauptstraße, unfreundlich, nachmittags, Leser* or *Krankheit* even though none of them is printed in the list, and you are expected to work them out.
- **Inference marks:** Paper 3 Section A has **two inference items** (1 mark each) on words that are **not on the list at all**, set as "Which of these is the best translation of the word ...?" with options A–C. Pearson's sample used *entwerfen* (design) in a text about buildings. Word parts plus context are how you get these marks.
- **Translation into English (Paper 3 Section B):** a derived word left untranslated, or translated too literally (*arbeitslos* as "workless"), costs accuracy in the holistic band.
- **Writing and speaking:** you may use any of these words yourself, and *Lieblings-* and *un-* are an easy way to add precision (*Mein Lieblingsfach ist Mathe*).

**Pearson's rule:** only **one** affix from this section is added to a base word in its papers. You will see *Häuschen* or *Lieblingshaus*, never *Lieblingshäuschen*. But normal grammar endings can still be added: *Beschreibung → Beschreibungen* (plural), *unglücklich → ein unglückliches Kind* (adjective ending).

**1. Prefixes (all tiers).**

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples (base word on the list) |
|---|---|---|
| **Lieblings-** + noun | favourite | *Lieblingsfilm, Lieblingsfach, Lieblingsessen, Lieblingsband, Lieblingsstadt* |
| **Haupt-** + noun | main | *Hauptstraße* (main road), *Hauptbahnhof* (main station), *Hauptsache* (main thing), *Hauptgrund* (main reason), *Hauptrolle* (main role, lead) |
| **un-** + adjective | the opposite | *unglücklich* (unhappy), *unruhig* (restless), *unfreundlich, unbequem* (uncomfortable), *unwichtig, ungesund* |

A word built with *un-* reverses the meaning, so check for it before you choose an answer: *Das Bett war **un**bequem* means the bed was uncomfortable. Some *un-* words are on the list in their own right (*unmöglich*, and Higher *unbekannt, unabhängig*).

**2. Suffixes (all tiers).**

**Ordinal numbers.** Add **-te** to numbers 1–19 and **-ste** from 20 upwards. The ordinal is an adjective, so it takes endings. Note the irregular four: **erste, dritte, siebte, achte**.

| Number | Ordinal | In use |
|---|---|---|
| eins, zwei, drei | erste, zweite, dritte | *der erste Tag, die zweite Straße links* |
| vier, sieben, acht | vierte, siebte, achte | *am siebten Juli* (on the seventh of July) |
| zehn, neunzehn | zehnte, neunzehnte | *im zehnten Jahr* |
| zwanzig, einundzwanzig | zwanzigste, einundzwanzigste | *mein zwanzigster Geburtstag* |
| dreißig, hundert | dreißigste, hundertste | *am dreißigsten Mai* |

Dates use *am* + ordinal + *-en*: *am ersten Januar, am dritten Oktober*.

**-ung: nouns from verbs.** Add **-ung** to the verb stem. The noun is **feminine**, plural **-en**, and its meaning is transparent.

| Verb | Noun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| wohnen | die Wohnung | flat (the place you live) |
| reservieren | die Reservierung | reservation, booking |
| beschreiben | die Beschreibung | description |
| verbessern | die Verbesserung | improvement |
| [H] lösen | die Lösung | solution |
| [H] einladen | die Einladung | invitation |
| [H] erklären | die Erklärung | explanation |
| [H] entscheiden | die Entscheidung | decision |

**-er: people from verbs.** Add **-er** to the stem of an *-en* verb for the **person who does it**. The noun is **masculine**; add *-in* for a woman (G1a): *Spieler → Spielerin*.

| Verb | Person | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| malen | der Maler | painter |
| spielen | der Spieler | player |
| besuchen | der Besucher | visitor |
| lesen | der Leser | reader |
| fahren | der Fahrer | driver |
| rauchen | der Raucher | smoker |
| gewinnen | der Gewinner | winner |
| schwimmen | der Schwimmer | swimmer |

**-s: adverbs from days and times.** Add **-s** to a day or time of day for "on ...s" or "in the ...s". The adverb has a **small letter**.

| Noun | Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Montag, Samstag | montags, samstags | on Mondays, on Saturdays |
| Morgen, Abend | morgens, abends | in the mornings, in the evenings |
| Nachmittag, Nacht | nachmittags, nachts | in the afternoons, at night |

*Montags habe ich Mathe* = I have maths on Mondays (every Monday). Compare *am Montag* = on Monday (one Monday). The *-s* tells you it is a **habit**, which can decide a reading question.

**3. [H] Higher suffixes.**

**-chen and -lein: "little".** The noun becomes **neuter**, the vowel usually takes an **umlaut**, and a final *-e* drops: *das Haus → das Häuschen* (little house), *die Katze → das Kätzchen* (kitten), *der Hund → das Hündchen* (little dog), *die Stadt → das Städtchen* (small town), *das Buch → das Büchlein* (little book). Pearson uses these only where English would say "little". Some listed words end in *-chen* without meaning "little" and are learnt as words: *das Mädchen* (girl).

**-heit and -keit: "-ness" or "-ty".** Added to adjectives to make **feminine** nouns. *-keit* follows adjectives ending in *-ig, -lich, -bar*; *-heit* is used for most others.

| Adjective | Noun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| krank | die Krankheit | illness |
| gesund | die Gesundheit | health |
| schön | die Schönheit | beauty |
| frei | die Freiheit | freedom (listed) |
| sicher | die Sicherheit | safety, security (listed, Higher) |
| möglich | die Möglichkeit | possibility (listed) |
| schwierig | die Schwierigkeit | difficulty (listed) |
| notwendig | die Notwendigkeit | necessity |
| ehrlich | die Ehrlichkeit | honesty |
| sauber | die Sauberkeit | cleanliness |
| traurig | die Traurigkeit | sadness |

**-los: "-less" or "without".** Added to nouns to make **adjectives**: *die Arbeit → arbeitslos* (unemployed, without work), *die Hilfe → hilflos* (helpless), *das Ende → endlos* (endless), *der Sinn → sinnlos* (pointless, senseless), *der Fehler → fehlerlos* (faultless, without mistakes). *Kostenlos* (free of charge) is on the list itself. **Arbeitslos does not mean hard-working**: *-los* always means "without".

**4. Working out an unlisted word (inference).** For the two inference marks, the word is not built from a list rule, so combine three clues:
- **Word parts you know:** compounds join two listed words (*Tier + Arzt → Tierarzt*, vet; *Feuer + Wehr → Feuerwehr*, fire brigade).
- **Context:** what does the sentence need? *Wir haben im Wald Pilze gesammelt und zu Hause eine Suppe gekocht* points to mushrooms, not stones or birds.
- **Grammar:** a capital letter means a noun; *-en* at the end of the clause after *zu* or a modal means a verb; an ending before a noun means an adjective.
Then check your choice back in the sentence. If two options fit the word parts, context decides.

**What makes grade 9.** Never skipping an unfamiliar word in reading: strip the prefix or suffix, find the base, apply the meaning, and keep negatives such as *un-* and *-los* in your translation. In translation into English, choose the natural English word (*Hauptbahnhof* = main station, *Lieblingsfach* = favourite subject, *nachmittags* = in the afternoons).

::: terms
- **Derivational morphology**: making new words from known ones with a prefix or suffix: Film → Lieblingsfilm.
- **Lieblings-**: prefix meaning favourite, added to nouns: Lieblingsessen, favourite food.
- **Haupt-**: prefix meaning main, added to nouns: Hauptstraße, main road.
- **un-**: prefix that makes the opposite adjective: unglücklich, unhappy.
- **-te / -ste**: ordinal endings, -te for 1–19 and -ste from 20: zweite, zwanzigste; irregular erste, dritte, siebte, achte.
- **-ung**: makes a feminine noun from a verb stem: beschreiben → die Beschreibung.
- **-er (agent)**: makes a masculine noun for the person who does the action: malen → der Maler.
- **-s adverb**: a day or time + s for a habit: montags, abends.
- **-chen / -lein**: neuter nouns meaning little, often with an umlaut: Häuschen, Kätzchen (Higher).
- **-heit / -keit**: feminine nouns for -ness or -ty: Krankheit, Möglichkeit (Higher).
- **-los**: adjective meaning without or -less: arbeitslos, unemployed (Higher).
- **Inference item**: a Reading question on an unlisted word, worked out from word parts and context.
:::

::: method
1. **Underline the unfamiliar word** and decide its word class: capital letter = noun; verb position = verb; ending before a noun = adjective.
2. **Look for a listed affix:** *Lieblings-, Haupt-, un-*; *-te / -ste, -ung, -er, -s*; Higher *-chen / -lein, -heit / -keit, -los*.
3. **Strip it and find the base** (undo any umlaut: *Häuschen → Haus*, *Kätzchen → Katze*).
4. **Apply the meaning** of the affix: favourite, main, opposite, ordinal, the action, the person, a habit, little, -ness, without.
5. **No listed affix?** It may be an inference word: split any compound into listed words and use the context.
6. **Test your meaning in the sentence** and choose the natural English word.
7. **Keep negatives:** *un-* and *-los* change the answer; check A–C options for the trap that ignores them.
:::

::: example Prefixes
What does each word mean? (a) *Lieblingsstadt* (b) *Hauptgrund* (c) *ungesund* (d) *unbequem* (4)

(a) favourite town / city (1). (b) main reason (1). (c) unhealthy (1). (d) uncomfortable (1).

Each is a listed base (*Stadt, Grund, gesund, bequem*) plus one prefix. *Ungesund* is "the opposite of healthy", which in natural English is "unhealthy".
:::

::: example -ung, -er and -s
Translate into English: *Die Spieler sind montags im Stadion. Nach dem Spiel gibt es eine Beschreibung des Spiels für die Leser der Zeitung.* (4)

The players are in the stadium on Mondays (1 + 1). After the match there is a description of the match (1) for the newspaper's readers / the readers of the newspaper (1).

*Spieler, Leser*: people from *spielen, lesen*. *Montags*: a habit. *Beschreibung* from *beschreiben*. *Des Spiels* is genitive (Higher, recognition only).
:::

::: example Ordinals and dates
Write in German: (a) on the first of May (b) the third road on the right (c) my twenty-first birthday (3)

(a) *am ersten Mai* (1). (b) *die dritte Straße rechts* (1). (c) *mein einundzwanzigster Geburtstag* (1).

*Erste* and *dritte* are irregular. From 20 the ending is *-ste*. The ordinal takes adjective endings: *am ersten* (dative), *mein ...ster Geburtstag* (masculine nominative after *mein*).
:::

::: example Higher suffixes [H]
Translate into English: *Mein Opa war zwei Jahre arbeitslos. Seine Krankheit war schlimm, aber er hat seine Ehrlichkeit nie verloren. Jetzt wohnt er in einem Häuschen am See.* (5)

My grandad was unemployed for two years (1). His illness was bad (1), but he never lost his honesty (1 + 1). Now he lives in a little house / cottage by the lake (1).

*Arbeitslos*: *Arbeit* + *-los*. *Krankheit*: *krank* + *-heit*. *Ehrlichkeit*: *ehrlich* + *-keit*. *Häuschen*: *Haus* + *-chen*, with an umlaut.
:::

::: example An inference item
Read: *Unsere Schule hat einen neuen Schulgarten. Die Schüler haben im Frühling Gemüse gepflanzt, und im Sommer konnten sie es ernten.* Which of these is the best translation of the word **gepflanzt**? **A** sold **B** planted **C** washed (1)

**B** (1).

*Gepflanzt* is a participle (*ge-...-t*), so it is a verb. The context is a school garden in spring, before a summer harvest. A and C do not fit the sequence: you cannot sell or wash vegetables before they grow. It also looks like English "planted". (*Ernten* is another unlisted word: "harvest".)
:::

::: watch
- **-los means without**: *arbeitslos* = unemployed, not hard-working; *kostenlos* = free of charge.
- **un- reverses the meaning**: read every adjective to the end of its prefix; *unbequem* is uncomfortable.
- **-s adverbs are habits**: *montags* = every Monday, not "on Monday" once.
- **Ordinals take endings**: *am dritten Mai*; irregular *erste, dritte, siebte, achte*.
- **-chen nouns are neuter**: *das Kätzchen, das Häuschen*, even if the base is masculine or feminine.
- **Undo the umlaut to find the base**: *Häuschen → Haus*, *Städtchen → Stadt*.
- **Natural English**: *Hauptbahnhof* is "main station", not "head train yard"; *Lieblingsfach* is "favourite subject".
- **Inference: context beats looks**: check your guess in the whole sentence before choosing A, B or C.
:::

Translate into English.
