## What it is

**Paper 1 (Speaking, 1GN1/1H)** is worth **50 marks, 25%** of the GCSE. Your teacher conducts it and records it in a five-week window in April and May, and Pearson marks the recording. It lasts **10–12 minutes** after **15 minutes' preparation**. The three tasks always come in the same order.

| Task | What you do | Marks | Recommended time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Read aloud | read a German text of 50–55 words (4 sentences), then answer 2 unprepared questions | 8 + 2 + 2 = 12 | 2–2.5 min |
| 2 Role play | five prompts in English on a transactional card; ask 2 questions, answer 1 about the future | 5 × 2 = 10 | 1–1.5 min |
| 3 Picture task | describe one of two photos (people, location, activity), answer 2 questions (the second in the past), then a conversation | 8 + 4 + 16 = 28 | 2–2.5 min + 5–5.5 min |

**Two weeks before**, Pearson offers you two of the six thematic contexts and you choose one. That choice decides your picture card and conversation. The read-aloud and role-play cards are then allocated from the other contexts, so prepare every context for those.

**The preparation (15 minutes).** The first 14 minutes are in a supervised room, where you must not read aloud. The last minute is in the exam room, recorded, and you may practise the read-aloud text out loud.
- You may make notes on **one A4 sheet** and glance at them during the test. Notes are for reference, not for reading out.
- You may **write on the read-aloud card** (mark sounds, stress, pauses). You may **not** write on the role-play or picture cards.
- You hand each card back at the end of its task. The read-aloud card goes back **before** the two questions, so those questions are genuinely unprepared.
- A good split of the 14 minutes: 3 on the read aloud, 3 on the role play, 7 on the picture, 1 checking (details in the method).

**Task 1: Read aloud (12).** The text uses only list vocabulary and is marked holistically for **pronunciation through the sound-symbol correspondences (SSCs)**. An English accent is not penalised; a sound that turns the word into a different word, or into nothing, is.

| Marks | What the examiner hears |
|---|---|
| 7–8 | consistently clear; any slips in SSCs don't affect the message |
| 5–6 | clear; slips have minimal effect |
| 3–4 | generally clear; slips have a limited effect |
| 1–2 | sometimes clear; slips occasionally make the message hard to follow |

Pearson's own examples of costly slips are *viele* said like *weil*, *bleiben* said as "bleeben" (it sounds like the past *blieben*), *zu* said like "zoo", and cognates such as *Sport* or *Club* said the English way. Then the teacher asks **two present-tense questions** about likes, dislikes or opinions linked to the text. Each scores **2** if fully communicated, **1** if partly (a one-word answer, or no verb), **0** if irrelevant. One sentence with a verb, an opinion and a reason is enough.

**The traps to mark on the card.**

| Spelling | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ei / ie | "eye" / "ee" | *Zeit, leider* / *viel, lieben* |
| w / v / z / j | v / f / ts / y | *wohnen; Vater; Zug; jung* |
| sp-, st- at the start of a word or word part | "shp", "sht" | *spielen, Stadt, verstehen*; but *liebsten, Fenster* keep "st" |
| final -b, -d, -g | p, t, k | *halb, Land, Tag* |
| -ig | like *-ich* | *wichtig, ruhig* |
| ch | hard after a, o, u, au; soft elsewhere | *Buch, machen* / *ich, Mädchen* |
| ä, ö, ü | "e", rounded "er", rounded "ee" | *spät, schön, Tür* |
| eu / äu | "oy" | *heute, Häuser* |
| -tion | "tsee-OHN", stressed | *Information* |
| unstressed -er, -e | "uh" | *Zimmer, Schule* |

**Task 2: Role play (10).** One of ten settings: café or restaurant; shop, market or shopping centre; hotel; train station; tourist information office; cinema, theatre or concert hall; campsite; leisure centre; doctor's surgery or hospital; in town. The card gives a scenario and five prompts in English; the teacher speaks first and asks fixed questions. At Higher, **two prompts say "Ask a question about …"** and **one prompt needs a future time frame** (the teacher asks it with *werden*). Each prompt scores **2** (fully communicated), **1** (ambiguous) or **0**. Only communication counts: a word-order slip still gets 2. The setting is formal, but you do not have to use *Sie*. A question prompt answered with a statement scores 0.

Question frames to have ready: *Wie viel kostet …? Um wie viel Uhr …? Gibt es hier …? Kann ich …? Darf ich …? Wo ist …? Wann …? Haben Sie …?*

**Task 3: Picture task (28).** You choose one of two photos and tell the teacher which one. The teacher then asks you to describe it and may prompt with *(Und) die Personen? (Und) wo ist das? (Und) was machen sie? Noch etwas?*
- **Description (8):** response to stimulus 4 + linguistic knowledge and accuracy 4. Top marks need all three bullets (people, location, activity), consistent detailed development and easy comprehension, plus a wide range of language that is consistently accurate. Only two bullets caps you at 2 out of 4 for response.
- **Two compulsory questions (2 + 2):** about your own experiences linked to the picture. At Higher **the first is present and the second past**. Marked for communication only.
- **Conversation (16), 5–5.5 minutes:** starts from a compulsory subject on the teacher's card and moves around the same context. Response to spoken language 12 (10–12 consistently relevant, consistently extended, easily understood; 7–9 frequently; 4–6 some; 1–3 occasional) + accuracy 4 (4 wide range, frequent complex language, **consistently successful three time frames**, any errors minor; 3 some complex language, two time frames frequently successful; 2 occasional complex language, two time frames generally successful; 1 straightforward language).

**Picture language.** *Auf dem Bild sehe ich …* / *Im Vordergrund … im Hintergrund …* / *links, rechts, in der Mitte* / *Sie tragen …* / *Das Wetter scheint schön zu sein* / *Sie spielen gerade Fußball* [*gerade*, Higher: "right now"] / *Ich denke, dass …* / *Wahrscheinlich …* [H].

**Minor or major?** Pearson's speaking and writing grids use the same idea. A **minor** error leaves the meaning clear: a wrong adjective ending or gender, *im meiner Gegend*, *weil es ist ruhig*, *Ich gehe auf Fuß*, *Wir gehen in der Stadt*. A **major** error stops the meaning: *Wir gehst*, *Wir gegehen*, *Morgen hat er Musik gehört* (wrong tense), *Yusuf spielen Fußball* (infinitive), an English word.

**What separates grade 9.** A clean read aloud where every *ei/ie*, *w/v/z* and final consonant is right; role-play answers that are full sentences with a verb; a description that covers all three bullets with opinions and guesses (*Ich glaube, dass sie Geschwister sind, weil …*); and a conversation in which **every** answer is developed (opinion, reason, example, another time frame) with complex language: *weil* and *dass* clauses, relative clauses, *um … zu*, *wenn ich … hätte, würde ich …*, the simple past (*war, hatte, gab, ging*), comparisons and superlatives.

::: terms
- **SSC (sound-symbol correspondence)**: the link between a spelling and its sound, e.g. ei sounds like English eye; the read aloud is marked on these.
- **Read-aloud follow-up questions**: two unprepared present-tense questions on likes or opinions linked to the text, 2 marks each.
- **Fully communicated**: the answer does exactly what the prompt asks with a clear verb; it scores 2 in the role play and short questions.
- **Partially communicated**: the message can be guessed but is ambiguous, for example a single word with no verb; it scores 1.
- **Role-play question prompt**: a prompt reading "Ask a question about …"; there are two at Higher and a statement scores 0.
- **Future prompt**: the role-play prompt the teacher asks with werden; answer with werden + infinitive or a present tense plus a future time word.
- **Compulsory questions**: the two questions after the picture description, present then past at Higher, about your own experience.
- **Development**: extra detail, reasons, justification or examples added to an answer; the conversation grid rewards it throughout.
- **Complex language**: subordinate, relative and infinitive clauses, several conjugated verbs in one sentence, word order unlike English.
- **Time frame**: past, present or future, expressed by any tense; the top accuracy band wants all three used successfully.
- **Minor error**: inaccurate but the meaning is still clear, e.g. a wrong adjective ending, gender or word order.
- **Major error**: inaccurate and the meaning breaks down, e.g. wrong verb form, wrong tense or an infinitive for a conjugated verb.
:::

::: method
1. **Read aloud card (3 minutes):** underline every *ei/ie*, mark *w, v, z, j*, initial *sp-/st-*, final *-b/-d/-g*, *-ig*, the umlauts and any cognate; mark the stressed syllable of long words.
2. **Role play (3 minutes):** write one short German sentence with a verb for each prompt; turn both "Ask a question" prompts into real questions; put *werden* in the future one.
3. **Picture (7 minutes):** choose the photo you can say more about; note two details each for people, location and activity, one opinion and one guess; predict a present and a past question and note an answer with a reason.
4. **In the room:** use the minute aloud on the hardest words of the read aloud, then read at a steady pace; a self-correction is fine.
5. **Follow-up and compulsory questions:** answer in a full sentence, using the question's own verb, plus a reason (*weil …*) or a detail.
6. **Conversation:** for every question give an answer, a reason, an example and a switch of time frame; ask for a repeat with *Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?* rather than guessing.
7. **Throughout:** keep verbs second, send them to the end after *weil, dass, wenn*, and use *haben/sein* + participle in the past.
:::

::: example Read aloud (Travel and tourism) with the traps marked
*Jedes Jahr verbringen wir zwei Wochen in einem kleinen Dorf in der Schweiz. Die Landschaft ist wirklich schön, und die Luft ist frischer als in der Stadt. Am liebsten wandere ich mit meinem Vater im Wald, aber meine Schwester geht lieber schwimmen. Leider ist die Reise ziemlich teuer, deshalb sparen wir das ganze Jahr.* (54 words) Mark the traps in the first two sentences. (8)

- *Jedes Jahr*: j = "y", long "ah" (1).
- *verbringen, zwei, Wochen, Schweiz*: v = "f"; z = "ts"; w = "v"; ei = "eye"; hard ch after o (1).
- *Dorf, Land(schaft), Stadt, Wald*: final d and dt = "t"; *Stadt* starts "sht" (1).
- *schön*: rounded ö, never *schon* (1).
- *liebsten*: ie = "ee", b before s = "p", and **st** inside the word stays "st" (1).
- *wandere, Vater, Schwester*: w = "v", v = "f", unstressed -er = "uh" (1).
- *Leider, Reise, ziemlich, teuer*: ei = "eye", s between vowels = "z", ie = "ee" with soft ch, eu = "oy" (1).
- *deshalb, sparen*: final b = "p"; initial sp = "shp" (1).

Follow-up questions might be *Was machst du gern im Urlaub?* and *Was ist deine Meinung zum Wandern?* Full answers: *Im Urlaub schwimme ich gern im Meer, weil ich das Wasser liebe.* / *Ich finde Wandern toll, weil man die Natur genießen kann.*
:::

::: example Role play: at a campsite
**Card.** You have arrived at a campsite in Austria. The teacher plays the receptionist and speaks first. Prompts: say how many nights you want to stay; say who you are with; ask a question about the swimming pool; say what you will do tomorrow; ask a question about the price. (10)

| Teacher | Model answer |
|---|---|
| *Guten Abend. Wie kann ich Ihnen helfen?* | *Wir möchten drei Nächte hier bleiben.* (2) |
| *Mit wem sind Sie hier?* | *Ich bin mit meinen Eltern und meinem Bruder hier.* (2) |
| *Haben Sie eine Frage?* | *Gibt es hier ein Schwimmbad?* (2) |
| *Was werden Sie morgen machen?* | *Morgen werden wir an den See fahren.* (2) |
| *Haben Sie noch eine Frage?* | *Wie viel kostet es pro Nacht?* (2) |

*Drei Nächte* alone would still communicate (2). *Schwimmbad?* with question intonation is ambiguous (1). *Ich mag schwimmen* at prompt 3 is not a question (0). *Morgen* alone at prompt 4 does not say what you will do (0).
:::

::: example Picture description (Lifestyle and wellbeing)
**Photo (imagine it):** four teenagers playing football in a city park on a sunny day; trees and a small café behind them; on the left a girl is drinking water. Describe the picture. (8)

*Auf dem Bild sehe ich vier junge Leute, zwei Mädchen und zwei Jungen. Sie sind ungefähr sechzehn Jahre alt und tragen bequeme Kleidung. Ein Mädchen hat lange, dunkle Haare und trägt ein rotes T-Shirt.* (people)
*Sie sind in einem Park in der Stadt. Im Hintergrund gibt es viele Bäume und ein kleines Café, und der Himmel ist blau. Es ist wahrscheinlich Sommer, weil die Sonne scheint.* (location)
*Die Jugendlichen spielen gerade Fußball. Ein Junge wirft den Ball, und die anderen laufen schnell. Links trinkt ein Mädchen Wasser, weil es so heiß ist.* (activity)
*Sie scheinen sehr glücklich zu sein. Ich denke, dass sie Freunde sind und dass sie jedes Wochenende zusammen Sport treiben. Ich würde gern mit ihnen spielen, weil Fußball mein Lieblingssport ist.*

Response 4: all three bullets, each developed with detail, then an opinion and guesses. Accuracy 4: adjective endings (*bequeme Kleidung, lange, dunkle Haare, ein rotes T-Shirt, ein kleines Café*), *gerade*, *scheinen … zu*, *weil* and *dass* clauses, a conditional.
:::

::: example Compulsory questions and the start of the conversation
After the description above the teacher asks: (a) *Treibst du gern Sport mit Freunden?* (b) *Was hast du letztes Wochenende gemacht, um fit zu bleiben?* Then the conversation starts on food and drink. (8)

(a) *Ja, ich spiele zweimal pro Woche Tennis mit meiner besten Freundin, weil das Spaß macht.* (2: present, fully communicated.)
(b) *Letzten Samstag bin ich mit meinem Bruder im Wald gelaufen.* (2: past tense, as Higher requires.)
**Teacher:** *Was isst du normalerweise zum Frühstück?* **Student:** *Unter der Woche esse ich meistens nur Brot mit Käse, weil ich keine Zeit habe. Am Wochenende essen wir aber zusammen, und mein Vater kocht Eier. Das finde ich super, weil wir dann reden können.* (present + reasons + contrast)
**Teacher:** *Was hast du gestern gegessen?* **Student:** *Gestern habe ich in der Schule eine Suppe gegessen, aber sie war leider kalt. Am Abend hat meine Mutter Gemüse gekocht, und das hat mir gut geschmeckt.* (past: perfect + *war*)
**Teacher:** *Wie wirst du in Zukunft gesünder leben?* **Student:** *Ich werde mehr Wasser trinken und weniger Zucker essen. Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich jeden Morgen joggen gehen, damit ich fit bleibe.* (future + conditional + *damit*)
Every answer is relevant, extended and uses complex language; across the conversation all three time frames are used successfully.
:::

At Higher, in which tense is the second compulsory question after the picture description, and what must your answer contain? (2)
---
The past tense (1); your answer must also be in the past, e.g. *haben/sein* + participle or *war/hatte*, and say something relevant about your own experience (1).

::: q
Describe how each word should sound: (a) *Zeit* (b) *wieder* (c) *Stück*. (3)
---
(a) "tsite": z = ts, ei = "eye" (1). (b) "VEE-duh": w = v, ie = "ee", final -er = "uh" (1). (c) "shtück": st = sht at the start, then a short rounded ü (lips as for "oo", tongue as for "ee") (1).
:::

::: q
This conversation answer uses one time frame. Add a past and a future sentence: *Ich spiele gern Computerspiele, weil sie spannend sind.* (3)
---
1 for a correct past sentence (*Gestern habe ich zwei Stunden lang gespielt.*), 1 for a correct future sentence (*Nächstes Jahr werde ich weniger spielen, weil ich Prüfungen habe.*), 1 for linking them naturally to the original answer.
:::

::: watch
- **Writing on the wrong card**: only the read-aloud card and your A4 sheet may be annotated.
- **ei / ie swaps**: *bleiben* said "bleeben", *viel* said like *weil*. These change words and cost the most read-aloud marks.
- **English cognates**: *Sport, Hotel, Club, Information* said the English way are treated as lapses.
- **Statements for question prompts**: two role-play prompts need real questions; a statement scores 0.
- **Ignoring the future prompt**: *Morgen* alone or a present tense with no time word does not answer *Was werden Sie …?*
- **Two picture bullets out of three**: people, location and activity must all appear.
- **Present-tense answer to the past question**: the second compulsory question needs a past tense.
- **Short conversation answers**: a sentence per question keeps you in the 4–6 band however accurate it is.
:::
