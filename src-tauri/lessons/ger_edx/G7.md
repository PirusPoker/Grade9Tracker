## What it is

**Sound-symbol correspondences (SSCs)** are the links between how German is **spelt** and how it is **said**. German spelling is far more regular than English: once you know that *ei* always sounds like English "eye" and *ie* like "ee", you can say any word you read and spell almost any word you hear. Edexcel lists the SSCs that differ from English, and they are tested twice:
- **Paper 1, Task 1 (Read aloud):** you read a 50–55-word German text aloud. **8 marks** are for pronunciation, judged on the SSCs. An English accent is not penalised, but a wrong SSC that turns a word into a different word is. Pearson's own examples: *bleiben* said as "bleeben", *viele* said like *weil*, *zu* said as English "zoo", and *Sport* said the English way.
- **Paper 2, Section B (Dictation, 10 marks):** six short sentences, each heard **three times**. In sentences 1 and 2 you write **three missing words** each; sentences 3 to 6 you write **in full** (five to seven words each). That makes **30 rewardable words**, turned into marks on a sliding scale: 28–30 words = 10, 25–27 = 9, 22–24 = 8, 19–21 = 7, 16–18 = 6, 13–15 = 5, 10–12 = 4, 7–9 = 3, 4–6 = 2, 1–3 = 1.

**What scores in the dictation.** A word scores if it is spelt correctly, or spelt differently but **with the same sound** and still recognisable (*sint* for *sind*, a doubled or single consonant). It does **not** score if an SSC is wrong (*schon* for *schön*, *Kase* for *Käse*) or the word is unrecognisable on its own. Missing **capital letters** are not penalised. A few words may come from outside the vocabulary list, precisely to test SSCs, so you must be able to spell a word you have never seen.

This app has no German audio, so the drills below describe sounds in plain English. "Sounds like 'SHPEE-len'" means: say it as an English speaker would read those letters, with the capitals stressed. Say every example aloud as you read.

**1. Vowels: long or short?** Every German vowel has a long and a short version. The **spelling tells you which**:
- **Long:** before a single consonant (*Tag, lesen, Buch*), before **h** (*Jahr, nehmen, Uhr*), when doubled (*Haar, leer, Boot*), and *ie*.
- **Short:** before a **double consonant** or **two or more consonants** (*zusammen, bitte, dort, Grund, Stück*).
- **ß or ss?** After a long vowel or *ei, au, eu* write **ß** (*Fuß, Straße, groß, heißen*); after a short vowel write **ss** (*dass, essen, Fluss, wissen*).

| Spelling | Long sound | Example | Short sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a / ah / aa | "ah" as in *father* | Tag, Jahr, Haar | a short, open "a" | zusammen |
| e / eh / ee | "ay" as in *day*, without sliding | lesen, nehmen, leer | "e" as in *bed* | lernen |
| i / ih / ie | "ee" as in *see* | wir, ihr, lieben | "i" as in *bit* | bitte |
| o / oh | "oh", pure, lips rounded | Person, ohne | "o" as in *hot* | dort |
| u / uh | "oo" as in *moon* | Buch, Uhr | "u" as in *put* (not *cup*) | Grund |

**2. Umlauts.** The umlaut changes the sound and often the meaning, so it is the commonest lost dictation mark.

| Spelling | How to make it | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| long ä / äh | like "air" without the r, or a long "ay" | spät, wählen |
| short ä | the same as short *e* in *bed* | Geschäft |
| long ö / öh | say "ay" with your lips rounded as for "oh" | Größe, höher, schön |
| short ö | say short "e" (*bed*) with rounded lips | zwölf, können |
| long ü / y / üh | say "ee" with your lips rounded as for "oo" | Tür, System, früh |
| short ü / y | say short "i" (*bit*) with rounded lips | Stück, Typ, fünf |

**Minimal pairs** (one sound, two words) that cost marks: *schon* (already) / *schön* (beautiful); *Bruder / Brüder*; *Mutter / Mütter*; *Stadt / Städte*; *war* (was) / *wäre* (would be); *konnte / könnte*; *wurde / würde*; *mochte / möchte*. In dictation, grammar helps: *zwei Brüder* must be plural; *Ich möchte gern ...* is "would like".

**3. Vowel pairs.**

| Spelling | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ei / ai | "eye" | Polizei, Mai, mein, schreiben |
| ie | "ee" | lieben, viel, Brief |
| au | "ow" as in *cow* | auch, Haus |
| eu / äu | "oy" as in *boy* | Euro, heute, Geräusch, Häuser |

**Ei or ie?** Say the English name of the **second** letter: *ei* ends in *i*, so "eye"; *ie* ends in *e*, so "ee". This matters in the past tense: *bleiben* (stay) but *blieb* (stayed); *schreiben* but *schrieb*. Saying *bleiben* as "bleeben" is exactly Pearson's example of a wrong SSC. *Äu* is used when the word is related to a word with *au* (*Haus → Häuser*); otherwise write *eu*.

**4. Consonants.**

| Spelling | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| w | English "v" | schwer, Wasser, wohnen |
| v | English "f" (in most German words) | Vater, viel, vier |
| z | "ts" | Zeit, zu, zwölf |
| j | English "y" | jemand, ja, jung |
| qu | "kv" | bequem, Qualität |
| th | plain "t" (never English "th") | Thema, Theater |
| sch | "sh" | schnell, Schule |
| sp- / st- at the start of a word or stem | "shp" / "sht" | spielen, stehen, verstehen |
| s- / -s- before a vowel | English "z" | singen, leise, Sonne |
| ß / ss / -s at the end or before a consonant | a hissing "s" | Fuß, dass, besonders |
| -tion | "tsee-OHN" | Situation, Information |

Note *zu* = "tsoo", never English "zoo", and *viele* = "FEE-leh", which is why Pearson says it must not sound like *weil* ("vile"). In a few borrowed words *v* sounds like English "v" (*Video, Universität*). In the middle or at the end of a word, *st* and *sp* are said as in English: *Fenster, Westen*.

**5. The two ch sounds.**
- **Hard ch** after *a, o, u, au*: a rough sound at the back of the throat, like Scottish *loch*: *machen, Buch, doch, auch*.
- **Soft ch** after *e, i, ä, ö, ü, ei, eu*, after consonants and in *-chen*: a soft hiss at the front of the mouth, like a strong "h" in *huge*: *Mädchen, ich, nicht, Milch, möchte*.
- **-ig** at the end of a word is said with the **soft ch**: *wenig* = "VAY-nich", *ruhig, zwanzig, lustig*. Spell it *-ig*, never *-ich*.

**6. Endings: hard b, d, g.** At the end of a word or syllable, *b, d, g* lose their voice and sound like **p, t, k**: *halb* = "halp", *Land* = "lant", *Tag* = "tahk", *Hund* = "hoont". When you hear "t" at the end of a word in dictation, think: could it be *d*? (*Kind, Freund, Geld, sind*.) The plural shows the spelling: *Tage, Länder, Kinder*.

**7. The letter r.**
- **Consonantal r** (at the start of a word or syllable, after a consonant): a gargled sound at the back of the throat: *Problem, rot, Straße*.
- **Vocalic r** (after a long vowel at the end of a word or syllable): it almost disappears into an "ah": *klar* = "klah", *wir* = "veeah", *mehr, Uhr, Tür*.
- **er** in a stressed syllable sounds like English "air": *erst, gern, Berg*.
- **Unstressed -er** at the end of a word is a short "uh" with no r: *Zimmer* = "TSIM-muh", *Vater, aber, besser, Lehrer*. In dictation, a final "uh" is almost always **-er** or **-e**: *Schwester* ("SHVES-tuh") vs *Straße* ("SHTRAH-seh").

**8. Stress.** Most German words are stressed on the **first syllable**: *SPIElen, BAHNhof, LEHrer*. The exceptions to learn: inseparable prefixes are never stressed (*beSUCHen, verGESSen, erZÄHlen*); separable prefixes are (*AUFstehen, EINkaufen*); *-ieren* verbs stress the *ie* (*stuDIEren*); many borrowed words stress the end (*PoliZEI, MuSIK, HoTEL, SituaTION, UniversiTÄT*).

**9. Capital letters.** All nouns have capitals (*das Haus, die Freiheit, das Schwimmen*). Dictation does not penalise missing capitals, but writing and translation do (a slight spelling error), and the capital is a quick check that you have recognised a noun.

**What makes grade 9.** In the read aloud: no English "th", "w" or "zoo" sounds, *ei / ie* never swapped, *sp- / st-* as "shp / sht", and clear umlauts. In the dictation: every umlaut, *ß* vs *ss* from the vowel length, *-ig* not *-ich*, final *d / g* recognised, and unstressed *-er* spelt correctly. Using the three hearings well: first listen for the whole sentence, second write, third check umlauts and endings.

::: terms
- **SSC**: sound-symbol correspondence, the link between a German spelling and its sound: ie = "ee".
- **Long vowel**: a vowel before h, a single consonant or doubled: Jahr, lesen, Boot.
- **Short vowel**: a vowel before a double consonant or two consonants: bitte, dort, Grund.
- **Umlaut**: the two dots on ä, ö, ü, changing the sound and often the meaning: schon / schön.
- **ei / ie**: ei sounds like "eye", ie like "ee"; say the English name of the second letter.
- **eu / äu**: both sound like "oy" in boy: heute, Häuser.
- **Hard ch**: the throat sound after a, o, u, au: machen, Buch.
- **Soft ch**: the front hiss after e, i, umlauts and consonants, and in -ig: ich, Mädchen, wenig.
- **Final devoicing**: b, d, g at the end of a word sound like p, t, k: halb, Land, Tag.
- **Vocalic r**: an r after a long vowel that sounds like "ah": klar, wir.
- **Unstressed -er**: a final "uh" with no r: Zimmer, Vater.
- **Dictation scale**: 30 rewardable words converted to 10 marks; a word scores if its sound is spelt correctly.
:::

::: method
1. **First hearing:** listen to the whole sentence for meaning. Note the subject, verb and any time phrase.
2. **Second hearing:** write every word. Use grammar to guide you: a noun after *die* in the plural may need an umlaut (*die Brüder*); a verb after *ich* ends in *-e*.
3. **Vowel length:** long → single consonant, *h*, double vowel or *ie*; short → double consonant. Long + "s" → *ß*; short + "s" → *ss*.
4. **Check the vowel pairs:** "eye" = *ei*, "ee" = *ie*, "oy" = *eu* (or *äu* if related to *au*), "ow" = *au*.
5. **Check the consonants:** "v" sound = *w*; "f" sound may be *v*; "ts" = *z*; "y" = *j*; "sh" before p / t at the start = *s*.
6. **Endings:** "t" may be *d*; "k" may be *g*; "ich" at the end of an adjective = *-ig*; final "uh" = *-er* or *-e*.
7. **Third hearing:** check umlauts, *ß / ss*, and that every word is there.
:::

::: watch
- **ei / ie swapped**: *bleiben* is "BLYE-ben", *blieb* is "BLEEP"; swapping them makes a different word.
- **w is "v", v is "f"**: *Wasser* = "VAS-suh", *viel* = "feel".
- **z is "ts"**: *zu* is never "zoo"; *Zeit* is "tsyte".
- **Missing umlauts**: *schon/schön, Bruder/Brüder, mochte/möchte* are different words and lose the dictation mark.
- **sp- and st- at the start**: "shp" and "sht": *Sport* is "shport", never the English word.
- **-ig, not -ich**: *wenig, ruhig, lustig*.
- **ß after long vowels, ss after short**: *Straße* but *Fluss*.
- **Final "t" may be d**: *Freund, Land, Kind*.
:::
