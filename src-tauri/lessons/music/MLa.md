## What it is

**Staff notation** is written music: notes placed on a five-line **stave** to show pitch, with their shapes showing duration. The spec expects you to **read and write** treble- and bass-clef notation in **simple time**, and to **read** it in **compound time**. In the exam, printed music appears in almost every question: the set-work scores in Q1 and Q7, melodies for dictation, and printed lines for "give a bar number where ..." items. Many of these marks can be earned from the page alone, in the silence after the last playing.

### The stave and the clefs
The stave has **five lines and four spaces**, counted from the bottom. Higher on the stave means higher in pitch. Notes use the letter names **A B C D E F G**, then start again.

**Treble clef** (the G clef; its curl wraps round the G line) is used for higher instruments and voices: violin, flute, trumpet, soprano, and the right hand of the piano.
- **Lines** from the bottom: **E G B D F**.
- **Spaces** from the bottom: **F A C E**.

**Bass clef** (the F clef; its two dots sit either side of the F line) is used for lower instruments: cello, double bass, bassoon, trombone, tuba, bass guitar, and the left hand of the piano.
- **Lines** from the bottom: **G B D F A**.
- **Spaces** from the bottom: **A C E G**.

Read each clef from **its own landmarks**. The commonest mistake is reading bass clef as if it were treble, which puts every note a third out (the bottom line is G in bass clef, not E).

**Middle C** sits on one **ledger line** below the treble stave, and on one ledger line above the bass stave. **Ledger lines** are short extra lines that extend the stave upwards or downwards:

| Position | Treble clef | Bass clef |
|---|---|---|
| second ledger line above | C | E |
| space above first ledger line | B | D |
| first ledger line above | A | middle C |
| space just above the stave | G | B |
| space just below the stave | D | F |
| first ledger line below | middle C | E |
| space below first ledger line | B | D |
| second ledger line below | A | C |

A useful extra: the **viola** normally reads the **alto clef**, whose centre line is middle C. It appeared in the 2025 Badinerie question, although only treble and bass clefs are on the spec.

### Note and rest values
| Note | Looks like | Beats (crotchet = 1) | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **semibreve** | open oval, no stem | 4 | a small block hanging below the fourth line; also used for a **whole bar's rest** in any time signature |
| **minim** | open oval with a stem | 2 | a small block sitting on the middle line |
| **crotchet** | filled oval with a stem | 1 | a zig-zag squiggle |
| **quaver** | crotchet plus one tail (or one beam) | ½ | a "7"-like stroke with one flag |
| **semiquaver** | two tails (or two beams) | ¼ | two flags |
| **demisemiquaver** | three tails (or three beams) | ⅛ | three flags |

- **Dotted notes:** a dot adds **half the note's value**. A dotted minim is 3 beats, a dotted crotchet 1½ and a dotted quaver ¾. A dotted quaver followed by a semiquaver makes one crotchet beat.
- **Tie:** a curved line joining **two notes of the same pitch**. Play the first and hold it for both values; don't re-strike.
- **Slur:** a curved line over **different pitches**, meaning play them smoothly (legato), in one bow or breath.
- **Triplet:** three notes with a "3" over them, played in the time of two. A quaver triplet fills one crotchet beat.
- **Beams** join quavers and shorter notes **in beats**, so you can see where each beat starts: in 4/4 quavers are beamed in 2s or 4s; in 6/8 they are beamed in 3s.

### Accidentals and key signatures
- **Sharp (#)** raises a note by a semitone.
- **Flat (b)** lowers it by a semitone.
- **Natural (♮)** cancels a sharp or flat.

An accidental lasts **until the end of the bar** and applies only to that pitch. A **key signature** at the start of each line applies its sharps or flats to every note of those letter names throughout (see MLb). A natural sign in a piece in D major is therefore cancelling one of the key signature's sharps. The exam may ask you to "write in the missing accidental": look for the note that must be raised or lowered to fit the key or the chord.

### Time signatures and bars
The **time signature** is two numbers at the start: the top number is how many beats, and the bottom number is what kind of beat (2 = minim, 4 = crotchet, 8 = quaver).
- **Simple time:** 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 (also written C) and 2/2 (split common time).
- **Compound time:** 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8, each beat a dotted crotchet (see MEc).

**Bar lines** divide the music into bars. Every bar adds up to the time signature, except an **anacrusis** at the start, which is made up by a shortened final bar. A **double bar line** marks the end of a section. **Repeat marks** (two dots beside a thick double bar) mean play the section again. **First- and second-time bars** give different endings for each playing. **Bar numbers** are printed at the start of each line; count from there. Remember that an anacrusis is bar 0, not bar 1.

**Other markings to find on a score:**
- a dot above or below the note = **staccato**;
- ">" = **accent**;
- **tr** = **trill**;
- a small note before a main note = **grace note** or appoggiatura;
- an arch with a dot = **pause**;
- dynamic letters (pp to ff, sfz) and hairpins (< crescendo, > diminuendo);
- Italian tempo words above the stave.

### Working out a missing time signature
Add up every note and rest in a complete bar, then look at the **beaming**:
- a minim + crotchet + two quavers = 4 crotchets, so **4/4**;
- a dotted crotchet + three quavers = 6 quavers in two groups of 3, so **6/8**;
- three crotchets = **3/4**;
- a minim and a minim = 2/2 or 4/4, and the beaming or the question will tell you which.

### Listening strategy
In the exam you follow the printed score while the recording plays. Practise with the topic videos (Music Learning Club on notation, JohnMcAllisterMusic on treble and bass note names, Serenity Music Tuition on grouping notes and rests). Then:
1. **Follow any score while listening,** keeping your finger on the bar. If you get lost, find a landmark (a long note, a rest, the start of a phrase) and jump back in.
2. **Count bars aloud** in the first playing, and write the bar numbers of the key moments in the margin.
3. **Scan the score before the music starts** for things you are likely to be asked about: ties, triplets, rests, accidentals, trills, slurs, the lowest and highest notes.
4. **Drill note names daily:** ten treble and ten bass notes, including ledger lines, until you can read them instantly.

::: terms
- **Stave**: the five horizontal lines and four spaces on which notes are written.
- **Treble clef**: the G clef, used for higher parts; lines E G B D F, spaces F A C E.
- **Bass clef**: the F clef, used for lower parts; lines G B D F A, spaces A C E G.
- **Ledger line**: a short extra line above or below the stave for notes beyond its range.
- **Middle C**: the C on one ledger line below the treble stave or one ledger line above the bass stave.
- **Tie**: a curved line joining two notes of the same pitch so they sound as one longer note.
- **Slur**: a curved line over notes of different pitch, meaning play them smoothly (legato).
- **Triplet**: three notes marked with a 3, played in the time of two of the same value.
- **Accidental**: a sharp, flat or natural written before a note, lasting to the end of the bar.
- **Demisemiquaver**: a note worth an eighth of a crotchet, drawn with three tails or beams.
- **Time signature**: the two numbers at the start showing the number of beats per bar and the beat value.
:::

::: method
1. **Check the clef first:** treble (E G B D F / F A C E) or bass (G B D F A / A C E G).
2. **Check the key signature** for sharps or flats that apply to every note of that letter name, then check for accidentals earlier in the same bar.
3. **For "give a bar number where ...",** scan bar by bar and give **one** clear bar number. The first answer is the one marked.
4. **For a note value,** count the tails or beams: 1 = quaver, 2 = semiquaver, 3 = demisemiquaver; open head = minim or semibreve.
5. **For a missing time signature,** add the bar's values and check the beaming (groups of 3 quavers mean compound).
6. **For a completed rhythm,** make sure every bar adds up exactly, and that the notes are grouped in beats.
7. **Tie against slur:** same pitch = tie; different pitches = slur.
:::

In a cello part in bass clef, the first note of bar 5 sits on the middle line of the stave. Give its note name. (1)

Bass clef lines from the bottom are G, B, D, F, A, so the middle line is **D** (1).
:::

1 + 1 + 2 = four crotchet beats: **4/4** (1).
:::

Two beams = **semiquaver** (1).

::: example Complete the rhythm
A bar of 3/4 has five notes. The first starts on beat 1 and lasts a beat and a half. The second lasts half a beat. Notes 3, 4 and 5 are marked with a 3 and share beat 3 equally. Give the value of each note. (5)

Note 1: **dotted crotchet** (1). Note 2: **quaver** (1). Notes 3, 4 and 5: **triplet quavers** (1 each) (3). Check: 1½ + ½ + 1 = 3 beats.
:::

---
A tie joins two notes of the same pitch so they are held as one (1); a slur covers different pitches and means play them smoothly / legato (1).

::: q
A bar in a piece with no key signature has an F sharp on beat 1 and another F on beat 3 with no sign in front of it. What is the second note? (1)
---
F sharp (the accidental lasts until the end of the bar).
:::

::: q
Give the time signature of a bar containing two dotted crotchets, with the quavers beamed in threes. (1)
---
6/8 (compound duple).
:::

::: watch
- Read bass clef **from its own landmarks**. Reading it as treble puts every note a 3rd wrong.
- **Anacrusis bars** are usually numbered 0, so count bar 1 from the first **full** bar.
- An accidental lasts **to the end of the bar only**, and applies only to that pitch.
- A **tie** and a **slur** look alike. Check whether the pitches are the same.
- Give a **single** bar number or note name. Extra answers are ignored after the first.
- A semibreve rest can mean a whole bar's silence even in 3/4 or 2/4.
- Write note names **with their accidental** when the key signature applies: "F sharp", not "F".
:::
