## What it is

*Africa* by **Toto** is the Area of Study 4 **set work** (prepared extract). **Question 7 is always a 12-mark question on it**. The paper prints part of the score and plays an extract three times. You must recognise **which section** it comes from, what comes **before and after**, and the **riffs, chords, keys, phrase lengths, voices and instruments** of that section. Every fact below comes from Eduqas's own background notes and analysis and from the real mark schemes. Learn it as thoroughly as you would a set text.

The app can't play audio. Watch the topic's videos, which are Eduqas-specific walk-throughs of *Africa* with the score, its chords and practice questions. Listen to the whole song several times with the structure table below in front of you, until you can name any section within a few seconds.

### Background

- **Toto** was an **American rock band**. They recorded *Africa* in **1981** for their fourth studio album, ***Toto IV***.
- It was written by two band members, **David Paich and Jeff Porcaro**.
- It was released as the **third single** from the album in **1982** (30 September) on Columbia Records. It reached **number one in the USA** (for one week, in February 1983) and the **top ten in the UK** and several other countries.
- **Genre**: **soft rock**. It is a love song with **features of African music**, which makes it a kind of **fusion**.

### The music at a glance

| Element | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Instrumentation** | A rock band: **drum kit with additional percussion**, **lead and bass guitars**, **synthesisers**, **male lead vocals** and **male backing vocals**. The recording also has sounds like **marimba and xylophone** (pitched percussion) and a **flute-like** countermelody. |
| **Tempo** | Moderately fast |
| **Dynamics** | Mostly **mezzo-forte (mf)**; the **choruses are forte (f)** |
| **Metre** | **2/2 (split common time)** throughout: **two minim beats per bar** (simple duple) |
| **Rhythm** | **Ostinato** rhythms made almost entirely of **quavers**, with constant **syncopation**. The vocal rhythm looks complicated but follows the natural rhythm of the words. |
| **Tonality** | Most of the song is in **B major**; **all the choruses are in A major** |
| **Harmony** | **Diatonic**; a mixture of root-position and **inverted** chords; the harmonic rhythm is mostly **one chord per bar** |
| **Melody** | Mostly **conjunct**, with some **pentatonic** writing; the vocal range is just under two octaves on the score and wider on the recording because of the **improvisations** at the end |
| **Texture** | **Homophonic** (melody and accompaniment) |
| **Structure** | **Verse–chorus form** |

The key is **arguably ambiguous**. Eduqas's analysis puts most of the song in B major, but it can also be heard in **E major** or **A lydian**. The chorus is definitely in A major. Eduqas says exam questions will be accessible whichever key you were taught. This lesson uses B major, as the Eduqas analysis does.

### Intro (bars 1–4): the two riffs

On the recording, an extra **eight-bar introduction of unpitched percussion alone**, with layered rhythms and ostinatos, comes before the scored intro.

The intro uses only **three chords: VII, vi and ii (A, G#m, C#m)**. It is made of two **riffs**, each lasting two bars:
- **Riff a** (bars 1–2) is a distinctive **syncopated chord rhythm**. It mostly repeats chord **VII (A)** and ends with **vi (G#m)** and **ii (C#m)**. Riff a is heard in **every section except the chorus**.
- **Riff b** (bars 3–4, with an **anacrusis**) is a **five-note ostinato** based on the **E major pentatonic scale**, played over a **sustained chord ii (C#m)**. Riff b is **developed in the Instrumental**.

Both riffs recall **traditional African music** through their **syncopation**, the **pentatonic scale**, and **irregular ostinato groupings** that cut across the 2/2 metre.

### Verse 1 (bars 5–39, 35 bars, B major)

- There are **four phrases**, of **9, 9, 9 and 8 bars**.
- **Nine-bar phrases are unusual.** The reason is that **riff a starts as the vocal line ends** in each phrase, which adds bars to the phrase.
- In the first three phrases, the **vocal melody** falls into two lines of **3 and 5 bars**, then a **silent bar**. It is **fairly low** in the voice, **conjunct**, **syncopated** and **mostly syllabic**.
- The **harmonic rhythm is slow**: **one chord per bar**, except where riff a's own chord pattern comes back.
- The chords of each nine-bar phrase, bar by bar:

| Bar 5 | Bar 6 | Bar 7 | Bar 8 | Bar 9 | Bar 10 | Bar 11 | Bars 12–13 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** (B) | **iii** (D#m) | **vi** (G#m) | **Ic** (B/F#) | **VIIc** (A/E) | E/F# | **vi** (G#m) | **Riff a** |

B/F# is chord **I in second inversion (Ic)**, because the 5th of the chord, F#, is in the bass. A/E is also a second inversion.

- **The final eight-bar phrase (bars 32–39)** starts like the others, but in its **fourth bar the melody jumps up an octave**. There it is **harmonised by a backing vocalist a 3rd below, in parallel motion**. Chord vi (G#m) lasts an **extra bar**, riff a comes in the 5th–6th bars, and chord **ii (C#m)** is held to the end. The chords are B | D#m | G#m | G#m | riff a (2 bars) | C#m | C#m.

### Chorus 1 (bars 40–57, 18 bars, A major)

- The key **changes to A major**, and the dynamic rises to **forte**.
- There are **four phrases** of **4, 4, 4 and 6 bars**.
- The number of voices increases: **phrase 1 is a solo, phrase 2 a duet, and phrases 3 and 4 are in three parts** (solo, then duet, then trio).
- The melody of the first three phrases uses **only two pitches: the tonic (A) and the leading note (G#)**. It is **high** in the voice, **syncopated** and **syllabic**.
- The chords are a typical pop pattern, **vi–IV–I–V (F#m–D–A–E)**, one per bar.
- The **final six-bar phrase** starts in the same way. In its fourth bar the melody and chords change to **prepare the return to B major**, and the melody ends with a **melisma**. The chords are F#m | D | A | C#m | E | F#m then E/G#.
- **Riff a is not heard in the chorus.**

### Links 1 and 2 (bars 58–65, 8 bars, B major)

The links repeat the **intro music**, played twice (4 + 4 bars).

### Verse 2 (bars 14–39, 26 bars, B major)

- It has the same music as Verse 1 with **new lyrics**. It is **shorter**, with **three phrases (9, 9, 8)** instead of four, because it leaves out one nine-bar phrase.
- A **countermelody / descant** on a **flute-like instrument** plays across the top of the texture. It moves **conjunctly**, changing pitch at the same speed as the harmonic rhythm.
- The **backing vocals** are more important. In the nine-bar phrases they enter in the **fifth bar** on **"ooo"**, then **join the lead singer on the last word of each phrase**. The start of the second nine-bar phrase is harmonised by a backing vocalist, first **a 4th above** and then **a 3rd above** the melody.
- In **bar 33** the melody changes: the singer **leaps up to D# and C#** on the words "deep in(side)".
- The texture is **thicker** than in Verse 1.

### Instrumental (bars 66–82, 17 bars, B major)

- It uses the **verse accompaniment** (riff a, the same chords and harmonic rhythm), but the vocal melody is replaced by an **instrumental melody on synthesiser, based on riff b**.
- It is an even **shorter verse**: **one nine-bar phrase and one eight-bar phrase**.
  - Bars 66–69: a **descending** melody on the **B major pentatonic** scale, in **groups of three** notes, ending with a **triplet**.
  - Bars 70–74: an **ascending and descending** melody on the **E major scale**, with more rhythmic variety.
  - Bars 75–77: an **ascending B major pentatonic** melody, again in groups of three, ending in a "pop" style with **grace notes** and **syncopation**.
  - Bars 78–82: **the voice returns** for the second half of the eight-bar phrase (the "Hurry boy" line, with slightly changed words).
- There is **no countermelody**, and the backing vocals are only used at the end.
- **Chorus 3** follows it.

### Chorus 3 (bars 40–92, 22 bars, A major)

This is Chorus 1 with **three changes**:
1. A **new electric guitar riff** in the **last bar of each phrase**.
2. **Phrase 3 is heard five times**, so the section is much longer.
3. **Solo vocal improvisations** at the end of the **first, second and fourth** repeats of phrase 3, each one different.

### Outro (bars 93–96, 4 bars, B major)

The outro repeats the **intro music**. On the recording it repeats again and again, and the **texture gradually reduces** until only the **rhythm track** from the opening and the **bass line of riff a** remain.

### How Toto suggest Africa

- **Sonority**: marimba- and xylophone-like pitched percussion sounds, extra layered percussion, and a flute-like countermelody.
- **Rhythm**: constant **syncopation** and **ostinato** patterns, with **irregular groupings** that cut across the 2/2 metre.
- **Melody**: **pentatonic** riffs and synth melodies.
- **Texture**: **layered** percussion ostinatos in the opening percussion intro.
- These are combined with **soft-rock** features: verse–chorus form, drum kit, bass guitar, synthesisers, pop chord progressions and backing vocals. The result is a **fusion**.

### Exam strategy: identify the section fast

| What you see or hear | Section |
|---|---|
| Riff a and riff b, no voice, 4 bars | Intro / Outro |
| The same, 8 bars, after a chorus | Link |
| Low syncopated vocal, nine-bar phrases, riff a | Verse |
| Verse music plus a flute-like descant and "ooo" backing vocals | **Verse 2** |
| Synth melody based on riff b over verse chords, voice only at the end | **Instrumental** |
| High two-note melody, solo then duet then trio, vi–IV–I–V in A major | Chorus |
| Chorus with a guitar riff at phrase ends, a repeated line and improvisations | **Chorus 3** |

::: terms
- **Soft rock**: a gentle, melodic style of rock with keyboards and synthesisers, the genre of Africa.
- **Riff a**: Africa's two-bar syncopated chord riff (A, G#m, C#m), heard in every section except the chorus.
- **Riff b**: Africa's two-bar, five-note pentatonic ostinato over chord ii, developed in the Instrumental.
- **Pentatonic scale**: a five-note scale, usually notes 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 of the major scale.
- **Split common time**: 2/2, with two minim beats in each bar.
- **Syncopation**: accenting notes off the beat, found throughout Africa's riffs and vocal lines.
- **Harmonic rhythm**: the rate at which the chords change; in Africa mostly one chord per bar.
- **Second inversion**: a chord with its 5th in the bass, such as B/F# (Ic) in Africa's verse.
- **Countermelody**: a second melody played against the main one, like Verse 2's flute-like descant.
- **Melisma**: several notes sung to one syllable, as at the end of the chorus's final phrase.
- **Grace notes**: extra decorative notes that are not essential to the melody or harmony.
- **Link**: the eight-bar passage after Choruses 1 and 2 that repeats the intro music.
:::

::: method
1. **Identify the section first** using the table above, then answer everything else from your knowledge of that section. Check the key, phrase lengths, voices, riffs and chords.
2. **"Section before or after"**: learn the order. Intro, V1, Ch1, Link 1, V2, Ch2, Link 2, Instrumental, Ch3, Outro. After the Instrumental comes **Chorus 3**. Before Verse 2 comes **Link 1**.
3. **Chords**: learn the verse (B, D#m, G#m, B/F#, A/E, E/F#, G#m) and the chorus (F#m, D, A, E), the Roman numerals in each key, and that **B/F# is second inversion**.
4. **Comparisons** (the same as or different from Verse 2): use the concrete facts. It is the same in key, tempo, metre, chords, riff a and the nine-bar phrases. It differs in length, the countermelody, the backing vocals, the synth melody and riff b.
5. **Keys in full**: write "B major" and "A major", never just "B" or "A".
6. **One fact per mark**, and use precise terms: syncopated, conjunct, syllabic, pentatonic, 2/2, homophonic.
:::

::: watch
- **Keys named in full.** The chorus is **A major** and the verse **B major**. "A" or "B" alone does not score.
- **Chorus 3 comes after the Instrumental**, not Verse 3 or the Outro.
- **Riff a is not in the chorus.** It is in every other section.
- **Nine-bar phrases** are a key fact, together with the reason: riff a starts as the vocal line ends.
- **Riff b is pentatonic** and **the Instrumental develops it**. Its motif first appears in the **Intro**.
- **Mixing up the verses.** Verse 2 has three phrases, the flute-like countermelody and "ooo" backing vocals. Verse 1 has four phrases and no countermelody.
- **Metre.** 2/2 means **two minim beats**, not two crotchets.
- **The year is 1982**, the single's release. The recording was made in 1981.
