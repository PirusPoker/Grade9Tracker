## What it is

This lesson covers the "how" of popular music. The spec asks you to study how:
- **instrumental and synthesised sound** is used;
- **original music may be modified**;
- **vocal sounds** are used;
- **instruments and voices are combined**;
- sound is **computer-generated and amplified**;
- **software and samplers** are used.

It also lists the features **loops, samples, panning, phasing, balance, lead and backing vocals, backing tracks** and **melismatic and syllabic writing**. Exam questions ask you to describe a singer's or an instrument's part, to name a technique, and to explain how technology is used. They can also carry the **10-mark** extended answer.

The app can't play audio. The topic's videos cover voices in pop, the instruments of pop, loops and samples, and audio effects. With each one, say what the sound is and how it has been made or changed.

### Voices

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lead vocal** | The main singer, who carries the melody |
| **Backing vocals** | Supporting singers who add **harmony**, "ooh/aah" **vocalisations**, echoes of the lead's words or **call and response** |
| **Syllabic** | One note per syllable, which makes the words clear |
| **Melismatic / melisma** | Several notes sung to one syllable, typical of soul, R&B and gospel runs |
| **Falsetto** | A light, high voice above a (usually male) singer's normal range |
| **Belt** | A powerful, bright, chest-voice sound high in the range, common in musical theatre and pop ballads |
| **Rap** | Rhythmic speech over a beat, typical of hip-hop |
| **Scat** | Improvised singing on nonsense syllables, from jazz |
| **Vibrato** | Slight, rapid wavering of pitch that adds warmth |
| **A cappella** | Voices alone, with no instrumental accompaniment |
| **Harmony / in 3rds** | Backing singers moving in parallel with the melody, often a 3rd apart |
| **Call and response** | The lead sings a phrase and the backing singers answer |
| **Ad lib** | Improvised vocal decoration, usually near the end |

Voice types are **soprano and alto** (female, high then lower) and **tenor and bass** (male, high then lower). A question may ask for the **range** (e.g. about an octave) or the **register** (high or low).

### Instruments of the pop/rock group

| Instrument | Role | Techniques to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead guitar** (electric) | Melody, solos, riffs, fills | **Distortion**, **pitch bend** (bending the string), **hammer-on**, slide/glissando, **power chords** (root + 5th, no 3rd), palm muting |
| **Rhythm guitar** | Chords and the strummed accompaniment | Strumming, power chords, **stab chords** |
| **Bass guitar** | The bass line, locking with the drums | **Slap bass**, walking bass, riffs, driving quavers |
| **Drum kit** | The beat, fills, backbeat | **Rim shot**, **fill**, hi-hat patterns, cymbal crashes, **drum roll** |
| **Keyboards / synthesiser** | Chords, pads, riffs, melodies | Sustained pads, arpeggiated patterns, synth bass |
| **Acoustic guitar** | Strummed or picked accompaniment, softer songs | Finger-picking |
| **Horn section** (trumpet, saxophone, trombone) | Riffs, stabs and solos in soul, funk and pop | Stab chords, call and response with the singer |
| **Strings** | A lush backing in ballads and pop orchestration | Legato sustained lines, pizzicato |

The **rhythm section** (drums, bass, rhythm guitar and/or keyboard) provides the harmony and beat. The lead instruments and voice sit on top.

### Technology: how sound is made, amplified and modified

**Amplification.** Electric guitars and basses need an **amplifier** to be heard. Microphones and a **PA system** amplify voices for big venues. Amplification made stadium rock possible and allows very quiet, breathy singing to be heard clearly.

**Computer-generated and synthesised sound.** A **synthesiser** creates sound electronically. A **drum machine** produces programmed beats. **MIDI** lets keyboards and computers send note information to one another. A **DAW** (digital audio workstation, the recording and editing software) and a **sequencer** let one person build a whole track.

**Samplers and samples.** A **sample** is a recorded sound or section of music that is reused. A **sampler** stores samples so they can be triggered, looped, re-pitched and layered. Hip-hop is built on samples of older records. A **loop** is a sample or pattern repeated continuously, such as a drum loop or a sampled bass line.

**Effects and mixing.**

| Effect / idea | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Reverb** | Adds reflections so a sound seems to be in a room, hall or cathedral |
| **Delay / echo** | Distinct repeats of the sound after a gap |
| **Distortion** | Overdrives the signal for a gritty, aggressive tone (rock guitar) |
| **Panning** | Places a sound **left or right** in the stereo field |
| **Phasing** | A sweeping, swirling "whoosh" made by mixing a sound with a slightly delayed, shifting copy |
| **Balance** | The relative volume of each part in the mix (e.g. the vocal clearly above the band) |
| **Auto-tune / pitch correction** | Corrects pitch; used heavily, it gives a robotic effect |
| **Multi-tracking / overdubbing** | Recording parts separately and layering them, e.g. a singer recording their own backing vocals |
| **EQ (equalisation)** | Boosting or cutting bass, middle or treble frequencies |
| **Compression** | Evens out the dynamics so the track sounds consistently loud |

**Backing track.** A pre-recorded accompaniment that a singer performs over, live or in the studio.

### How original music may be modified

- **Cover version**: another artist records an existing song, often changing the style, tempo, key or instrumentation.
- **Remix**: an existing recording is reworked with technology. The producer may add new beats, samples, effects, change the structure or tempo, or loop sections. A real exam used a film theme remixed by a rock band.
- **Arrangement**: the same song reorganised for different forces, for example from a band to an orchestra, or to an **acoustic** or **a cappella** version.
- **Sampling** an older song inside a new one.

### How instruments and voices are combined

Describe **texture** and **balance**. Is the voice **accompanied by chords** (homophonic)? Is there a **countermelody**? Do the backing vocals **harmonise in 3rds** or answer in **call and response**? Are instruments added section by section (**layering**) so the chorus is **thicker** than the verse? Does an instrument **double** the vocal line in unison?

### Listening strategy

1. **Voice first**: who is singing (male or female, lead or backing)? Is it syllabic or melismatic? Is there a special technique such as falsetto, belt or rap? Are the backing singers in harmony, on "ooh", or in call and response?
2. **Instruments next**: listen from the bottom up. What is the bass doing? What is the drum pattern? Is the guitar clean or distorted? Is there a synth?
3. **Technology last**: is there reverb (space), echo (repeats), panning (left–right), phasing (swirl), a loop or sample (exact repetition), auto-tune?

::: terms
- **Syllabic**: word-setting with one note for each syllable.
- **Melismatic**: word-setting in which one syllable is sung to several notes.
- **Falsetto**: a light, high vocal register above a singer's normal voice, usually used by male singers.
- **Belt**: a powerful, bright singing style using chest voice high in the range.
- **Backing vocals**: supporting singers who add harmony, vocalisations or echoes behind the lead vocal.
- **Loop**: a section of music or a sample repeated continuously.
- **Sample**: a recorded sound or passage of music reused in a new piece.
- **Panning**: positioning a sound between the left and right speakers.
- **Balance**: the relative volume of the different parts in a mix.
- **Distortion**: an effect that overdrives the sound, giving a gritty, aggressive tone.
- **Backing track**: a pre-recorded accompaniment that a performer sings or plays along with.
- **Remix**: a new version of a recording made by altering it with technology, e.g. new beats or effects.
:::

::: method
1. For **"describe two/three features of the X part"**, give one separate feature per mark. Cover what it plays (melody, chords, riff), how (technique, articulation), its pitch and range, its rhythm, and when it enters.
2. For **backing vocals**, the usual marks are harmony or in 3rds, vocalisations ("ooh"), sustained notes, call and response, echoing the lead, and joining on key words.
3. For **"explain three ways music technology is used"**, give three different technologies, each with its use, e.g. "reverb on the vocal makes it sound distant". Don't repeat echo/delay.
4. **Name techniques exactly**: falsetto, not "high voice"; pitch bend, not "wobbly note"; slap bass, not "funky bass".
5. For the **10-mark answer**, work through voice, instruments, technology, texture and structure, and link each feature to its **effect** on the song's mood or style.
:::

::: example Backing vocals
In a chorus, the backing singers hold long notes on "ooh", moving by step in harmony a 3rd below the melody, then echo the singer's last word. Describe two features of the backing vocals. (2)

They sing **vocalisations / "ooh"** on **sustained** notes (1). They sing in **harmony, in 3rds**, and **echo** the lead singer's words (1).
:::

::: example Guitar technique
A guitarist pushes a string sideways so that a note slides up in pitch. Name the technique. (1)

**Pitch bend / string bend** (1).
:::

::: example Technology
Explain three ways music technology could be used to create a remix of a song. (3)

A new **drum loop / drum machine** beat could replace the original drums (1). **Samples** of the original vocal could be chopped and repeated (1). Effects such as **reverb, delay or filtering** could change the sound, and the tempo could be changed with **time-stretching** (1).
:::

::: example Cover versions
State two ways a cover version might differ from the original recording. (2)

Any two: different **style/genre** (1); different **tempo** or **key** (1); different **instrumentation**, e.g. acoustic; different singer's vocal style; a new arrangement or structure.
:::

::: example Word-setting
A singer sings the word "love" to seven different notes in a fast run. Name the word-setting, and name one style in which this is common. (2)

**Melismatic / melisma** (1); **soul / R&B / gospel** (accept pop ballad) (1).
:::

Describe the role of the bass guitar and drum kit in a pop band. (2)
---

Explain what is meant by balance in a recording. (1)
---

::: watch
- **Panning is not volume, and phasing is not echo.** Panning is left–right placement. Phasing is a swirling, sweeping sound.
- **Listing instruments** when the question asks how they are **used**. Say what they play and how.
- **Vague vocal answers.** "High" could be falsetto or belt, and "lots of notes on one word" is melismatic. Use the exact term.
- **Echo counted twice.** Reverb and echo/delay are separate effects, but "echo" and "delay" together earn only one mark.
- **Backing vocals described as "backing".** Say how: in harmony, in 3rds, on "ooh", call and response, sustained.
- **A 10-mark answer that only lists effects.** Link each one to the mood or style: "the distortion makes the guitar sound aggressive, suiting the rock style".
:::
