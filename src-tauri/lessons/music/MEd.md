## What it is

**Sonority** (also called **timbre** or **tone colour**) is the character of a sound: what makes a flute sound different from a trumpet playing the same note. It covers **which** instruments and voices you hear, **how** they are played, and any **technology** applied to them. **Dynamics** is the **volume**, and how it changes.

In the exam, sonority is the most common 1-mark question type: "Name the brass instrument", "Name the family", "Underline the performance technique", "Name the type of ensemble". It also appears in 3-mark "describe how instrumentation is used" questions, where the instruction **"do not simply list instruments"** means you must say **what** each instrument does and **how** it is played.

### Instruments by family
| Family | Instruments (highest to lowest) | How the sound is made, and what to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| **Strings** | **violin**, **viola**, **cello**, **double bass**; **harp** | bowed (**arco**) or plucked (**pizzicato**). The violin is bright and agile; the viola is slightly darker; the cello is rich and singing; the double bass is the deep foundation. The harp is plucked, with rippling **spread chords** and **glissandos** |
| **Woodwind** | **flute**, **oboe**, **clarinet**, **saxophone**, **bassoon** | the flute is breathy and pure (no reed); the oboe is reedy and nasal (double reed); the clarinet is smooth and warm with a wide range (single reed); the saxophone is a metal-bodied single-reed instrument, central to jazz; the bassoon is low and dry (double reed) |
| **Brass** | **trumpet**, **French horn**, **trombone**, **tuba** | lips buzzing into a mouthpiece. The trumpet is bright and heroic (fanfares); the French horn is mellow and noble; the trombone is powerful and can **slide**; the tuba is the deep bass |
| **Percussion (tuned)** | **timpani**, **glockenspiel**, **xylophone** | the timpani are large tuned "kettle" drums, used for rolls and dramatic strokes; the glockenspiel has metal bars (bell-like, sparkling); the xylophone has wooden bars (dry, bright) |
| **Percussion (untuned)** | **snare drum**, **cymbal**, **drum kit**, **hand-held percussion** (tambourine, shaker, triangle) | the snare has a crisp rattle (**drum roll**, **rim shot**); cymbals crash or shimmer; the **drum kit** combines bass drum, snare, hi-hat, toms and cymbals for one player |
| **Keyboards** | **piano**, **organ**, **harpsichord**, synthesiser | the piano's strings are hit by hammers, so it can play loud or soft; the organ has pipes and sustained notes and is associated with church; the **harpsichord** plucks its strings, giving a jangling, crisp sound with little dynamic change, and is the Baroque continuo instrument |
| **Guitars** | **classical / Spanish guitar**, **electric guitar**, **bass guitar** | the classical guitar has nylon strings and a soft, warm tone; the electric guitar is **amplified** and often has effects such as **distortion**; the bass guitar plays the bass line (plucked, or **slap bass**) |
| **Indian and bhangra instruments** | **sitar**, **sarangi**, **tumbi**, **tabla**, **dhol** | the sitar is a long-necked plucked string instrument with a buzzing, shimmering tone and sliding pitches; the sarangi is a bowed instrument with a voice-like tone; the tumbi is a small, high, single-stringed plucked instrument heard in bhangra riffs; the **tabla** is a pair of hand drums from Indian classical music; the **dhol** is a large double-headed barrel drum played with two sticks, the heartbeat of bhangra |

### Voices
**Soprano** is the highest female voice, **alto** the lower female voice, **tenor** the higher male voice and **bass** the lowest male voice.
- A **solo** is one performer, a **duet** two and a **trio** three.
- **Backing vocals** support the lead singer with harmonies, "ooh"s or echoes.
- **A cappella** means voices alone, with no instruments.
- A **chorus** or choir is a large group of singers. In a song, "chorus" also means the repeated refrain.

### Groupings
- **Orchestra**: strings, woodwind, brass and percussion together. A **string orchestra** has strings only.
- **String quartet**: two violins, viola and cello.
- **Basso continuo**: the Baroque bass line played by a bass instrument (cello, double bass or bassoon) together with a chord instrument (harpsichord, organ or lute) filling in the harmony.
- **Pop/rock group**: lead vocals, lead and rhythm guitars, bass guitar, drum kit, often keyboards.
- **Rhythm section**: drums, bass and a chord instrument (piano or guitar), the engine of jazz and pop bands.
- **Acoustic**: not electrically amplified, as in an "acoustic guitar" or an "acoustic set".

### Performance techniques and articulation
| Group | Technique | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | **humming** | singing with the mouth closed |
| | **syllabic** / **melismatic** | one note per syllable / many notes sung to one syllable |
| | **scat** | improvised nonsense syllables in jazz |
| | **vibrato** | a slight, rapid wobble in pitch to warm the tone (also used by string and wind players) |
| | **falsetto** | a light, high male "head voice" above the normal range |
| | **belt** | a powerful, loud chest voice pushed high, typical of musical theatre and soul |
| | **rap** | rhythmic speech over a beat |
| **Strings** | **arco** / **pizzicato** | bowed / plucked |
| | **divisi** | a section splits to play two or more separate parts |
| | **double stopping** | playing two strings at once |
| | **tremolo** | very fast repeated bow strokes on one note, creating a shivering, tense sound |
| | **muted** | a mute on the bridge gives a veiled, soft tone |
| **Guitar and bass** | **distortion** | an effect that makes an amplified guitar sound fuzzy and aggressive |
| | **hammer-on** | sounding a note by hammering a finger onto the fret without plucking again |
| | **slap bass** | the thumb slaps the bass string, a percussive funk sound |
| | **pitch bend** | pushing a string, or using a synth wheel, to raise the pitch smoothly |
| **Drums** | **rim shot** | the stick hits the snare's rim and head together: a sharp crack |
| | **drum roll** | very rapid strokes making a sustained rumble, often with a crescendo |
| **Brass and wind** | **muted** | a mute in the bell gives a thin, buzzy or distant sound |
| | **tongued** / **slurred** | each note separately started with the tongue / notes joined in one breath |
| **Any instrument** | **staccato** / **legato** | short and **detached** / smooth and joined |
| | **sustained** | notes held for their full length or longer |
| | **accent** | a note given extra emphasis (>) |
| | **glissando** / **slide** | sliding continuously between two pitches (harp, trombone, strings, piano) |
| | **plucked** | strings played with the fingers or a pick |

### Technology
- **Synthesised** or **electronic** sounds are created or shaped electronically.
- **Amplified** means made louder through amps and speakers.
- A **sample** is a recorded snippet re-used in a new track.
- **Reverb** adds the sense of a space or hall. **Echo** is a distinct repeat of the sound.
- **Panning** places a sound left or right in the stereo field.
- **Phasing** is a sweeping, swirling effect made from two copies of a sound slightly out of time.

These are covered more fully in AoS4b and AoS3c.

### Dynamics
| Marking | Italian | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **pp** | pianissimo | very quiet |
| **p** | piano | quiet |
| **mp** | mezzo piano | moderately quiet |
| **mf** | mezzo forte | moderately loud |
| **f** | forte | loud |
| **ff** | fortissimo | very loud |
| cresc. / < | **crescendo** | gradually getting louder |
| dim. / > | **diminuendo** (decrescendo) | gradually getting quieter |
| **sfz** | **sforzando** | a sudden, strong accent on one note or chord |

Sudden changes from loud to soft, typical of Baroque music, are called **terraced dynamics**. A **wide dynamic range** (from pp to ff) is typical of Romantic and film music. In the exam, "louder" or "crescendo" is better than "loud" when the question asks how the music changes.

### Describing instrumentation well
"Violins, cellos, trumpets" is a list and scores little. Instead, say for each point **who** is playing, **what** they play and **how** they play it:
- "The strings play a **tremolo** accompaniment."
- "The **muted** trumpets play **stab chords**."
- "The clarinet has the **solo**, accompanied by the saxophones in **harmony**."
- "The double bass plays a **pizzicato walking bass**."

### Listening strategy
Use the topic videos (Music Learning Club on sonority and on dynamics, HPA Music on dynamics, and INA Music on vocal and instrumental techniques). Build a mental sound library:
1. **Family first:** decide whether the sound is bowed, blown, struck or electronic, then narrow it down by pitch. For example, a high, bright, buzzy brass sound is a trumpet; a mellow, round one is a French horn.
2. **Technique second:** plucked or bowed? Detached or smooth? Shivering (tremolo) or sliding (glissando)?
3. **Dynamics:** name the starting level, then the change. Listen for a single violent accent (sforzando).
4. **Easily confused pairs:** oboe and clarinet (nasal or smooth); viola and violin (darker or brighter); glockenspiel and xylophone (ringing metal or dry wood); tabla and dhol (hand drums with pitch bends, or a big drum played with sticks); harpsichord and piano (plucked jangle or hammered warmth).

::: terms
- **Sonority**: the characteristic sound or tone colour of an instrument or voice; also called timbre.
- **Pizzicato**: plucking the strings of a bowed string instrument with the fingers.
- **Arco**: playing a string instrument with the bow, the instruction after a pizzicato passage.
- **Tremolo**: very rapid repetition of a note (fast bow strokes), giving a shivering, tense effect.
- **Double stopping**: playing two strings at the same time on a bowed string instrument.
- **Falsetto**: a light, high male singing voice above the normal chest-voice range.
- **Belt**: a powerful, loud chest-voice singing style pushed into a high range, common in musicals.
- **Melismatic**: singing several notes to one syllable, the opposite of syllabic word setting.
- **A cappella**: singing by voices alone without any instrumental accompaniment.
- **Basso continuo**: the Baroque bass line played by a bass instrument with a harpsichord or organ filling in chords.
- **Sforzando**: a sudden, strong accent on a single note or chord, marked sfz.
- **Distortion**: an electronic effect that makes an amplified guitar sound fuzzy, gritty and aggressive.
:::

::: method
1. **Name questions:** give **one** answer only, because the first answer is marked. Be specific: "trumpet", not "brass"; "drum kit", not "drums".
2. **Family questions:** strings, woodwind, brass or percussion. Remember the saxophone is woodwind.
3. **Technique questions:** match the sound to the technique: plucked = pizzicato; shivering = tremolo; sliding = glissando; high male = falsetto; loud chest voice = belt.
4. **Ensemble questions:** orchestra, string quartet, string orchestra, rock band, jazz trio or big band, basso continuo, choir. Choose the grouping, not a single instrument.
5. **Describe instrumentation:** for each mark give an instrument **plus** what it plays or how (role, technique, register, dynamic).
6. **Dynamics:** name the level with the Italian term, then any change (crescendo, diminuendo, sforzando, terraced).
:::

::: example Name the technique
In a film cue the violins play one note with very rapid, repeated bow strokes that create a shivering effect. Name the performance technique. (1)

**Tremolo** (1).
:::

::: example Name the ensemble
An extract is played by two violins, a viola and a cello. Name the type of ensemble. (1)

**String quartet** (1).
:::

::: example Describe instrumentation without listing
A big band extract features saxophones playing the tune together, trumpets adding short loud chords between phrases, a double bass plucking one note per beat and a clarinet solo near the end. Describe how the instrumentation is used. Do not simply list instruments. (3)

Any three: the saxophones play the **melody in unison** (1); the trumpets play **stab chords** / short punctuating chords (1); the double bass plays a **pizzicato walking bass** / on-the-beat bass (1); the clarinet takes an (improvised) **solo** towards the end (1).
:::

::: example Dynamics in order
Write these dynamic markings in order from quietest to loudest: mf, pp, f, mp. (1)

**pp, mp, mf, f** (1).
:::

::: example Voices
A male singer moves from his normal voice into a light, high, airy tone for the top notes of the chorus. Name this vocal technique, and name the male voice type that sings higher than a bass. (2)

**Falsetto** (1); **tenor** (1).
:::

::: q
Name the drum used in bhangra, and explain how it differs from the tabla. (2)
---
Dhol (1); it is a large double-headed barrel drum played with two sticks, whereas the tabla is a pair of hand drums played with the fingers (1).
:::

::: q
Name the keyboard instrument that plucks its strings and was typical of Baroque music. (1)
---
Harpsichord.
:::

::: q
Name two instruments that could play the chord part of a basso continuo. (2)
---
Any two: harpsichord (1); organ (1); lute / theorbo (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Be specific:** "trumpet" not "brass", "drum kit" not "drums". The examiners have refused "drums" where "drum kit" was required.
- **Do not simply list instruments** in a "how is instrumentation used" question. Add the role or technique for every mark.
- Give **one** answer to a "name" question. With more than one, only the first is marked.
- **Dhol, not tabla,** for bhangra.
- **Tremolo** (fast repeated notes) is not the same as **vibrato** (a pitch wobble).
- Dynamics describe **volume**. "Crescendo" is not "faster".
- The **viola** and **cello** are different instruments: the viola is held under the chin, the cello between the knees.
:::
