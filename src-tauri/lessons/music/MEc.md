## What it is

Three elements tell you how music moves in time:
- **Tempo** is the **speed** of the beat.
- **Metre** is how the beats are **grouped** into bars: in 2s, 3s or 4s, with the beat split into halves or thirds. The **time signature** shows it.
- **Rhythm** is the pattern of **long and short sounds** (and silences) laid over the beat.

In the exam these come up as 1-mark items ("Underline a suitable tempo marking", "Give the time signature", "Explain rubato", "Describe the rhythm of the bass line") and inside 3-mark "describe how rhythm and metre are used" questions.

### Tempo
The spec uses Italian terms. Learn them in order from slow to fast.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lento** / **Adagio** | slow |
| **Andante** | at a walking pace, flowing |
| **Moderato** | at a moderate speed |
| **Allegretto** | fairly fast, a little slower than allegro |
| **Allegro** | fast and lively |
| **Vivace** | lively and quick |

Changes of tempo:
- **Accelerando** (accel.): gradually getting faster. It builds excitement, as in a chase.
- **Ritardando** (rit.) or **rallentando** (rall.): gradually getting slower, usually at an ending.
- **Rubato** ("robbed time"): the performer pushes and pulls the tempo expressively, speeding up and slowing down within a phrase. It is typical of Romantic piano music and of expressive ballad singing. The 2025 mark scheme accepted "stolen time" or "bending and stretching the tempo".
- **Pause** (fermata, drawn as an arch over a dot): a note or rest held for longer than its written value.

**Tempo is not dynamics.** "It gets faster" (accelerando) and "it gets louder" (crescendo) are different answers, and confusing them loses marks every year. Tempo is also not the same as note values: a slow piece can be full of short notes.

### Metre
The **pulse** or **beat** is the steady heartbeat of the music. **Metre** is how the beats are grouped, with the first beat of each bar usually **accented** (stronger).
- **Duple**: 2 beats per bar (strong-weak), as in a march.
- **Triple**: 3 beats per bar (strong-weak-weak), as in a waltz or minuet.
- **Quadruple**: 4 beats per bar (strong-weak-medium-weak), the most common metre in pop.

**Simple time** means each beat divides into **two**. Count "1 and 2 and".

| Time signature | Beats per bar | Beat value | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2/4** | 2 | crotchet | simple duple (the Badinerie) |
| **3/4** | 3 | crotchet | simple triple |
| **4/4** (or C, common time) | 4 | crotchet | simple quadruple |
| 2/2 (split common time) | 2 | minim | simple duple (*Africa*: "two minim beats per bar") |

**Compound time** means each beat is a **dotted crotchet** that divides into **three** quavers. Count "1-and-a 2-and-a".

| Time signature | Beats per bar | Quavers per bar | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6/8** | 2 dotted crotchets | 6 | compound duple |
| **9/8** | 3 dotted crotchets | 9 | compound triple |
| **12/8** | 4 dotted crotchets | 12 | compound quadruple |

The top number tells you how many units are in a bar, and the bottom number tells you what the unit is: 2 = minim, 4 = crotchet, 8 = quaver. In compound time, **divide the top number by 3** to find the number of beats.

**How to tell simple from compound by ear:** tap the beat, then tap twice as fast. If the fast taps fit the music's subdivisions comfortably (a "march" feel), it is simple time. If the music keeps falling into groups of three per beat (a "lilting", galloping or jig feel), it is compound. Then count the beats to a bar for duple, triple or quadruple. A 12/8 slow ballad and a 4/4 piece with triplets can sound alike; the mark scheme will not credit both "12/8" and "triplets" for the same point.

**Regular metre** keeps the same time signature. **Irregular metre** uses an uneven number of beats, such as **5/4** (the *Mission Impossible* theme) or **7/8** (felt as 2 + 2 + 3 or 3 + 2 + 2). Irregular metre sounds lopsided, restless or urgent. Music can also **change metre** part-way through.

### Rhythm
| Note | Rest | Value in crotchet beats |
|---|---|---|
| semibreve | semibreve rest | 4 |
| minim | minim rest | 2 |
| crotchet | crotchet rest | 1 |
| quaver | quaver rest | ½ |
| semiquaver | semiquaver rest | ¼ |

- A **dot** adds half the note's value: a dotted minim is 3 beats and a dotted crotchet is 1½.
- **Associated rests** are the silences of the same lengths.
- A **dotted rhythm** is a long-short pair such as dotted quaver + semiquaver. It sounds jaunty, jerky, march-like or majestic.
- A **triplet** is three notes played in the time of two. It is shown by a 3 over the group.
- **On the beat** notes fall on the pulse. **Off-beat** notes fall between beats. Reggae guitar chops on the off-beats, for example, and the hi-hat in swing often marks beats 2 and 4.
- **Syncopation** places accents on weak beats or between beats, against the pulse. It sounds catchy and energetic, and it drives jazz, pop, Latin music and *Africa*.
- **Swing rhythms**: pairs of quavers are played unevenly, long-short, with a lilting triplet feel. This is the heart of jazz and blues.
- **Driving rhythms**: constant, repeated short notes, often quavers, that push the music forward. They are heard in rock and in film action cues.
- **Dance rhythms**: patterns tied to a dance, such as the waltz's "oom-pah-pah" in 3/4, the tango, or the bouncing bhangra beat.
- **Rock rhythms**: bass drum on beats 1 and 3, snare **backbeat** on 2 and 4, and steady quavers on the hi-hat.
- **Chaal**: the characteristic bouncing dhol rhythm of **bhangra**. Each crotchet beat is divided into three, giving a long-short, swung feel with strong accents that drive the dance.
- An **ostinato** is a rhythm or melody repeated over and over (AoS1c).

### Describing a rhythm or a bass line
For "Describe the rhythm of the bass line" (1 mark), the accepted answers were on the beat, regular, constant crotchets, quavers or walking bass. For "Describe how rhythm and metre are used" (3 marks), cover the time signature, whether the metre is regular, the note values, and devices such as syncopation, triplets, ostinato and driving rhythm.

### Listening strategy
Practise with the topic videos (HPA Music on rhythm and on tempo, Music Learning Club on tempo, metre and rhythm, and Music Matters on simple against compound time):
1. **Tap the pulse** with your foot throughout. Is it slow, walking or fast? Pick the Italian term.
2. **Find beat 1.** Count the beats until the strongest one comes back: 2, 3 or 4 for simple time, or an odd number for 5/4 and 7/8.
3. **Split the beat.** Do the subdivisions fall in 2s or 3s? That tells you simple or compound time.
4. **Listen for accents against your tap:** syncopation, off-beats or swing.
5. **Listen for change:** does the tempo speed up or slow down, or does it bend freely (rubato)? Is there a pause?

::: terms
- **Tempo**: the speed of the beat, given by Italian terms such as Andante or Allegro.
- **Rubato**: expressive "robbed time", pushing and pulling the tempo within a phrase.
- **Accelerando**: gradually getting faster; ritardando and rallentando mean gradually getting slower.
- **Metre**: the grouping of beats into bars (duple, triple, quadruple), shown by the time signature.
- **Simple time**: a metre whose beat divides into two, e.g. 2/4, 3/4, 4/4.
- **Compound time**: a metre whose dotted-crotchet beat divides into three quavers, e.g. 6/8, 9/8, 12/8.
- **Irregular metre**: an uneven number of beats per bar such as 5/4 or 7/8, sounding lopsided.
- **Syncopation**: accenting weak beats or the spaces between beats, against the regular pulse.
- **Swing rhythm**: pairs of quavers played long-short with a lilting triplet feel, typical of jazz.
- **Triplet**: three notes played in the time normally taken by two of the same value.
- **Dotted rhythm**: a long-short pattern such as dotted quaver and semiquaver, sounding jaunty.
- **Chaal**: the bouncing, swung dhol rhythm of bhangra, with each beat divided into three.
:::

::: method
1. **Tempo question:** choose the Italian term for the overall speed; if the speed changes, name the change (accelerando, rallentando, rubato, pause).
2. **Metre question:** count the beats in a bar, then decide whether the beat splits into 2 (simple) or 3 (compound). Combine them: 3 beats, split into 2 = 3/4; 2 beats, split into 3 = 6/8.
3. **Time signature from notation:** add up the note values in one bar in quavers. Beamed in 3s = compound (6/8, 9/8, 12/8); beamed in 2s or 4s = simple (2/4, 3/4, 4/4).
4. **Rhythm description:** use exact terms such as on the beat, syncopated, dotted, triplets, swing, driving, ostinato and note values, each with a location if possible.
5. **Never confuse tempo with dynamics** or with note values.
:::

::: example Time signature from note values
One bar contains a dotted crotchet, three quavers and a dotted crotchet, beamed in groups of three quavers. Give the time signature. (1)

Dotted crotchet = 3 quavers, plus 3 quavers, plus 3 quavers = 9 quavers in three dotted-crotchet beats, so **9/8** (1).
:::

::: example Six quavers two ways
Bar A contains six quavers beamed 3 + 3. Bar B contains six quavers beamed 2 + 2 + 2. Give a time signature for each bar. (2)

Bar A: **6/8** (two dotted-crotchet beats) (1). Bar B: **3/4** (three crotchet beats) (1).
:::

::: example Metre for a split common time song
A pop song's score is marked 2/2. Is the metre (a) two crotchet beats per bar, (b) two minim beats per bar, (c) four crotchet beats per bar or (d) four minim beats per bar? (1)

**(b) Two minim beats per bar** (1). The bottom number 2 means a minim beat, and the top 2 means two of them.
:::

::: example Explain rubato
A singer performs a ballad using rubato. Explain the meaning of this word. (1)

**Stolen or robbed time: the performer bends and stretches the tempo** expressively, pushing ahead and holding back instead of keeping strict time (1).
:::

::: example Rhythm and metre in an accompaniment
In a film overture the accompaniment repeats a one-bar pattern throughout. It has twelve quavers in each bar in groups of three, with accents often falling between the beats. Describe how rhythm and metre are used in the accompaniment. (3)

Any three: **compound time / 12/8** throughout (1); **regular** metre (1); **ostinato** / repeated one-bar pattern (1); **quavers** / short note values (1); **syncopation** / off-beat accents (1); **driving** rhythm (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Tempo = speed; dynamics = volume.** Don't write "faster" when the question is about getting louder.
- **2/2 is two minim beats,** not four crotchets, even though a bar holds the same total length as 4/4.
- In compound time, **divide the top number by 3** for the beats: 6/8 is duple, not six beats.
- **Syncopation** is accenting the off-beat, not just "fast" or "complicated" rhythm.
- **Rubato** is flexible tempo, not slowing down only. Mention both pushing and pulling.
- Don't credit yourself twice: the examiners do not accept both "12/8" and "triplets" for the same feature.
:::
