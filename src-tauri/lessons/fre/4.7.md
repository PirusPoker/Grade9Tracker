## What it is

Paper 4 is the Writing exam: **1 hour 15 minutes at Higher, 50 marks, 25% of the GCSE**. All instructions are in English and you write in French. No dictionaries. There are three questions:

- **Question 1: translation into French (10 marks).** Five English sentences, **at least 50 words** in total. The mark scheme splits them into **15 elements** (short chunks of meaning) and marks them on two grids:
  - **Grid one, rendering of meaning (5 marks).** The examiner ticks each element whose meaning is communicated, even with minor inaccuracies. Ticks become marks: **13 to 15 ticks = 5**, 10 to 12 = 4, 7 to 9 = 3, 4 to 6 = 2, 1 to 3 = 1.
  - **Grid two, knowledge of vocabulary and grammar (5 marks)**, judged across all five sentences together. For 5: very good knowledge of the vocabulary, few if any wrong or missing items, and grammar that is highly accurate, with only minor errors.
  - A zero on grid one means a zero on grid two.
- **Question 2: the 90-word task (15 marks).** A choice of **2.1 or 2.2**. Each has **three compulsory bullet points**; this question is shared with Foundation Question 5. Marked for **content and communication (10)** and **language (5)**:
  - **Content, top band (9 to 10).** All three bullets are covered, communication is clear, and ideas are regularly developed with a lot of relevant information.
  - **Language, top band (5).** A good variety of vocabulary, regular attempts at complex language, references to **all three time frames** that are mainly successful, and errors that are mainly minor.
- **Question 3: the 150-word task (25 marks).** A choice of **3.1 or 3.2**, each with **two compulsory bullet points**, and a more open, creative task. Marked for **content and communication (15)**, **range of language (5)** and **accuracy (5)**:
  - **Content, top band (13 to 15).** A lot of information relevant to the task, regular successful development of ideas, accounts or description, and communication that is clear with very few or no lapses.
  - **Range, top band.** A very good variety of vocabulary and grammatical structures, with complex language regularly attempted and often successful.
  - **Accuracy, top band.** Usually accurate (occasional major and some minor errors, especially in complex structures) and **secure verbs and tenses**.
  - Covering only one bullet caps content at 12 out of 15.

The word counts are **approximate**. Examiners mark everything you write, but the descriptors matter more than the count. Aim for about **100 words for Q2 and about 170 for Q3**: enough to develop every bullet, not so much that accuracy collapses. The bullets do not need equal coverage, but every one must be covered. Language beyond the AQA list gets equal credit if it is correct.

A grade 9 script has a precise Q1 where every English word has a French home. Its Q2 and Q3 answer every bullet with **past, present and future**, opinions with reasons, and a spread of Higher structures from the list (*depuis, venir de, avant de, après avoir, en + -ant, y, en, que, où*, the future, the conditional, superlatives, *il vaut mieux*), all with secure verb forms.

::: terms
- **element**: one of the 15 chunks of meaning in the Q1 translation; each is ticked if its meaning is communicated despite minor slips
- **Grid one (Q1)**: rendering of meaning; 13 to 15 ticked elements = 5 marks, 10 to 12 = 4, 7 to 9 = 3, 4 to 6 = 2, 1 to 3 = 1
- **Grid two (Q1)**: knowledge of vocabulary and grammar out of 5, judged across all five sentences as a whole
- **bullet point**: a compulsory content point; Q2 has three and Q3 has two, and every one must be covered
- **development**: going beyond the bare point with reasons, examples, consequences and opinions
- **time frames**: past, present and future references; Q2 top band needs all three, mainly successful
- **complex language**: longer sentences and Higher structures used to express more, such as après avoir mangé, en travaillant, le meilleur, j'irai
- **secure verbs**: verb forms and tenses almost always right; the key phrase in the top accuracy band of Q3
- **major error**: an error that affects communication, such as a wrong tense or a wrong word that changes the meaning
- **minor error**: an error that does not affect communication, such as a wrong gender or a missing accent
- **approximately 90 / 150 words**: guides, not limits; the descriptors decide the mark, not the word count
- **equal credit**: correct language from beyond the AQA list is credited just like listed language
:::

::: method
1. **Time plan (75 minutes):** Q1 about 15 minutes, Q2 about 20, Q3 about 35, and 5 minutes to check.
2. **Q1:** underline each verb and decide its tense and person. Box the small words (*often, very, also, every*): each can be an element. Translate the meaning, not word for word, but leave nothing out. Check agreements.
3. **Q2 and Q3: choose the task** whose bullets you can cover best with past, present and future.
4. **Plan in two minutes.** For each bullet, write the tense it needs and three ideas: point, reason, example.
5. **Write each bullet as a short paragraph.** Use the bullet's time frame, then add another (*À l'avenir... / Quand j'étais petit...*). Give an opinion with a reason in every paragraph.
6. **Build in Higher structures deliberately**, about one per two sentences, and only ones you can get right.
7. **Check with a list:** verb endings, *être* verbs and agreements, adjective agreements, accents, *de* after negatives, and that every bullet is covered.
:::

::: example Q1 translation: 15 elements across 5 sentences
**English:** I often eat fruit because it is healthy. Last week, my sister went to the gym with her friends. We must drink more water every day. Tomorrow I will go to the swimming pool with my cousin if the weather is nice. My father has been smoking for twenty years, but he would like to stop.

1. *Je mange souvent* / *des fruits* / *parce que c'est bon pour la santé.* (*souvent* goes after the verb.)
2. *La semaine dernière,* / *ma sœur est allée* / *à la salle de sport avec ses amies.* (*aller* takes *être*; *allée* agrees with *sœur*.)
3. *Nous devons* / *boire plus d'eau* / *chaque jour.* (*plus de* becomes *plus d'* before a vowel.)
4. *Demain, j'irai à la piscine* / *avec mon cousin* / *s'il fait beau.* (*irai* is the listed future of *aller*.)
5. *Mon père fume* / *depuis vingt ans,* / *mais il voudrait arrêter.* (*depuis* + **present** for "has been smoking"; *voudrait*, the listed conditional.)

Fifteen elements, each carrying one idea. "Il a fumé depuis vingt ans" loses the element and counts against grid two.
:::

::: example Q2 model answer, about 100 words
**Task 2.1:** Your French friend asks about your school. Write approximately 90 words in French. You must write something about: your school; what you did at school last week; your plans for next year.

*Mon collège se trouve près de chez moi, donc j'y vais à pied. Il est assez grand et les profs sont sympas, mais on a trop de devoirs. Ma matière préférée est l'histoire, parce que c'est passionnant. La semaine dernière, nous avons fait une sortie scolaire au musée. C'était génial, car j'ai appris beaucoup de choses sur la guerre. Après avoir visité le musée, nous avons mangé dans un parc. L'année prochaine, j'irai au lycée, où j'étudierai les langues. Je voudrais devenir professeur, parce que j'aime aider les autres.*

Three bullets covered; present, past and future; reasons; *y*, *après avoir*, *où*, *j'irai*, *j'étudierai*, *je voudrais*. Content 10 and language 5.
:::

::: example Q3 model answer, about 170 words
**Task 3.1:** You are writing an article about the environment where you live. Write approximately 150 words in French. You must write something about: what you do to protect the environment; how your town could be improved in the future.

*J'habite dans une petite ville au nord de l'Angleterre. À mon avis, protéger l'environnement est le problème le plus important aujourd'hui, car la planète est en danger.*

*Chez nous, on fait attention. Je vais au collège à vélo au lieu de prendre la voiture, et nous recyclons le papier et le plastique. Le mois dernier, j'ai aidé une association à nettoyer le parc. En travaillant ensemble, nous avons rempli vingt sacs de déchets ! C'était difficile, mais je suis fier de nos efforts.*

*À l'avenir, ma ville sera plus verte, j'espère. Il y a trop de voitures dans le centre, donc il vaut mieux encourager les gens à prendre le bus. Si c'était possible, je fermerais le centre-ville aux voitures le dimanche et je créerais un grand jardin pour les habitants. Ce serait le meilleur moyen de protéger la nature, et tout le monde serait plus heureux.*

Both bullets developed; three time frames; superlatives, *en* + *-ant*, *au lieu de*, *il vaut mieux*, conditional of *-er* verbs, *serait*.
:::

::: example Upgrading a flat sentence
- **Flat:** *J'aime le sport. C'est bien.*
- **Developed:** *J'aime le sport parce que c'est bon pour la santé.*
- **Grade 9:** *Je joue au foot depuis cinq ans, et l'année dernière, mon équipe a gagné un match très important. Après avoir joué, je suis toujours fatigué mais content. L'été prochain, j'irai en France pour jouer contre une équipe française.*

The last version adds *depuis* + present, a perfect tense, *après avoir*, an opinion and a future. That is the jump from band 3 to band 5 on range.
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher Writing paper, and how are the 50 marks split?
---
1 hour 15 minutes: Q1 translation 10, Q2 (90 words) 15, Q3 (150 words) 25.
:::

::: q
How many elements are in the Q1 translation, and how many ticks earn 5 on grid one?
---
15 elements; 13 to 15 ticks earn 5 marks.
:::

::: q
How many bullet points are there in Q2 and in Q3?
---
Three in Q2, two in Q3; all are compulsory, but coverage need not be equal.
:::

::: q
What is the most you can score for content in Q3 if you only cover one bullet?
---
12 out of 15.
:::

::: q
Which time-frame requirement is in the top language band of Q2?
---
References to all three time frames (past, present, future) which are mainly successful.
:::

::: q
Translate: *My brother has been living in Paris for two years.*
---
*Mon frère habite à Paris depuis deux ans.* (Depuis + present tense.)
:::

::: q
Translate: *Yesterday, my friends (f) arrived late.*
---
*Hier, mes amies sont arrivées en retard.* (Être verb, feminine plural agreement.)
:::

::: q
Does writing 250 words for Q3 get you a better mark?
---
Not by itself: all of it is marked, but more words usually means more errors. The descriptors decide the mark, so aim for about 170 well-controlled words.
:::

::: watch
- **Missing bullets**: an uncovered bullet costs content marks, and in Q3 caps you at 12 out of 15.
- **Only one time frame in Q2**: the top language band needs past, present and future.
- **Q1 small words dropped**: *often, very, also, every, more* are often whole elements.
- **Depuis with a past tense** instead of the present: *j'habite ici depuis cinq ans*.
- **Être verbs and agreement**: *elle est allée*, *nous sommes partis*.
- **Future and conditional of irregulars** outside the listed ones: use *aller* + infinitive if unsure.
- **English word order**: *souvent* after the verb, most adjectives after the noun.
- **De after negatives and quantities**: *je n'ai pas de frère*, *beaucoup de devoirs*.
- **Pre-learnt paragraphs that ignore the bullet** convey no relevant information.
:::

Je joue souvent (1); au foot / au football (1); avec mes amis / amies (1).

L'année dernière (1); nous sommes allés / on est allés (1); en France en train (1).

::: test
Translate into French: *My sister has been learning Spanish for three years, but she finds it difficult.* (4)
---
Ma sœur apprend l'espagnol (1); depuis trois ans (1); mais elle trouve ça / le trouve (1); difficile (1).
:::

::: test
Write a sentence in French using *après avoir* + a past participle about your weekend. (2)
---
Correct structure *après avoir* + past participle (1); accurate main clause in the past (1). For example: *Après avoir fini mes devoirs, je suis allé(e) en ville.*
:::

::: test
Write a sentence in French about your future plans using the conditional of a regular -er verb and a reason. (2)
---
Correct -er conditional, such as *j'aimerais / je travaillerais / j'habiterais* (1); a reason with *parce que* or *car* (1). For example: *J'aimerais travailler en France parce que j'adore la langue.*
:::

::: test
Fill the gaps with the correct form: (a) *Demain, je (aller) ___ au cinéma.* (future) (b) *Quand j'étais petit, je (jouer) ___ au parc.* (imperfect) (c) *Elles (partir) ___ hier.* (perfect) (3)
---
(a) irai (1). (b) jouais (1). (c) sont parties (1).
:::

::: test
A student's Q2 answer covers all three bullets but is entirely in the present tense. Which band description for language does it miss, and how should it be fixed? (2)
---
It misses the top-band requirement of references to all three time frames, mainly successful (1); add past and future sentences, such as a perfect tense for last week and a future or aller + infinitive for plans (1).
:::

::: test
Write two sentences in French for the Q3 bullet "how your town could be improved in the future", using one conditional verb and *il vaut mieux*. (3)
---
A relevant idea (1); a correct conditional, such as *je créerais* or *ce serait* (1); *il vaut mieux* + infinitive used correctly (1). For example: *Si c'était possible, je créerais plus de parcs. Il vaut mieux prendre le bus que la voiture.*
:::
