## What it is

The dictation is **Section B of Paper 1, worth 10 of the 50 Listening marks**. At Higher you hear short sentences totalling **at least 30 words**, and you write them down in French. Each sentence is played **three times**: first as a whole sentence, then in short sections with pauses, then as a whole sentence again. The sentences use the AQA vocabulary list, plus **two words from outside the list**. You cannot know those two words, so you must spell them from the sound alone. That is exactly what the dictation tests: whether you know how French sounds are spelt.

It is marked as a whole across all the sentences, on two grids of 5 marks each:

- **Communication of meaning (5 marks).** Top band: the meaning of the extracts is communicated very clearly throughout. A reader who knows French must be able to tell what was said.
- **Transcription and grammatical accuracy (5 marks).** Top band: words are always or nearly always transcribed correctly, with a very high level of grammatical accuracy. Accents, silent endings and agreements all count here.

A zero for meaning means a zero for accuracy too. Otherwise the two marks are independent.

The spec lists **the sound-symbol correspondences (SSCs)** you must know, with example words in Appendix 1. The same list is used for the reading aloud task in the Speaking exam, so learning it pays twice. The key idea: many French letters are written but **not pronounced** (silent final consonants, silent -e, the plural -ent of verbs), and several different spellings make **the same sound** (é, -er, -ez all sound like "ay"). Your ear gives you the sound; **grammar** tells you which spelling is right. That is why the dictation has a grammar mark.

A grade 9 dictation has every silent ending in place (*ils parlent*, *elles sont arrivées*, *les petites maisons*), the right choice of homophone (*a/à, et/est, son/sont, ces/ses/c'est*), and sensible, rule-based spellings for the two unknown words.

::: terms
- **silent final consonant**: most final consonants are not pronounced (sommes, effort, petit, grand, trop); c, r, f and l are often exceptions (avec, sur, neuf, il)
- **silent final e**: a final -e is not pronounced but makes the consonant before it heard (jeune, chaque; petit is 'p'tee', petite is 'p'teet')
- **é, -er, -ez**: all make the same 'ay' sound (moitié, parler, vous parlez); grammar decides the spelling
- **nasal vowels**: vowel + n or m said through the nose: en/an/em/am (grand, immédiatement), on/om (tomber, compétence), ain/in/aim/im (matin, faim), un (lundi)
- **liaison**: a normally silent final consonant is pronounced before a word starting with a vowel or silent h: s and x sound like z (les amis, deux enfants), t like t (petit ami), n like n (mon ami)
- **è / ê / ai**: the open 'eh' sound (collège, fête, vrai)
- **oi / oy**: 'wa' (mois, moyen)
- **ou versus u**: ou is 'oo' (sous, toujours); u is 'ee' with rounded lips (dur, connu)
- **ch, j, qu**: ch is 'sh' (chose), j is the 's' in 'measure' (jeunesse), qu is 'k' (banque)
- **ç and soft c**: c before e or i, and ç before a, o or u, sound like s (français, façon)
- **-s- between vowels**: sounds like z (maison, cuisine); ss is a hard s (poisson)
- **-ill- / -ille**: usually 'y' (famille, brouillard); ville, mille and tranquille keep an 'l' sound
- **-gn-**: 'ny' as in onion (campagne, gagner)
- **th and h**: th is plain t (thème, bibliothèque); h is never pronounced (homme, hôpital)
- **homophones**: words that sound the same but are spelt differently: a/à, et/est, son/sont, on/ont, ces/ses/c'est/s'est
:::

::: method
1. **First hearing: meaning only.** Listen for the sense and the grammar: who is the subject (one person or several)? Which tense? Write nothing, or just a skeleton of key words.
2. **Second hearing, in sections: write.** Write each section as you hear it. Spell each sound with its most common spelling.
3. **Fix the homophones by grammar.** Is it *a* (has, can be replaced by *avait*) or *à* (to/at)? *Et* (and) or *est* (is, replace with *était*)? *Son* (his/her) or *sont* (are)? *Ces* (these), *ses* (his/her plural), *c'est* (it is) or *s'est* (reflexive past)?
4. **Fix the 'ay' endings.** After a preposition or a second verb, write the infinitive **-er** (*je vais manger*). After *avoir* or *être*, write the past participle **-é** (*j'ai mangé*). With *vous*, write **-ez**. Test it: swap in *vendre*. If *vendre* fits, write -er; if *vendu* fits, write -é.
5. **Add the silent endings.** Plural nouns and adjectives take -s (*des petits chats*). Verbs with *ils/elles* take -ent. Feminine adjectives and participles take -e, and past participles with *être* agree (*elles sont parties*).
6. **Check liaisons.** A 'z' sound between two words is usually the silent s or x of the first word (*les amis*, *deux ans*). Do not write an extra letter.
7. **Third hearing: check.** Read along with the whole sentence. Check accents, especially *é, è, à, ç*.
8. **Unknown words:** build them sound by sound from the SSC list. A sensible spelling still communicates the meaning.
:::

::: example A full sentence, sound by sound
**Heard** (English-letter approximation): *may grahn-pah-RAHN ah-BEET dahn-zuhn p'tee vee-LAHZH ah lah kahm-PAN-yuh*

1. *may*: possessive before a plural noun, so **mes**, not *mais* (but) or *mets*.
2. *grahn-pah-RAHN*: nasal 'ahn' written **an** or **en**. The vocabulary word is **grands-parents**: silent d, both parts plural with a silent s.
3. *ah-BEET*: silent h, and the subject is plural, so the ending is the silent **-ent**: **habitent**.
4. *dahn-zuhn*: the z is the s-liaison of **dans** before **un**. Write *dans un*, never *danzun*.
5. *p'tee vee-LAHZH*: **petit village**. There is no liaison because *village* starts with a consonant. The final t of *petit* is silent.
6. *ah*: preposition "in/to", so **à** with an accent (you cannot replace it with *avait*).
7. *kahm-PAN-yuh*: 'ny' is **-gn-**, and there is a silent final e: **campagne**.

**Written:** *Mes grands-parents habitent dans un petit village à la campagne.*
:::

::: example Choosing between é, -er and -ez
**Heard:** *ee-YAIR, noo-zah-VOHN day-see-DAY duh vee-zee-TAY luh mew-ZAY, meh voo-zah-VAY pray-fay-RAY res-TAY ah lo-TEL*

- *day-see-DAY* after *avons* (avoir): past participle **décidé**. Test: *nous avons vendu*, so -é.
- *vee-zee-TAY* after *de*: infinitive **visiter**. Test: *de vendre*, so -er.
- *mew-ZAY*: a noun, spelt **musée** (a masculine noun ending in -ée; learn it).
- *voo-zah-VAY*: *vous* takes -ez: **vous avez**. The z sound is the liaison of *vous*.
- *pray-fay-RAY*: after *avez*, a past participle: **préféré**.
- *res-TAY*: after *préféré*, a second verb, so an infinitive: **rester**.

**Written:** *Hier, nous avons décidé de visiter le musée, mais vous avez préféré rester à l'hôtel.*
:::

::: example Liaison tells you the grammar
**Heard:** *eel-ZOHN* versus *eel-SOHN*

- *eel-ZOHN*: the z is the liaison of *ils* before a vowel, so this is **ils ont** (they have).
- *eel-SOHN*: a hard s, no liaison, so this is **ils sont** (they are).

So *eel-ZOHN uhn SHYEN* is **Ils ont un chien** (they have a dog), and *eel-SOHN tah-ree-VAY* is **Ils sont arrivés** (they arrived). With *être*, the past participle agrees: a plural subject adds a silent **-s**. The t-liaison in *sont arrivés* is the silent t of *sont*, not an extra letter.
:::

::: example Spelling a word from outside the list
**Heard:** *lah see-TROO-yuh* (the meaning is "pumpkin", a word not on the list)

- *see*: an 's' sound before i. It could be *si* or *ci*, because c before i is soft. Both are rule-based guesses.
- *TROO*: the 'oo' sound is spelt **ou**.
- *yuh* at the end after a vowel: **-ille**.

A student who writes *citrouille* is fully correct. *Sitrouille* follows the rules too and still communicates the word. *Citrooyuh* follows English rules and does not.
:::

::: q
How many times is each dictation sentence played, and how?
---
Three times: once as a whole sentence, once in short sections with pauses, then again as a whole sentence.
:::

::: q
How many words from outside the vocabulary list are in the Higher dictation, and what is the minimum length?
---
Two words from outside the list, in a dictation of at least 30 words.
:::

::: q
Which is correct: *il a* or *il à* in "il ___ quinze ans"?
---
*Il a*: it is the verb avoir (you can replace it with *avait*).
:::

::: q
Complete: *Nous allons regard___ un film* and *Nous avons regard___ un film*.
---
*Regarder* after *allons* (infinitive); *regardé* after *avons* (past participle).
:::

::: q
Why do you write *parlent* in *elles parlent* when you only hear 'parl'?
---
The subject is plural, and the third person plural ending -ent is silent.
:::

::: q
What sound does *-gn-* make, and which listed word from Appendix 1 shows it?
---
'ny' as in onion; *campagne* or *gagner*.
:::

::: q
In *mes amis*, you hear a z between the two words. What letter produces it?
---
The silent final s of *mes*, sounded as a z in liaison before a vowel.
:::

::: q
How is *th* pronounced in *thème* and *bibliothèque*?
---
As a plain t: the h is silent.
:::

::: watch
- **Missing silent endings**: -s on plural nouns and adjectives, -ent on *ils/elles* verbs, -e on feminine forms. These cost the accuracy mark more than anything else.
- **é versus -er**: after *avoir* or *être* write -é; after a preposition or a second verb write -er. Use the *vendre/vendu* test.
- **Homophones**: *a/à, et/est, son/sont, ou/où, ces/ses/c'est*. Decide by grammar, not by ear.
- **Liaison written as a letter**: *les amis*, never *les zamis*.
- **Ils ont / ils sont**: a z sound means *ont*, a hard s means *sont*.
- **Past participles with être** agree with the subject: *elle est partie*, *ils sont allés*.
- **Accents missing**: *à, é, è, ç* are part of the spelling.
- **Unknown words**: spell them with French rules, not English ones.
:::

::: test
A girl is talking about her sister. You hear: *el-AH kah-TORZ AHN*. Write the sentence in French. (3)
---
Elle a (1); quatorze (1); ans, with the silent -s (1). Do not accept *à* for *a*.
:::

::: test
You hear: *ee-YAIR SWAHR, zhay mahn-ZHAY dew poo-LAY*. Write the sentence in French. (3)
---
Hier soir (1); j'ai mangé, with -é after avoir (1); du poulet (1).
:::

::: test
Talking about her two female friends, the speaker says: *may-zah-MEE sohn trah-vah-YUHZ*. Write the sentence, making every agreement. (4)
---
Mes, not *mais* (1); amies, with feminine plural -es (1); sont (1); travailleuses, with feminine plural -euses (1).
:::

::: test
You hear: *voo-zah-LAY PRAHN-druh luh TRAN*. Write the sentence in French. (3)
---
Vous allez, with -ez and no extra z letter (1); prendre (1); le train (1).
:::

::: test
Complete each word with the right ending (-é, -ée, -er or -ez): (a) *Demain, je vais visit___ Paris.* (b) *Ma mère est arriv___ hier.* (c) *Vous parl___ français ?* (3)
---
(a) visiter: infinitive after vais (1). (b) arrivée: past participle with être, agreeing with a feminine subject (1). (c) parlez: vous form (1).
:::

::: test
You hear two short sentences: (a) *eel-ZOHN uhn SHAH* and (b) *eel-SOHN kohn-TAHN*. Write both in French. (4)
---
(a) Ils ont (1) un chat (1). (b) Ils sont (1) contents, with the plural -s (1). Accept elles ont / elles sont.
:::

::: test
A word from outside the list is heard: *lah gruh-NOO-yuh* ("frog"). Using the sound-symbol rules, write it and name the rule behind two parts of your spelling. (3)
---
grenouille (1); 'oo' spelt ou (1); 'y' after a vowel at the end spelt -ille (1). Accept any spelling that follows French rules for the first mark, such as *greunouille*.
:::

::: test
You hear: *NO-truh pay-EE ay TRAY BO*. Write the sentence, and explain why *beau* has no final consonant sound. (3)
---
Notre pays (1); est très beau (1); the 'oh' sound at the end is spelt -eau, and there is no consonant to pronounce (1).
:::
