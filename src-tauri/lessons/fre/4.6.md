## What it is

Paper 3 is the Reading exam: **1 hour at Higher, 50 marks, 25% of the GCSE**. No dictionaries.

- **Section A (40 marks)**: French texts with **questions in English**, answered in English or by ticking, circling or writing letters. The texts include public notices, adverts, brochures, newspaper and magazine articles, emails, websites and blogs, across all three themes. The questions ask for the **overall message, key points, details and opinions**. At Higher you must also **infer**, and **work out the meaning of a single unknown word** from its context and from the grammar you know.
- **Section B (10 marks)**: a **translation from French into English** of five or so sentences, **at least 50 words** at Higher. "Translation" means an appropriate and sufficient rendering of the meaning, so natural English wins over word-for-word English.

**Words you are not expected to know.** A Higher text may contain a small number of words outside the AQA list. Their English meanings are **glossed** next to the text, and no more than **2% of the words** in a text can be glossed this way (one word in a 50-word text). A further 2% can be **cognates** that are not on the list (*la catastrophe*, *le zoo*). Proper nouns may be explained in a note. You are also expected to recognise **derived words** in reading (spec 3.2.1.6 and 3.2.2.6):
- adverbs in *-ment* (*rapide* gives *rapidement*);
- nouns in *-ion/-ation* (*préparer* gives *préparation*);
- adjectives in *-able* (*porter* gives *portable*);
- opposites with *in-/im-* (*possible* gives *impossible*);
- ordinals in *-ième*;
- at Higher, people nouns in *-eur/-ateur* (*consommer* gives *consommateur*).

Section A **question types**:
- multiple choice;
- **which person** (letters A to D, sometimes used more than once);
- **true / false / not mentioned**;
- **positive / negative / positive and negative** opinions;
- short answers in English;
- **inference** questions, such as "what does this suggest about...", "which word best describes..." or "what does the underlined word probably mean?".

The traps are the same as in Listening: negatives (*ne...plus, ne...que, ne...jamais*), time frames, changes of mind after *mais* or *par contre*, and **false friends**. A grade 9 reader reads the whole sentence, and the one after it, before deciding. They answer in precise English and give exactly the number of details asked for.

The **translation** mark scheme divides the passage into **short sections, worth a mark each**, and lists the English that is accepted and rejected for each. A section scores if its meaning is fully conveyed: the right tense, every word accounted for, negatives and time words included. English spelling slips do not matter if the meaning is clear. **Missing out a word, getting a tense wrong or falling for a false friend** loses that section.

::: terms
- **Section A**: 40 marks of comprehension questions in English on French texts; answers in English or non-verbal
- **Section B**: the translation into English, at least 50 words at Higher, 10 marks
- **gloss**: an English meaning printed next to the text for a word outside the list; at most 2% of the words in a text
- **cognate**: a word spelt almost the same with the same meaning in both languages (la nature, la pollution, important)
- **false friend**: a word that looks English but means something else: journée (day, not journey), actuellement (at the moment), car (because)
- **inference**: working out something the text implies but does not state, from clues such as tone, adjectives or consequences
- **derivation**: building an unknown word from a known one: chanteur (singer) from chanter; rapidement (quickly) from rapide
- **not mentioned**: the text neither confirms nor contradicts the statement; do not guess from what is likely
- **section (element)**: one chunk of the translation passage, worth one mark, which must be fully and accurately conveyed
- **sensible**: false friend meaning sensitive, not sensible; il est très sensible = he is very sensitive
- **assister à**: to attend (j'ai assisté à un concert = I attended a concert), not to assist
- **passer un examen**: to take or sit an exam, not to pass one
- **le collège**: secondary school for 11 to 15-year-olds, not a college; le lycée is the school for 15 to 18-year-olds
- **il y a + time**: ago (il y a deux ans = two years ago), as well as there is / there are
:::

::: method
1. **Read the title, the English question and any glosses first.** They tell you the topic and the type of answer (a person, a reason, a time).
2. **Find the right place in the text.** Questions usually follow the order of the text.
3. **Read the whole sentence and the next one.** Check for negatives, time words, *mais* and *par contre*.
4. **For unknown words**, look for a gloss, a cognate, a derivation (*-ment, -ion, -able, in-, -eur*) and the context: what kind of word fits the sentence?
5. **For inference**, find the evidence first (an adjective, a consequence, a reaction), then name the feeling or attitude it shows.
6. **Answer in precise English**, with the number of details asked. Never copy French into an English answer.
7. **Translation**: write a full draft sentence by sentence. Then tick every French word against your English, check each verb's tense and person, and rewrite anything clumsy into natural English.
:::

::: example Which person? Reading for the whole sentence
*Léo : Je ne fais plus de sport depuis mon accident, mais je vais à la piscine pour aider mon père, qui est professeur de natation.*
*Amira : Je n'ai jamais aimé le sport. Je préfère lire ou dessiner.*
*Nathan : Je joue au foot trois fois par semaine, et je viens de gagner un match important.*

**Who used to do sport but no longer does?** Léo: *ne...plus* (no longer), with *depuis mon accident* as the reason. Amira has never liked it (*ne...jamais*), so she is the distractor: she did not stop, she never started.

**Who has recently had a success?** Nathan: *je viens de gagner* (I have just won).
:::

::: example Working out an unknown word
*Les consommateurs achètent de plus en plus de produits locaux au marché.*

**What does *consommateurs* mean?** It is built on *consommer* (to consume) plus *-ateur*, a person who does the action. So it means **consumers, or customers**. The context agrees: they buy (*achètent*) products at a market.

*Le chanteur est arrivé rapidement sur la scène.* Here *chanteur* is a singer (from *chanter*) and *rapidement* is quickly (from *rapide*).
:::

::: example Inference from a story
*Chloé attendait devant la fenêtre. Personne n'appelait. Chaque fois qu'une voiture passait dans la rue, elle courait à la porte, puis elle revenait, les yeux pleins de larmes.*
Glossed: *larmes* = tears.

**How is Chloé feeling? Give two pieces of evidence.** She is anxious or disappointed while she waits for someone. Evidence: she is waiting at the window; nobody calls (*personne n'appelait*); she runs to the door every time a car passes; her eyes are full of tears. Any two.
:::

::: example A full translation, section by section
*Dans mon quartier, il y a beaucoup de voitures et l'air est sale. Depuis deux ans, je vais au collège à vélo. Hier, nous avons nettoyé le parc avec une association. Il faut protéger la planète, car nous n'avons qu'une Terre. À l'avenir, j'aimerais habiter à la campagne, où c'est plus tranquille.*

| French | Accepted English | Traps |
|---|---|---|
| Dans mon quartier, il y a beaucoup de voitures | In my area / neighbourhood there are lots of cars | quartier is not quarter |
| et l'air est sale | and the air is dirty / polluted | |
| Depuis deux ans, je vais au collège à vélo | For two years I have been going / have gone to school by bike | depuis + present: not "I go since two years" |
| Hier, nous avons nettoyé le parc | Yesterday we cleaned (have cleaned) the park | perfect tense |
| avec une association | with an organisation / association / a charity | |
| Il faut protéger la planète | We must / You have to protect the planet | il faut is not "it needs" |
| car nous n'avons qu'une Terre | because / as we only have one Earth | car = because, not car; ne...que = only |
| À l'avenir, j'aimerais | In the future I would like | conditional: not "I will like" |
| habiter à la campagne | to live in the countryside | |
| où c'est plus tranquille | where it is quieter / calmer | comparative: not "quiet" |
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher Reading paper, and how is it split?
---
1 hour, 50 marks: Section A 40 marks of questions in English, Section B a 10-mark translation into English of at least 50 words.
:::

::: q
What is the maximum share of words in a text that can be glossed?
---
2% of the words (rounded to the nearest whole word); a further 2% can be unlisted cognates.
:::

::: q
What does *car* mean in *Je reste à la maison car il pleut*?
---
Because: I am staying at home because it is raining.
:::

::: q
Translate *J'apprends le français depuis quatre ans.*
---
I have been learning French for four years.
:::

::: q
Translate *Je ne bois que de l'eau.*
---
I only drink water.
:::

::: q
Work out the meaning of *impatiemment* in *Elle attendait impatiemment ses résultats.*
---
Impatiently: *im-* (opposite) + *patient* + *-emment* (-ly). She was waiting impatiently for her results.
:::

::: q
Translate *Il y a trois ans, j'ai passé une journée à Paris.*
---
Three years ago, I spent a day in Paris. (*Journée* is a day, not a journey.)
:::

::: q
What is the difference between *je mangeais* and *j'ai mangé* in a translation?
---
*Je mangeais* is imperfect: I used to eat / I was eating. *J'ai mangé* is perfect: I ate / I have eaten.
:::

::: watch
- **Negatives ignored**: *ne...plus* (no longer), *ne...jamais* (never) and *ne...que* (only) change the meaning completely.
- **False friends**: *journée, actuellement, car, sensible, assister à, attendre* (to wait), *rester* (to stay), *passer un examen* (to sit).
- **Tenses flattened**: perfect, imperfect, future and conditional must come out as different English tenses.
- **Depuis with the present** means "have been ...ing for".
- **Words left out of the translation**: every word needs a home, especially small ones (*très, aussi, déjà, toujours, encore*).
- **Word-for-word English**: "I have 15 years" for *j'ai 15 ans* loses the section. Write "I am 15".
- **Not mentioned answered from common sense.**
- **French copied into English answers** scores nothing.
:::

::: test
Read: *Actuellement, Sophie travaille dans une banque, mais elle voudrait devenir médecin, car elle aime aider les gens.* (a) What is Sophie's job at present? (b) What would she like to become? (c) Why? (3)
---
(a) She works in a bank (1). (b) A doctor (1). (c) She likes helping people (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *Hier soir, nous avons regardé un film, mais il était trop long.* (3)
---
Yesterday evening / Last night (1); we watched (have watched) a film (1); but it was too long (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *Depuis septembre, ma sœur ne mange plus de viande.* (3)
---
Since September (1); my sister (1); has not eaten / no longer eats / doesn't eat meat any more (1).
:::

::: test
Read: *Marc : J'adore mon collège, les profs sont sympas. Par contre, les journées sont longues et on a trop de devoirs.* Is Marc's opinion of school positive, negative, or positive and negative? Give one detail for each side. (3)
---
Positive and negative (1); positive: he loves it / the teachers are nice (1); negative: the days are long / too much homework (1).
:::

::: test
Using what you know about word building, give the English for: (a) *lentement* (b) *la préparation* (c) *inutile* (3)
---
(a) Slowly: lent(e) + -ment (1). (b) (The) preparation: préparer + -ation (1). (c) Useless: in- + utile (1).
:::

::: test
Read: *Avant, j'habitais en ville, mais il y avait trop de bruit. Maintenant, j'habite à la campagne et je dors mieux.* True, false or not mentioned? (a) The writer lives in a town now. (b) The writer sleeps better now. (c) The writer misses the town. (3)
---
(a) False (1). (b) True (1). (c) Not mentioned (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *À l'avenir, je voudrais travailler à l'étranger, où je parlerai d'autres langues.* (4)
---
In the future (1); I would like to work abroad (1); where I will speak (1); other languages (1).
:::

::: test
Read: *Thomas a ouvert la lettre, a lu la première ligne, puis il a crié et il a commencé à danser dans la cuisine.* What can you infer about the letter? Give one piece of evidence. (2)
---
It contained good news (1); he shouted and started dancing in the kitchen after reading the first line (1).
:::
