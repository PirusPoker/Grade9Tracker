## What it is

The present tense is the tense you use most, and the AQA specification builds the whole verb system on it. You need **regular -er verbs** (parler), **seven "anchor" verbs** whose patterns cover most other common verbs (**choisir, partir, venir, ouvrir, entendre, prendre, traduire**), and the four big irregulars **aller, avoir, être, faire**, all in every person. On top of that come nine verbs you need in the **singular** only at Foundation (**boire, connaître, courir, croire, écrire, recevoir, rire, suivre, voir**), four negatives (**ne … pas, ne … jamais, ne … rien, ne … personne**) and three ways of asking a question.

One French present covers three English ideas: **je joue** = I play, I am playing, and (with a time word) I am going to play: **je travaille ce soir** (I'm working this evening), **les vacances commencent demain** (the holidays start tomorrow). There is no separate "-ing" form: *je suis jouer* is a classic error.

In the exam, the present tense is the backbone of the **Speaking** conversation and the photo card (what is happening in the picture: **les jeunes jouent au foot**), the **role-play** (you must ask a question), and the **Writing** tasks, whose bullet points almost always include one on the present. In **Listening** and **Reading**, negatives are where marks hide: **je ne mange jamais de viande** and **je ne connais personne** reverse the meaning of the sentence, and the negative words are short and easy to miss.

A grade 9 answer uses a range of verbs from different clusters accurately, including the plural forms that trip people up (**ils prennent, ils viennent, ils font, ils vont**), uses **avoir** expressions where English uses "to be" (**j'ai faim, j'ai quinze ans**), varies its negatives and asks questions with inversion (**Que fais-tu le week-end ?**). At **Higher**, you also need the full present of the **connaître** and **écrire** clusters (**nous connaissons, ils écrivent**).

::: terms
- **le présent**: the present tense; je joue = I play, I am playing, or I'm playing (tomorrow) with a time word
- **le radical (stem)**: the part you add endings to: parler → parl- → nous parlons
- **le verbe modèle (anchor verb)**: a verb whose pattern many others copy: prendre → apprendre, comprendre
- **les verbes en -er**: the biggest group: -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent: je parle, ils parlent
- **avoir + nom**: avoir faim, soif, froid, chaud, peur, quinze ans, where English uses "to be": j'ai froid = I am cold
- **le changement orthographique**: small spelling changes that keep the sound: nous mangeons, nous commençons, je jette, je mène
- **ne … pas / jamais / rien / personne**: not / never / nothing, not anything / nobody, not anyone; ne and the second word go round the verb: je ne fais rien
- **l'intonation**: a question made by raising your voice at the end: Tu aimes le sport ?
- **est-ce que**: a question starter that keeps normal word order: Est-ce que tu as un animal ?
- **l'inversion**: verb before subject, joined by a hyphen: Aimes-tu le sport ? Où habites-tu ?
:::

::: method
1. **Find the infinitive** and decide its family: -er, one of the seven anchors, or an irregular.
2. **Take the stem**: for -er verbs drop -er (**parl-**); for anchors use the stems in the table (note the extra -ss- of **choisissons** and the stem changes of **viennent, prennent**).
3. **Add the ending for the subject.** Remember **on** takes the il/elle form: **on va**.
4. **Check spelling changes**: nous mang**e**ons, nous commen**ç**ons, je m**è**ne, je je**tt**e, j'ach**è**te.
5. **Negative?** Put **ne** (n' before a vowel) in front of the verb and **pas / jamais / rien / personne** after it.
6. **Question?** Choose intonation (**Tu pars quand ?**), est-ce que (**Quand est-ce que tu pars ?**) or inversion (**Quand pars-tu ?**).
:::

| tu | parles | choisis | pars | viens | ouvres | entends | prends | traduis |
| il/elle/on | parle | choisit | part | vient | ouvre | entend | prend | traduit |
| nous | parlons | choisissons | partons | venons | ouvrons | entendons | prenons | traduisons |

Verbs that copy each anchor: **choisir**: finir, réussir, remplir. **partir**: sortir, dormir, servir. **venir**: devenir, revenir, tenir, obtenir. **ouvrir**: découvrir, souffrir (and offrir, Higher). **entendre**: attendre, vendre, répondre, perdre, descendre, rendre. **prendre**: apprendre, comprendre. **traduire**: conduire, construire, détruire.

| | aller | avoir | être | faire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| je/j' | vais | ai | suis | fais |
| tu | vas | as | es | fais |
| il/elle/on | va | a | est | fait |
| nous | allons | avons | sommes | faisons |
| vous | allez | avez | êtes | faites |

Singular only at Foundation: **boire** (bois, bois, boit), **connaître** (connais, connais, connaît), **courir** (cours, cours, court), **croire** (crois, crois, croit), **écrire** (écris, écris, écrit), **recevoir** (reçois, reçois, reçoit), **rire** (ris, ris, rit), **suivre** (suis, suis, suit), **voir** (vois, vois, voit). **Higher** adds the plurals of the connaître and écrire clusters: nous connaissons, vous connaissez, ils connaissent (also reconnaître, disparaître); nous écrivons, vous écrivez, ils écrivent (also décrire). Other irregular verbs, such as **mettre, dire, lire, devoir**, are learned from the vocabulary list.

::: example Building a sentence with a plural verb and a negative
**English:** My friends never take the bus; they come to school by bike.

1. "My friends" = **mes amis**, so the verb is in the **ils** form.
2. "take" = **prendre** → **ils prennent** (double n in the ils form).
3. "never" = **ne … jamais** round the verb: **ne prennent jamais**.
4. "they come" = **venir** → **ils viennent**.
5. "to school by bike" = **au collège à vélo**.

**French:** **Mes amis ne prennent jamais le bus ; ils viennent au collège à vélo.**
:::

::: example Three ways to ask the same question
**What do you do at the weekend?**
- Intonation: **Tu fais quoi le week-end ?** (question word at the end, used in speech)
- Est-ce que: **Qu'est-ce que tu fais le week-end ?**
- Inversion: **Que fais-tu le week-end ?**

**Where do you live?** **Tu habites où ?** / **Où est-ce que tu habites ?** / **Où habites-tu ?**

In inversion with **il/elle/on** and a verb ending in a vowel, add **-t-** for the sound: **Où va-t-il ?** In the role-play, any of the three forms scores, as long as the question is clear. Inversion shows range in the conversation.
:::

::: example Present tense for the future and for "being"
- **Ce soir, je regarde un film avec mon frère.** This evening I'm watching a film with my brother.
- **Le train part à huit heures demain.** The train leaves at eight tomorrow.
- **J'ai faim et j'ai froid !** I'm hungry and I'm cold! (avoir, not être)
- **Ma sœur a douze ans.** My sister is twelve. (never *est douze*)
- **Je ne vois personne et je n'entends rien.** I can't see anyone and I can't hear anything.
:::

::: q
Give the correct present tense form: (a) nous (finir) (b) ils (prendre) (c) elle (venir) (d) vous (faire) (e) ils (aller) (f) je (attendre)
---
(a) finissons; (b) prennent; (c) vient; (d) faites; (e) vont; (f) j'attends.
:::

::: q
Fill the gap with the right form of avoir or être: (a) Mon frère ___ quinze ans. (b) Nous ___ faim. (c) Tu ___ fatigué ? (d) J'___ peur des chiens.
---
(a) a; (b) avons; (c) es; (d) ai.
:::

::: watch
- There is no "am -ing" in French: **je joue** = I am playing. *Je suis jouer* scores nothing.
- The ils forms are where accuracy marks go: **prennent, viennent, font, vont, sont, ont, choisissent**. Learn them.
- **avoir** for age, hunger, thirst, cold, heat and fear: **j'ai quinze ans**, not *je suis quinze*.
- **ne** goes before the verb and the negative word after it. With a following noun, du/de la/des become **de**: **je ne bois jamais de café**.
- **ne** becomes **n'** before a vowel: **je n'aime pas**.
- **on** takes the il form: **on va, on fait, on prend**.
- Keep the sound: **nous mangeons, nous commençons**.
- In the role-play, when told to ask a question, a statement with rising intonation is fine in speech, but inversion or est-ce que is clearer.
:::
