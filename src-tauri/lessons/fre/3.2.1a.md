## What it is

Every French noun is **masculine or feminine**, and the small word in front of it, the **article**, has to match the noun's gender and number. English gets away with "the" and "a" for everything; French needs **le, la, l', les**, **un, une, des**, and the "some/any" words **du, de la, de l', des**. This lesson covers all of that, plus how person nouns change for a woman (**un acteur, une actrice**), how nouns go plural (**un gâteau, des gâteaux**), and the rule that turns every "some" or "a" into plain **de** after a negative or a quantity (**je n'ai pas de frère**, **beaucoup de devoirs**).

It turns up in every paper. In the **Listening dictation** (10 marks) you have to hear the difference between **le** and **les**, or **du** and **des**, and spell it. In **Reading**, **pas de** is often the only sign that something is missing, and a multiple-choice question will test whether you saw it. In the **Writing translation into French** (10 marks), articles are the commonest small error: "Health is important" is **La santé est importante**, with an article English does not have. In **Speaking**, every noun you say carries one.

The core structures are: the definite article for "the" **and** for general statements and likes (**j'aime le sport**, **la musique est importante**); the definite article with days for "on …days" (**le mercredi**); no article with a job after **être** (**elle est professeur**); the partitive for an unmeasured amount (**du pain, de l'eau**) and after **faire** and **jouer** (**faire du sport, jouer de la musique**); **de/d'** after negatives and quantities; and the infinitive used as a noun where English says "-ing" (**Lire, c'est ma passion** = Reading is my passion).

A grade 9 answer gets these small words right every time, especially in the translation, where each one is a mark-bearing detail. It switches confidently between **j'aime le poisson** (general like) and **je mange du poisson** (some fish), and between **j'ai un chien** and **je n'ai pas de chien**. At **Higher**, you also use the partitive with uncountable ideas: **il faut du courage**, **avoir de la chance**, **gagner de l'argent**.

::: terms
- **le genre (gender)**: every noun is masculine or feminine; learn it with the article: le livre (m), la maison (f)
- **l'article défini**: le, la, l', les = the; also used for general statements and likes: j'aime le sport
- **l'article indéfini**: un, une = a/an; des = some (plural): un chien, une voiture, des amis
- **l'article partitif**: du, de la, de l', des = some/any, part of something: je bois de l'eau
- **l'élision**: le/la and de become l' and d' before a vowel or silent h: l'école, l'hôpital, pas d'argent
- **pas de**: after a negative, un/une/des/du/de la all become de (d'): je n'ai pas de voiture
- **l'expression de quantité**: beaucoup de, trop de, assez de, un peu de + noun with no article: trop de devoirs
- **le pluriel**: most nouns add -s; -eau and -eu add -x (des gâteaux, des jeux); -s and -x do not change (des prix)
- **le nom de personne au féminin**: add -e (une amie), no change (une élève), -eur to -rice/-euse (une actrice, une chanteuse), -en to -enne (une Européenne)
- **l'infinitif comme nom**: the infinitive used where English uses -ing: Voyager coûte cher = Travelling is expensive
- **l'adjectif comme nom**: le/la + adjective makes a noun: le seul (the only one), l'anglais (English, the language), une Anglaise (an Englishwoman)
:::

::: method
1. **Find the noun and its gender and number.** If you do not know the gender, you must have learned it with its article. Plural? Add -s, or -x after -eau/-eu.
2. **Decide what you mean.** A specific thing, or things in general, or a like/dislike: **le, la, l', les**. One of something: **un, une**. Some or any, an amount you do not measure: **du, de la, de l', des**.
3. **Check for a negative.** After **ne … pas / jamais**, un, une, des, du, de la and de l' all become **de** (or **d'** before a vowel). Exception: after **être** the article stays: **ce n'est pas un problème**.
4. **Check for a quantity.** After **beaucoup, trop, assez, un peu, un verre** and similar, use **de/d'** with no article: **beaucoup d'amis**.
5. **Check for the other French uses.** Days for a habit: **le samedi**. A job after être: no article. A language: **le français** (small letter). A nationality as a noun: capital letter, **un Français, une Anglaise**.
6. **Elide.** le, la, de before a vowel or silent h become **l', d'**.
:::

::: example Translating a sentence about food
**English:** Health is important, so I eat fruit and vegetables every day, but I never eat meat.

1. "Health" is a general idea, so French needs the definite article: **la santé** (feminine), and **importante** agrees.
2. "I eat fruit and vegetables" means some, an unmeasured amount: **des fruits et des légumes**.
3. "every day" = **tous les jours**.
4. "I never eat meat" is a negative: **de la viande** becomes **de viande**.

**French:** **La santé est importante, donc je mange des fruits et des légumes tous les jours, mais je ne mange jamais de viande.**

Note the two different "no article in English" cases: **la santé** (general) and **des fruits** (some). Leaving either out loses the accuracy mark.
:::

::: example Likes versus amounts
**English:** I love fish, but I don't eat fish at school, because there are too many chips.

1. "I love fish" is a general like: **j'adore le poisson**.
2. "I don't eat fish" is a negative amount: **je ne mange pas de poisson**.
3. "at school" = **à l'école** (elision before a vowel).
4. "too many chips" is a quantity: **trop de frites**.

**French:** **J'adore le poisson, mais je ne mange pas de poisson à l'école, parce qu'il y a trop de frites.**

The same noun takes three different forms depending on what you mean. That is exactly what the translation question tests.
:::

::: example Where French and English differ
- **Le mercredi, je joue au foot.** = On Wednesdays I play football. (le + day = every week)
- **Mercredi, je vais au cinéma.** = On Wednesday (this one) I'm going to the cinema.
- **Ma mère est médecin.** = My mother is a doctor. (no article with a job after être)
- **J'apprends le français et l'anglais.** = I'm learning French and English. (article, small letter)
- **C'est une Française.** = She's a Frenchwoman. (capital letter for the person)
- **Lire, c'est ma passion.** / **Nager est bon pour la santé.** = Reading is my passion. / Swimming is good for your health.
- **Je fais du sport et je joue de la musique.** = I do sport and I play music.
:::

::: q
Fill each gap with le, la, l', les, un, une, du, de la, de l', des or de/d'.
(a) J'aime ___ musique. (b) Je bois ___ eau. (c) Il n'a pas ___ argent. (d) Il y a beaucoup ___ touristes. (e) Je voudrais ___ gâteau, s'il vous plaît.
---
(a) la (general like); (b) de l' (some water, vowel); (c) d' (negative, vowel); (d) de (quantity); (e) un (one cake).

::: q
Spot and correct the error in each sentence: (a) Mon père est un professeur. (b) Je n'ai pas des frères. (c) J'aime du fromage. (d) Il y a beaucoup des magasins.
---
(a) Mon père est professeur (no article with a job after être). (b) Je n'ai pas de frères (de after a negative). (c) J'aime le fromage (definite article after a verb of liking). (d) Il y a beaucoup de magasins (de after a quantity).
:::

::: q
Rewrite in the negative near future (je ne vais pas + infinitive): (a) Je mange du pain. (b) Nous achetons des légumes. (c) Elle boit de l'eau.
---
(a) Je ne vais pas manger de pain. (b) Nous n'allons pas acheter de légumes. (c) Elle ne va pas boire d'eau. The negative still turns the partitive into de/d'.
:::

Read: *Chez moi, on mange du poisson le vendredi. Mon frère ne mange pas de viande, et ma mère dit que les légumes sont bons pour la santé. Moi, je bois trop de café !*
(a) When does the family eat fish? (b) What does the brother not eat? (c) What does the mother say about vegetables? (d) What does the writer admit?
---
(a) On Fridays. (b) Meat. (c) They are good for your health. (d) They drink too much coffee.
:::

::: watch
- The translation into French rewards every article: **la santé**, **le mercredi**, **des légumes**. Missing ones are the commonest lost accuracy mark.
- **pas un / pas des / pas du** is wrong after a negative verb: it must be **pas de** (**d'** before a vowel). The one exception is **être**: **ce n'est pas un problème**.
- After **aimer, adorer, préférer, détester**, use **le/la/les**, never **du**: **j'aime le lait**, not *j'aime du lait*.
- After quantities, **de** with no article: **beaucoup de gens**, not *beaucoup des gens*.
- No article with a job after être: **il est serveur**.
- Languages are masculine and have a small letter: **le français**. People have a capital: **un Français**.
- Plural -x only after -eau and -eu (**des jeux, des gâteaux**); nouns ending in -s or -x do not change (**des prix**).
- In the dictation, **le** (one) and **les** (more than one) sound different; listen for it and make the noun plural to match.
:::
