## What it is

Paper 1 is the Listening exam: **45 minutes at Higher, 50 marks, 25% of the GCSE**. That 45 minutes includes **five minutes' reading time at the start** and **two minutes' checking time at the end**. Section A is **40 marks of comprehension questions in English**, answered in English or by ticking, circling or writing a letter. Section B is the **10-mark dictation**, which has its own lesson (4.4b). You may make notes at any time, including in the reading time. No dictionaries.

Every Section A item is **heard twice**, with pauses built into the recording for you to read the questions first and then answer. The speakers are native speakers talking clearly at no more than a moderate pace, with no background noise. The texts are announcements, short conversations, news bulletins, podcasts and descriptions, and the questions ask you to pick out the **overall message, key points, details and opinions**. Everything is built from the AQA vocabulary and grammar list, and the first part of the Higher paper overlaps with the end of the Foundation paper, so the questions get harder as you go.

The difficulty at Higher is rarely the vocabulary. It is the **distractor**: the recording mentions two or three of the options, and only one survives. The speaker says what she *used to* do and then what she does *now*; names a price and then a reduced price; mentions football and then says *je ne joue plus au foot*. The examiner wrote the question so that a student who catches one keyword ticks the wrong box. The five traps are:

1. **Negatives**: *ne...pas, ne...jamais, ne...rien, ne...personne*, and the Higher ones *ne...plus* (no longer), *ne...que* (only, which is not a negative at all), *ne...ni...ni* (neither...nor), *personne ne*, *rien ne* and *aucun*.
2. **Time words and tenses**: *avant, quand j'étais petit, d'habitude, maintenant, l'année dernière, bientôt, depuis, venir de*. The question says "now" or "last year"; the recording gives both.
3. **Changes of mind**: *mais, pourtant, par contre, cependant, finalement, en fait, sauf*.
4. **Not mentioned**: in true / false / not mentioned questions, something that sounds likely but is never said.
5. **Sound-alike pairs**: *deux/douze, trois/treize, quatre/quatorze, six/dix, cent/sans, vingt/vin*, and the 70 to 99 numbers.

What separates a grade 9 listener: they use the reading time to predict the French they will hear, they wait for the **whole sentence** before writing, and they write **precise English** that contains every detail the question asks for, no more and no less.

::: terms
- **distractor**: an option the recording mentions but which is not the answer, because it is negated, in the wrong time frame or replaced
- **reading time**: the five minutes at the start of Paper 1 to read every question and predict vocabulary before the audio begins
- **ne...plus**: no longer, not any more (je ne vais plus au club = I don't go to the club any more). Higher
- **ne...que**: only; a positive meaning (je ne bois que de l'eau = I only drink water, so she DOES drink water). Higher
- **ne...ni...ni**: neither...nor (je ne mange ni viande ni poisson = I eat neither meat nor fish). Higher
- **avant / quand j'étais petit**: before / when I was little; the signal that a habit or fact is no longer true
- **d'habitude**: usually; often followed by mais and an exception for this week or this year
- **venir de + infinitive**: to have just done something (je viens de manger = I have just eaten). Higher
- **depuis + present tense**: has been doing for (j'apprends le français depuis cinq ans = I have been learning French for five years). Higher
- **par contre / pourtant**: on the other hand / however; what comes after usually carries the answer
- **not mentioned**: the third option in true / false / not mentioned; the text neither confirms nor contradicts the statement
- **P / N / P+N**: opinion questions asking whether a view is positive, negative, or both positive and negative
- **quatre-vingt-dix**: ninety, literally four twenties and ten; numbers 70 to 99 are built by adding to soixante or quatre-vingts
:::

::: method
1. **Reading time (5 minutes).** Read every question. Underline the question word (*when, why, how much, two details*) and the time frame (*now, last year, in the future*). Note how many details each answer needs.
2. **Predict the French.** Beside each question, jot two or three French words you expect to hear: for a question about a job, *métier, travailler, salaire*; for a price, *euros, coûter, gratuit*.
3. **First listening: understand, do not write yet.** Let the whole sentence finish. The answer often comes after *mais*, *par contre* or a negative.
4. **Note in the margin**, in French or English, anything you are unsure of: numbers, times, names.
5. **Second listening: confirm.** Check each answer against the traps: negated? wrong time frame? replaced by a change of mind? really mentioned?
6. **Write precise English.** Give exactly the number of details asked for. Do not add a second, different answer as insurance: a wrong extra detail can cancel a right one, and two ticks where one is asked scores nothing.
7. **Checking time (2 minutes).** Fill every gap with your best guess. A blank always scores zero.
:::

::: example The ne...plus trap
**Recording:** *Le samedi, je ne vais plus au club de foot parce que je travaille dans un café. Avant, j'y allais tous les week-ends avec mon frère.*

**Question:** What does Inès do on Saturdays now? (1)

**Reasoning:** Two activities are mentioned. *Foot* comes first and is the obvious keyword, but *ne...plus* means "no longer", and *avant* ("before") confirms the football is in the past. The present tense *je travaille* is the current activity.

**Answer:** She works in a café. Writing "plays football" scores zero; writing "works in a café and plays football" also scores zero, because the second detail contradicts the first.
:::

::: example The time-frame trap
**Recording:** *L'année dernière, nous sommes allés en Angleterre. C'était génial, mais cette année, nous allons rester en France chez mes grands-parents, parce que c'est moins cher.*

**Question:** (a) Where is Hugo's family going this year? (b) Why? (2)

**Reasoning:** *L'année dernière* plus a perfect tense (*nous sommes allés*) places England in the past. *Cette année* plus *nous allons rester* (near future) is the answer to (a). The reason follows *parce que*: *moins cher* is a comparative, so "cheaper" or "less expensive", not simply "cheap".

**Answer:** (a) Staying in France at his grandparents' house (1). (b) It is cheaper (1).
:::

::: example Prices and the reduced price
**Recording:** *Le billet coûte quatre-vingt-quinze euros, mais pour les jeunes de moins de dix-huit ans, c'est soixante-quinze euros.*

**Question:** How much does a ticket cost for a 16-year-old? (1)

**Reasoning:** *Quatre-vingt-quinze* is 4 × 20 + 15 = 95. *Soixante-quinze* is 60 + 15 = 75. *Moins de dix-huit ans* means under 18, which includes a 16-year-old. The first price is the distractor.

**Answer:** 75 euros.
:::

::: example True, false or not mentioned
**Recording:** *Ma sœur Clara adore son collège. Elle aime surtout les maths, mais elle trouve l'histoire ennuyeuse. Elle voudrait être professeure plus tard.*

Statements: (a) Clara likes her school. (b) Clara enjoys history. (c) Clara is good at maths.

**Reasoning:** (a) *adore son collège*: **true**. (b) *trouve l'histoire ennuyeuse*: she finds it boring, so **false**. (c) She *likes* maths, but nothing is said about being good at it. Liking is not ability, so **not mentioned**. This third kind is where most marks are lost: do not fill the gap with what is likely.
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher Listening paper, and how much of it is reading and checking time?
---
About 45 minutes: five minutes' reading time at the start and two minutes' checking time at the end.
:::

::: q
How many times do you hear each Section A item?
---
Twice, with pauses built in to read the questions before and to answer after.
:::

::: q
The speaker says *Je ne mange que des légumes*. Does she eat vegetables?
---
Yes. *Ne...que* means "only": she only eats vegetables.
:::

::: q
The speaker says *Quand j'étais petit, je faisais de la natation, mais maintenant je joue au foot.* What does he do now?
---
Plays football. *Quand j'étais petit* and the imperfect *faisais* put swimming in the past; *maintenant* marks the present.
:::

::: q
Write the numbers: *soixante-dix-huit*, *quatre-vingts*, *quatre-vingt-douze*.
---
78 (60 + 18); 80 (4 × 20); 92 (80 + 12).
:::

::: q
What is the difference between "false" and "not mentioned"?
---
False means the text says the opposite of the statement; not mentioned means the text says nothing either way.
:::

::: q
Why should you not write two different answers to a one-mark question?
---
A wrong extra detail can cancel the right one, so you may score zero even though the correct answer is there.
:::

::: q
The speaker says *Je viens de finir mes devoirs.* Has she finished her homework?
---
Yes, she has just finished it. *Venir de* + infinitive means "to have just done".
:::

::: watch
- **Negatives reversed**: *ne...plus* (no longer) and *ne...jamais* (never) turn the keyword you heard into the wrong answer.
- **Ne...que misread as a negative**: it means "only" and is positive.
- **Wrong time frame**: the question asks about now and you write what they used to do (imperfect) or will do (future).
- **Answer before the sentence ends**: the real answer usually follows *mais, par contre, pourtant* or *finalement*.
- **Not mentioned filled with common sense**: if the speaker did not say it, it is not mentioned.
- **Imprecise English**: "cheap" for *moins cher* (cheaper), "sometimes" for *souvent* (often). The detail is the mark.
- **Numbers 70 to 99** and pairs such as *deux/douze*, *six/dix*, *cent/sans* cost easy marks every year.
- **Blanks**: guess in the checking time; a blank never scores.
:::

::: test
Transcript: *D'habitude, je vais au collège à vélo, mais cette semaine je prends le bus parce qu'il pleut.* How is the speaker getting to school this week, and why? (2)
---
By bus (1); because it is raining (1). Do not accept "by bike" (the usual method, not this week).
:::

::: test
Transcript: *Je ne regarde plus la télé le soir. Maintenant, je lis ou j'écoute de la musique.* Give two things the speaker does in the evening now. (2)
---
Reads (1); listens to music (1). Watching TV scores nothing: *ne...plus* means no longer.
:::

::: test
Transcript: *Mon cousin habite à Lyon depuis trois ans. Avant, il habitait à Paris.* (a) Where does the cousin live now? (b) For how long has he lived there? (2)
---
(a) Lyon (1). (b) Three years (1). Paris is the distractor (imperfect *habitait*, with *avant*).
:::

::: test
Transcript: *Le concert commence à vingt heures trente, mais les portes ouvrent à dix-neuf heures.* At what time do the doors open? Give the time in figures. (1)
---
19:00 / 7 pm (1). Do not accept 20:30, which is when the concert starts.
:::

::: test
Transcript: *Moi, je ne mange ni viande ni poisson, mais j'adore le fromage.* Which two foods does the speaker not eat, and which food does she love? (3)
---
Meat (1); fish (1); she loves cheese (1).
:::

::: test
Transcript: *Les réseaux sociaux sont utiles pour parler avec mes amis. Par contre, je passe trop de temps sur mon portable et je dors mal.* Is the speaker's opinion of social media positive, negative, or positive and negative? Give one reason for your answer. (2)
---
Positive and negative (1); useful for talking to friends / but spends too much time on the phone / sleeps badly (1).
:::

::: test
Transcript: *Julie travaille dans un supermarché le week-end. Elle gagne dix euros de l'heure et elle aime bien ses collègues.* True, false or not mentioned? (a) Julie works at the weekend. (b) Julie earns twelve euros an hour. (c) Julie wants to change her job. (3)
---
(a) True (1). (b) False: she earns ten euros (1). (c) Not mentioned (1).
:::

::: test
Transcript: *Finalement, nous n'avons pas visité le musée, parce qu'il était fermé. Au lieu de ça, nous avons fait une promenade au bord de la mer.* (a) Why didn't they visit the museum? (b) What did they do instead? (2)
---
(a) It was closed (1). (b) Went for a walk by the sea / along the seafront (1).
:::
