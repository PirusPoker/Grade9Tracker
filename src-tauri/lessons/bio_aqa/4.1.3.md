## What it is

Substances move into and out of cells across the cell membrane in three ways: **diffusion**, **osmosis** and **active transport**. This topic is the same for AQA Biology 8461 and Combined Science Trilogy 8464, with no Higher-tier-only content. Only the practical number differs (see below).

### Diffusion

**Diffusion** is the spreading out of the particles of any substance in solution, or particles of a gas, resulting in a **net movement** from an area of **higher concentration** to an area of **lower concentration**. It is **passive**: no energy from respiration is needed, because the particles move randomly on their own.

Examples in living things:
- **oxygen** diffuses from the alveoli into the blood, and from the blood into respiring cells;
- **carbon dioxide** diffuses from respiring cells into the blood, and from the blood into the alveoli to be breathed out (gas exchange);
- **urea** (a waste product made in the liver) diffuses from cells into the **blood plasma**, to be removed by the kidneys (excretion).

**Factors affecting the rate of diffusion:**

| Factor | Effect | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Concentration gradient** (difference in concentration) | steeper gradient → faster | more particles moving one way than the other per second |
| **Temperature** | higher → faster | particles have more kinetic energy and move faster |
| **Surface area of the membrane** | larger → faster | more particles can cross at the same time |

(Distance also matters: a thinner membrane gives a shorter diffusion path and faster diffusion. This appears in the exchange-surface list below.)

### Surface area to volume ratio

$$\text{SA : V ratio} = \dfrac{\text{surface area}}{\text{volume}}$$

For a cube of side $L$: surface area $= 6L^2$, volume $= L^3$.

| Cube side | Surface area | Volume | SA : V |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 cm | 6 cm² | 1 cm³ | 6 : 1 |
| 2 cm | 24 cm² | 8 cm³ | 3 : 1 |
| 4 cm | 96 cm² | 64 cm³ | 1.5 : 1 |

As an object gets **bigger**, its SA : V ratio gets **smaller**: volume increases faster than surface area.

- A **single-celled organism** has a **relatively large SA : V ratio**. Its surface is big enough, and distances short enough, for diffusion across the membrane to supply all its needs.
- A **multicellular organism** has a **small SA : V ratio**, and many cells are far from the surface. Diffusion across the body surface alone would be far too slow. So multicellular organisms need **specialised exchange surfaces** (to increase surface area) and a **transport system** (blood, xylem and phloem) to carry substances to and from every cell.

*Mini-example (cuboid)*: 2 cm × 3 cm × 5 cm. Surface area $= 2(2\times3 + 2\times5 + 3\times5) = 2(6+10+15) = 62$ cm². Volume $= 30$ cm³. Ratio $= 62 \div 30 = 2.1 : 1$ (2 s.f.).

### Exchange surfaces

The effectiveness of an exchange surface is increased by:
1. a **large surface area**;
2. a **thin membrane**, giving a **short diffusion path**;
3. (in animals) an **efficient blood supply**, which carries substances away and keeps the concentration gradient steep;
4. (in animals, for gas exchange) being **ventilated**, which brings in fresh air or water and keeps the gradient steep.

| Exchange surface | Adaptations |
|---|---|
| **Small intestine** (absorbs digested food) | millions of **villi**, each with **microvilli**, give a huge surface area; villus wall **one cell thick**; dense **capillary network** carries absorbed food away |
| **Lungs** (gas exchange) | millions of **alveoli** give a huge surface area; alveolus and capillary walls **one cell thick**; rich **capillary network**; **ventilated** by breathing |
| **Gills in fish** | many **gill filaments** covered in **lamellae** give a large surface area; thin lamellae; many capillaries; **water flows over** them constantly (ventilation); blood flows in the opposite direction to water, keeping a gradient along the whole lamella |
| **Roots in plants** | **root hair cells** give a large surface area for absorbing water and mineral ions |
| **Leaves in plants** | **flat, thin** shape gives a large surface area and short distance; **stomata** let carbon dioxide in and oxygen and water vapour out; **air spaces** in the spongy mesophyll give a large internal surface area for gas exchange |

### Osmosis

**Osmosis** is the **diffusion of water** from a **dilute solution** to a **more concentrated solution** through a **partially permeable membrane**. A dilute solution has lots of water and little solute; a partially permeable membrane lets small water molecules through but not larger solute molecules such as sugar.

| Cell placed in | Animal cell (e.g. red blood cell) | Plant cell |
|---|---|---|
| Solution more dilute than the cell | water enters by osmosis; the cell swells and may **burst** (no wall) | water enters; vacuole swells, cell presses on the wall and becomes **turgid** (firm), but the wall stops it bursting |
| Same concentration as the cell | no net movement of water | no net movement of water |
| Solution more concentrated than the cell | water leaves; the cell **shrinks** and crinkles | water leaves; the cell becomes **flaccid**, then the cytoplasm pulls away from the wall (**plasmolysis**) |

**Drinks in sport (WS 1.5).** An **isotonic** sports drink has about the same solute concentration as body fluids, so it replaces water, sugar and salts lost in sweat without causing a big osmotic change. A very sugary **high-energy** drink is more concentrated than the body fluids, so at first water can move by osmosis out of cells into the gut, which slows rehydration even though it supplies energy.

### Required practical 3: effect of concentration on the mass of plant tissue (Trilogy: Required practical 2)

Method:
1. Use a **cork borer** and scalpel to cut potato cylinders of the **same diameter and length**, from the same potato.
2. **Blot** each dry and record its **initial mass** on a balance (to 0.01 g).
3. Place one cylinder in each **concentration** of sugar (or salt) solution, e.g. 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 mol/dm³, same volume in each tube.
4. Leave for the same time (e.g. 24 hours, or at least 30 minutes) at the same temperature.
5. Remove, **blot gently** to remove surface solution, and record the **final mass**.
6. Repeat each concentration (e.g. three times) and calculate the mean.

$$\text{percentage change in mass} = \dfrac{\text{final mass} - \text{initial mass}}{\text{initial mass}} \times 100$$

Percentage change is used because the cylinders do not all start with exactly the same mass, so it allows a fair comparison. A **gain** is positive, a **loss** is negative.

Variables: independent = concentration of solution; dependent = percentage change in mass; control = size and source of potato, volume of solution, time, temperature, blotting method.

**Typical results and graph.** Plot percentage change in mass (y-axis) against concentration (x-axis) with a line of best fit. In dilute solutions the tissue **gains** mass (water moved in by osmosis); in concentrated solutions it **loses** mass (water moved out). Where the line **crosses the x-axis** (0 % change), there is no net osmosis: the solution has the **same concentration as the potato cell contents**. Typical value for potato: about 0.2–0.3 mol/dm³.

**Rate of water uptake** (a simple compound measure): mass change ÷ time. A cylinder that gains 0.60 g in 30 minutes takes up water at $0.60 \div 30 = 0.02$ g/min.

Errors and improvements: inconsistent blotting (blot the same way each time); cylinders drying out while weighing (weigh quickly); leave longer so changes are bigger and easier to measure; repeat and calculate means; use more concentrations near the crossing point to pin it down. Risks: take care with the scalpel and cork borer (cut on a tile, away from the body).

### Active transport

**Active transport** moves substances from a **more dilute** solution to a **more concentrated** solution, **against a concentration gradient**. This requires **energy from respiration**, so cells that do a lot of it contain **many mitochondria**.
- **Root hair cells**: mineral ions (e.g. nitrate, magnesium) are absorbed from the **very dilute** solution in the soil into the root hair, where the ion concentration is higher. Plants need these ions for healthy growth.
- **Small intestine**: **sugar** (glucose) molecules are absorbed from the gut, where their concentration is **lower**, into the blood, where it is **higher**. Glucose is needed for respiration.

### Comparing the three processes

| | Diffusion | Osmosis | Active transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| What moves | particles of any substance in solution, or gases | **water** only | particles such as ions and sugar |
| Direction | higher → lower concentration (down the gradient) | dilute → more concentrated solution | lower → higher concentration (**against** the gradient) |
| Energy from respiration | not needed (passive) | not needed (passive) | **needed** |
| Membrane | not required | **partially permeable** membrane required | cell membrane (with carrier proteins) |
| Examples | O₂, CO₂ in gas exchange; urea into plasma | water into root hair cells; potato cylinders | mineral ions into root hairs; glucose from gut to blood |

::: terms
- **Diffusion**: the spreading out of particles resulting in a net movement from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration.
- **Concentration gradient**: the difference in concentration of a substance between two areas.
- **Osmosis**: the diffusion of water from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane.
- **Partially permeable membrane**: a membrane that lets small molecules such as water pass through but not larger solute molecules.
- **Active transport**: movement of substances from a more dilute to a more concentrated solution, against the gradient, using energy from respiration.
- **Surface area to volume ratio**: the surface area of an object divided by its volume; it falls as the object gets larger.
- **Exchange surface**: a specialised surface, such as alveoli or villi, adapted for efficient exchange of materials.
- **Turgid**: describes a plant cell that has taken in water by osmosis so its contents press firmly against the cell wall.
- **Plasmolysis**: when a plant cell loses so much water that the cytoplasm pulls away from the cell wall.
:::

::: method
**Command words.** *Describe* how a substance moves = name the process, give the direction relative to the gradient, and say whether energy is needed. *Explain* = add the reason (e.g. "because respiration uses oxygen, so the concentration inside the cell stays low"). *Compare* = put both processes in the same sentence with "whereas". *Suggest* = apply ideas to an unfamiliar organism or surface.

**Which process?** Water across a membrane → osmosis. Against the gradient, or needing energy → active transport. Anything else moving down a gradient → diffusion.

**Definitions.** Diffusion: "net movement from higher to lower concentration". Osmosis: "water", "dilute to more concentrated solution", "partially permeable membrane": all three. Active transport: "against the concentration gradient" **and** "energy from respiration".

**Exchange-surface questions.** Name the feature, then link it to rate: large surface area → more particles cross at once; thin/one cell thick → short diffusion path; good blood supply or ventilation → maintains a steep concentration gradient. Use the organ's real structures (villi, alveoli, lamellae, root hairs, stomata).

**SA : V calculations.** Work out surface area (add all faces) and volume, divide, and write as "n : 1". Then explain: smaller ratio → diffusion alone is not enough → exchange surfaces and transport systems are needed.

**Osmosis practical.** Percentage change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100; keep the sign. Explain why percentage is used, read the x-intercept from the graph and say what it means, identify anomalies (a point far from the line), and give improvements (blot consistently, repeat, more concentrations near the intercept).

**6-mark answers.** Levels of response reward a logical sequence and linked explanation. For "compare the three transport processes", cover what moves, direction, energy and membrane, with an example of each. For the practical, write the method as numbered steps, including the calculation and what the graph shows.
:::

::: example Calculate: SA : V ratio
Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a cube with sides of 3 cm. Give your answer as a ratio. (3)

Surface area = 6 × 3 × 3 = 54 cm² (1).
Volume = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm³ (1).
Ratio = 54 : 27 = **2 : 1** (1).
:::

::: example Calculate: percentage change in mass
A potato cylinder had a mass of 2.50 g. After 24 hours in 0.8 mol/dm³ sugar solution its mass was 2.15 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass and explain the result. (4)

Change = 2.15 − 2.50 = −0.35 g (1).
Percentage change = −0.35 ÷ 2.50 × 100 = **−14 %** (1).
The sugar solution was more concentrated than the potato cell contents (1), so water left the cells by osmosis through the partially permeable cell membranes (1).
:::

::: example Explain: active transport in roots
Explain why root hair cells need many mitochondria. (3)

Root hair cells absorb mineral ions from the soil, where the ion concentration is lower than inside the cell (1). So the ions must be moved against the concentration gradient by active transport (1), which needs energy released by respiration in the mitochondria (1).
:::

::: example Explain: adaptations of the lungs
Explain how the alveoli are adapted for efficient gas exchange. (4)

There are millions of alveoli, giving a very large surface area, so more oxygen can diffuse at once (1). The alveolus and capillary walls are only one cell thick, giving a short diffusion path (1). A dense capillary network carries oxygenated blood away and brings carbon dioxide (1), and breathing ventilates the alveoli; both keep the concentration gradient steep (1).
:::

::: example Interpret: osmosis graph
In an osmosis investigation the line of best fit crosses 0 % change at 0.32 mol/dm³. What does this tell you, and how could the student find this value more precisely? (2)

The potato cells have the same concentration as a 0.32 mol/dm³ solution, so there is no net movement of water (1). Test more concentrations close to 0.32 mol/dm³ (e.g. 0.25, 0.30, 0.35) and repeat each (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a large multicellular organism needs a transport system but an amoeba does not. (3)
---
The amoeba is small with a large surface area to volume ratio, so diffusion across its surface meets its needs (1). - Active transport: **against the concentration gradient** AND **energy from respiration**. Both every time.
- Diffusion is **net** movement down a gradient; the particles move randomly in all directions.
- Percentage change: divide by the **initial** mass, and keep the **minus sign** for a loss.
- SA : V falls as size increases; say what that **means** (diffusion alone is too slow → exchange surfaces and transport systems).
- Animal cells **burst** or **shrink**; plant cells become **turgid** or **plasmolysed**. Do not swap them.
- Blood supply and ventilation help because they **maintain a steep concentration gradient**: say that phrase.
