## What it is

**Separate science only: this whole subsection (4.5.4 Plant hormones) is Biology 8461 only.** Combined Science: Trilogy students do not need it. Within it, gibberellins, ethene and **all of 4.5.4.2 (uses of plant hormones) are Higher tier only**; auxins, tropisms and Required practical 8 are for all Biology students.

### Control and coordination in plants

Plants have no nervous system. Instead they **produce hormones to coordinate and control growth** and their **responses to light and gravity**. These growth responses are called **tropisms**:

| Tropism | Stimulus | Shoots | Roots |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phototropism** | Light | Grow **towards** light (positive) | Grow away from light / little response |
| **Gravitropism** (also called **geotropism**) | Gravity | Grow **against** gravity — upwards (negative) | Grow **with** gravity — downwards (positive) |

Why they matter: shoots growing towards light get more light for **photosynthesis**; roots growing down anchor the plant and reach **water and mineral ions**; a shoot growing up from a buried seed reaches the light.

### Auxin

**Auxin** is a plant hormone made in the **tips** of shoots and roots. It moves (diffuses) back from the tip to the region just behind it, where it controls **cell elongation** (cells getting longer). **Unequal distributions of auxin cause unequal growth rates in plant roots and shoots** — one side grows faster than the other, so the shoot or root **bends**.

The key fact: auxin has **opposite effects** in shoots and roots.
- In **shoots**, auxin **stimulates** cell elongation (more auxin → faster growth).
- In **roots**, auxin **inhibits** cell elongation (more auxin → slower growth).

**Phototropism in a shoot (light from one side):**
1. Light shines on one side of the shoot tip.
2. Auxin **moves to the shaded side**, so there is more auxin on the shaded side.
3. Cells on the **shaded side elongate faster** than those on the lit side.
4. The shoot **bends towards the light**.

**Gravitropism in a shoot (plant lying on its side):**
1. Gravity causes auxin to **collect on the lower side** of the shoot.
2. Cells on the lower side elongate faster.
3. The shoot **bends upwards**, away from gravity.

**Gravitropism in a root (plant lying on its side):**
1. Auxin **collects on the lower side** of the root.
2. In roots, auxin **inhibits** elongation, so the lower side grows **more slowly**; the upper side grows faster.
3. The root **bends downwards**, towards gravity.

**Classic evidence.** Experiments with young oat or grass seedling shoots (coleoptiles):

| Treatment | Result | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Light from one side, tip intact | Bends towards the light | Normal phototropism |
| Tip removed | No growth, no bending | The tip produces the substance (auxin) |
| Tip covered with a foil cap | Grows straight up | The **tip** detects the light |
| Base covered, tip exposed | Bends towards the light | The response is to light at the tip |
| Tip cut off, put on agar block, block placed on one side of cut stump | Bends away from the side the block is on | A chemical diffuses from the tip and stimulates growth on that side |

### Gibberellins and ethene (Higher tier)

- **Gibberellins** are important in **initiating seed germination** — they break dormancy so a seed starts to grow (they trigger the production of enzymes that break down the stored starch for the embryo).
- **Ethene** is a gas that **controls cell division** and the **ripening of fruits**. Ripening fruits give off ethene, which speeds up ripening of fruits nearby.
- You do **not** need to know the mechanisms of how gibberellins and ethene work.

### Required practical 8: the effect of light or gravity on the growth of newly germinated seedlings

**Light (phototropism) — method:**
1. Put the same number (e.g. 10) of cress or mustard seeds on damp cotton wool in each of three Petri dishes. Germinate them in the same conditions until shoots are a few mm tall.
2. Measure and record the starting length of each seedling with a ruler (mm).
3. Place one dish in a box with a slit so light comes from **one side only**; one in a box in **complete darkness**; one in light from **all directions** (or on a window sill, rotated).
4. Leave for the same time (e.g. 5 days), keeping the cotton wool damp with the same volume of water each day.
5. Measure the length of each seedling again and calculate the mean change in length. Note the **direction** of growth.
6. Make **careful, labelled biological drawings** of typical seedlings from each dish.

**Gravity (gravitropism) — method:** germinate seeds (e.g. broad beans or cress) on damp cotton wool in a Petri dish, then fix the dish **vertically** (on its edge) in the dark so light does not interfere, with seeds arranged so their roots point in different directions. After a few days, observe and draw the direction of root and shoot growth. A **clinostat** (slowly rotating turntable) can be used as a control: gravity acts on all sides equally, so roots and shoots grow straight.

| Variable | Example |
|---|---|
| Independent | Direction of light (one side / all round / dark), or orientation of the seedling relative to gravity |
| Dependent | Length of seedlings (mm) and direction of growth/curvature |
| Control | Type of seed; number of seeds; volume of water; temperature; time; distance from light source; light intensity |

**Recording results** — both as **length measurements** (in a table, then mean) **and** as **labelled biological drawings**: sharp pencil, smooth continuous lines, no shading, drawn to show proportions, labels with ruled lines not crossing, a title and the magnification or scale.

**Typical results:**
- **Light from one side:** shoots bend **towards** the light; moderate growth.
- **Darkness:** shoots grow **tallest** but are thin, pale yellow (no chlorophyll — **etiolated**) and straight; the plant is using its energy store to grow up quickly to find light.
- **All-round light:** shoots grow straight up, shorter, green and healthy.
- **Gravity:** roots curve **downwards** and shoots curve **upwards**, whatever way the seed was placed.

**Errors and improvements:** seedlings are curved, so straighten them along a piece of string or measure with a thread then a ruler; use more seeds per dish so one unusual seedling has less effect; measure to the nearest mm; make sure no light leaks into the dark box.

**Risks:** low — wash hands after handling seeds (some are treated with fungicide); take care with lamps (hot) and water near electrics.

**Worked mini-example.** In darkness, 10 seedlings grew a total of 342 mm in 5 days. Mean growth per seedling $= \frac{342}{10} = 34.2$ mm; mean rate $= \frac{34.2}{5} = 6.8$ mm per day (2 s.f.).

### Separate science only: uses of plant hormones (Higher tier)

**Plant growth hormones are used in agriculture and horticulture** to control plant growth.

| Hormone | Use | How it helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Auxins** | **Weed killers** (selective herbicides) | Many weed killers are synthetic auxins that are absorbed more by **broad-leaved weeds**; the weeds grow too fast, abnormally and die. Narrow-leaved crops such as wheat and grass are less affected. |
| **Auxins** | **Rooting powders** | Cut stems dipped in rooting powder **grow roots quickly**, so gardeners can make many **clones** of a plant from cuttings. |
| **Auxins** | **Promoting growth in tissue culture** | Added to the growth medium to make small groups of cells grow and develop **roots and shoots**, producing many clones. |
| **Ethene** | **Controlling ripening of fruit during storage and transport** | Fruit (e.g. bananas) is picked unripe and firm, so it is not damaged in transport; it is stored with low ethene (or ethene removed), then **ethene is added** near the shop so it ripens just in time to sell. Less waste. |
| **Gibberellins** | **Ending seed dormancy** | Seeds germinate at a time the grower chooses, even if they would normally need a cold period; used in brewing to make barley germinate. |
| **Gibberellins** | **Promoting flowering** | Plants flower earlier or under conditions where they otherwise would not, or flower all together. |
| **Gibberellins** | **Increasing fruit size** | Larger fruits (e.g. seedless grapes) sell for more. |

**Weed killers and biodiversity (WS 1.3, 1.4).** Selective weed killers raise crop yield because crops no longer **compete** with weeds for light, water and mineral ions. But removing weeds **reduces biodiversity**: fewer wild plant species means less food and habitat for insects, and so fewer birds and other animals further up food chains. The herbicide may also run off into rivers or harm non-target plants. Evaluating this means weighing higher food production against the loss of biodiversity.

::: terms
- **Auxin**: a plant hormone made in shoot and root tips that controls cell elongation; unequal distribution causes bending.
- **Phototropism**: a growth response of a plant to light; shoots grow towards light.
- **Gravitropism**: a growth response of a plant to gravity, also called geotropism; roots grow down and shoots grow up.
- **Cell elongation**: cells getting longer; auxin stimulates it in shoots but inhibits it in roots.
- **Gibberellins**: plant hormones important in initiating seed germination; used to end dormancy and increase fruit size (Higher tier).
- **Ethene**: a gaseous plant hormone that controls cell division and the ripening of fruits (Higher tier).
- **Rooting powder**: a powder containing auxin that makes cuttings grow roots quickly (Higher tier).
- **Selective weed killer**: a synthetic auxin that kills broad-leaved weeds but not narrow-leaved crops (Higher tier).
:::

::: method
**Command words on this topic**
- **Explain** a tropism — always give the chain: *stimulus* → *auxin moves to one side* → *that side grows faster (shoot) / slower (root)* → *direction of bending*. Say **cell elongation**, not "cell division" or "the cells grow towards the light".
- **Describe** an experiment's results (Required practical 8) — use numbers: mean lengths, which dish grew most, which way shoots bent.
- **Suggest** — interpret unfamiliar experiments (e.g. seedlings with foil caps, agar blocks, mica sheets that block auxin). Ask: where is the auxin, and which side elongates more?
- **Describe uses** (Higher tier) — hormone + use + *why it helps the grower*.
- **Evaluate** weed killers (Higher tier) — yield and profit vs biodiversity and pollution, with a conclusion.

**Required practical questions.** Identify IV, DV and controls (seed type, number of seeds, water, temperature, time, light intensity). Explain why a mean of many seedlings is used (one seedling may be unusual; improves repeatability). Suggest improvements: more seeds, measure curved seedlings with string, check the dark box has no light leaks. Results must be recorded as **lengths and labelled drawings**.

**Calculations.** Mean length, mean growth rate (mm per day), percentage change. Show the division and keep units (mm, mm/day). Round sensibly (2 or 3 s.f.).

**6-mark answers.** "Explain how a shoot grows towards light and how you could investigate it" — Level 3 needs the auxin mechanism **and** a workable method with controls and how results are recorded.
:::

::: example Phototropism
A plant on a window sill bends towards the window. Explain why. (3)

Light comes from one side, so auxin moves to the shaded side of the shoot (1). Auxin stimulates cell elongation, so cells on the shaded side grow longer/faster (1). The shoot grows more on the shaded side and bends towards the light (1).
:::

::: example Gravitropism in a root
A bean seedling is laid on its side. Explain why its root grows downwards. (3)

Auxin collects on the lower side of the root due to gravity (1). In roots, auxin inhibits cell elongation, so the lower side grows more slowly (1). The upper side grows faster, so the root bends downwards (1).
:::

::: example Required practical 8 results
Seedlings were grown for 5 days. Mean increase in length: darkness 31 mm; light from one side 22 mm; all-round light 18 mm. Describe and explain the result in darkness. (3)

Seedlings in darkness grew the most — 31 mm, compared with 18 mm in all-round light (1). With no light, auxin is evenly distributed and the shoots elongate rapidly, growing straight up (1), using stored energy to try to reach light; they would be pale/yellow because no chlorophyll is made (1).
:::

::: example Agar block experiment
The tip of a shoot was cut off and placed on an agar block for an hour. The block was then placed on the left side of the cut stump, in darkness. Predict and explain what happens. (3)

The shoot bends to the right (1). Auxin diffused from the tip into the agar block and then into the left side of the stump (1). Auxin stimulates cell elongation, so the left side grows faster than the right, making the shoot bend to the right (1).
:::

::: example Ethene (Higher tier)
Explain why bananas are picked unripe and then exposed to ethene before sale. (3)

Unripe bananas are firm, so they are not damaged during transport (1). Ethene controls ripening (1), so adding it just before sale makes them ripen at the right time, reducing waste (1).
:::

::: watch
- Auxin causes **cell elongation**, not cell division, and it does not make cells "grow towards" light.
- Auxin moves to the **shaded** side — not the lit side. Many answers say it is "destroyed by light"; say it **moves** away from the light.
- Roots respond the **opposite** way: auxin **inhibits** elongation in roots. Do not apply the shoot rule to roots.
- Gravitropism and geotropism are the same thing; spell it right.
- Required practical 8: results must include **lengths and labelled drawings**; name specific control variables (number of seeds, volume of water, temperature).
- Gibberellins and ethene: AQA does not want mechanisms — just the effects and uses. Keep **gibberellins = germination, flowering, fruit size** and **ethene = ripening** separate.
- Weed killers: say they reduce competition, then the biodiversity cost — both sides for an evaluation.
:::
