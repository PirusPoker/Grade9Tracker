## What it is

**Paper 1 Listening** is 25% of the GCSE: 50 marks in about **45 minutes** at Higher tier. That time includes **five minutes' reading time** at the start and **two minutes' checking time** at the end. You may make notes at any point, including in the reading time. No dictionary.

**Section A (40 marks)** is comprehension. Every question is in **English**, and you answer in English or by ticking, circling or writing a letter. Each recording is played **twice**, with pauses built in. The items are announcements, short conversations, bulletins, podcasts and spoken descriptions, and they test four things: the overall message, key points, details and opinions. The German is only from the AQA vocabulary list and grammar, spoken at no more than a moderate pace. Marks are lost on **traps**, not on speed. The speakers mention two things and only one answers the question. They say **nicht** or **kein** in the middle of a sentence. They move from **früher** to **jetzt**, or they talk about the topic without ever giving the detail the question asks for.

**Section B (10 marks)** is the **dictation**. At Higher tier it is at least **30 words** in short sentences. Each sentence is heard **three times**: first as a whole, then in short sections, then as a whole again. **Two words come from outside the vocabulary list**, so you must spell them purely from their sound. The dictation is marked as a whole on two grids:
- **AO1, communication of meaning (5).** The top mark goes to answers where "the meaning is communicated very clearly throughout". A reader of your German must understand exactly what was said.
- **AO3, transcription and grammatical accuracy (5).** The top mark needs words "always or nearly always transcribed correctly with a very high level of grammatical accuracy". That means capital letters on nouns, umlauts, ß, and endings that fit the grammar.

A zero for meaning means a zero for accuracy too. So write *something* for every section, even if you have to guess.

The dictation tests the German **sound-symbol correspondences** (spec 3.2.3). These are the places where German spelling and sound differ from English: ei/ie, w, z, sch/sp/st, ch, the umlauts, ß/ss and -er. There is no audio in this lesson, so each sound is described in plain English. Say the words out loud as you read.

**What separates grade 9.** A grade 9 candidate uses the five minutes' reading time to predict the vocabulary and underline the trap words in each question: *now*, *not*, *first*, *two reasons*. They answer only what is asked, and they write English answers that are short and precise. In the dictation, they use grammar to fix what their ear cannot. *ein schönes Haus* needs an *-es*, even if the speaker swallows it.

::: terms
- **distractor**: a detail the speaker mentions that fits the question but is not the answer, e.g. the sport someone used to play when you are asked what they play now.
- **früher / jetzt**: 'in the past, used to' / 'now'. The classic distractor pair: *Früher habe ich Fußball gespielt, jetzt schwimme ich.*
- **nicht / kein**: 'not' / 'not a, no'. One short word in the middle of a sentence reverses the answer: *Ich habe keine Zeit.*
- **nie / niemand / nichts**: 'never' / 'nobody' / 'nothing', three more negatives that flip the meaning of a sentence.
- **nicht mehr / kein ... mehr**: 'no longer, not any more'. *Ich esse kein Fleisch mehr* means she used to eat meat and now does not.
- **sondern**: 'but rather', used after a negative. The correct answer comes after it: *nicht mit dem Bus, sondern mit dem Zug*.
- **seit**: 'since, for'. With the present tense it means something that started in the past and is still going on: *seit einem Jahr arbeite ich* = I have been working for a year.
- **not mentioned**: an option that is about the topic but that the recording never states. Choose it only when nothing you heard confirms or contradicts the statement.
- **dictation**: Section B. Short sentences heard three times, which you write down in German. Marked for meaning (5) and for transcription with grammar (5).
- **sound-symbol correspondence**: the link between a German sound and its spelling, e.g. the sound 'eye' is spelt *ei*, as in *frei*.
- **Umlaut**: the two dots on ä, ö, ü. Leaving them off changes the word: *schon* (already) and *schön* (beautiful).
- **ß (Eszett)**: 'sharp s'. Use it after a long vowel or ei/au/eu (*groß, heißen, Straße*). Use *ss* after a short vowel (*Wasser, muss, dass*).
- **unstressed -er**: at the end of a word it sounds like the 'a' at the end of 'sofa', as in *Vater, Theater, aber*.
- **final devoicing**: b, d and g at the end of a word sound like p, t and k (*halb, Hund, Weg*). You still write b, d and g.
:::

::: method
1. **Reading time (5 minutes).** Read every question, in order. Underline the question word and the traps: *now, used to, next year, not, two reasons, why*. Next to each question, jot 3 or 4 German words you expect to hear (question on pets: *Hund, Katze, Tier*).
2. **First listening.** Get the gist and note possible answers in the margin, in English or in German, whichever is faster. Do not commit yet.
3. **Second listening.** Check each note against the traps. Was there a *nicht*, *kein*, *nie* or *nicht mehr*? Was it *früher* or *jetzt*? Did *sondern* correct what came before?
4. **Write short English answers**, one detail per mark. For 'Give two reasons', give exactly two. Extra wrong details can cancel a right one.
5. **Dictation, hearing 1.** Listen without writing, or write only the first letter of each word, so you know how many words there are.
6. **Dictation, hearing 2.** This is the one in sections. Write each chunk, using the sound rules: 'eye' is *ei*, 'ee' is *ie*, 'v' is *w*, 'ts' is *z*, 'sh' is *sch*.
7. **Dictation, hearing 3.** Fill gaps and check the grammar. Every noun has a capital, the verb agrees with its subject (*wir fahren*, *sie spielt*), and adjective and article endings fit the case.
8. **Checking time (2 minutes).** Go back to Section A answers you left blank, then reread the dictation for umlauts and ß.
:::

::: example Section A: a distractor pair with früher
You hear: *"Früher bin ich jeden Samstag ins Kino gegangen, aber seit einem Jahr arbeite ich samstags im Supermarkt. Das Geld spare ich für eine Reise nach Spanien."*

**Question: What does Jonas do on Saturdays now? Why?** (2)

- *ins Kino gegangen* comes straight after **früher**, so the cinema is what he *used to* do. That is the distractor.
- **seit einem Jahr arbeite ich** is present tense with *seit*, so it is true now. He works in a supermarket (1).
- *Das Geld spare ich für eine Reise nach Spanien*: he is saving for a trip to Spain (1).

Answer: **He works in a supermarket (1); to save money for a trip to Spain (1).** 'He goes to the cinema' scores zero. It is a real detail from the recording, but it answers a different question.
:::

::: example Section A: negatives and sondern
You hear: *"Letzten Sommer haben wir nicht im Hotel gewohnt, sondern bei meiner Tante in Hamburg. Es war super, aber ich esse kein Fleisch mehr und meine Tante kocht jeden Tag Fleisch!"*

**Question: Where did the family stay? What was the problem?** (2)

- *nicht im Hotel ... sondern bei meiner Tante*. The hotel is negated and *sondern* gives the truth: **with her aunt (in Hamburg)** (1).
- *kein Fleisch mehr* means 'no longer eats meat'. *kocht jeden Tag Fleisch* means her aunt cooks meat every day. **She no longer eats meat, but her aunt cooks meat every day** (1).

A candidate who writes 'in a hotel' has heard the right word and missed the *nicht* in front of it, which is the most common way to lose a mark.
:::

::: example Section B: a dictation sentence, sound by sound
You hear: *"Meine Schwester spielt jeden Mittwoch Tennis, aber heute ist sie leider krank."*

| You hear (in English sounds) | Rule | You write |
|---|---|---|
| 'my-nuh' | 'eye' sound = **ei** | Meine |
| 'shvess-tuh' | *sch* = 'sh'; **w** = English 'v'; unstressed *-er* = 'uh'; st in the middle stays 's-t' | Schwester |
| 'shpeelt' | **sp** at the start of a word = 'shp'; 'ee' = **ie** | spielt |
| 'yay-den' | **j** = English 'y' | jeden |
| 'mit-voch' (throaty ch) | w = 'v'; **ch** after o is the throaty 'loch' sound; days are nouns, so a capital | Mittwoch |
| 'hoy-tuh' | 'oy' = **eu** (äu only when the word is related to a word with *au*, like *Häuser* from *Haus*) | heute |
| 'lie-duh' | 'eye' = ei; final -er = 'uh' | leider |

Then run the grammar check. *Schwester*, *Mittwoch* and *Tennis* are nouns, so they take capitals. *sie* is 'she' because of *ist*, so the verb is *spielt* with a **-t** ending. The finished sentence: **Meine Schwester spielt jeden Mittwoch Tennis, aber heute ist sie leider krank.**
:::

::: example Section B: a word from outside the list
Two dictation words are not on the vocabulary list, so you cannot rely on memory. Say you hear a word that sounds like **'tsvee-bel'** in *"Ich kaufe eine ... für das Essen."*

- The 'ts' sound at the start of a German word is spelt **z** (as in *Zimmer, Zug*).
- A 'v' sound is spelt **w**.
- A long 'ee' is almost always **ie**.
- The ending 'bel' is simply **-bel**.
- It is the thing you buy, so it is a noun and takes a capital.

You write **Zwiebel** (onion). You do not need to know the word. You only need the sound rules. The same method gives you *Wespe* (wasp) from 'vess-puh'. Notice that **sp in the middle of a word is plain 's-p'**. It is only 'shp' at the start of a word or word-part (*spielen, Sport, versprechen*).
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher Listening paper, and what two fixed periods does that time include?
---
About 45 minutes, including five minutes' reading time at the start and two minutes' checking time at the end.
:::

::: q
You hear: *Ich fahre nie mit dem Bus, sondern immer mit dem Fahrrad.* How does the speaker travel?
---
Always by bike. *nie* negates the bus, and *sondern* gives the real answer.
:::

::: q
What does *Ich spiele kein Tennis mehr* tell you about the past and the present?
---
The speaker used to play tennis but no longer does. *kein ... mehr* means 'no longer'.
:::

::: q
How many times is each dictation sentence heard, and in what form each time?
---
Three times: first as a whole sentence, then in short sections, then as a whole sentence again.
:::

::: q
You hear a word that sounds like 'shtatt'. How is the start spelt, and why?
---
*St-*, as in *Stadt*. At the start of a word, *st* is pronounced 'sht', and *sp* is pronounced 'shp'.
:::

::: q
How do you decide between *ß* and *ss* when you can only hear the word?
---
Listen to the vowel before it. After a long vowel or ei/au/eu, write ß (*groß*, *heißen*, *draußen*). After a short vowel, write ss (*Wasser*, *muss*, *dass*).
:::

::: q
A word ends with the soft 'ish' sound after an i, like 'rish-tish'. How is the ending spelt?
---
*-ig*, as in *richtig* or *ruhig*. The -ig ending is pronounced like the soft *ch*.
:::

::: q
Why does grammar matter in the dictation if it is a listening test?
---
The AO3 grid rewards 'transcription and grammatical accuracy'. Capitals on nouns, verb endings and adjective endings all count, and grammar tells you endings your ear might miss.
:::

::: watch
- **The early word is often the distractor.** Speakers often mention the wrong answer first (*früher*, *zuerst*, *eigentlich*) and correct it afterwards (*jetzt*, *aber*, *sondern*).
- **Negatives in the middle of a sentence**: *nicht, kein, nie, niemand, nichts, nicht mehr*. Missing one is the single biggest cause of lost marks.
- **Answer the exact question**: 'now', 'next year', 'two reasons', 'what she dislikes'. A true detail that answers a different question scores nothing.
- **No extra details.** If a question is worth one mark and you give one right and one wrong detail, the wrong one can cancel the mark.
- **Dictation: capitals on every noun**, including days (*Montag*) and nominalised verbs (*das Schwimmen*).
- **Dictation: ei and ie reversed.** Say the second letter's English name: *ei* sounds like 'eye' (the letter i), *ie* sounds like 'ee' (the letter e).
- **Dictation: missing umlauts.** *schon/schön*, *wurde/würde*, *zahlen/zählen* are different words.
- **Never leave a dictation section blank.** A zero for meaning means zero for accuracy too.
:::

::: test
You hear: *"Früher habe ich viel Cola getrunken, aber jetzt trinke ich lieber Wasser, weil es gesünder ist."* What does the speaker drink now, and why? (2)
---
Water (1); because it is healthier (1). 'Cola' scores 0, because that was in the past.
:::

::: test
You hear: *"Mein Bruder wohnt nicht mehr bei uns. Er studiert seit September in Berlin und kommt nur in den Ferien nach Hause."* Give **three** details about the brother. (3)
---
Any three: he no longer lives at home / with the family (1); he has been studying since September (1); he studies in Berlin (1); he only comes home in the holidays (1).
:::

::: test
You hear: *"Nächstes Jahr werde ich in einem Hotel arbeiten. Letztes Jahr habe ich in einem Café gearbeitet, und im Moment arbeite ich im Supermarkt."* Match each job to its time frame: past, present or future. (3)
---
Hotel: future (1); café: past (1); supermarket: present (1).
:::

::: test
Statement: *Mia goes to the gym three times a week.* You hear: *"Mia macht viel Sport. Sie geht gern schwimmen und spielt am Wochenende Tennis."* Is the statement True, False or Not mentioned? Explain. (2)
---
Not mentioned (1). She does a lot of sport, but the gym and 'three times a week' are never stated (1).
:::

::: test
Write the German words described. (4)

(a) sounds like 'tsy-toong', a newspaper (1)
(b) sounds like 'shpayt', meaning late (1)
(c) sounds like 'hy-sen', meaning to be called (1)
(d) sounds like 'fee-lysht', meaning perhaps (1)
---
(a) Zeitung: z = 'ts', ei = 'eye', capital for a noun (1). (b) spät: sp- = 'shp', long ä (1). (c) heißen: ß after the 'eye' diphthong (1). (d) vielleicht: v = 'f', ie = 'ee', ei = 'eye', soft ch before t (1).
:::

::: test
Correct the four errors in this dictation answer: *Ich spile jeden tag fusball mit meinem bruder.* (4)
---
*spiele* ('ee' = ie, plus -e for ich) (1); *Tag* (noun, capital) (1); *Fußball* (ß after long 'oo', and a capital) (1); *Bruder* (noun, capital) (1).
:::

::: test
Choose the correct spelling in each pair, and give the rule. (3)

(a) Straße / Strasse (b) Wasser / Waßer (c) weiß / weiss
---
(a) Straße: long vowel before the s-sound (1). (b) Wasser: short vowel, so ss (1). (c) weiß: ß after the diphthong ei (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how the Higher dictation is marked, and what the top mark in each grid requires. (3)
---
10 marks, judged across the whole dictation: 5 for communication of meaning and 5 for transcription and grammatical accuracy (1). Top meaning mark: the meaning is communicated very clearly throughout (1). Top accuracy mark: words always or nearly always transcribed correctly with a very high level of grammatical accuracy (1).
:::
