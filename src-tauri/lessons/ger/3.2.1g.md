## What it is

In German an adjective **after** the noun, following *sein*, *werden* or *finden*, has **no ending**: *Der Film ist **lustig**. Ich finde Mathe **schwer**.* An adjective **in front of** a noun must take an ending, which depends on the article before it and on the noun's gender, number and case: *ein **lustiger** Film, mit meinem **alten** Freund*. This lesson also covers **comparisons** (*größer als*, *so groß wie*) and **gern / lieber** for saying what you like and prefer doing.

Adjective endings are the grammar point that most clearly separates grade 7 from grade 9 writing. They affect every description you write: your family in Theme 1, a festival in Theme 2, your town in Theme 3. In the translation into German they often decide whether the grammar grid gives you 4 or 5. In the photo card, *Ich sehe eine große Familie* is better than *Ich sehe eine Familie*, as long as the ending is right.

**After der, die, das** (and the der-words *dieser, jeder, welcher*): **-e** in the five "easy" slots (all nominative singulars, plus feminine and neuter accusative), **-en** everywhere else.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nom | der alt**e** Mann | die alt**e** Stadt | das alt**e** Haus | die alt**en** Häuser |
| acc | den alt**en** Mann | die alt**e** Stadt | das alt**e** Haus | die alt**en** Häuser |
| dat | dem alt**en** Mann | der alt**en** Stadt | dem alt**en** Haus | den alt**en** Häusern |

**After ein, eine, kein and the possessives.** These are the same as above, except in the three places where *ein* has no ending of its own. There the adjective shows the gender instead: **-er** for masculine nominative and **-es** for neuter nominative and accusative.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural (kein/mein) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nom | ein alt**er** Mann | eine alt**e** Stadt | ein alt**es** Haus | meine alt**en** Freunde |
| acc | einen alt**en** Mann | eine alt**e** Stadt | ein alt**es** Haus | meine alt**en** Freunde |
| dat | einem alt**en** Mann | einer alt**en** Stadt | einem alt**en** Haus | meinen alt**en** Freunden |

**Plural with no article**: nominative and accusative **-e**, dative **-en**. *Ich habe nett**e** Freunde. Ich spiele mit nett**en** Freunden.* (Singular nouns with no article, as in *kaltes Wasser*, are not required by AQA.)

**Comparisons.** Add **-er** and use **als** for "than": *Mathe ist **einfacher als** Chemie.* Many one-syllable adjectives add an umlaut: *alt → älter, groß → größer, jung → jünger, kurz → kürzer, lang → länger, warm → wärmer*. The irregulars are *gut → **besser***, *hoch → **höher***, *viel → **mehr***, and *gern → **lieber***. Use **so … wie** for "as … as": *Ich bin **so groß wie** mein Vater.* *nicht so … wie* means "not as … as". A few adjectives change their spelling: *teuer → teurer*, *dunkel → dunkler*. At Foundation that is not individually credited, but at Higher it is (3.2.2d). Adverbs compare the same way: *Sie läuft **schneller als** ich.*

**gern and lieber.** German has no verb for "like doing". Put **gern** after the verb: *Ich **spiele gern** Tennis* (I like playing tennis). Use **lieber** for "prefer": *Ich **schwimme lieber*** (I prefer swimming). For "most of all", *am liebsten*, see the superlatives in 3.2.2d, which are Higher. Use *mögen* for liking things and people: *Ich mag Hunde.*

At grade 9 you put the right ending on every adjective, even in the dative, and compare using *als*, *so … wie* and the irregulars. Instead of *ich mag schwimmen*, you use *gern / lieber* naturally.

::: terms
- **predicative adjective**: an adjective after the verb, with no ending: Das Hotel ist modern.
- **attributive adjective**: an adjective before a noun, which needs an ending: ein modernes Hotel
- **weak ending**: -e or -en after der/die/das and the der-words: der neue Film, mit dem neuen Film
- **mixed ending**: after ein-words; -er / -es where ein has no ending: ein neuer Film, ein neues Buch
- **plural without article**: -e in the nominative and accusative, -en in the dative: gute Noten, mit guten Noten
- **comparative**: adjective + -er, often with an umlaut: klein → kleiner, alt → älter
- **als**: than, after a comparative: Er ist älter als ich.
- **so … wie**: as … as: Das Buch ist so spannend wie der Film.
- **irregular comparative**: gut → besser, hoch → höher, viel → mehr, gern → lieber
- **gern**: placed after the verb to mean like doing: Ich lese gern.
- **lieber**: prefer (doing): Ich fahre lieber mit dem Bus.
- **adverb**: describes a verb; in German it usually has the same form as the adjective: Sie singt gut.
:::

::: method
1. **Is the adjective before the noun?** If not, as after *ist* or *finde*, it takes no ending. Stop.
2. **Find the noun's gender, number and case** (3.2.1a).
3. **What comes before the adjective?** *der/dieser/jeder* → the der-table. *ein/kein/mein* → the ein-table. Nothing, in the plural → -e (nom/acc) or -en (dat).
4. **Default to -en.** The only exceptions are the -e slots (all nominative singulars, plus feminine and neuter accusative) and, after *ein*, **-er** (masculine nominative) and **-es** (neuter nominative/accusative).
5. **Comparing?** Add -er (plus an umlaut if needed) and use *als*, or use *so … wie* for "as … as".
6. **Liking doing something?** Verb + *gern*; preferring → verb + *lieber*.
:::

::: example Endings after ein in three cases
**Translate: "I have a little brother and a big sister. My brother is cheeky, but he has a sweet dog."**

1. "a little brother": *haben*, so accusative masculine after *einen* → **einen kleinen Bruder**.
2. "a big sister": accusative feminine → **eine große Schwester**.
3. "is cheeky": after *ist*, so no ending → **ist frech**.
4. "a sweet dog": accusative masculine → **einen süßen Hund**.

**Ich habe einen kleinen Bruder und eine große Schwester. Mein Bruder ist frech, aber er hat einen süßen Hund.**
:::

::: example Dative endings
**Translate: "We live in an old house with a big garden near the new station."**

1. "in an old house": position, so *in* + dative. Neuter dative after *einem* → **in einem alten Haus**.
2. "with a big garden": *mit* + dative, masculine → **mit einem großen Garten**.
3. "near the new station": *in der Nähe* + *von* + dative, masculine after *dem* → **in der Nähe von dem neuen Bahnhof**, shortened to **vom neuen Bahnhof**.

**Wir wohnen in einem alten Haus mit einem großen Garten in der Nähe vom neuen Bahnhof.**

Every dative adjective ends in **-en**, whatever the article.
:::

::: example Plural with no article
**Translate: "I have nice friends. At the weekend we watch funny films with other young people."**

1. "nice friends": no article, plural, accusative → **nette Freunde**.
2. "funny films": no article, plural, accusative → **lustige Filme**.
3. "with other young people": *mit* + dative plural → **mit anderen Jugendlichen**. The adjective takes -en; *Jugendliche* is itself an adjective used as a noun, so it becomes *Jugendlichen*.

**Ich habe nette Freunde. Am Wochenende sehen wir lustige Filme mit anderen Jugendlichen.**
:::

::: example Comparing and preferring
**Translate: "My sister is older than me, but she is not as tall as me. I like playing tennis, but I prefer swimming."**

1. "older than me": *alt → älter* + *als ich* → **älter als ich**.
2. "not as tall as me": *nicht so groß wie ich*.
3. "I like playing tennis": verb + *gern* → **Ich spiele gern Tennis**.
4. "I prefer swimming": verb + *lieber* → **ich schwimme lieber**.

**Meine Schwester ist älter als ich, aber sie ist nicht so groß wie ich. Ich spiele gern Tennis, aber ich schwimme lieber.**
:::

::: watch
- **No ending after sein and finden**: *Die Stadt ist schön*, never *schöne*.
- **The dative is always -en** after any article: *mit meinem neuen Handy*, *in der alten Stadt*.
- **After ein, show the gender**: *ein alter Mann*, *ein altes Haus*. This is where most errors happen.
- **als, not wie**, after a comparative; **wie** only in *so … wie*.
- **Never *mehr* + adjective** for a comparative: *interessanter*, not *mehr interessant*.
- **Umlauts in comparatives**: *älter, größer, jünger, wärmer*.
- **gern, not *ich mag* + infinitive**, for "like doing": *Ich tanze gern.*
:::
