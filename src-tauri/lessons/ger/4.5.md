## What it is

**Paper 2 Speaking** is 25% of the GCSE: 50 marks, conducted and recorded by your teacher in April or May and marked by an AQA examiner. At Higher tier the test lasts **10 to 12 minutes**, and **marking stops at 12 minutes**, so anything you say after that earns nothing. Straight before the test you get **15 minutes' supervised preparation** with three cards: the role-play, the reading-aloud text and the photo card. You may write **as many notes as you like** on the additional answer sheet, including full sentences and conjugated verbs, and use them at any point in the test. No dictionary. The reading-aloud card and the photo card are always from **different themes**.

**Part 1: Role-play (10 marks, 1 to 1.5 minutes).** The card sets **five tasks in English**; your teacher plays the other person in German. Each task earns **2 marks if the message is conveyed without ambiguity**, 1 if it is partly conveyed or ambiguous, 0 if nothing is conveyed. If a task asks for two details and one is unclear, you get 1. The spec has one fixed rule: **you must ask a question** in one of the tasks. It does not fix the tenses. The wording of each task tells you the time frame, so 'say what you did yesterday' needs a past tense and 'say what you want to do next year' needs a future or *möchte*. Once a task is done, any extra *wrong* information is ignored, but extra words waste time and add risk.

**Part 2: Reading aloud (15 marks, 3 to 3.5 minutes in total).** You read a text of **at least 50 words**, which you prepare silently (you may **not** read it aloud in the preparation room). The words assessed focus on the German **sound-symbol correspondences**. Pronunciation earns **5 marks**: the top mark is for pronunciation that is 'always or nearly always accurate', with at most an occasional minor error. Then the teacher asks **four compulsory questions** on the topic of the text, exactly as printed. They are unprepared and marked **as a whole out of 10**. The top band (9 to 10) needs **all four answered clearly, at least two with an extended response and at least one other developed well**.

**Part 3: Photo card (25 marks, 6 to 7 minutes).** The card has two photos from one theme. The teacher says '**Tell me about the photos**', and you talk for about **1.5 minutes**. This part earns 5 marks, with the top mark for 'a lot of information', 'always conveyed clearly'. You must say at least one thing about **each** photo: talking about only one photo costs a mark. Then comes an **unprepared conversation of 4.5 to 5.5 minutes** on any of the three topics of that theme. It is marked out of 15 for communication and 5 for range and accuracy. The top communication band needs 'a lot of information', '**consistent good development with regular extended responses**' and clarity 'always or nearly always'. The top range band needs a '**wide variety of vocabulary and structures**', with few or no major errors when you attempt complex language. A zero for communication means a zero for language too.

**What separates grade 9.** Every answer in the conversation is *extended*: a direct answer, then a reason, an example, a second time frame and an opinion, in 4 to 6 linked sentences. You use Higher structures naturally: *wenn ich ... hätte, würde ich ...*, *man sollte ...*, *seit* with the present, and two-verb subordinate clauses (*weil ich ... machen musste*). In speech, use the **perfect tense for the past**. The simple past of ordinary verbs is only listed for Higher *written* narrative, but *war*, *hatte*, *gab es* and past modals (*konnte*, *musste*, *wollte*) are all fine in speech.

::: terms
- **supervised preparation time**: the 15 minutes before the test with all three cards. You can take unlimited notes in with you, but no dictionary.
- **role-play task**: one of five instructions in English. Each is worth 2 marks if the message is conveyed without ambiguity.
- **ask a question**: the one fixed role-play rule. Use a question word + verb + subject (*Wann beginnt der Unterricht?*) or put the verb first (*Hast du ein Haustier?*).
- **reading-aloud text**: at least 50 words at Higher. Pronunciation is marked out of 5, and you may not practise it aloud in preparation.
- **compulsory questions**: the four questions after the reading-aloud text, asked exactly as written and marked together out of 10.
- **developed well**: an answer with a reason or extra detail beyond the minimum, e.g. *Ich fahre gern ans Meer, weil ich gern schwimme.*
- **extended response**: several linked sentences adding reason, example, opinion and another time frame. You need at least two of these in the reading-aloud questions for the top band.
- **'Tell me about the photos'**: the teacher's opening prompt on the photo card. Aim for about 1.5 minutes covering both photos.
- **unprepared conversation**: 4.5 to 5.5 minutes on the photo card's theme. It carries 20 of the 25 photo-card marks.
- **major error**: a mistake that affects communication, such as the wrong verb or tense, or a word that changes the meaning.
- **minor error**: a mistake that does not affect communication, such as a wrong gender or adjective ending.
- **wenn-Satz**: *Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich mehr lesen*. A Higher conditional, and one of the quickest ways to show range.
:::

::: method
1. **Preparation, minutes 0 to 4: role-play.** Write a full German answer for every task, and underline the task that needs a question. Check the time frame in each instruction.
2. **Minutes 4 to 8: reading aloud.** Read silently and mark the traps with a pencil: *ei* (eye), *ie* (ee), *w* (v), *v* (f), *z* (ts), *sp-/st-* at the start of a word or word-part (shp, sht), hard or soft *ch*, umlauts, final *-er* (uh), final b/d/g (p/t/k). Then jot 4 or 5 phrases for the likely questions on that topic in past, present and future.
3. **Minutes 8 to 15: photo card.** Write 4 or 5 sentences about each photo: who, where, what they are doing, what they might be feeling, and your opinion. Add one link to your own life in the past or the future.
4. **In the role-play**, say your prepared line clearly, one task at a time. Short, accurate and unambiguous beats long and risky.
5. **In the reading aloud**, read at a steady pace. If you slip, go back and say the word again: the examiner hears the correction.
6. **Answer every conversation question in layers**: answer, reason (*weil*), example or detail, a second time frame (*früher*, *letztes Jahr*, *nächstes Jahr*), then an opinion or a conditional.
7. **Keep talking until the teacher moves on.** Silence cannot be marked. If you do not understand, ask: *Können Sie die Frage bitte wiederholen?*
:::

::: example Role-play: a full card with model answers
Card: *You are talking to your German exchange partner about school.*

| Task on the card | Model answer | Why it scores 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Say what your favourite subject is and give one reason. | *Mein Lieblingsfach ist Geschichte, weil der Lehrer sehr lustig ist.* | Subject + reason, verb sent to the end after *weil* |
| Describe your school uniform (two details). | *Ich trage eine schwarze Jacke und ein weißes Hemd.* | Two details. The endings are accusative: *eine schwarze*, *ein weißes* |
| Say what you did at school yesterday. | *Gestern habe ich in Mathe eine Prüfung geschrieben.* | Past time frame, as the task demands |
| Say what you want to do after your exams. | *Nach den Prüfungen möchte ich eine Ausbildung machen.* | *möchte* + infinitive at the end |
| Ask your friend a question about their school day. | *Wann beginnt der Unterricht bei dir?* | A real question: question word, then verb, then subject |

Notice what is *not* here: no second reasons, no flourishes. Every extra clause is a chance to make the message ambiguous. Save range for Part 3.
:::

::: example Reading aloud: marking the traps
Text (55 words): *Letzten Sommer bin ich mit meiner Familie nach Österreich gefahren. Wir haben in einem kleinen Hotel in den Bergen gewohnt. Jeden Tag sind wir früh aufgestanden und viele Stunden gewandert. Das Wetter war meistens schön, aber manchmal hat es geregnet. Ich möchte nächstes Jahr wieder nach Österreich fahren, weil die Luft dort so frisch ist.*

The traps you pencil in:
- **Sommer, Wetter, aber**: final unstressed *-er* sounds like 'uh', not a hard English 'r'.
- **meiner, meistens, weil**: *ei* = 'eye'. **viele**: *v* = 'f' and *ie* = 'ee', so 'fee-luh'.
- **Wir, Wetter, wieder, gewohnt, gewandert**: *w* = English 'v'.
- **Österreich**: long *ö* (say 'ay' with rounded lips), then *eu* = 'oy' and a soft *ch* after *ei*.
- **Familie**: stress on the second syllable, 'fa-MEE-lyuh'.
- **aufgestanden, Stunden**: *st* at the start of a word or word-part = 'sht'.
- **früh**: long *ü* (say 'ee' with rounded lips); the *h* is silent and only lengthens the vowel.
- **Jeden, Jahr**: *j* = 'y'. **möchte**: soft *ch* after *ö*, not the throaty *ch* of *mochte*.
- **geregnet, Tag, Bergen**: *g* is always hard, and final *-g* in *Tag* sounds like 'k'.
:::

::: example Reading-aloud questions: minimal versus extended
Question: *Was hast du letztes Jahr in den Ferien gemacht?*

**Minimal:** *Ich bin nach Spanien gefahren.* It is clear, but it earns little.

**Extended (grade 9):** *Letzten Sommer bin ich mit meinen Eltern ans Meer gefahren. Wir haben zwei Wochen in einem Zelt direkt am Strand gewohnt, und jeden Tag sind wir im Meer geschwommen. Am besten fand ich einen Ausflug nach Hamburg, weil die Stadt so schön war. Nächstes Jahr würde ich lieber mit meinen Freunden nach Berlin fahren, weil ich die Hauptstadt noch nie gesehen habe.*

This has four sentences, three time frames, a superlative (*am besten*), a conditional (*würde ... fahren*) and a two-verb *weil* clause. It is exactly the 'extended response' the top band asks for.
:::

::: example Photo card: description, then conversation
Theme: *Communication and the world around us*. Photo 1: young people on their phones. Photo 2: teenagers collecting litter in a park.

**'Tell me about the photos' (about 1.5 minutes):** *Auf dem ersten Foto sehe ich vier junge Leute, die im Wohnzimmer auf dem Boden sitzen. Sie haben alle ein Handy in der Hand, und niemand spricht. Vielleicht schreiben sie Nachrichten oder sehen Videos. Auf dem zweiten Foto gibt es eine Gruppe von Jugendlichen in einem Park. Sie sammeln Müll, und im Hintergrund sieht man viele Bäume. Es ist sonnig, und sie scheinen glücklich zu sein. Ich finde das zweite Foto positiver, weil die Jugendlichen etwas für die Umwelt tun. Letzte Woche habe ich auch mit meiner Klasse Müll gesammelt.*

**Teacher:** *Wie benutzt du dein Handy?*

**Extended answer:** *Ich benutze mein Handy jeden Tag, um mit meinen Freunden in Kontakt zu bleiben. Früher habe ich jeden Abend viele Stunden Videos gesehen, aber dann habe ich schlecht geschlafen und war in der Schule immer müde. Seit einem Jahr lasse ich mein Handy nach neun Uhr in der Küche. Ich finde, man sollte klare Regeln haben, weil soziale Medien süchtig machen können. Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich lieber mehr Sport treiben.*

The answer uses *um ... zu*, the perfect tense, *seit* with the present, *sollte* + infinitive, a two-verb *weil* clause and *hätte/würde*. That is a 'wide variety of vocabulary and structures', said in under a minute.
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher speaking test, and what happens after 12 minutes?
---
10 to 12 minutes. Marking stops at 12 minutes, so anything said later earns nothing.
:::

::: q
What is the one fixed rule the spec sets for every role-play?
---
You must ask a question in one of the five tasks.
:::

::: q
A role-play task asks for two details and you give one clearly and one ambiguously. What do you score?
---
1 mark out of 2: the message is only partly conveyed.
:::

::: q
Can you practise the reading-aloud text out loud during preparation?
---
No. You may only read it silently and mark it up. Aloud practice is not allowed in the preparation room.
:::

::: q
What does the top band for the four reading-aloud questions require?
---
All four answered clearly, at least two with an extended response and at least one other developed well.
:::

::: q
What happens if you describe only one of the two photos?
---
You lose one mark from the 5 for the description, unless your mark would already be 1.
:::

::: q
Why use the perfect tense rather than the simple past (*ich spielte*) when speaking about the past?
---
The simple past of ordinary verbs is listed only for Higher written narrative. In speech the perfect is natural and expected. *war*, *hatte* and the past modals are fine in both.
:::

::: q
Give the five layers of an extended conversation answer.
---
A direct answer, a reason, an example or detail, a second time frame, and an opinion or conditional.
:::

::: watch
- **The role-play question**: it must be a real German question with the verb in the right place. A statement said with rising intonation is risky.
- **Time frames in role-play tasks**: 'yesterday', 'last weekend' and 'next year' in the English are instructions. Answer in that tense.
- **Reading aloud**: *ei/ie* swapped, English *w* for German *w*, a hard 'r' at the end of *-er* words, *st/sp* at the start said as in English, and umlauts ignored (*schon* for *schön*).
- **Minimal answers in the conversation** cap you in the lower bands, however accurate they are. Develop every answer.
- **Only one photo described** costs a mark. Say at least one thing about each.
- **Over-rehearsed monologues** that do not answer the question asked earn little for communication. Adapt your prepared material to the question.
- **Running out of time**: nothing after 12 minutes is marked, so do not spend 3 minutes on the role-play.
:::

::: test
Give German role-play answers for these two tasks. (a) Say what you did last weekend (one detail). (b) Ask your friend what their favourite film is. (4)
---
(a) Past-tense sentence with a correct auxiliary and participle, e.g. *Letztes Wochenende habe ich Fußball gespielt* (1); the message is unambiguous (1). (b) A question form, e.g. *Was ist dein Lieblingsfilm?* (1); verb in second place after the question word (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how each role-play task is marked. (2)
---
Up to 2 marks per task: 2 if the message is conveyed without ambiguity (1); 1 if partly conveyed or ambiguous, 0 if nothing is conveyed (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how to pronounce the marked part of each word: (a) *spät*, (b) *Wetter*, (c) *Zeit*. (3)
---
(a) *sp-* at the start = 'shp', and a long *ä* like 'ai' in 'fair' (1). (b) *w* = English 'v', and final *-er* = 'uh' (1). (c) *z* = 'ts', and *ei* = 'eye' (1).
:::

::: test
Extend this minimal answer into a developed answer of at least three sentences with two time frames: *Ich mag Sport.* (4)
---
Any accurate answer with a reason (1), a detail or example (1), a second time frame (1) and an opinion or conditional (1). For example: *Ich mag Sport, weil ich gern mit meinen Freunden zusammen bin. Jeden Samstag spiele ich Fußball in einem Verein. Letztes Jahr haben wir ein wichtiges Spiel gewonnen. Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich auch schwimmen gehen.*
:::

::: test
Write a Higher-tier sentence using *wenn* + *hätte* + *würde* about the environment. (2)
---
For example *Wenn ich ein Auto hätte, würde ich trotzdem mit dem Fahrrad fahren*. *hätte* at the end of the *wenn* clause (1); *würde ich* + infinitive at the end in the main clause (1).
:::

::: test
Correct the three errors in this spoken answer: *Letzte Jahr ich habe nach Italien gefahren, weil meine Familie will Rom sehen.* (3)
---
*Letztes Jahr* (neuter ending) and verb second: *Letztes Jahr bin ich ...* (1); *gefahren* takes *sein*: *bin ... gefahren* (1); verb to the end after *weil*: *weil meine Familie Rom sehen wollte* (or *will*) (1).
:::

::: test
How should you split the 15 minutes of preparation, and what can you take into the test? (3)
---
About 4 minutes for the role-play, writing full answers (1); about 4 for marking the reading-aloud traps and preparing likely questions, 7 for the photo card (1); unlimited notes on the additional answer sheet, usable at any point, but no dictionary (1).
:::

::: test
List three things the top band for the unprepared conversation rewards. (3)
---
Any three: a lot of information conveyed (1); consistent good development with regular extended responses (1); information always or nearly always conveyed clearly (1); a wide variety of vocabulary and structures with few or no major errors in complex language (1).
:::
