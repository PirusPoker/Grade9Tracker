## What it is

This is the **Higher** extension of 3.2.1a and 3.2.1b. It covers weak masculine nouns, adjectives used as nouns (*die Reichen*, *das Gute*, *etwas Neues*), and the **genitive**, which you only need to recognise in Listening and Reading. It adds the **plural object pronouns**, the order of two objects, **relative clauses with *wo* and *was***, and **dative reflexive pronouns**. It also includes the Higher word-building suffixes *-chen/-lein*, *-heit/-keit* and *-los*.

These structures appear in the harder Reading and Listening texts and in the Higher translation into German. They also let you write the kind of sentence the 150-word task rewards: *Das Beste, was ich je gemacht habe…*, or *Man sollte den Armen helfen*. Each one is a way of showing complexity with listed grammar.

**Weak masculine nouns** such as *der Junge, der Mensch, der Kollege, der Kunde, der Student, der Tourist, der Polizist, der Nachbar* and *der Patient* make their plural with **-(e)n**: *die Jungen, die Menschen, die Studenten, die Nachbarn*. In the singular they also add -(e)n in every case except the nominative (*Ich kenne den Jungen*, *mit dem Studenten*). AQA does not credit that singular form individually, but it is correct German.

**Adjectival nouns.** An adjective with a capital letter becomes a noun and keeps its adjective ending. You need them in the nominative and accusative:
- plural people: *die Reichen* (the rich), *die Armen* (the poor), *die Jugendlichen*, *die Erwachsenen*, *die Deutschen*
- abstract neuter ideas: *das Gute* (the good thing), *das Beste* (the best thing)
- after *etwas, viel, wenig* → **-es**: *etwas Neues* (something new), *viel Interessantes*, *wenig Gutes*; after *alles* → **-e**: *alles Gute* (all the best)

**The genitive** (Listening and Reading only) shows possession, "of": *das Auto **meines Vaters*** (my father's car), *die Hälfte **der Schüler*** (half of the pupils). It also follows listed prepositions such as ***trotz*** (despite), ***wegen*** (because of), ***während*** (during) and ***statt*** (instead of): *trotz **des Wetters***, *wegen **der Kosten***. Look for **des / eines** and a noun ending in **-s / -es** (masculine and neuter), or **der / einer** (feminine and plural).

**All the personal pronouns.** The Higher additions are in bold.

| nom | ich | du | er | sie | es | wir | ihr | Sie | sie |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| acc | mich | dich | ihn | sie | es | **uns** | **euch** | Sie | **sie** |
| dat | mir | dir | ihm | ihr | ihm | **uns** | **euch** | Ihnen | **ihnen** |

**Two objects.** With two nouns, the **dative comes before the accusative**: *Ich gebe **meinem Bruder das Buch***. With one pronoun and one noun, the **pronoun comes first**, whatever its case: *Ich gebe **ihm** das Buch. Ich gebe **es** meinem Bruder.*

**Relative clauses with wo and was** (verb to the end). Use ***wo*** for a place: *die Stadt, **wo** ich wohne*. Use ***was*** after *alles, etwas, nichts, das* and superlative neuter nouns: *alles, **was** ich brauche*; *das Beste, **was** ich gegessen habe*.

**Dative reflexive pronouns** (*mir, dir, sich, uns, euch, sich*) are used when the verb already has a separate accusative object, and with listed verbs such as *sich (dat.) vorstellen* (to imagine), *sich (dat.) ansehen* (to have a look at), *sich (dat.) merken* (to remember, note) and *sich (dat.) leisten* (to afford): *Ich kann **mir** das nicht leisten.* Only *mir* and *dir* differ from the accusative forms.

**Word building (Higher).** **-chen / -lein** mean "little" and always make the noun **neuter**, usually with an umlaut: *das Häuschen, das Kätzchen, das Brüderchen*. **-heit / -keit** turn adjectives into **feminine** nouns ("-ness", "-ty"): *frei → die Freiheit, gesund → die Gesundheit, möglich → die Möglichkeit, schwierig → die Schwierigkeit*. **-los** means "-less" or "without": *arbeitslos* (unemployed), *nutzlos* (useless), *kostenlos* (free of charge).

::: terms
- **weak masculine noun**: a masculine noun that adds -(e)n in the plural and all singular cases except the nominative: der Junge → die Jungen
- **adjectival noun**: an adjective used as a noun with a capital and adjective endings: die Reichen, ein Jugendlicher
- **abstract neuter noun**: das + adjective with a capital for an idea: das Gute, das Beste
- **etwas / viel / wenig + -es**: something / much / little + adjective as a noun: etwas Neues, viel Gutes
- **alles + -e**: everything + adjective as a noun: Alles Gute zum Geburtstag!
- **genitive**: the case for of / 's, recognised in Listening and Reading only: die Farbe des Autos
- **genitive preposition**: trotz, wegen, während, statt + genitive: wegen des Wetters
- **plural object pronouns**: accusative uns, euch, Sie, sie; dative uns, euch, Ihnen, ihnen
- **object order**: dative noun before accusative noun; a pronoun before a noun: Ich zeige ihr das Foto.
- **wo as relative**: where, for places: das Dorf, wo ich geboren bin
- **was as relative**: that / which after alles, etwas, nichts, das, and superlatives: alles, was du willst
- **dative reflexive**: mir, dir, sich, uns, euch, sich: Ich sehe mir den Film an.
- **diminutive**: -chen / -lein for little, always neuter: das Kätzchen (kitten)
- **-heit / -keit**: suffix making a feminine noun from an adjective: die Freiheit, die Möglichkeit
- **-los**: suffix meaning -less / without: arbeitslos, nutzlos
:::

::: method
1. **A person noun ending in -e, or on the weak list?** Make the plural with -(e)n: *die Kunden, die Touristen*.
2. **"The rich / the young / the good thing"?** Capitalise the adjective and give it its adjective ending: *die Reichen, das Gute*. After *etwas/viel/wenig* add -es; after *alles* add -e.
3. **Reading a des / eines / -s noun?** It is genitive: translate it as "of the" or "'s". Do not use it in your own writing unless you are sure.
4. **Replacing a plural noun?** Accusative *uns / euch / sie / Sie*; dative *uns / euch / ihnen / Ihnen*.
5. **Two objects?** Two nouns: dative then accusative. A pronoun and a noun: the pronoun first.
6. **"where" or "what/that" in a relative clause?** Place → *wo*; after *alles, etwas, nichts, das Beste* → *was*. Send the verb to the end.
7. **Reflexive verb with another object?** Use the dative reflexive (*mir, dir*): *Ich stelle mir das vor.*
:::

::: example Two objects and pronoun swaps
**Start with: "I give my sister the present."**

1. Two nouns: dative *meiner Schwester* before accusative *das Geschenk* → **Ich gebe meiner Schwester das Geschenk.**
2. Replace the sister with a pronoun: feminine dative *ihr*. The pronoun comes first → **Ich gebe ihr das Geschenk.**
3. Replace the present instead: neuter accusative *es*. The pronoun comes first → **Ich gebe es meiner Schwester.**

Now with plurals: "I show my parents the photos. I show them the photos." → **Ich zeige meinen Eltern die Fotos. Ich zeige ihnen die Fotos.**
:::

::: example Relative clauses with wo and was
**Translate: "Hamburg is the city where I was born. Everything that I earn, I spend on clothes."**

1. "the city where": a place, so **wo**, with the verbs to the end: *die Stadt, wo ich geboren bin*. This is a two-verb subordinate clause, so the finite verb *bin* goes last (3.2.2d).
2. "Everything that I earn": after *alles* use **was**: *Alles, was ich verdiene*.
3. The whole relative structure is idea 1, so the main verb comes next: *gebe ich … aus* (*ausgeben*, separable).

**Hamburg ist die Stadt, wo ich geboren bin. Alles, was ich verdiene, gebe ich für Kleidung aus.**
:::

::: example Adjectival nouns in an argument
**Translate: "The rich must support the poor. Young people often do something good, and that is the best thing about our town."**

1. "the rich": plural adjectival noun, nominative → **Die Reichen**.
2. "the poor": plural, accusative → **die Armen**: *müssen die Armen unterstützen*.
3. "Young people" → **Die Jugendlichen** (listed).
4. "something good": *etwas* + -es → **etwas Gutes**.
5. "the best thing": abstract neuter → **das Beste**.

**Die Reichen müssen die Armen unterstützen. Die Jugendlichen machen oft etwas Gutes, und das ist das Beste an unserer Stadt.**
:::

::: example Recognising the genitive in Reading
*Wegen des schlechten Wetters hat das Konzert spät begonnen. Trotz der langen Pause hat die Hälfte der Besucher bis zum Ende gewartet. Die Tochter meines Nachbarn war auch da.*

- *wegen des schlechten Wetters* → "because of the bad weather"
- *trotz der langen Pause* → "despite the long break"
- *die Hälfte der Besucher* → "half of the visitors"
- *die Tochter meines Nachbarn* → "my neighbour's daughter" (*Nachbar* is weak, so the genitive is *-n*)

"Because of the bad weather the concert started late. Despite the long break, half of the visitors waited until the end. My neighbour's daughter was there too."
:::

::: watch
- **Pronoun before noun, dative noun before accusative noun**: *Ich gebe es meinem Vater*, *Ich gebe meinem Vater das Geld*.
- **ihnen vs Ihnen**: a small *i* means "to them", a capital means formal "to you". Check the capital in the role-play and in formal letters.
- **Adjectival nouns keep adjective endings**: *die Reichen* (plural), *etwas Neues*, but *alles Gute*.
- **wo for places, was after alles/etwas/nichts/das Beste**: *alles, das* is wrong.
- **The genitive is for recognition.** In your own writing, *von* + dative is always acceptable: *das Auto von meinem Vater*.
- **Dative reflexive** when there is another object: *Ich sehe mir den Film an*.
- **-chen nouns are always neuter**: *das Mädchen*, *das Häuschen*.
:::
