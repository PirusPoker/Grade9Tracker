## What it is

Every German noun has a **gender**: masculine (**der**), feminine (**die**) or neuter (**das**). The plural article is always **die**. The article then changes with the noun's job in the sentence, its **case**. At Foundation you need three cases: the **nominative** for the subject, the **accusative** for the direct object, and the **dative** for the indirect object ("to/for someone") and after dative prepositions. **kein** ("not a", "no", "not any") takes exactly the same endings as **ein**. Every noun starts with a capital letter, in the middle of a sentence too.

This topic is in every paper. In Paper 4 the translation into German (at least 50 words at Higher) is marked in 15 meaning elements plus a 5-mark grid for vocabulary and grammar, and the wrong article is the error examiners see most. In the Paper 1 dictation (10 marks, at least 30 words at Higher) you must hear *den* versus *dem* and remember the capital on every noun. In Reading and Listening, the case often tells you who is doing what to whom.

**The article table.** Learn it across, not down: only the masculine changes in the accusative, and the dative changes everything.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **nominative** (subject) | der / ein / kein | die / eine / keine | das / ein / kein | die / – / keine |
| **accusative** (direct object) | **den / einen / keinen** | die / eine / keine | das / ein / kein | die / – / keine |
| **dative** (indirect object) | **dem / einem / keinem** | **der / einer / keiner** | **dem / einem / keinem** | **den** / – / **keinen** + noun **-n** |

AQA lists *kein* for the nominative and accusative only at this level. The dative forms (*keinem*, *keiner*) simply copy *ein*.

**Plurals.** There is no single rule, so learn each plural with the noun. These patterns cover most of the list:

| pattern | examples |
|---|---|
| most masculine and neuter: **-e** (sometimes with an umlaut) | der Tag → die Tage, der Zug → die Züge, das Jahr → die Jahre |
| nouns in **-el/-en/-er**: article only (sometimes an umlaut) | der Lehrer → die Lehrer, das Zimmer → die Zimmer, der Garten → die Gärten |
| most feminine: **-n / -en** | die Schule → die Schulen, die Frau → die Frauen, die Zeitung → die Zeitungen |
| feminine **-in**: **-nen** | die Lehrerin → die Lehrerinnen, die Ärztin → die Ärztinnen |
| some masculine and neuter: **-er** plus umlaut | das Buch → die Bücher, das Haus → die Häuser, das Kind → die Kinder, der Mann → die Männer |
| borrowed words: **-s** | das Handy → die Handys, das Auto → die Autos, das Hotel → die Hotels |
| some one-syllable feminine: **-e** plus umlaut | die Stadt → die Städte, die Hand → die Hände, die Nacht → die Nächte |
| **-nis**: **-nisse** | das Zeugnis → die Zeugnisse, das Ergebnis → die Ergebnisse |
| some neuter: **-(e)n** | das Auge → die Augen, das Bett → die Betten, das Ende → die Enden |

Some nouns have no plural at all (*Obst*, *Eis*), and listed irregulars must be learnt (*der Bus → die Busse*, *die Firma → die Firmen*, *das Museum → die Museen*). In the **dative plural**, add **-n** to the noun unless it already ends in -n or -s: *mit den Kindern*, *in den Städten*, but *mit den Frauen*, *mit den Autos*.

**Building nouns.** A **compound noun** takes its gender from the **last** word: *die Stadt + das Zentrum = das Stadtzentrum*. Many add a linking letter (*die Arbeitsstunde*, *das Geburtstagsgeschenk*), and some drop an -e (*die Schule + das Buch = das Schulbuch*). Adjectives and verbs compound too: *dunkelgrün*, *ausgehen*. Male person nouns add **-in** for the female: *der Freund → die Freundin*, *der Schüler → die Schülerin*. An infinitive used as a noun is neuter with a capital: *schwimmen → das Schwimmen*, *wandern → das Wandern*. Languages work the same way: *Englisch*, *Deutsch*.

A grade 9 answer gets the case right automatically, including the traps: accusative after **es gibt** and **haben**, the dative plural **-n**, and **kein** instead of *nicht ein*. The **genitive** (*des Vaters*, *trotz des Wetters*) is Higher and only has to be recognised in Listening and Reading. It is covered in 3.2.2a.

::: terms
- **gender**: every noun is masculine (der), feminine (die) or neuter (das), e.g. der Hund, die Katze, das Pferd
- **case**: the form an article takes to show the noun's job: nominative, accusative or dative
- **nominative**: the case of the subject, the person or thing doing the verb: Der Hund schläft.
- **accusative**: the case of the direct object, the thing the verb is done to: Ich habe einen Hund.
- **dative**: the case of the indirect object (to/for someone) and after dative prepositions: Ich schicke dem Lehrer eine E-Mail.
- **definite article**: der, die, das (the), which changes to den, dem and so on with case
- **indefinite article**: ein, eine (a, an). It has no plural: Ich habe Geschwister.
- **kein**: not a, no, not any. It takes the endings of ein and replaces nicht ein: Ich habe keinen Bruder.
- **compound noun**: two or more words joined; the last word sets the gender: das Schulbuch, die Hausaufgabe
- **dative plural -n**: an -n added to the plural noun in the dative: mit meinen Freunden
- **nominalised infinitive**: a verb used as a neuter noun with a capital: das Schwimmen, das Lesen
- **feminine person noun**: a female form made by adding -in: die Lehrerin, die Ärztin (plural -innen)
- **es gibt + accusative**: there is / there are: Es gibt einen Park und keinen Bahnhof.
:::

::: method
1. **Find the noun and its gender.** Learn every noun with its article. For a compound, look at the last part.
2. **Decide its job.** Ask who or what is doing the verb (nominative), what the verb is done to (accusative), and who it is given, shown or sent to (dative). The words after *sein*, *werden* and *heißen* stay nominative: *Das ist ein Hund.*
3. **Check for a trigger.** *es gibt* and *haben* always take the accusative. A dative preposition (*mit*, *bei*, *zu*, *von*, *aus*, *nach*) forces the dative (see 3.2.1h).
4. **Pick the article from the table.** Masculine accusative = *den / einen / keinen*. Dative = *dem / der / dem / den*.
5. **Plural?** Use the right plural form. In the dative plural add **-n** to the noun unless it already ends in -n or -s.
6. **Negating a noun?** Use *kein* with the same ending *ein* would have had, never *nicht ein*.
7. **Final check.** Capital letter on every noun, including nominalised verbs such as *das Lesen*.
:::

::: example Translation: there is / there isn't
**Translate: "In my town there is a station, a church and a cinema, but there is no park."**

1. "there is" = *es gibt*, which always takes the **accusative**.
2. *der Bahnhof* is masculine, so the accusative is **einen Bahnhof**.
3. *die Kirche* is feminine, so the accusative stays **eine Kirche**.
4. *das Kino* is neuter, so it stays **ein Kino**.
5. "no park": *der Park* is masculine accusative, so **keinen Park**.
6. The sentence starts with "In my town", so the verb comes second: *gibt es* (3.2.1d).

**In meiner Stadt gibt es einen Bahnhof, eine Kirche und ein Kino, aber es gibt keinen Park.**
:::

::: example Nominative, accusative and dative in one sentence
**Translate: "The teacher sends the girl an email."**

1. Who sends? The teacher, so this is the subject: **der Lehrer** (nominative).
2. What is sent? An email, so this is the direct object. *die E-Mail* is feminine, so it stays **eine E-Mail**.
3. Who is it sent to? The girl, so this is the indirect object. *das Mädchen* is neuter, so the dative is **dem Mädchen**.
4. With two nouns the dative comes before the accusative. The rule itself is Higher (3.2.2a), but copy it now.

**Der Lehrer schickt dem Mädchen eine E-Mail.**

Swap the genders and watch the articles move: *Die Lehrerin schickt dem Vater einen Brief.* (*der Vater* becomes dative *dem Vater*; *der Brief* becomes accusative *einen Brief*.)
:::

::: example Plurals and the dative plural
**Translate: "The children play with the dogs in the gardens."**

1. *das Kind* → plural **die Kinder** (the -er pattern). This is the subject, so it is nominative.
2. *der Hund* → plural *die Hunde*. After *mit* (dative) this becomes **den Hunden**, with the extra -n.
3. *der Garten* → plural *die Gärten*. After *in* for position it is dative (3.2.1h): **in den Gärten**. There is no extra -n, because it already ends in -n.

**Die Kinder spielen mit den Hunden in den Gärten.**
:::

::: example Compound nouns and gender
Work out the gender from the **last** part:

- *das Haus* + *die Aufgabe* → **die Hausaufgabe**; plural *die Hausaufgaben*
- *der Geburtstag* + *das Geschenk* → **das Geburtstagsgeschenk** (linking -s)
- *die Stadt* + *das Zentrum* → **das Stadtzentrum**
- *wohnen* + *das Zimmer* → **das Wohnzimmer** (a verb stem as the first part)

So "I've got no homework today" is *Heute habe ich keine Hausaufgaben*: feminine plural, accusative *keine*.
:::

Fill the gap with the right form of *ein*: *Ich habe ___ Bruder und ___ Schwester.*
---
einen (masculine accusative), eine (feminine accusative)
:::

::: q
Translate into German: "There is no cinema in my village."
---
Es gibt kein Kino in meinem Dorf. (Or: In meinem Dorf gibt es kein Kino.) *das Kino* is neuter, so the accusative is *kein*.
:::

::: q
Error-spotting: *Wir spielen oft mit den Kinder im Park.* Find and correct the error.
---
The dative plural needs -n: *mit den Kindern*.
:::

::: q
Give the plural of *der Lehrer*, *die Zeitung*, *das Buch* and *das Handy*.
---
die Lehrer, die Zeitungen, die Bücher, die Handys
:::

::: q
What is the gender of *Fußballspiel*, and why?
---
Neuter, *das Fußballspiel*, because the last part is *das Spiel*.
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *Die Ärztin gibt dem Mann ein Glas Wasser.*
---
The doctor (female) gives the man a glass of water. *die Ärztin* is the subject; *dem Mann* is the dative, "to the man".
:::

::: q
Error-spotting: *Ich habe nicht einen Hund, aber ich habe ein Pferd.*
---
*nicht einen* should be *keinen*: *Ich habe keinen Hund, aber ich habe ein Pferd.*
:::

::: q
Reorder into a correct sentence: *Kuchen / Mutter / einen / meine / kauft*
---
Meine Mutter kauft einen Kuchen. (Or, with inversion: Einen Kuchen kauft meine Mutter.)
:::

::: watch
- **Masculine accusative** is the most common error at every grade: *Ich habe einen Bruder*, *Es gibt einen Park*, *Ich trinke keinen Kaffee*.
- **kein, not nicht ein**, whenever you negate a noun that would have *ein* or no article.
- **Dative plural -n**: *mit meinen Freunden*, *in den Ferien*. Examiners use it to separate grade 7 from grade 9 translations.
- **Capital letters** on every noun, including *das Lesen* and *das Schwimmen*. In the dictation a lower-case noun counts as a transcription error.
- **Compound gender comes from the last word**: *die Hausaufgabe*, not *das*.
- Do not use an article with a job after *sein*: *Mein Vater ist Lehrer*, not *ein Lehrer*.
- The genitive is for recognition only. Do not force *des* into your own writing.
:::

::: test
Explain the difference between *Der Hund sieht den Mann* and *Den Hund sieht der Mann*, and translate both. (3)
---
First: the dog sees the man (1). Second: the man sees the dog (1). The case, not the word order, shows who does what: *der* marks the subject and *den* the object (1).
