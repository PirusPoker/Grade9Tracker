## What it is

A **preposition** (with, for, in, to…) fixes the **case** of the noun after it. You cannot choose the case yourself: you learn which case each preposition takes. At Foundation AQA lists three groups. Four take the **accusative**, six take the **dative**, and three are **dual-case**: accusative for movement *to* a place, dative for position *at* a place.

| accusative | dative | dual case (acc = movement / dat = position) |
|---|---|---|
| **bis** (until) | **aus** (out of, from) | **an** (at, on, to) |
| **durch** (through) | **bei** (at someone's, with) | **auf** (on, onto) |
| **für** (for) | **mit** (with, by) | **in** (in, into) |
| **ohne** (without) | **nach** (after, to a town or country) | |
| | **von** (from, of) | |
| | **zu** (to) | |

Ask **wohin?** (where to?) → accusative: *Ich gehe **in die** Stadt.* Ask **wo?** (where?) → dative: *Ich wohne **in der** Stadt.* Some prepositions shorten with the article, and you are expected to use these forms: **ans** (an das), **am** (an dem), **beim** (bei dem), **ins** (in das), **im** (in dem), **vom** (von dem), **zum** (zu dem), **zur** (zu der). *nach* is for towns, countries and home (*nach Berlin*, *nach Hause*). *zu* is for people and buildings (*zu meiner Oma*, *zum Bahnhof*).

Some verbs have a fixed preposition, which is listed with the verb in the AQA vocabulary: *warten **auf*** (wait for), *sich interessieren **für*** (be interested in), *sich freuen **auf*** (look forward to). The listed da-compounds *darauf, damit, dafür, davon* replace a preposition + "it": *Ich interessiere mich **dafür**.* The extra dual-case prepositions (*über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen*) and *gegen, um, seit, laut* are Higher: see 3.2.2d.

**Word building (derivational morphology).** AQA can put a derived word in a Reading text even if only the base word is on the list, so you need to decode these patterns:

| pattern | meaning | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Lieblings-** + noun | favourite | das Lieblingsfach, der Lieblingsfilm, das Lieblingsessen |
| **Haupt-** + noun | main | die Hauptstadt (capital), die Hauptstraße |
| **un-** + adjective | the opposite | unmöglich, unglücklich, unfreundlich, ungesund |
| number + **-te** (1–19), **-ste** (20+) | ordinal (1st, 2nd…) | zweite, vierte, zehnte, zwanzigste, einunddreißigste |
| verb stem + **-ung** | a feminine noun | lösen → die Lösung, wohnen → die Wohnung, meinen → die Meinung |
| verb stem + **-er** | a person who does it (masculine) | besuchen → der Besucher, spielen → der Spieler, fahren → der Fahrer |
| day / time of day + **-s** | every… (adverb, lower case) | montags, samstags, morgens, nachmittags, abends |

The ordinals *erste* (1st), *dritte* (3rd), *siebte* (7th) and *achte* (8th) are irregular. Ordinals take adjective endings: *am **ersten** Mai*, *am **zwanzigsten** Juli*. Female agent nouns add *-in*: *die Spielerin*.

You will need prepositions every time you say where, when, how and with whom. That means every photo card, every 90-word task and every translation. A grade 9 answer never loses the case after a preposition, uses *am, im, zum, zur* and *ins* naturally, and distinguishes *ins Kino gehen* from *im Kino sein*. In Reading it decodes words like *Unsicherheit* or *Besucher* from their parts.

::: terms
- **preposition**: a small word such as mit, für or in that sets the case of the noun after it
- **accusative preposition**: bis, durch, für, ohne: für meinen Bruder, durch den Park
- **dative preposition**: aus, bei, mit, nach, von, zu: mit dem Zug, bei meiner Oma
- **dual-case preposition**: an, auf, in: accusative for movement (ins Kino), dative for position (im Kino)
- **wohin? / wo?**: the test for dual-case prepositions: where to? = accusative; where? = dative
- **contraction**: preposition + article in one word: am, im, ins, beim, vom, zum, zur, ans
- **nach vs zu**: nach for towns, countries and home (nach Wien, nach Hause); zu for people and buildings (zur Schule)
- **da-compound**: da(r) + preposition meaning with it, for it, and so on: damit, dafür, darauf, davon
- **Lieblings-**: prefix meaning favourite: meine Lieblingsband, mein Lieblingsfach
- **Haupt-**: prefix meaning main: der Hauptbahnhof, die Hauptstadt
- **un-**: prefix giving the opposite of an adjective: möglich → unmöglich
- **ordinal number**: first, second…: number + -te (up to 19) or -ste (20 upwards): der zweite, der zwanzigste
- **-ung noun**: a feminine noun made from a verb: bewegen → die Bewegung
- **agent noun**: verb stem + -er for the person who does it: lesen → der Leser
- **-s adverb**: day or time + -s for a habit: sonntags (on Sundays), abends (in the evenings)
:::

::: method
1. **Spot the preposition** and recall its group: accusative (*bis, durch, für, ohne*), dative (*aus, bei, mit, nach, von, zu*) or dual (*an, auf, in*).
2. **Dual case?** Ask *wohin?* (movement to) → accusative, or *wo?* (position) → dative.
3. **Find the noun's gender and number** and choose the article or adjective ending for that case (3.2.1a, 3.2.1g).
4. **Contract** where German does: *in dem → im*, *zu der → zur*, *an dem → am*.
5. **Towns and countries**: *nach Berlin*, but *in die Schweiz*, because countries with an article take *in*. People and places in town take *zu*: *zum Arzt*.
6. **Unknown word in Reading?** Strip off *un-, Lieblings-, Haupt-, -ung, -er, -s* and look at the base word.
:::

::: example A journey full of prepositions
**Translate: "After school I go into town by bus with my friend. We buy a present for my mother and then we go to the cinema."**

1. "After school": *nach* + dative, *die Schule* → **nach der Schule**. It is idea 1, so the verb comes next: *fahre ich*.
2. "into town": movement, so *in* + accusative → **in die Stadt**.
3. "by bus": *mit* + dative → **mit dem Bus**.
4. "with my friend": *mit* + dative, masculine → **mit meinem Freund**. Put it in Time – Manner – Place order: manner phrases before *in die Stadt*.
5. "for my mother": *für* + accusative, feminine → **für meine Mutter**.
6. "to the cinema": movement into a building, so **ins Kino** (in das).

**Nach der Schule fahre ich mit meinem Freund mit dem Bus in die Stadt. Wir kaufen ein Geschenk für meine Mutter und dann gehen wir ins Kino.**
:::

::: example Movement or position?
| wohin? → accusative | wo? → dative |
|---|---|
| Ich gehe **in den** Park. | Ich spiele **im** Park. |
| Wir fahren **an den** Strand. | Wir liegen **am** Strand. |
| Ich lege das Buch **auf den** Tisch. | Das Buch liegt **auf dem** Tisch. |
| Sie geht **in die** Küche. | Sie kocht **in der** Küche. |

The verb usually tells you which. *gehen, fahren, legen* describe movement; *sein, wohnen, liegen, spielen* describe position.
:::

::: example Decoding derived words in a Reading text
*Samstags besuche ich den Hauptbahnhof. Mein Lieblingszug ist der ICE, aber für viele Besucher ist die Fahrt zu teuer. Das finde ich unfair. Die Lösung? Am zwanzigsten Juni ist die Fahrt billiger.*

- *samstags* → *Samstag* + -s → "on Saturdays"
- *Hauptbahnhof* → *Haupt-* + *Bahnhof* → "main station"
- *Lieblingszug* → "favourite train"
- *Besucher* → *besuchen* + -er → "visitors"
- *unfair* → *un-* + *fair* → "unfair"
- *Lösung* → *lösen* + -ung → "solution"
- *zwanzigsten* → 20 + -ste(n) → "twentieth"

Translation: "On Saturdays I visit the main station. My favourite train is the ICE, but for many visitors the journey is too expensive. I think that is unfair. The solution? On the twentieth of June the journey is cheaper."
:::

::: watch
- **mit and zu always take the dative**: *mit dem Bus*, *zur Schule*. *mit den Bus* is one of the commonest errors.
- **für and ohne always take the accusative**: *für meinen Vater*.
- **in: movement or position?** *Ich gehe ins Kino* but *Ich bin im Kino*.
- **nach for places on the map, zu for people and buildings**: *nach Hamburg*, *zum Supermarkt*, *nach Hause* (going home) but *zu Hause* (at home).
- **Contract**: *in dem Park* is not wrong, but *im Park* is what a fluent writer uses.
- **Ordinals need endings**: *am ersten*, *der zweite Tag*.
- In Reading, do not skip a long word. Break it down: *Lieblings-*, *Haupt-*, *un-*, *-ung*.
:::
