## What it is

The **present tense** is the tense you will use most in every paper. German has only one present tense, so *ich spiele* means "I play", "I am playing" and "I do play". With a time word it also means the future: *Morgen fahre ich nach Berlin* ("Tomorrow I'm going to Berlin"). In stories and articles it is sometimes used to tell events in the past, which you only need to recognise in Reading.

To form it, take the **stem** (the infinitive minus *-en*: *spiel-*, *wohn-*, *lern-*) and add the endings below. **Weak (regular) verbs** never change their stem. **Strong verbs** keep the same endings but change the vowel in the *du* and *er/sie/es/man* forms: *fahren → du fährst, er fährt*; *essen → du isst, sie isst*; *sehen → du siehst, er sieht*. Four very common verbs, **haben, sein, werden and wissen**, are irregular and must be learnt by heart.

| | spielen (weak) | fahren (a → ä) | sprechen (e → i) | lesen (e → ie) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | spiel**e** | fahr**e** | sprech**e** | les**e** |
| du | spiel**st** | f**ä**hr**st** | spr**i**ch**st** | l**ie**st |
| er / sie / es / man | spiel**t** | f**ä**hr**t** | spr**i**ch**t** | l**ie**st |
| wir | spiel**en** | fahr**en** | sprech**en** | les**en** |
| ihr | spiel**t** | fahr**t** | sprech**t** | les**t** |
| sie / Sie | spiel**en** | fahr**en** | sprech**en** | les**en** |

Other strong verbs from the AQA list: **a → ä** *schlafen, tragen, gefallen, fallen*, and **au → äu** *laufen (läuft)*; **e → i** *essen (isst), geben, helfen, nehmen (nimmst, nimmt), treffen (triffst), vergessen, sterben*; **e → ie** *sehen, lesen*.

| | haben | sein | werden | wissen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | habe | bin | werde | weiß |
| du | hast | bist | wirst | weißt |
| er / sie / es | hat | ist | wird | weiß |
| wir | haben | sind | werden | wissen |
| ihr | habt | seid | werdet | wisst |
| sie / Sie | haben | sind | werden | wissen |

**haben + noun** often matches English "be + adjective": *Ich habe Hunger* (I'm hungry), *Durst* (thirsty), *Angst* (scared), *Lust* (I feel like it), *Kopfschmerzen* (I've got a headache). *werden* means "to become" (*Ich werde Arzt*, I'm going to be a doctor) and is also the helper for the future tense (3.2.1f). *wissen* means to know a fact; to know a person or place is *kennen* (*Ich kenne ihn*).

**Small spelling changes** are not individually credited at this level, but get them right if you can. Stems ending in -d or -t add an -e: *du arbeitest, er findet*. Stems ending in -s, -ß or -z just add -t for *du*: *du heißt, du tanzt*. And *sammeln* gives *ich sammle*.

**Questions.** There are three ways to ask one. You can keep statement order and raise your voice (*Du kommst mit?*). You can put the verb first (*Spielst du Tennis?*). Or you can start with a question word and put the verb second: **was, wann, wie, wer, wo, wohin, woher, warum**, as in *Wo wohnst du? Warum lernst du Deutsch?* In the speaking role-play you must ask at least one question, usually with *Sie*: *Wann beginnt das Konzert? Haben Sie ein Zimmer frei?*

What lifts an answer to grade 9 is accuracy on the verbs that change, like *er fährt*, *sie isst*, *er nimmt* and *du weißt*. Correct *du* and *Sie* forms matter too, and so does using the present with a time phrase to talk about the future.

::: terms
- **infinitive**: the dictionary form of a verb, ending in -en or -n: spielen, wandern
- **stem**: the infinitive minus -en, which takes the endings: spiel-, wohn-, arbeit-
- **weak verb**: a regular verb whose stem never changes: machen, wohnen, kaufen
- **strong verb**: a verb whose stem vowel changes in some forms, e.g. fahren → er fährt, essen → er isst
- **vowel change**: in the present it only happens in the du and er/sie/es forms: ich lese, du liest
- **haben + noun**: German for be hungry, thirsty and so on: Ich habe Durst.
- **wissen**: to know a fact; ich weiß and er weiß have no ending: Ich weiß es nicht.
- **kennen**: to know a person or a place: Kennst du Berlin?
- **question word**: was, wann, wie, wer, wo, wohin, woher, warum; the verb comes straight after it
- **verb-first question**: a yes/no question with the verb at the start: Hast du Geschwister?
- **Sie form**: the formal you, which uses the plural verb: Wohnen Sie hier?
- **present for future**: the present tense plus a time word: Nächstes Jahr fahre ich nach Spanien.
- **es gibt**: there is / there are, followed by the accusative: Es gibt viele Geschäfte.
:::

::: method
1. **Find the subject** and decide which person it is (ich, du, er/sie/es/man, wir, ihr, sie/Sie).
2. **Is the verb haben, sein, werden or wissen?** Use the learnt form from the table.
3. **Otherwise take the stem**: the infinitive minus -en.
4. **Strong verb?** If the subject is *du* or *er/sie/es/man*, change the vowel (a → ä, e → i, e → ie). Leave every other person alone.
5. **Add the ending**: -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en. Add an -e- before -st/-t after -d/-t stems (*du findest*).
6. **Place the verb second** in a statement, or first in a yes/no question, or straight after the question word.
7. **"I am doing" is never *ich bin* + verb.** Use the simple present: *ich lese* = I am reading.
:::

::: example Conjugating a strong verb step by step
**Give all the present forms of *nehmen* (to take).**

1. Stem: *nehm-*. It is a strong e → i verb, and it also doubles the m in *du* and *er*.
2. ich nehm**e**, du **nimmst**, er/sie/es **nimmt**, wir nehm**en**, ihr nehm**t**, sie/Sie nehm**en**.

Use it: *Ich nehme den Bus, aber mein Bruder nimmt das Fahrrad.* ("I take the bus but my brother takes his bike.")
:::

::: example Translation: "I am doing" and "I will do"
**Translate: "My sister is reading and my parents are playing cards. Tomorrow we are going to Munich."**

1. "is reading" is simple present in German: *liest*. *lesen* is strong, so *sie* gets the change: **Meine Schwester liest**.
2. "are playing cards": plural subject, so **meine Eltern spielen Karten**.
3. "Tomorrow we are going" is a future meaning, but the present plus a time word is enough. *Morgen* comes first, so the verb comes second: **Morgen fahren wir nach München**.

**Meine Schwester liest und meine Eltern spielen Karten. Morgen fahren wir nach München.**
:::

::: example haben + noun
**Translate: "I'm hungry but my brother is thirsty. Are you scared?"**

1. "be hungry" = *Hunger haben*: **Ich habe Hunger**.
2. "is thirsty" = *Durst haben*, er form: **mein Bruder hat Durst**.
3. "Are you scared?" = *Angst haben*, question with the verb first: **Hast du Angst?** (or *Haben Sie Angst?* to an adult).

**Ich habe Hunger, aber mein Bruder hat Durst. Hast du Angst?**
:::

::: example Building questions for the role-play
You need to ask a hotel receptionist about breakfast, the price and the swimming.

1. When? → *wann* + verb + subject: **Wann gibt es Frühstück?**
2. How much? → *Wie viel kostet* + subject: **Wie viel kostet das Zimmer?**
3. A yes/no question with *Sie*, verb first: **Kann man hier schwimmen?** or **Haben Sie ein Zimmer frei?**

Each question has the verb straight after the question word, or first when there is no question word.
:::

::: watch
- **Never *ich bin* + verb** for "I am doing". *Ich bin spielen* is a classic error. Write *ich spiele*.
- **Vowel changes only in du and er/sie/es**: *er liest* but *wir lesen*. Examiners see *er lest* and *wir liest* in equal measure.
- **sein vs haben**: *Ich habe Hunger*, not *ich bin Hunger*. Say *Ich bin 15 Jahre alt*.
- **Sie takes the plural verb**: *Wohnen Sie hier?*, not *Wohnt Sie*.
- **Question word + verb**: *Wo wohnst du?*, never *Wo du wohnst?* in a main question.
- **wissen / kennen** are confused in almost every set of translations.
- Use the present with a time word for future plans. It is accurate and it counts as a future time reference.
:::
