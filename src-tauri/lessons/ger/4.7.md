## What it is

**Paper 4 Writing** is 25% of the GCSE: 50 marks in **1 hour 15 minutes** at Higher tier. All instructions are in English and there is no dictionary. Language beyond the AQA list gets **equal credit** if it is correct and does the job. It has three questions:

- **Question 1: translation into German (10 marks).** English sentences of **at least 50 words**. There are two grids:
  - **Grid one** divides the passage into **15 elements**. You get a tick for each element whose meaning you convey, even with minor slips. The ticks convert to a mark out of 5: 13 to 15 ticks = 5, 10 to 12 = 4, 7 to 9 = 3, 4 to 6 = 2, 1 to 3 = 1.
  - **Grid two** gives a mark out of 5 for **vocabulary and grammar across the whole translation**. The top mark needs 'very good knowledge of the required vocabulary, few if any inappropriate or omitted items' and grammar that is 'highly accurate', where 'any errors that occur are only minor'. A zero in grid one means a zero in grid two.
- **Question 2: the 90-word task (15 marks).** You choose **Question 2.1 or 2.2**. Each has **three compulsory bullet points**, and the same question also appears as Foundation Question 5. It is marked on two grids:
  - **Content (10).** The top band (9 to 10) needs all three bullets covered, clear communication, and ideas 'regularly developed with a lot of relevant information'.
  - **Language (5).** The top mark needs a good variety of vocabulary, regular attempts at complexity, **references to all three time frames which are mainly successful**, and errors that are mainly minor.
  - The bullets need not be covered equally, and 90 words is a guide, not a limit.
- **Question 3: the 150-word task (25 marks).** You choose **Question 3.1 or 3.2**. Each has **two compulsory bullet points**, and it is marked on three grids:
  - **Content (15).** The top band (13 to 15) needs 'a lot of information', 'regular successful development of ideas, accounts and/or description', and communication 'clear with very few or no lapses'. **If you cover only one bullet, the most you can get for content is 12.**
  - **Range (5).** The top mark needs a 'very good variety' of vocabulary and structures, with **complex language 'regularly attempted and often successful'**.
  - **Accuracy (5).** The top mark needs a response that is 'usually accurate', with **'verbs and tense formations secure'**. Occasional major errors in ambitious sentences are tolerated.

**What separates grade 9.** A grade 9 answer:
- plans before writing;
- covers every bullet;
- develops each idea with reasons, examples and consequences;
- puts three time frames into both extended tasks;
- deliberately uses the Higher grammar: *hätte, wäre, würde, sollte*, two-verb subordinate clauses, *seit* with the present, superlatives, and *um ... zu*. In a written narrative, it can also use **the simple past** (*ich fuhr, wir besuchten*), which the spec lists for Higher written narrative only.

What it does not do: the genitive and the passive are receptive or avoided in the list, so a grade 9 writer uses *von* + dative and *man* + active instead.

**Timing.** Spend about 15 minutes on Question 1, 20 on Question 2, 35 on Question 3, and 5 on checking verbs, word order and endings.

::: terms
- **element**: one of the 15 chunks of the Question 1 translation. Each earns a tick if its meaning is conveyed, despite minor errors.
- **compulsory bullet point**: a content point you must cover: three in Question 2 and two in Question 3. You do not need to cover them equally.
- **time frames**: past, present and future. All three are needed for the top language mark in Question 2, and they give range in Question 3.
- **development**: adding a reason, example, consequence or opinion to an idea: *Ich spiele Tennis, weil ... Letztes Jahr ... Nächstes Jahr ...*
- **complex language**: subordinate clauses with the verb at the end, infinitive constructions, conditionals, and a range of tenses and modals, e.g. *obwohl ich müde war*.
- **simple past (Präteritum)**: *ich fuhr, sie kam, wir spielten*. Listed for Higher written narrative, so it is ideal for a story in Question 3.
- **wenn-Satz**: *Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich ...* The Higher conditional, with *hätte* or *wäre* in the *wenn* clause and *würde* + infinitive in the main clause.
- **seit + present**: *Ich lerne seit vier Jahren Deutsch* = I have been learning German for four years. Using the perfect tense here is a classic error.
- **verb second (inversion)**: after a time phrase at the start of a sentence, the verb comes next: *Letztes Jahr bin ich ...*, never *Letztes Jahr ich bin*.
- **word order 3**: after *weil, dass, wenn, obwohl, als*, the verb goes to the end. With two verbs, the finite one is last: *weil ich arbeiten musste*.
- **als (comparison)**: 'than': *älter als ich*. The pronoun after *als* stays nominative.
- **man + active verb**: the spec's way to avoid the passive: *Man sollte weniger fliegen* = People should fly less.
:::

::: method
1. **Question 1, first pass.** Underline the 15 likely elements: every subject, verb, time phrase, object and qualifier (*very, often, together, two years*).
2. **Question 1, translate sentence by sentence.** For each verb, decide the tense, the auxiliary (*haben* or *sein*) and the position: second, or at the end after a conjunction. Check each noun's gender and case, and whether it needs a capital.
3. **Questions 2 and 3: choose the task** whose bullets you can develop best, not the topic you like most.
4. **Plan for 3 to 5 minutes.** For each bullet, note two or three ideas in German key words, a time frame, and one Higher structure to use: *hätte/würde*, *seit*, a superlative, *um ... zu*, *obwohl*.
5. **Write in paragraphs, one per bullet.** Open each paragraph by answering the bullet directly, then develop it: a reason, an example, a consequence, then your opinion.
6. **Keep to the word guide**, about 90 and 150 words. Examiners mark everything, but a longer answer means more errors to count and less checking time.
7. **Check in three sweeps.** First, the verbs: ending, tense, auxiliary and past participle. Second, word order: verb second, and verb at the end after a conjunction. Third, nouns and endings: capitals, genders, *den/dem*, umlauts, ß.
:::

::: example Question 1: translating into German, with the reasoning
English (57 words): *My sister is two years older than me. Last weekend we went to the cinema together, but the film was boring. I have been learning German for four years because I would like to work in Austria. If I had more money, I would travel to Switzerland. Tomorrow I am going to visit my grandparents.*

1. **Meine Schwester ist zwei Jahre älter als ich.** The comparative *älter* gains an umlaut. After *als*, the pronoun stays nominative *ich*, not *mich*. Elements: my sister / is two years older / than me.
2. **Letztes Wochenende sind wir zusammen ins Kino gegangen, aber der Film war langweilig.** The time phrase goes first, so the verb comes second. *gehen* takes *sein* in the perfect. *ins* = *in das*, the accusative for motion. After *aber* the word order is normal. Elements: last weekend / we went to the cinema together / the film was boring.
3. **Ich lerne seit vier Jahren Deutsch, weil ich in Österreich arbeiten möchte.** *seit* + present, and *Jahren* has the dative plural *-n*. After *weil*, *möchte* goes to the end, after the infinitive. Elements: I have been learning German / for four years / because I would like to work in Austria.
4. **Wenn ich mehr Geld hätte, würde ich in die Schweiz reisen.** *hätte* ends the *wenn* clause, then verb, comma, verb: *würde ich*. *die Schweiz* keeps its article. Elements: if I had more money / I would travel / to Switzerland.
5. **Morgen werde ich meine Großeltern besuchen.** 'Going to' is *werden* + infinitive, and the infinitive goes to the end. Elements: tomorrow / I am going to visit / my grandparents.
:::

::: example Question 2: a model 90-word answer
*You are writing to your German friend about your free time. Write approximately 90 words in German. You must write something about each bullet point. Mention:*
- *what you usually do in your free time*
- *a special event you went to recently*
- *your plans for next weekend.*

*Hallo Lena, in meiner Freizeit spiele ich am liebsten Basketball, weil es schnell und spannend ist. Außerdem lese ich jeden Abend, um mich zu entspannen. Letzten Samstag bin ich mit meiner Cousine zu einem Konzert in Manchester gefahren. Die Musik war fantastisch, aber die Karten waren leider sehr teuer. Nächstes Wochenende werde ich mit meiner Mannschaft an einem Wettbewerb in Leeds teilnehmen. Wenn wir gewinnen, gehen wir danach zusammen essen. Ich hoffe, dass das Wetter schön sein wird! Was machst du gern in deiner Freizeit? Bis bald, Sam* (88 words)

Why it reaches the top bands:
- It covers all three bullets and develops each one with a reason or detail.
- It has the present, the perfect with *sein* and the future with *werden*.
- It shows complexity: *weil*, *um ... zu*, a reflexive verb, *wenn*, and a two-verb *dass* clause.
:::

::: example Question 3: a model 150-word answer
*You are writing an article for a German school magazine about travel. Write approximately 150 words in German. You must write something about both bullet points. Describe:*
- *a memorable trip you have been on*
- *why travel is important for young people.*

*Letzten Sommer fuhr ich mit meiner Familie mit dem Zug nach Wien. Am ersten Tag regnete es stark, also wollten wir ein Museum besuchen, weil mein Vater sich für Kunst interessiert. Leider war es geschlossen! Am nächsten Tag war das Wetter endlich schön, und wir fuhren mit dem Fahrrad durch die Stadt. Für mich war das der schönste Tag, obwohl ich danach sehr müde war.*

*Meiner Meinung nach ist Reisen für junge Leute sehr wichtig. Man lernt andere Kulturen kennen, und man kann eine Sprache üben. Seit meiner Reise nach Wien lerne ich noch lieber Deutsch, weil ich die Leute dort besser verstehen möchte. Allerdings sollte man an die Umwelt denken. Wenn es möglich wäre, würde ich immer lieber mit dem Zug als mit dem Flugzeug fahren. Nächstes Jahr werde ich mit meinen Freunden nach Berlin fahren, um die Hauptstadt zu entdecken. Ich freue mich schon darauf!* (about 150 words)

What earns the marks:
- Bullet 1 is a real account, in the **simple past** (*fuhr, regnete, wollten, war*), with a problem and a feeling.
- Bullet 2 gives opinions with reasons and a consequence.
- Complex language appears regularly: *weil*, *obwohl*, *seit*, a superlative, *sollte*, *wäre/würde*, *lieber ... als*, *um ... zu*, and *sich freuen auf* with *darauf*.
- All three time frames appear, and the verbs are secure.
:::

::: example Planning Question 3 in four minutes
Task: *Describe a problem you had at school and say what schools should do to help young people.*

| Bullet | Key ideas (German key words) | Time frame | Higher structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. A problem at school | Mathe, schwierig, Prüfung, Angst, die Lehrerin half mir | past: simple past narrative (*ich hatte, ich war, sie half*) | *obwohl ich jeden Abend lernte*; *um ... zu* |
| 2. What schools should do | mehr Pausen, weniger Hausaufgaben, mehr Sport | present and future | *Schulen sollten ...*; *Wenn ich Direktor wäre, würde ich ...* |

Note the decisions in the plan. The pluperfect ('I had learnt') is not on the Higher list, so the plan uses the simple past instead. 'Extra tutoring' has no listed word, so say *Sie hat mir nach der Schule geholfen*. If a word or tense is not on the list and you are unsure of it, **rephrase with what you know**. Accuracy grids reward secure verbs, not risky vocabulary.
:::

::: q
How is the Question 1 translation marked?
---
Grid one: 15 elements, with a tick for each one conveyed, turned into a mark out of 5 (13 to 15 ticks = 5). Grid two: a mark out of 5 for vocabulary and grammar across the whole translation.
:::

::: q
How many bullet points do Questions 2 and 3 have, and do you have a choice of task?
---
Question 2 has three compulsory bullets and Question 3 has two. In each, you choose one of two tasks (2.1 or 2.2, 3.1 or 3.2).
:::

::: q
What is the maximum content mark in Question 3 if you cover only one bullet?
---
12 out of 15.
:::

::: q
Which language feature does the top AO3 band of Question 2 explicitly require?
---
References to all three time frames which are mainly successful, alongside variety and regular attempts at complexity.
:::

::: q
Translate: *I have been living here for two years.*
---
*Ich wohne seit zwei Jahren hier.* Use *seit* + present, not the perfect tense.
:::

::: q
Why is *Letztes Jahr ich bin nach Spanien gefahren* wrong?
---
The verb must come second after the opening time phrase: *Letztes Jahr bin ich nach Spanien gefahren.*
:::

::: q
When can you use the simple past (*ich fuhr, wir spielten*) in the exam?
---
In Higher written narrative, e.g. telling a story in Question 3. In speaking and elsewhere, use the perfect.
:::

::: q
How long should you spend on each writing question?
---
About 15 minutes on Question 1, 20 on Question 2, 35 on Question 3, and 5 for checking.
:::

::: watch
- **Missed bullets**: a Question 3 answer covering one bullet is capped at 12 of 15 for content. In Question 2, missing a bullet drops you out of the top two bands.
- **Two time frames only in Question 2**: the top language mark needs all three.
- **Verb position**: verb second after an opening phrase, and verb at the end after *weil, dass, wenn, obwohl*.
- **haben versus sein**: verbs of movement and change (*fahren, gehen, fliegen, bleiben*) take *sein*.
- **seit with the perfect tense**: always use *seit* + present for 'have been ...ing'.
- **Translation omissions**: leaving out *very*, *together*, *two years* or *tomorrow* loses the element's tick.
- **English word order in German**: *Ich möchte arbeiten in Österreich* should be *Ich möchte in Österreich arbeiten*.
- **Nouns without capitals, and missing umlauts**: *älter, würde, hätte, wäre, für, schön* all need them.
:::

Translate into German: *I have been playing football for six years.* (2)
---
*Ich spiele seit sechs Jahren Fußball.* *seit* + present tense (1); *sechs Jahren* with the dative plural and a capital on *Fußball* (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into German: *Yesterday I went into town with my friends.* (3)
---
*Gestern bin ich mit meinen Freunden in die Stadt gegangen / gefahren.* *Gestern bin ich* with inversion (1); *sein* + a correct participle (1); *mit meinen Freunden* (dative plural) and *in die Stadt* (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into German: *If I had more time, I would help more at home.* (3)
---
*Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte, würde ich zu Hause mehr helfen.* *Wenn ich mehr Zeit hätte* (1); *würde ich* with verb-comma-verb order (1); *zu Hause mehr helfen*, with the infinitive at the end (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into German: *My brother is taller than my father.* (2)
---
*Mein Bruder ist größer als mein Vater.* *größer* with the umlaut, since Higher credits the spelling change (1); *als mein Vater* (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into German: *We must protect the environment, so we never travel by plane.* (3)
---
*Wir müssen die Umwelt schützen* (1); *also fahren wir nie / reisen wir nie*, with the verb second after *also* (1); *mit dem Flugzeug* (1).
:::

::: test
Rewrite in the simple past for a written narrative: *Ich bin in die Stadt gefahren und habe einen Freund gesehen.* (2)
---
*Ich fuhr in die Stadt* (1); *und sah einen Freund* (1).
:::

::: test
Write one sentence in German for a Question 3 answer that combines a past time frame with a *weil* clause containing two verbs. (2)
---
For example *Letztes Jahr bin ich zu Hause geblieben, weil ich für meine Prüfungen lernen musste.* Correct past tense (1); *weil* with *lernen musste* at the end (1).
:::

::: test
State what the top Question 3 Range and Accuracy grids each reward. (2)
---
Range: a very good variety of vocabulary and structures, with complex language regularly attempted and often successful (1). Accuracy: usually accurate, with secure verbs and tense formations (occasional major errors in complex structures are tolerated) (1).
