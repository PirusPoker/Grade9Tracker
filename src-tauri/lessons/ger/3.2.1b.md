## What it is

A **determiner** sits in front of a noun and tells you which one or whose: *dieser* (this), *jeder* (every), *welcher?* (which?), *mein* (my). A **pronoun** replaces a noun: *er*, *ihn*, *ihm*. Both change with case, just like *der* and *ein* in 3.2.1a. The good news is that there are only two patterns. The **der-words** (*dieser, jeder, letzter, nächster, welcher*) copy the endings of *der/die/das*. The **ein-words** (*mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr, ihr*, and *kein*) copy the endings of *ein*.

You use these in every paper. You cannot describe your family (Paper 2 photo card, Paper 4 90-word task) without *mein Bruder*, *meine Eltern*, *mit meiner Mutter*. You cannot answer a follow-up question without pronouns: *Wie findest du den Film? – Ich finde **ihn** toll.* The **relative pronoun** (*der, die, das*: "who, which") is the quickest way to make a sentence longer and more complex: *Ich habe eine Schwester, **die** sehr sportlich ist.*

**The der-words.** They take the same endings as the definite article. *jeder* is singular only.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nom | dies**er** | dies**e** | dies**es** | dies**e** |
| acc | dies**en** | dies**e** | dies**es** | dies**e** |
| dat | dies**em** | dies**er** | dies**em** | dies**en** (+ noun -n) |

**The possessives.** my *mein*, your *dein*, his *sein*, her *ihr*, its *sein*, our *unser*, your (plural) *euer*, your (formal) *Ihr*, their *ihr*. They take the endings of *ein*, and their plural copies *keine*: *meine Eltern*, *mit meinen Eltern*. *euer* loses its middle *e* when it takes an ending: *euer Hund* but *eure Schwester*.

**Quantifiers.** Use *viel* and *wenig* with uncountable singular nouns (*viel Geld*, *wenig Zeit*) and *viele, wenige, alle, einige* with plurals (*viele Leute*, *alle Schüler*, *einige Freunde*).

**Personal pronouns.** At Foundation you need all the subject pronouns, and the singular object pronouns:

| nominative | ich | du | er | sie | es | man | Sie (formal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **accusative** | mich | dich | ihn | sie | es | einen | Sie |
| **dative** | mir | dir | ihm | ihr | ihm | einem | Ihnen |

The plural subject pronouns are *wir, ihr, Sie, sie*. Plural object pronouns (*uns, euch, ihnen* as objects) are Higher: see 3.2.2a. Use *du* for one friend or relative, *ihr* for several, and *Sie* for any adult you do not know. The role-play in Paper 2 almost always needs *Sie*. A pronoun follows the gender of the noun it replaces, so a masculine thing is *er*: *Der Film? **Er** ist lustig.*

**Reflexive pronouns** (accusative, all persons, Foundation): *mich, dich, sich, uns, euch, sich*. They go with verbs such as *sich freuen*, *sich interessieren für*, *sich fühlen*, *sich entspannen* and *sich treffen*. With a plural subject they can also mean "each other": *Wir verstehen **uns** gut.* The **indefinite pronouns** are *jemand* (someone) and *niemand* (no one), with the accusative forms *jemanden* and *niemanden*. The question word **wer?** (who?) becomes **wen?** in the accusative and **wem?** in the dative: *Mit wem gehst du ins Kino?*

A grade 9 answer uses possessives with the correct dative endings, swaps nouns for pronouns so that it does not repeat itself, and adds relative clauses with the verb correctly sent to the end.

::: terms
- **der-word**: a determiner with the endings of der/die/das: dieser, jeder, letzter, nächster, welcher
- **ein-word**: a determiner with the endings of ein: mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr, kein
- **possessive adjective**: shows who owns something, e.g. sein Hund (his dog), ihr Hund (her dog, their dog)
- **subject pronoun**: replaces the subject: ich, du, er, sie, es, man, wir, ihr, Sie, sie
- **direct object pronoun**: accusative: mich, dich, ihn, sie, es, e.g. Ich finde ihn nett.
- **indirect object pronoun**: dative: mir, dir, ihm, ihr, ihm, Ihnen, e.g. Das gefällt mir.
- **relative pronoun**: der, die, das (plural die) meaning who/which; it sends the verb to the end: der Mann, der hier wohnt
- **relative clause**: a clause that describes a noun, marked off by a comma: eine Stadt, die sehr schön ist
- **reflexive pronoun**: mich, dich, sich, uns, euch, sich, used when the subject acts on itself: Ich freue mich.
- **reciprocal use**: a plural reflexive meaning each other: Wir treffen uns am Samstag.
- **man**: one, you, people; it takes the er/sie form of the verb: Man kann hier schwimmen.
- **jemand / niemand**: someone / no one; accusative jemanden / niemanden
- **wer / wen / wem**: who (subject) / whom (accusative) / to or with whom (dative)
- **quantifier**: a word giving an amount: viel, wenig (singular); viele, wenige, alle, einige (plural)
:::

::: method
1. **Identify the noun's gender, number and case** exactly as in 3.2.1a.
2. **der-word or ein-word?** *dieser, jeder, welcher, letzter, nächster* take der-endings (*diesen Film*). Possessives and *kein* take ein-endings (*meinen Film*).
3. **Choose the right possessive.** Ask who owns it: *his* = *sein*, *her* and *their* = *ihr*, formal *your* = *Ihr*. Then add the ending the noun needs.
4. **Replacing a noun?** Pick the pronoun by the noun's **gender** (*der Rock* → *er / ihn / ihm*) and then by its **case** in your sentence.
5. **Relative clause.** Add a comma, then the relative pronoun with the **same gender and number** as the noun it describes, then send the verb to the **end**: *…, der in Berlin wohnt.*
6. **Reflexive verb?** Match the pronoun to the subject: *ich … mich*, *wir … uns*, *er/sie/man … sich*.
7. **Check the verb** agrees with the subject pronoun, especially *man* (er form) and formal *Sie* (plural form).
:::

::: example Possessives in the dative
**Translate: "I go to the cinema with my brother and his friends."**

1. *mit* always takes the dative.
2. *der Bruder* is masculine, so the dative of *mein* is **meinem Bruder**.
3. "his friends" is plural, so *sein* takes the dative plural ending: **seinen**, and the noun adds -n: **Freunden**.
4. "to the cinema" = *ins Kino* (3.2.1h).

**Ich gehe mit meinem Bruder und seinen Freunden ins Kino.**

Change the owner and the word changes: "with my sister and her friends" = *mit meiner Schwester und ihren Freunden*.
:::

::: example Replacing nouns with pronouns
**Answer the questions without repeating the noun.**

*Wie findest du den Film?* → *der Film* is masculine, and it is the object of *finden*, so the accusative is **ihn**: *Ich finde **ihn** sehr lustig.*

*Wie findest du die Jacke?* → feminine accusative **sie**: *Ich finde **sie** zu teuer.*

*Gefällt dir das Kleid?* → *gefallen* works back to front: "the dress pleases me". The dress is the subject, so **es** is nominative and the person is dative **mir**: *Ja, **es** gefällt **mir**.*

*Kennst du meinen Onkel?* → *Ja, ich kenne **ihn**. Ich spiele oft Tennis mit **ihm**.* After *mit* you need the dative, **ihm**.
:::

::: example Building a relative clause
**Translate: "I have a sister who is very sporty and a brother who reads a lot."**

1. First clause: *Ich habe eine Schwester*. Then a comma.
2. "who" refers to *die Schwester*, which is feminine, and she is the subject of "is", so the relative pronoun is **die**.
3. Verb to the end: *die sehr sportlich **ist***.
4. Second clause: *und einen Bruder* (masculine accusative after *habe*), then a comma.
5. "who" refers to *der Bruder*, and he is the subject of "reads", so the relative pronoun is nominative **der**, even though *einen Bruder* was accusative.
6. Verb to the end: *der viel **liest***.

**Ich habe eine Schwester, die sehr sportlich ist, und einen Bruder, der viel liest.**
:::

::: example Reflexive and reciprocal verbs
**Translate: "My friend and I get on well. We meet every Saturday and I am looking forward to the holidays."**

1. "get on well (with each other)" = *sich gut verstehen*. The subject is *wir*, so the pronoun is **uns**: *Wir verstehen **uns** gut.*
2. "meet (each other)" = *sich treffen*: *Wir treffen **uns** jeden Samstag.* (*der Samstag*, masculine accusative time phrase: *jeden*.)
3. "look forward to" = *sich freuen auf* + accusative: *Ich freue **mich** auf die Ferien.*

**Mein Freund und ich verstehen uns gut. Wir treffen uns jeden Samstag und ich freue mich auf die Ferien.**
:::

::: watch
- **his vs her**: *sein* = his (and its), *ihr* = her and their. Learners write *sein Mutter* for "her mother". It is *ihre Mutter*.
- **Possessives need endings**: *mit meiner Mutter*, *mit meinem Vater*, *mit meinen Eltern*. A bare *mein* after *mit* costs accuracy marks.
- **ihn vs ihm**: *ihn* is accusative (*Ich sehe ihn*); *ihm* is dative (*mit ihm*).
- **Relative clauses**: comma, pronoun with the noun's gender, verb at the end. Missing any one of these is an error.
- **Things have gender**: *Der Film ist gut. Er ist spannend*, never *es* for a masculine noun.
- **Du, ihr, Sie**: the role-play needs *Sie* consistently, including the verb (*Haben Sie…?*) and *Ihnen*.
- **Reflexives**: *Ich freue mich*, not *Ich freue*. Leaving out the pronoun changes or breaks the meaning.
:::
