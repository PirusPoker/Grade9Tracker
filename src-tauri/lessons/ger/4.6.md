## What it is

**Paper 3 Reading** is 25% of the GCSE: 50 marks in **1 hour** at Higher tier, with no dictionary.

**Section A (40 marks)** has questions in **English**, answered in English, by letter or by tick. The texts include public notices, adverts, brochure extracts, newspaper and magazine articles, emails, websites and literary extracts. They cover all three themes and ramp up from short notices to longer articles. The questions test four things: the overall message, key points, details and opinions. They come in several formats:
- multiple choice;
- 'which person' (match a statement to a name);
- **true / false / not mentioned**;
- positive, negative, or positive and negative;
- short answers in English.

The spec adds one Higher skill on top: you must **infer the meaning of single words from outside the vocabulary list** from their context and their parts. Higher texts can also contain the **genitive** after words like *wegen*, *trotz* and *während* (*wegen des Wetters*, because of the weather). You only need to recognise it, not write it.

**Section B (10 marks)** is a **translation into English** of German sentences, **at least 50 words** at Higher. The spec defines translation as 'an appropriate and sufficient rendering of the meaning'. It leaves the detail to each year's mark scheme, and AQA's approach is to split the passage into short **elements** (a phrase or clause each). Each element earns its mark when its full meaning is in your English. Clumsy but accurate English still scores. A wrong tense, a missed negative, the wrong person (*sie* 'she' instead of *sie* 'they') or a false friend loses that element. In this app's mock papers, each of the five sentences has two elements worth 1 mark each.

**What separates grade 9.** A grade 9 candidate reads the question first, so they know what they are hunting for. They treat every *nicht*, *kein*, *nie* and *nur* as a potential mark. They infer unknown words from prefixes, suffixes and compounds, and they write translations in natural English that keep every detail: tense, time phrase, quantity and negative. They also spend about **15 minutes on the translation**, including a final check against the German, word group by word group.

::: terms
- **not mentioned**: the statement is about the topic, but the text neither confirms nor contradicts it. Choose it only when you can find no evidence either way.
- **false**: the text says something that contradicts the statement, e.g. it says *nie* (never) and the statement says 'often'.
- **inference**: working out a meaning or an attitude the text implies but does not state, e.g. *Ich zähle die Tage bis zu den Ferien* implies she is looking forward to the holidays.
- **derived word**: a listed word with one prefix or suffix added: *un-* (opposite), *-ung* (noun from verb), *-heit/-keit* (-ness, -ty), *-los* (-less), *-chen/-lein* (little).
- **compound noun**: two or more nouns joined, with the gender of the last one: *die Hauptstadt* (capital city), *das Wohnzimmer* (living room).
- **genitive**: the 'of' case, recognised in Higher listening and reading only: *das Auto meines Vaters* (my father's car), *trotz des Regens* (despite the rain).
- **element**: one chunk of the translation that earns a mark when its meaning is fully conveyed.
- **seit + present**: translate with 'have been ...ing' or 'have ...' plus 'for' or 'since': *Ich lerne seit drei Jahren Deutsch* = I have been learning German for three years.
- **man**: 'you', 'people' or 'one', or an English passive: *Man spricht hier Deutsch* = German is spoken here.
- **gern / lieber / am liebsten**: like / prefer / like most of all: *Ich lese lieber* = I prefer reading.
- **false friend**: a German word that looks like an English one but means something else, e.g. *bekommen* = to get, receive (not 'become').
- **distractor**: a word in the text that matches a wrong option, e.g. the text mentions a museum but says it was closed.
:::

::: method
1. **Read the title, the rubric and the questions first.** Underline the key word in each question (*why*, *two*, *now*, *before*) and predict the German you need (*weil*, *früher*, *jetzt*).
2. **Find the section of text for each question.** Questions follow the order of the text.
3. **Read the whole sentence around the answer**, plus the next one. Look for negatives, *aber*, *sondern*, *obwohl* and time words, which often reverse the first impression.
4. **For true / false / not mentioned**, find the evidence. If it contradicts the statement, answer False. If it confirms it, answer True. If you cannot find any, answer Not mentioned.
5. **For an unknown word**, strip off prefixes and suffixes, split compounds, and check the context: what kind of word must fill that gap?
6. **Write English answers briefly and precisely.** Give only the number of details asked for.
7. **Translation**: translate phrase by phrase, keeping the tense, person and every qualifier (*sehr*, *nur*, *nie*, *schon*, *noch*). Then read your English aloud in your head. If it is not natural English, rephrase without losing any meaning.
8. **Final check**: cover the German, read your English, then compare element by element. Is anything missing?
:::

::: example True, false or not mentioned, with the genitive
*Mein Name ist Lukas. Seit zwei Jahren wohne ich mit meiner Mutter in einer kleinen Wohnung. Mein Vater wohnt in Köln, aber ich sehe ihn jedes zweite Wochenende. Ich verstehe mich gut mit meiner Mutter, obwohl wir manchmal über meine Noten streiten. Wegen ihrer Arbeit ist sie oft nicht zu Hause, also koche ich zweimal pro Woche für uns beide.*

| Statement | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Lukas has lived in the flat for two years. | True | *Seit zwei Jahren wohne ich*: *seit* + present |
| Lukas never sees his father. | False | *jedes zweite Wochenende*, every other weekend |
| Lukas's father has a new partner. | Not mentioned | Nothing is said about a partner |
| Lukas and his mother sometimes argue about his marks. | True | *manchmal über meine Noten streiten*: *Noten* = marks, grades |
| Lukas cooks every day. | False | *zweimal pro Woche*, twice a week |

Two traps are worth noting. *Wegen ihrer Arbeit* is a genitive, meaning 'because of her work'. *also* means 'so' or 'therefore', not 'also'.
:::

::: example Inferring a word from outside the list
*Nach der Party war die Küche in totaler Unordnung: Überall standen leere Flaschen und schmutzige Gläser.*

**Question: What was the kitchen like after the party?**

- *Unordnung* is not on the list, but *Ordnung* (order, tidiness) is, and *un-* makes the opposite.
- The context confirms it: empty bottles and dirty glasses everywhere.
- Answer: **It was a mess / very untidy.**

The same method works on other derived words:
- *Freundlichkeit* is *freundlich* + *-keit*, so 'friendliness'.
- *arbeitslos* is *Arbeit* + *-los*, so 'without work', meaning unemployed.
- *Häuschen* is *Haus* + *-chen*, so 'little house'.
- *Lieblingsessen* is *Lieblings-* + *Essen*, so 'favourite food'.
:::

::: example Translation into English, element by element
*Letztes Jahr habe ich zwei Wochen in einem Krankenhaus gearbeitet. Seit Januar arbeite ich samstags in einem Geschäft. Man muss dort sehr früh aufstehen, aber ich bekomme gutes Geld.*

| German element | Accepted English | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| *Letztes Jahr habe ich zwei Wochen* | Last year I (spent) two weeks | *letztes* = last, not 'latest' |
| *in einem Krankenhaus gearbeitet* | working / worked in a hospital | Perfect tense becomes an English simple past |
| *Seit Januar arbeite ich* | Since January I have been working | *seit* + present = 'have been ...ing' |
| *samstags in einem Geschäft* | on Saturdays in a shop | *samstags* = on Saturdays; *Geschäft* = shop, business |
| *Man muss dort sehr früh aufstehen* | You have to get up very early there | *man* = you or one; keep *sehr* and *dort* |
| *aber ich bekomme gutes Geld* | but I get / earn good money | *bekomme* = get, not 'become' |

Full version: **Last year I worked in a hospital for two weeks. Since January I have been working in a shop on Saturdays. You have to get up very early there, but I get good money.**

Writing 'I work since January' loses an element: it is not English. 'I become good money' loses one too.
:::

::: example Common howlers: false friends and grammar traps
| German | Means | Not |
|---|---|---|
| *bekommen* | to get, receive | to become (that is *werden*) |
| *das Handy* | mobile phone | handy |
| *also* | so, therefore | also (that is *auch*) |
| *eventuell* | possibly, perhaps | eventually (that is *schließlich*, *endlich*). Not on the AQA list, so you may never meet it, but know it |
| *der Chef / die Chefin* | boss | chef (a cook) |
| *sensibel* | sensitive | sensible. Not on the AQA list |
| *aktuell* | current, up to date | actual |
| *fast* | almost, nearly | fast (that is *schnell*) |
| *die Art* | kind, type, way | art (that is *die Kunst*) |
| *das Gymnasium* | grammar school | gym |
| *ich will* | I want | I will (that is *ich werde*) |
| *die Note* | mark, grade | a note (message) |
| *wer / wo* | who / where | where / who, the classic swap |

Grammar traps:
- *sie spielen* can mean 'they play' or, with a capital, 'you play' (*Sie*). *sie spielt* means 'she plays'.
- *hätte* means 'would have' and *wäre* means 'would be'. Do not read them as *hatte* 'had' and *war* 'was'.
- In a long subordinate clause, the verb waits at the end: find it before you translate.
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher reading paper, and how is it split?
---
One hour and 50 marks: Section A has 40 marks of questions in English, and Section B is a 10-mark translation into English of at least 50 words.
:::

::: q
When should you answer 'Not mentioned' rather than 'False'?
---
When the text contains nothing that confirms or contradicts the statement. False needs evidence that contradicts it.
:::

::: q
Translate: *Ich spiele seit fünf Jahren Tennis.*
---
I have been playing tennis for five years.
:::

::: q
What does *also* mean in *Es hat geregnet, also sind wir zu Hause geblieben*?
---
So / therefore: 'It rained, so we stayed at home.'
:::

::: q
Work out the meaning of *Sauberkeit* in *Im Hotel war die Sauberkeit ein großes Problem.*
---
Cleanliness: *sauber* (clean) + *-keit* (-ness).
:::

::: q
Translate *trotz des schlechten Wetters*, and name the case.
---
Despite / in spite of the bad weather. It is the genitive after *trotz*.
:::

::: q
Why does *Ich habe einen Brief bekommen* not mean 'I have become a letter'?
---
*bekommen* is a false friend meaning to get or receive: 'I (have) received / got a letter'.
:::

::: q
How is the translation into English marked?
---
In elements: each chunk scores if its full meaning is conveyed in acceptable English. Wrong tenses, missed negatives, wrong persons and false friends lose elements.
:::

::: watch
- **Negatives and restrictors**: *nicht, kein, nie, nur, kaum, noch nicht*. 'Only once' is not 'once'.
- **Tense in translation**: perfect becomes the English past (*ich bin gegangen* = I went). *seit* + present becomes 'have been ...ing'. *werden* + infinitive becomes 'will'.
- **False friends**: *bekommen, also, Handy, Chef, fast, aktuell, Art, will, Note*.
- **Not mentioned versus false**: if you cannot point to contradicting words, it is not False.
- **Too many details**: for a one-mark question, give one answer. A wrong extra detail can cancel a right one.
- **Leaving translation words out**: *sehr, oft, schon, auch, dort, zusammen* are all part of an element.
- **Unnatural English**: 'I have to it do' is not acceptable. Rearrange into normal English order.
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *Mein Bruder hat zum Geburtstag ein neues Handy bekommen.* (2)
---
My brother got / received a new mobile phone (1); for his birthday (1). 'Became' scores 0 for that element.
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *Wir wohnen seit drei Jahren in einem Dorf in der Nähe von Bonn.* (2)
---
We have been living / have lived in a village for three years (1); near Bonn (1).
:::

::: test
Read: *Anna geht nie ins Kino, weil es zu teuer ist. Sie sieht lieber Filme zu Hause mit ihrer Schwester.* True, false or not mentioned? (a) Anna often goes to the cinema. (b) Anna prefers watching films at home. (c) Anna's sister is older than her. (3)
---
(a) False: *nie*, never (1). (b) True: *lieber*, prefers (1). (c) Not mentioned (1).
:::

::: test
What do these words most likely mean? Give the part you recognise. (a) *hoffnungslos* (b) *Kätzchen* (c) *unfreundlich* (3)
---
(a) Hopeless: *Hoffnung* + *-los* (1). (b) Kitten or little cat: *Katze* + *-chen* (1). (c) Unfriendly: *un-* + *freundlich* (1).
:::

::: test
Read: *Ich finde meine Schule toll, weil die Lehrer hilfsbereit sind. Aber das Essen in der Kantine ist schrecklich.* Is the writer's opinion of school positive, negative, or positive and negative? Give evidence. (2)
---
Positive and negative (1): teachers helpful / school great, but the canteen food is terrible (1).
:::

::: test
Give the meaning of each false friend: (a) *der Chef* (b) *also* (c) *fast* (d) *aktuell* (4)
---
(a) Boss (1). (b) So / therefore (1). (c) Almost / nearly (1). (d) Current / up to date (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *Man sollte weniger Fleisch essen, also esse ich jetzt ab und zu Fisch.* (2)
---
You / people / one should eat less meat (1); so now I eat fish now and again / from time to time (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: *Wenn ich reich wäre, würde ich ein Haus am Meer kaufen.* (2)
---
If I were / was rich (1); I would buy a house by the sea (1).
:::
