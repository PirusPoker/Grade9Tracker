## What it is

Almost every business has **competitors**: other businesses selling the same or similar products to the same customers. The **competitive environment** is the strength and number of those rivals. A business must understand its competitors' **strengths and weaknesses** and decide how to respond, because competition shapes nearly every decision it makes.

### Judging competitors: the five criteria

Edexcel names five things to compare:

| Criterion | Questions to ask about a rival | A strength looks like | A weakness looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Price** | Is it cheaper or dearer than us? Does it run offers? | Low prices that attract budget customers | High prices that leave room to undercut |
| **Quality** | How good is the product or service? What do reviews say? | Excellent reviews, premium materials | Poor reviews, frequent complaints |
| **Location** | Where is it, and how easy is it to reach? | Busy high street, free parking, near customers | Hard to find, no parking, far from its market |
| **Product range** | How much choice does it offer? | Wide range covering many needs | Narrow range, so some needs are unmet |
| **Customer service** | How helpful, quick and friendly is it? What happens when things go wrong? | Knowledgeable staff, easy returns, fast replies | Long waits, unhelpful staff, slow responses |

A simple **competitor grid** scores each rival on the five criteria. The business can then see where rivals are weak (an opportunity to beat them) and where they are strong (an area to avoid competing head-on).

| | Price | Quality | Location | Range | Service |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supermarket café | Strong (cheap) | Weak | Strong (car park) | Weak | Weak |
| Chain coffee shop | Weak (dear) | Medium | Strong (high street) | Medium | Medium |
| **Gap for a new independent café** | Mid-price | **Strong** (fresh, local) | Side street | **Strong** (homemade cakes, vegan) | **Strong** (personal) |

A new small business rarely beats a large rival on price, because the large rival buys in bulk. It usually competes on **quality, range or customer service**, which are areas where a small owner-run business can be strong.

### The impact of competition on business decision making

When competition increases, a business has to decide how to respond. Competition affects:

- **Pricing**: lowering prices to keep customers, matching a rival's offers, or deliberately charging more and justifying it with quality. Lower prices reduce profit per item.
- **Product**: improving quality, widening the range or adding a unique selling point (1.1.3) so customers have a reason to choose it.
- **Promotion**: spending more on advertising, loyalty schemes or social media to stay visible.
- **Customer service**: training staff, faster delivery, easier returns.
- **Costs**: looking for cheaper suppliers or cutting waste, so prices can fall without losing all the profit.
- **Location and place**: moving, opening longer hours, or selling online (1.4.2, 1.5.2).
- **Market research**: keeping a closer watch on rivals and customers' changing needs.

The more competitors there are, and the closer they are to the business's position on a market map (1.2.3), the more pressure there is on prices and profit. A business with **few competitors** can charge higher prices and has less need to innovate.

### Competition and customers

Competition is generally **good for customers**:
- **Lower prices**, as businesses compete to win sales.
- **Better quality** and service, as businesses try to stand out.
- **More choice**, as businesses add products and features.
- **Innovation**, as new ideas are needed to stay ahead.

For businesses it is a **threat** (lost customers, lower prices, lower profit) but it also pushes them to improve and become more efficient.

### Responding to a large competitor

When a large chain opens nearby, a small business might:
- focus on a **niche** the chain does not serve;
- offer **personal service** and local knowledge;
- build **loyalty** through schemes and relationships;
- stress **local** or **specialist** products;
- avoid a **price war** it cannot win, because the chain's costs per unit are lower.

The best response depends on what the business is strong at and what its customers value most (1.2.1).

::: terms
- **Competitor**: another business selling the same or similar products to the same customers.
- **Competitive environment**: the number and strength of the rivals a business faces in its market.
- **Competitor grid**: a table comparing rivals on criteria such as price, quality, location, product range and customer service.
- **Product range**: the variety of products or services a business offers.
- **Customer service**: how well a business looks after customers before, during and after a sale.
- **Price war**: rival businesses repeatedly cutting prices to win customers, which reduces everyone's profit.
- **Direct competitor**: a rival offering a very similar product to the same target market.
- **Niche**: a small, specialised part of a market that larger rivals may ignore.
:::

::: method
1. **Use the five criteria**: price, quality, location, product range, customer service. Say which is a strength and which a weakness for the named rival.
2. **For "impact of competition"**, name a **decision** the business would change (price, product, promotion, service, costs) and develop the effect on sales, costs or profit.
3. **Outline (2)**: one impact plus a development that uses the case. Most students lose the second mark by not mentioning the business, its product or its rival.
4. **For customer questions**: lower prices, better quality, more choice, developed to what customers gain.
5. **Small v large rival**: a small business wins on quality, range in a niche or service, not usually on price.
6. **Justify and evaluate**: weigh the response's cost and risk against how well it plays to the business's strength; conclude with what it depends on (how many customers value that strength, the rival's prices).
:::

::: example Explain - benefit to customers
Explain **one** advantage to customers of increased competition. (3)

Lower prices (1). Businesses reduce their prices to attract customers away from rivals (1), so customers pay less for the same product and have more money left to spend on other things (1).
:::

::: example Outline - impact in context
Spin Cycle is an independent bike shop in Cambridge. A large national bike retailer has just opened a store on the same road.
Outline **one** impact competition could have on Spin Cycle. (2)

Spin Cycle may lose customers to the national retailer, which can sell bikes more cheaply (1), so it may have to lower its own bike prices, reducing its profit on each bike, or focus more on repairs where its expertise is a strength (1).
:::

::: example Explain - impact on decisions
Explain **one** way competition can affect a business's pricing decisions. (3)

A business may have to set prices close to or below its rivals' (1), because customers can easily compare prices and will buy from the cheaper business (1), which means it has less freedom to raise prices and may earn less profit on each sale (1).
:::

::: example Analyse - responding to a rival
Sofia owns Bella Pasta, a family Italian restaurant in Lancaster. A large chain Italian restaurant has opened nearby with prices about 20% lower.
Analyse the impact of this competition on Bella Pasta. (6)

The chain's prices are 20% lower, so price-sensitive customers such as students and families on tight budgets are likely to try it, reducing Bella Pasta's covers and revenue (AO2). Because Bella Pasta's rent and staff costs are fixed, fewer customers means lower profit, and Sofia may be tempted to cut prices, but matching a chain that buys in bulk would squeeze her profit on every meal (AO3a).

However, competition may push Sofia to play to her strengths. As a family-run restaurant she can offer fresh homemade pasta and personal service that a chain finds harder to match (AO2). By promoting these, she can keep customers who value quality, and justify her higher prices, so the impact could be a smaller but more loyal customer base rather than a collapse in sales (AO3a).
:::

::: example Evaluate - a large competitor opening nearby
Hollow Tree is an independent bookshop in a market town in Herefordshire. A large chain bookshop is opening in the town next month. Hollow Tree's prices are similar to the chain's recommended prices, but the chain often runs offers. Hollow Tree runs children's story sessions and has staff who give recommendations. Evaluate the likely impact of the new competitor on Hollow Tree. (12)

Opening as a direct competitor with offers, the chain is likely to attract customers buying bestsellers, where price is the main need, so Hollow Tree's sales of popular titles may fall (AO1b, AO2). With lower revenue but the same rent and wages, its profit will fall, and if it tries to match the chain's offers its profit on each book will fall as well (AO3a).

On the other hand, Hollow Tree has strengths the chain may lack: staff who know customers and give personal recommendations, and children's story sessions that bring families into the shop (AO2). Customers who value this service may stay loyal, and the sessions build relationships that lead to regular sales, so it can compete on customer service and range rather than price (AO3a).

Overall the impact is likely to be a fall in sales of popular titles, but not necessarily a threat to survival. It depends on how many of Hollow Tree's customers buy for the experience rather than the price: if most are families and regulars who value advice and events, the impact will be limited, especially if Hollow Tree promotes these strengths before the chain opens (AO3b).
:::

1 mark plus up to 2. For example: offer better customer service (1), such as expert advice and remembering regular customers (1), so customers feel valued and return, keeping sales even though prices are not the lowest (1).

::: q
Explain **one** disadvantage to a business of increased competition. (3)
---
1 mark plus up to 2. For example: it may lose customers to rivals (1), so its sales and revenue fall (1), and with fixed costs unchanged its profit falls, possibly into a loss (1).

Discuss the impact of competition on the decisions made by a small business. (6)

::: q

(6)
---

- "They will lose customers" is only the start. Say **what decision** the business makes in response and what that does to costs, revenue or profit.
