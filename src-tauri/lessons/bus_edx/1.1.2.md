## What it is

Starting a business means giving something up in the hope of getting something better. Edexcel names **three risks** and **three rewards**, and examines those exact words.

### The risks

| Risk | What it means | How it hits the owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Business failure** | The business closes because it cannot make enough sales or cannot pay its debts | The owner loses the business, the time invested in it and possibly their reputation; a good share of new businesses close within their first few years |
| **Financial loss** | The owner loses money invested in the business, or is left owing money | Savings put into the business are lost; loans must still be repaid; with **unlimited liability** (sole traders and most partnerships, 1.4.1) personal possessions such as a car or house can be used to pay business debts |
| **Lack of security** | Giving up the steady income and benefits of being an employee | No regular wage, so income varies month to month and may be zero; no sick pay or paid holiday; no employer pension contributions; it can be harder to get a mortgage or loan without proof of steady income |

**Lack of security** is the risk students misread most. It has nothing to do with security guards, locks or cyber attacks. It means the owner no longer knows how much they will earn. An employee on £2,200 a month knows the money will arrive; a new café owner might take home £3,000 in a busy month, nothing in a quiet one, and still has household bills every month.

### The rewards

| Reward | What it means | Why it attracts entrepreneurs |
|---|---|---|
| **Business success** | The business survives, grows and builds a good reputation | Personal satisfaction and pride; the business may become valuable and could later be sold |
| **Profit** | Revenue is greater than total costs | The owner keeps the profit (after tax) rather than earning a fixed wage, so income can be far higher than in a job |
| **Independence** | Being your own boss | The owner makes the decisions, chooses the hours and the direction of the business, and does work they enjoy |

**Independence** links straight to the **non-financial objectives** in 1.3.1 (independence and control, personal satisfaction, challenge). **Profit** links to the financial objectives. Many owners of small businesses earn less than they would as employees but stay in business for the independence.

### Why entrepreneurs take the risk

An entrepreneur starts a business when they judge that the **expected reward outweighs the risk**. In general, **the higher the risk, the higher the possible reward**: an original product with no competitors (1.1.1) is risky but could be very profitable; a franchise (1.4.1) is safer but the franchisor takes a share of the rewards.

Owners can **reduce** risk, but never remove it:
- **Market research** (1.2.2) checks there is demand before money is spent.
- A **business plan** (1.4.4) forces the owner to forecast costs and cash flow and plan for problems.
- **Starting part-time** while keeping a job reduces the lack of security.
- **Starting small** (from home, selling online) keeps fixed costs and possible losses low.
- Forming a **private limited company** gives limited liability, protecting personal possessions.
- Choosing a **franchise** uses a proven model.

### Applying it to a case

In Section B, a question may ask about "the owners" of a business. Use what the case tells you: did they leave jobs (lack of security)? Did they put in savings or take a loan (financial loss)? Do they have family to support? Is there strong competition (business failure)? The strongest answers link the risk to a consequence for the person, not just the business: "Because Amir left his salaried job, he has no guaranteed income while the café builds up customers, so he may struggle to pay his mortgage in the first few months."

::: terms
- **Risk**: the possibility that starting or running a business leads to an unwanted outcome such as a loss.
- **Reward**: the benefit an entrepreneur hopes to gain from running a business.
- **Business failure**: when a business closes because it cannot make enough sales or pay its debts.
- **Financial loss**: losing money invested in a business, or being left owing money when it fails.
- **Lack of security**: giving up a regular wage, sick pay and holiday pay, so income is no longer guaranteed.
- **Business success**: the business survives, grows and achieves its owner's aims.
- **Profit**: the amount by which revenue is greater than total costs, which belongs to the owner.
- **Independence**: being your own boss, free to make the decisions and choose how and when you work.
- **Unlimited liability**: the owner is personally responsible for all business debts, so personal possessions are at risk.
:::

::: method
1. **Use the spec words**: business failure, financial loss, lack of security; business success, profit, independence.
2. **Explain (3)**: name one risk or reward (1), then two linked steps showing what it means for the owner (2). No case needed.
3. **Outline (2)**: one risk or reward plus a development that uses the owner's situation from the case.
4. **Analyse (6)**: build chains from the case. Who is the owner, what did they give up or invest, and what happens to them personally if sales are slow?
5. **Weigh**: in justify and evaluate, set the size of the risk (savings, loans, dependants, competition) against the size of the reward (likely profit, independence) and ways to reduce the risk.
6. **Conclude** with a new "depends on" factor, such as how much research the owner has done or whether they could return to their old job.
:::

::: example Explain - a reward
Explain **one** reward for an entrepreneur of starting a business. (3)

Independence (1). The entrepreneur is their own boss, so they make the decisions about what to sell and when to work (1), which brings personal satisfaction and lets them fit work around commitments such as family, something an employee often cannot do (1).
:::

::: example Explain - lack of security
Explain **one** reason why lack of security is a risk of starting a business. (3)

The entrepreneur gives up a regular wage from employment (1), so their income now depends on how well the business does each month (1), which means they may not be able to pay personal bills such as rent or a mortgage if sales are low (1).
:::

::: example Outline - from a case
Amir left his job as a bank clerk to open a café in Derby. He used £15,000 of his savings and has a mortgage to pay.
Outline **one** possible risk to Amir from setting up the café. (2)

Financial loss (1): Amir has put £15,000 of his own savings into the café, so if it fails he could lose all of it, money he cannot easily replace without his bank salary (1).
:::

::: example Analyse - lack of security
Jess and Callum were both nurses in Exeter. They resigned to open a dog-daycare centre together. They have two young children.
Analyse the impact on Jess and Callum of their lack of security. (6)

Because both left their nursing jobs, the household has lost two regular salaries at once, and the daycare's income depends on how many dogs are booked each week (AO2). In the first months bookings are likely to be low while they build a reputation, so their take-home pay may be well below their old wages, making it hard to pay for childcare and household bills (AO3a).

They have also lost sick pay and holiday pay, so if one of them is ill the centre may have to turn customers away, and they earn nothing for those days (AO2). This puts pressure on them to work long hours without breaks, which could affect their health and family life; on the other hand, as their own bosses they can set their hours around the children once the business is established (AO3a).

(Level 3: chains of reasoning developed from cause to consequence, using Jess and Callum's situation throughout.)
:::

::: example Justify - keep the job or go full-time
Marta works as a hairdresser in a salon in Cardiff, earning £1,900 a month. She wants to start her own mobile hairdressing business. Option 1: leave her job and start full-time now. Option 2: keep her job four days a week and run the business on the fifth day and weekends. Justify which **one** of these options Marta should choose. (9)

Option 2 means Marta keeps most of her salary, so she still has a regular income while she builds a customer base, reducing her lack of security (AO2). If the mobile business is slow to start, she can still pay her bills, so the risk of financial loss forcing her to give up is much lower (AO3a).

However, with only one weekday and weekends free she cannot take many bookings, so the business will grow slowly and she may lose customers who want weekday appointments to rival mobile hairdressers (AO3a).

Marta should choose Option 2 because it lets her test whether there is enough demand before giving up a guaranteed £1,900 a month. This depends on whether her salon allows staff to work for themselves: if her contract stops her taking clients in the same area, Option 2 may not be possible and she would need savings to cover several months before choosing Option 1 (AO3b).
:::

::: q
Which **one** of the following is a reward of starting a business?

A Lack of security

B Financial loss

C Independence

D Business failure

Select one answer. (1)
---
C (1). A, B and D are the three risks in the specification.
:::

::: q
State **one** risk for an entrepreneur of starting a business. (1)
---
Any one (1): business failure; financial loss; lack of security.
:::

::: q
Explain **one** way an entrepreneur could reduce the risk of starting a business. (3)
---
1 mark for a way, up to 2 for development. For example: carry out market research (1) to check there are enough customers before spending money (1), so the owner is less likely to invest in an idea that fails, reducing financial loss (1). Or start part-time while keeping a job, developed into keeping a regular income.
:::

::: q
Explain **one** reason why profit is a reward for an entrepreneur. (3)
---
1 mark, plus up to 2 for development. For example: the owner keeps the profit the business makes (1), rather than receiving a fixed wage (1), so if the business is successful they can earn more than they would as an employee (1).
:::

::: q
Discuss why entrepreneurs are willing to take risks when starting a business. (6)
---
Indicative content: expected rewards (profit, independence, success) outweigh the risks; higher risk can mean higher reward; risks can be reduced by research, planning, starting small, limited liability; some value independence above a higher, secure wage; some cannot find suitable employment so the comparison favours starting up.
Level 3 (5–6): two developed lines of reasoning with accurate terms. Level 2 (3–4): some development. Level 1 (1–2): simple points.
:::

::: q
Priya opened a gift shop in Whitby as a sole trader. She borrowed £20,000 from a bank, using her house as security for the loan.

Analyse the impact on Priya of the risk of financial loss. (6)
---
Indicative content: the £20,000 loan must be repaid whatever the shop's sales; as a sole trader she has unlimited liability, so business debts are her personal debts; if the shop fails the bank could claim her house; seasonal tourist trade in Whitby means winter sales may be low, increasing the chance she cannot repay; she could reduce the risk with a cash-flow forecast or by forming a private limited company.
Level 3 (5–6): developed chains applied to Priya throughout. Level 2 (3–4): some development and application. Level 1 (1–2): isolated points. No context: maximum 3.
:::

::: watch
- **Lack of security means no guaranteed income**: no regular wage, sick pay or holiday pay. It is not about crime, locks or data.
- Use the **exact spec words** for risks and rewards. "Stress" or "long hours" are not spec risks; link them to lack of security or failure if you mention them.
- A risk answer should end with what happens to the **owner personally**: lost savings, unpaid bills, a home at risk.
- **Financial loss is worse with unlimited liability** (1.4.1). Say so when the case is a sole trader or partnership.
- **Independence** is a non-financial reward; **profit** is financial. Do not mix them up in multiple choice.
- In an **analyse**, every paragraph must use the case: names, jobs given up, savings, dependants. Without context the cap is 3 marks.
:::

::: test
Zara has invested £25,000 of her savings to open a vintage clothing shop in York as a sole trader. Two similar shops opened in the same street last year.

Analyse the impact on Zara of the risk of business failure. (6)
---
Indicative content:

- AO3a: competition splits the customers, so sales may be too low to cover rent and stock costs; if the shop fails she loses her savings; as a sole trader her liability is unlimited, so any remaining debts could fall on her personal possessions; failure could damage her reputation and confidence for a future business; she could reduce the risk by finding a point of difference, for example a narrower range or online sales.

::: test
Discuss why the reward of profit may encourage people to start a business. (6)
---
Indicative content (AO1b, AO3a):
- The owner keeps all the profit rather than receiving a fixed wage, so income is potentially unlimited.
- If the business grows, profits can rise year after year, and the business itself may be sold for a large sum.
- Profit can be reinvested to grow the business, increasing future reward.
- But profit is not guaranteed; most start-ups make little or no profit at first, so the reward is uncertain and balanced against lack of security.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): accurate understanding; two developed lines of reasoning.
- Level 2 (3–4): some chains of reasoning, partly developed.
- Level 1 (1–2): isolated points.
:::

::: test
Owen is a chef in a restaurant in Bath earning £28,000 a year. He has £12,000 of savings and wants to start his own catering business.
Option 1: leave his job and open a small café, which would need all his savings and a £10,000 loan.
Option 2: keep his job and run a weekend catering service for weddings and parties from a rented kitchen, which would cost £3,000 to start.

Justify which **one** of these two options Owen should choose. (9)
---
Indicative content:
- Option 1: full independence; café earns every day; but loses his £28,000 salary (lack of security), risks all savings plus a loan (financial loss), and faces café competition (business failure).
- Option 2: keeps his salary, so low lack of security; only £3,000 at risk; tests demand; but slow growth, tiring to work seven days, and limited independence while still employed.
- Conclusion adds a new factor, for example: depends on whether he has secured advance bookings, or whether he has family who rely on his income.

Levels:
- Level 3 (7–9): one option weighed with benefits and drawbacks, applied to Owen throughout; a conclusion with a new "depends on" factor.
- Level 2 (4–6): some developed reasoning and use of the case; a partly supported judgement.
- Level 1 (1–3): isolated points; little use of the case.
A one-sided answer cannot reach Level 3.
:::

Evaluate whether Lucy should leave her job to start the business. You should use the information provided as well as your knowledge of business. (12)
---
Indicative content:

- Level 1 (1–4): isolated knowledge, little application; an assertion rather than a judgement.
