## What it is

Recruiting good people (2.5.2) is only the start. To get the best from them, and to keep them, a business must **train** and **develop** them. Edexcel asks for:
1. **How businesses train and develop employees**: **formal and informal training**, **self-learning**, **ongoing training for all employees**, and the **use of target setting and performance reviews**.
2. **Why businesses train and develop employees**: **the link between training, motivation and retention**, and **retraining to use new technology**.

**Training** means teaching employees the **skills and knowledge** to do their **current** job well. **Development** is broader: helping employees **grow** their abilities over time, preparing them for more responsibility and future roles.

### Ways of training and developing employees

**Formal training**

Formal training is **planned and structured**, with set content and usually a set time and place. It may be delivered **in-house** (by the business's own trainers) or **externally** (at a college, by a training company, or online with a recognised course), and may lead to a **qualification**.
- Examples: an **induction** programme for new starters (introducing the business, its rules, health and safety, and the job); a health and safety course; a management qualification; a first-aid certificate; an apprenticeship with college days.

| Advantages of formal training | Disadvantages of formal training |
|---|---|
| **consistent**: every employee learns the same standards | **expensive**: course fees, trainers, travel, materials |
| delivered by **experts**, so high-quality, up-to-date knowledge | staff are **away from work**, so output is lost or cover is needed |
| can lead to **qualifications**, which motivates staff | may be general, not tailored to the exact job |
| can be planned around the business's needs | qualified staff may be **poached** by rivals or leave for better jobs |

**Informal training**

Informal training happens **on the job**, as part of normal work, usually by **learning from experienced colleagues**.
- Examples: watching and copying an experienced worker ("sitting with Nellie"); **job shadowing**; a supervisor showing someone how to use a till; **mentoring** or **coaching** by a more senior colleague; asking colleagues for help.

| Advantages of informal training | Disadvantages of informal training |
|---|---|
| **cheap**: no course fees | **inconsistent**: quality depends on the colleague doing the training |
| staff stay **productive** while they learn | experienced staff may pass on **bad habits** or shortcuts |
| learning is **specific** to the exact job and the business's ways | the experienced worker is slowed down, so their own output falls |
| quick to arrange | no qualification, so may be less motivating; gaps in knowledge |

**Self-learning**

Employees take responsibility for **learning on their own**, at their own pace: online courses and videos, e-learning modules provided by the business, reading manuals and guides, or studying for a qualification in their own time.
- **Advantages**: cheap for the business; flexible, as staff learn when it suits them; staff who choose to learn are often **motivated**; can be done remotely.
- **Disadvantages**: depends on staff being **self-motivated**; no one checks understanding immediately; some staff may not do it; staff may feel they are expected to learn in their own time.

**Ongoing training for all employees**

Training is **not a one-off** event at the start of a job. **Ongoing training** (sometimes called continuous professional development) keeps **all** employees' skills **up to date** throughout their careers:
- refreshers on **health and safety** and **legal requirements** (for example food hygiene, data protection, safeguarding);
- updates on **new products, processes and systems**;
- **customer service** training to keep standards high;
- development for promotion (leadership and management skills).

**Why for all employees?** Markets, laws and technology keep changing, so skills go out of date; it makes sure **every** employee works to the same standard; it shows all staff, not just the most senior, that they are **valued**.

**Target setting and performance reviews**

These are **development** tools: they help employees improve over time.

- **Target setting**: managers agree **targets** with employees (for example sales targets, output targets, customer-satisfaction scores, completing a qualification). Good targets are **clear, measurable, achievable** and have a **deadline**. Targets give employees **direction** and something to aim for, link their work to the business's **objectives**, and let progress be measured. But targets that are **too hard** demotivate, and targets on one thing (speed) can lead staff to neglect another (quality).
- **Performance reviews** (appraisals): **regular meetings** (often once or twice a year) between an employee and their manager to **review performance** against targets, give feedback, identify **strengths** and **weaknesses**, agree **training needs** and set **new targets**. They may be linked to **pay rises or promotion** (2.5.4).
  - **Benefits**: staff get **feedback and recognition**, which motivates; **training needs** are spotted, so training money is spent where it is needed; poor performance is tackled early.
  - **Drawbacks**: they take **managers' time**; if handled badly or seen as unfair, they **demotivate**; staff may feel judged.

### Why businesses train and develop employees

**The link between training, motivation and retention**

This is the key chain for the exam:

**Training → employees feel valued and more capable → higher motivation → they are more likely to stay (retention) → lower staff turnover → lower recruitment and training costs, and skills stay in the business**

- **Motivation**: training shows employees the business **values** them and is **investing** in them. They feel more **confident** and **competent** in their job, and development opens the way to **promotion**. Motivated employees work harder, take more care and give better customer service (link to 2.5.4).
- **Retention**: employees who are trained and developed are **less likely to leave**, because they can progress in the business. **Staff turnover** falls, so the business spends less on **recruiting** (2.5.2) and on training replacements, and keeps experienced staff who know its customers and systems.
- **Productivity and quality**: skilled staff work **faster** and make **fewer mistakes**, so output per worker rises (2.3.1) and quality improves (2.3.3).

**The counter-argument**: a business that trains staff may lose them to rivals who offer higher pay to already-trained workers. Training does improve retention, but only if it is combined with pay, progression and good management.

**Retraining to use new technology**

When a business introduces **new technology** (new machines, software, tills, automation; 2.3.1 and 2.5.1), its employees must be **retrained**:
- so they can use it **properly and safely**, getting the full **productivity** and **quality** benefits the business paid for;
- to avoid **mistakes, breakdowns** and accidents caused by misuse;
- so that staff whose old jobs have been replaced by technology can move into **new roles**, instead of being made redundant (saving redundancy costs and keeping experienced, loyal staff);
- to reduce **anxiety** and resistance to change, which supports motivation.

Retraining costs money and time, and output may fall while staff learn, but without it the investment in technology is wasted.

::: terms
- **Training**: teaching employees the skills and knowledge they need to do their job well.
- **Development**: helping employees grow their skills and abilities over time, preparing them for future roles.
- **Formal training**: planned, structured training with set content, often delivered by specialists and sometimes leading to a qualification.
- **Informal training**: training that happens on the job, usually by learning from experienced colleagues.
- **Self-learning**: employees learning independently, at their own pace, for example through online courses.
- **Ongoing training**: training given throughout employees' careers to keep their skills and knowledge up to date.
- **Target setting**: agreeing measurable goals with employees for them to achieve within a set time.
- **Performance review**: a regular meeting between an employee and manager to review performance, give feedback and agree targets and training.
- **Retention**: a business's ability to keep its employees rather than having them leave.
- **Staff turnover**: the rate at which employees leave a business and have to be replaced.
- **Retraining**: training existing employees in new skills, for example to use new technology.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the method** precisely (formal, informal, self-learning, ongoing, target setting, performance reviews) and say **how** it works.
2. **Explain (3)**: point → effect on employees (skills, confidence, motivation) → effect on the business (productivity, quality, retention, costs).
3. **Use the chain**: training → motivation → retention → lower recruitment costs. Most "why train" questions are answered by developing along it.
4. **Weigh cost against benefit**: formal training costs fees and lost output; informal is cheap but inconsistent.
5. **Use the case**: the type of job (safety-critical? technical?), number of staff, staff turnover figures, new technology being introduced.
6. **Justify (9)**: choose one method; give its benefits and drawbacks for this business; conclude with a "depends on" factor, such as how complex the skills are or how much consistency matters.
:::

::: example Explain - reason to train
Explain **one** reason why businesses train their employees. (3)

Training improves employees' skills, so they can do their job faster and with fewer mistakes (1). This raises productivity, as each employee produces more in the same time (1), which lowers the cost per unit and can increase the business's profit (1).
:::

::: example Explain - performance reviews
Explain **one** benefit to a business of using performance reviews. (3)

In a performance review the manager and employee identify the employee's weaknesses (1), so the business can arrange training targeted at exactly the skills that person needs (1), meaning training money is not wasted and the employee's performance improves (1).
:::

::: example Outline - informal training
Rowan's Bakery Ltd has six shops in Norwich. New sales assistants spend their first week working alongside an experienced assistant. Outline **one** benefit to Rowan's Bakery of this informal training. (2)

New assistants learn exactly how Rowan's serves customers and handles the tills while working in the shop (1), so the bakery has no course fees to pay and the new assistant helps serve customers from day one (1).
:::

::: example Analyse - training, motivation and retention
Velo Couriers plc employs 2,000 delivery riders. It gives new riders a two-hour induction and no further training. Staff turnover is 55% a year, and recruiting and training each new rider costs £900.

Analyse how better training could benefit Velo Couriers. (6)

With 55% turnover, Velo replaces about 1,100 riders a year at £900 each, roughly £990,000 (AO2). If ongoing training and development showed riders that Velo values them and offered a path to roles such as team leader, riders would be more motivated and more likely to stay, so even a small fall in turnover would save a large share of this cost (AO3a).

A two-hour induction gives riders little skill in safe riding or customer service (AO2). Training them properly would reduce accidents, damaged parcels and late deliveries, so fewer customers complain and Velo's reputation with the businesses that use it improves, helping it win and keep delivery contracts (AO3a).
:::

::: example Justify - formal or informal training
Kellerby Pharmacies plc has 220 pharmacies. It wants to train 900 counter assistants to give advice on common medicines.
Option 1: a formal external course leading to a recognised qualification, costing £600 per assistant plus three days away from work.
Option 2: informal on-the-job training, with each assistant shadowing the pharmacist in their shop.

Justify which **one** of these two options Kellerby should choose. (9)

The formal course would give all 900 assistants the same expert knowledge of medicines, so advice is accurate and consistent in all 220 pharmacies (AO2). Because giving advice on medicines affects customers' health, consistent, recognised training reduces the risk of mistakes that could harm customers and damage Kellerby's reputation; the qualification may also motivate assistants and help retain them (AO3a).

However, it costs £600 × 900 = £540,000, plus 2,700 days of lost work, which means paying for cover in busy shops (AO2). Qualified assistants may also be targeted by rival pharmacies offering higher pay (AO3a).

Kellerby should choose the formal course. Shadowing is cheaper, but the quality would depend on each of 220 pharmacists finding time to teach while dispensing, so standards would vary, which is a serious risk where health advice is involved. This depends on Kellerby spreading the course over several months so shops stay staffed, and on linking the qualification to a pay rise so that trained assistants stay (AO3b).
:::

::: q
Marrick Hotels plc has 40 hotels. It sets every receptionist a target of checking guests in within 3 minutes. Guest complaints about rude or rushed receptionists have risen by 25%.

Analyse the problems that Marrick's use of target setting may be causing. (6)
---
Indicative content:
- AO2: 40 hotels; 3-minute check-in target; complaints up 25%.
- AO3a: the target focuses receptionists on speed, so they rush guests and neglect friendliness and answering questions; quality of service falls, reflected in the 25% rise in complaints; unhappy guests leave poor reviews and book elsewhere, reducing revenue; receptionists under pressure may feel stressed and demotivated, raising turnover; a mix of targets (including satisfaction scores) reviewed in performance reviews would balance speed and quality.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): developed chains applied to Marrick throughout.
- Level 2 (3–4): some developed reasoning with some use of the case.
- Level 1 (1–2): isolated points.
An answer with no reference to the context is capped at 3 marks.
:::

::: watch
- **Develop to the business**: "staff feel happy" is not enough. Go on to retention (lower recruitment costs) or productivity and quality.
- Formal is **structured** (courses, qualifications); informal is **on the job** (shadowing, learning from colleagues). Do not mix them up.
- **Self-learning** depends on employees being self-motivated: say so when weighing it.
- Target setting and performance reviews are **development** tools on the spec. Know how each works and what can go wrong (targets that are too hard, reviews seen as unfair).
- For **retraining**, link to the technology: without training, the investment does not deliver productivity or quality gains.
- In a justify, include the **cost** of training (fees, time away from work) and the risk that trained staff leave.
:::
