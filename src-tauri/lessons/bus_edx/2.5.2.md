## What it is

A growing business needs more people, and the right people. Recruiting the wrong person is expensive: they may underperform, leave quickly, and the whole process has to be repeated. Edexcel asks for:
1. **Different job roles and responsibilities**: the **key job roles** and their responsibilities: **directors**, **senior managers**, **supervisors/team leaders**, and **operational** and **support staff**.
2. **How businesses recruit people**: the **documents** (**person specification**, **job description**, **application form**, **CV**) and the **recruitment methods** used to meet different business needs: **internal** and **external** recruitment.

### Key job roles and their responsibilities

| Role | Main responsibilities | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Directors** | decide the business's **overall aims and strategy** (long-term direction); in a company, they are appointed by and accountable to the **shareholders**; make the biggest decisions (entering new markets, takeovers, major investment) | managing director, finance director, marketing director |
| **Senior managers** | turn the directors' strategy into **plans** for their area of the business; manage **budgets** and set targets for departments; manage other managers; make medium-term decisions | head of a region, head of human resources, operations manager |
| **Supervisors / team leaders** | oversee the **day-to-day work** of a team; allocate tasks and rotas; check work is done properly; train and support team members; pass information between managers and staff | shift supervisor in a warehouse, team leader in a call centre |
| **Operational staff** | carry out the **core, day-to-day tasks** that produce the goods or provide the service | production-line workers, sales assistants, chefs, delivery drivers, care workers |
| **Support staff** | provide services that **help the rest of the business run** but are not the core product | IT, cleaning, reception, administration, maintenance, payroll |

**Links**: the more levels of roles, the taller the structure (2.5.1). Directors and senior managers make strategic decisions; supervisors and operational staff carry them out. In a small business, one person may hold several roles: the owner may be director, manager and operational worker at once.

### The recruitment process

**Recruitment** is the process of **finding and attracting** suitable people to apply for a job, then **selecting** the best one.

A typical process:
1. **Identify the vacancy**: someone leaves, the business grows, or a new skill is needed. Could the work be done another way (overtime, a temporary worker, technology)?
2. **Write the job description** and **person specification**.
3. **Advertise** the job, internally or externally.
4. **Applicants send** an **application form** and/or a **CV** (often with a covering letter).
5. **Shortlist** the best applicants by comparing them with the person specification.
6. **Select**: interviews, and sometimes tests, presentations or work trials; take up references.
7. **Appoint** and give a **contract of employment**, followed by induction training (2.5.3).

### The recruitment documents

| Document | What it is | Written by | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Job description** | describes the **job**: job title, main duties and responsibilities, who the job holder reports to, place of work, hours and pay | the **business** | tells applicants what the job involves; later used to set targets and in performance reviews |
| **Person specification** | describes the **ideal person** for the job: qualifications, skills, experience and personal qualities, divided into **essential** and **desirable** | the **business** | helps applicants decide whether to apply; used to **shortlist** and to judge candidates fairly |
| **Application form** | a standard form designed by the business, which applicants fill in with their details, education, employment history and why they want the job | designed by the **business**, completed by the **applicant** | every applicant gives the **same information** in the same order, so they are easy to compare |
| **CV (curriculum vitae)** | a summary of the applicant's personal details, education, qualifications, work experience, skills and interests | the **applicant** | lets applicants present themselves in their own way; quick for them to send |

**The pair to get right**: the **job description** is about the **job**; the **person specification** is about the **person**. Edexcel multiple-choice questions test this every few years.

**Which documents does each side send?** The **business** sends (or publishes) the job description and person specification, and the application form for applicants to complete. The **applicant** sends back a completed **application form** and/or a **CV**.

**Application form or CV?** Application forms are easier to compare and ask exactly what the business wants to know, but take longer for applicants to fill in. CVs are quick to send but vary in layout and content, so they are harder to compare.

### Internal and external recruitment

**Internal recruitment**: filling a vacancy with someone **already working for the business**, for example through promotion or a transfer. Jobs are advertised on staff noticeboards, the intranet, emails or newsletters.

**External recruitment**: filling a vacancy with someone **from outside the business**. Methods include job websites, the business's own website and social media, recruitment agencies, local or national newspapers and specialist magazines, job centres, and recruitment at universities and colleges.

| | Internal recruitment | External recruitment |
|---|---|---|
| **Advantages** | **cheaper** and **quicker** (little advertising, shorter selection); the candidate **already knows** the business, its systems and culture, so needs less induction training; managers already know the candidate's **strengths and record**, reducing the risk of a poor appointment; promotion chances **motivate** staff and help **retain** them | a **wider pool** of applicants, so more chance of finding the best person; brings **new ideas, skills and experience** from other businesses; can fill roles needing skills no current employee has; no knock-on vacancy |
| **Disadvantages** | **smaller pool** of candidates, so the best person may not be found; **no new ideas** come in; creates **another vacancy** to fill (the person's old job); can cause **resentment** among colleagues who were not chosen | **more expensive** (advertising, agency fees, longer selection); **slower**; the new person does not know the business, so needs more **induction** training; **higher risk**, as the business only knows the candidate from an interview and references; may demotivate internal candidates who hoped for promotion |
| **Best for** | promotions to supervisor or manager roles where knowledge of the business matters; when cost and speed matter; to reward and keep good staff | specialist or new skills; senior roles needing fresh ideas or a change of direction; when the business is growing fast and needs many new staff |

**Recruitment methods meet different business needs**: a supermarket hiring 200 seasonal staff might use its website, social media and job centres (cheap, reaching many local people); a technology firm needing a senior software engineer might use a specialist recruitment agency or specialist job sites (expensive, but finds rare skills); a business needing a new store manager who knows its systems might promote an assistant manager internally.

**Selection** (in brief, as it is not named in the spec): **interviews** test communication and attitude; **tests** check skills (typing, maths, aptitude); **work trials** show how candidates actually perform; **references** from previous employers check claims.

**The cost of poor recruitment**: if the wrong person is appointed, the business may suffer lower productivity, mistakes, poor customer service and a poor effect on team morale; if the person leaves, the business pays to recruit and train again. This is why careful documents and selection are worth the cost.

::: terms
- **Director**: a senior person who sets the overall aims and strategy of a business and is accountable to its owners.
- **Senior manager**: a manager who turns strategy into plans for a department or area and manages its budget and other managers.
- **Supervisor or team leader**: a person who oversees the day-to-day work of a team and supports team members.
- **Operational staff**: employees who carry out the core day-to-day tasks that produce goods or provide services.
- **Support staff**: employees who provide services that help the rest of the business run, such as IT, cleaning or administration.
- **Recruitment**: the process of finding, attracting and selecting suitable people to fill a job vacancy.
- **Job description**: a document describing a job: its title, duties, responsibilities and who the holder reports to.
- **Person specification**: a document describing the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities of the ideal person for a job.
- **Application form**: a standard form, designed by the business, that applicants complete with details about themselves.
- **CV (curriculum vitae)**: a document written by an applicant summarising their education, qualifications, experience and skills.
- **Internal recruitment**: filling a vacancy with a person who already works for the business.
- **External recruitment**: filling a vacancy with a person from outside the business.
:::

::: method
1. **Roles**: match responsibilities to the right level: strategy (directors), plans and budgets (senior managers), day-to-day team work (supervisors), core tasks (operational), helping the business run (support).
2. **Documents**: say who writes it and what it is used for. Job description = the job; person specification = the person; application form and CV = sent by the applicant.
3. **Explain (3)**: point → how it affects cost, time, risk or motivation → effect on the business (productivity, staff turnover, costs, customer service).
4. **Internal or external**: use the case. What skills does the role need? Is there a suitable internal candidate? How urgent is it? Does the business need new ideas?
5. **Justify (9)**: pick one method, give its benefits and drawbacks for this role, then conclude with a "depends on" factor, such as whether any current employee has the skills.
:::

::: example Explain - internal recruitment
Explain **one** advantage to a business of using internal recruitment. (3)

The person appointed already works for the business, so managers know their skills and record (1), which reduces the risk of appointing someone who cannot do the job (1), so the business is less likely to suffer poor performance or have to pay to recruit again (1).
:::

::: example Explain - person specification
Explain **one** benefit to a business of using a person specification when recruiting. (3)

A person specification sets out the essential and desirable skills and qualifications for the job (1), so applications can be compared against the same criteria when shortlisting (1), helping the business choose candidates who can do the job well and saving time interviewing unsuitable people (1).
:::

::: example Outline - responsibility of a role
Harrowgate Hotels plc has 35 hotels. Each hotel has a general manager who reports to a regional director. Outline **one** responsibility of a Harrowgate hotel general manager. (2)

A general manager is responsible for meeting the budget and targets set for their hotel by the regional director (1), for example controlling staffing costs and room occupancy so that the hotel contributes to Harrowgate's profit (1).
:::

::: example Analyse - external recruitment
Novalis Robotics plc makes warehouse robots. It wants to launch software that uses artificial intelligence, but none of its current engineers has experience in this area.

Analyse the benefits to Novalis of using external recruitment for this project. (6)

None of Novalis's engineers has AI experience, so recruiting externally is the only way to gain the skills quickly (AO2). Using a specialist agency or specialist job sites gives Novalis a much wider pool of experienced AI engineers, so the software is more likely to be developed well and launched sooner, helping Novalis stay ahead of rival robot makers (AO3a).

External recruits also bring new ideas from other businesses (AO2). An engineer who has worked on AI elsewhere will know what has worked and what has failed, so Novalis avoids costly mistakes, and the knowledge can be passed on to existing engineers, raising the skills of the whole team for future products (AO3a).
:::

::: example Justify - internal or external
Caraway Supermarkets plc needs a new manager for its largest store, which employs 300 staff. The store's deputy manager has worked there for 8 years and is popular with staff. The store's sales have been flat for three years.
Option 1: recruit internally by promoting the deputy manager.
Option 2: recruit externally through a recruitment agency, costing £14,000.

Justify which **one** of these two options Caraway should choose. (9)

Promoting the deputy manager is quicker and cheaper, saving the £14,000 agency fee, and after 8 years they already know the store, its systems and its 300 staff (AO2). Because they are popular, staff are likely to support them, and seeing a colleague promoted shows others that hard work is rewarded, which motivates staff and helps Caraway keep good employees (AO3a).

However, the store's sales have been flat for three years, and the deputy has been part of the team running it during that time (AO2). They may continue doing things the same way, so sales stay flat, whereas an external manager could bring new ideas from another retailer; promoting the deputy also creates a new vacancy that must be filled (AO3a).

Caraway should promote the deputy manager, because their knowledge of the store and support from staff mean they can start making changes immediately, and the £14,000 can be spent on management training instead. This depends on the deputy having clear ideas to grow sales; if they cannot show a plan in the interview, an external candidate would be the better choice (AO3b).
:::

::: q
Discuss the benefits to a business of using internal recruitment. (6)
---
Indicative content (AO1b, AO3a):
- Cheaper and faster: little advertising, shorter selection.
- Candidate knows the business, so less induction training and productive sooner.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): accurate understanding; two developed lines of reasoning.
- Level 2 (3–4): some developed reasoning.

::: q
Brackenfield Care plc runs 60 care homes. It needs 400 new care assistants across England this year, and many of its job adverts get few applicants.

Analyse how Brackenfield could use different recruitment methods to meet its needs. (6)
---
Indicative content:
- AO2: 60 care homes; 400 care assistants; few applicants.
- AO3a: external recruitment is needed for 400 operational staff; local job centres, its own website and social media reach many local people cheaply; colleges with care courses provide trained applicants; agencies are faster but expensive across 400 roles; internal recruitment could promote experienced care assistants to senior roles, freeing entry-level posts and motivating staff; clear job descriptions and person specifications help attract suitable people; selection needs to check values and care skills, as poor appointments harm residents and reputation.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): developed chains applied to Brackenfield throughout.
- Level 2 (3–4): some developed reasoning with some use of the case.
- Level 1 (1–2): isolated points.
An answer with no reference to the context is capped at 3 marks.
:::

::: watch
- **Job description = the job. Person specification = the person.** Swapping them is the most frequent error.
- Know **who sends what**: the business writes the job description and person specification; the applicant sends a CV and/or completed application form.
- Match responsibilities to the right role: directors set **strategy**; supervisors manage **day-to-day** work.
- Develop internal or external recruitment to a business effect (cost, time, risk, motivation, new ideas). "It is cheaper" alone earns 1 mark.
- Internal recruitment **creates another vacancy**. Strong answers remember this.
- In a justify, use the case: the skills the role needs and whether anyone inside has them.
:::
