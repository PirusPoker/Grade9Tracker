## What it is

Making a good product is not enough: the business also has to **sell** it well and look after customers before, during and after the sale. Edexcel asks for two things:
1. **The sales process**: **product knowledge**, **speed and efficiency of service**, **customer engagement**, **responses to customer feedback** and **post-sales service**.
2. **The importance to businesses of providing good customer service**.

Think of the **sales process** as the customer's journey with the business: from first contact, through buying, to the support they get afterwards. Each stage is a chance to win or lose the customer.

### The five elements of the sales process

| Element | What it means | In a shop or service | Online |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Product knowledge** | staff (or information) can explain features, uses, differences between products and suitability for the customer | a phone-shop assistant explaining which contract suits a light user; a mechanic explaining a repair | detailed product descriptions, size guides, comparison tools, a live chat with trained staff |
| **Speed and efficiency of service** | customers are served quickly, accurately and without hassle | short queues, enough tills, orders that are right first time | a fast website, simple checkout, quick delivery, quick replies to messages |
| **Customer engagement** | building a relationship with customers by communicating with them and making them feel valued | greeting customers, asking about their needs, remembering regulars, loyalty schemes | personalised emails and recommendations, social media conversations, loyalty apps |
| **Responses to customer feedback** | listening to what customers say (praise and complaints) and acting on it | dealing with complaints politely and quickly; changing products or processes after feedback | replying to online reviews; surveys after purchase; showing "you said, we did" changes |
| **Post-sales service** | support after the sale | warranties, repairs, servicing, installation, help lines, easy returns and refunds | tracking, easy returns, online help and set-up guides, follow-up emails |

**Product knowledge** matters most for **complex or expensive** products (cars, computers, holidays, financial services), where customers need advice before they commit. A knowledgeable assistant can match the product to the customer's needs, which closes the sale and reduces returns. They may also **upsell** (a more expensive model) or **cross-sell** (a case for the phone).

**Speed and efficiency** matter most where customers are short of time: fast food, supermarkets, train stations, online shopping. Slow, inaccurate service loses sales directly (customers walk out of long queues or abandon online baskets).

**Customer engagement** turns one-off buyers into **loyal** customers. Engagement also gives the business information about what customers want (market research, 1.2.3).

**Responses to customer feedback**: complaints are free information about what is going wrong. A complaint handled well can leave a customer **more** loyal than before, because they see the business cares. Ignored feedback, especially public reviews, puts off future customers.

**Post-sales service** gives customers **confidence to buy** (they know they will be supported if something goes wrong) and creates further revenue from servicing, accessories and repeat purchases. For products such as cars or boilers, regular servicing keeps the customer in contact with the business.

### Why good customer service matters

**Customer service** is the way a business treats its customers before, during and after a sale. Good customer service meets or exceeds customer expectations. It matters because:

- **Customer loyalty and repeat custom**: satisfied customers come back. Keeping an existing customer is usually **cheaper** than attracting a new one through promotion.
- **Reviews and word of mouth**: happy customers recommend the business to friends and leave good online reviews, which attract new customers **at little cost**. Unhappy customers tell others too, and online reviews are seen by many potential buyers.
- **Ability to charge a higher price**: customers will pay more for a business that looks after them, so profit margins can be higher. Good service can **differentiate** a business whose products are similar to rivals', giving a **competitive advantage** (2.2.5).
- **Higher sales per customer**: knowledgeable, engaged staff can upsell and cross-sell.
- **Reputation and brand image**: consistent service builds trust in the brand.
- **Feedback for improvement**: engaged customers tell the business what to improve, helping it stay ahead of changing customer needs.
- **Motivated staff**: staff who are trained and proud of the service they give may be more motivated (link to 2.5.3 and 2.5.4).

### The costs of poor customer service

- **Lost sales**: customers leave queues, abandon online baskets or buy elsewhere.
- **Lost customers**: they switch to rivals and may never return.
- **Bad reviews and word of mouth**, putting off new customers. Negative social media posts can spread widely.
- **Extra costs**: handling complaints, refunds, compensation, and repeat work (an order that was wrong the first time).
- **Damage to reputation**, which takes much longer to rebuild than to lose.
- **Legal problems** if service breaks consumer rights, for example refusing a refund for faulty goods (1.5.1).

### Improving the sales process: the trade-offs

Improving service usually **costs money**: training staff, hiring more staff at busy times, better websites, longer warranties. A business must weigh these costs against the extra sales, loyalty and higher prices. **Technology** is a common answer (self-service tills, chatbots, apps, online tracking): it can raise **speed and efficiency** and cut wage costs, but it may lose the **personal engagement** and **product knowledge** that some customers value, and frustrate customers when it goes wrong. The right balance depends on the **product** (simple or complex) and the **customers** (price-focused or service-focused).

::: terms
- **Sales process**: the stages a business goes through with a customer from first contact, through the sale, to support afterwards.
- **Product knowledge**: understanding a product's features, uses and benefits well enough to advise customers.
- **Customer engagement**: building a relationship with customers through communication so that they feel valued and return.
- **Customer feedback**: information from customers about their experience, including reviews, surveys and complaints.
- **Post-sales service**: support given to customers after they have bought, such as warranties, repairs, servicing and returns.
- **Customer service**: the way a business treats its customers before, during and after a purchase.
- **Customer loyalty**: when customers keep buying from the same business rather than switching to rivals.
- **Word of mouth**: customers telling other people about their experience of a business, good or bad.
- **Repeat custom**: sales to customers who have bought from the business before.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the element** of the sales process the question is about, using the spec's words: product knowledge; speed and efficiency of service; customer engagement; responses to customer feedback; post-sales service.
2. **Explain (3)**: point → how it affects the customer → effect on the business (loyalty, repeat custom, reviews, a higher price, sales, profit).
3. **Always finish on a business gain or loss.** "Customers are happy" earns 1 mark at most; "so they return and buy again, increasing sales" develops it.
4. **Use the case**: the product (complex or simple), the customers, the review scores, complaint figures or waiting times given.
5. **Weigh the cost**: better service costs money (training, staff, technology). In a justify, set the extra costs against the extra sales.
6. **Justify (9)**: one option, its benefits and drawbacks, then a conclusion with a new "depends on" factor, such as whether customers value speed or personal advice more.
:::

::: example Explain - benefit of good customer service
Explain **one** benefit to a business of providing good customer service. (3)

Customers who receive good service are more likely to return (1), so the business gains repeat custom without having to spend as much on promotion to attract new buyers (1), which increases sales revenue and helps keep costs down, raising profit (1).
:::

::: example Explain - post-sales service
Explain **one** way post-sales service can help a business increase its sales. (3)

Post-sales service, such as a three-year warranty and free repairs, reduces the risk to the customer if the product goes wrong (1), so customers feel more confident buying, especially an expensive item (1), which persuades more people to buy from this business rather than a rival without such support (1).
:::

::: example Outline - product knowledge
Volta Cycles plc has 85 bike shops in the UK selling electric bikes priced from £1,500 to £4,000. Outline **one** benefit to Volta Cycles of staff having good product knowledge. (2)

E-bikes from £1,500 to £4,000 are expensive and technical, so staff who can explain battery range and motor types help customers choose the right bike with confidence (1), making them more likely to buy from Volta and less likely to return the bike, increasing sales (1).
:::

::: example Analyse - responses to customer feedback
Lantern Cinemas plc runs 70 cinemas. Its online reviews mention dirty screens and long queues for food, and it has not replied to any reviews in the last year. Its average rating has fallen to 3.2 stars, while a rival chain has 4.4.

Analyse the impact on Lantern Cinemas of not responding to customer feedback. (6)

By ignoring reviews about dirty screens and long food queues, Lantern is not fixing the problems customers keep raising, so the same poor experiences continue across its 70 cinemas (AO2). Customers who complained feel ignored and will choose the rival chain next time, so Lantern loses ticket sales and, importantly, high-margin food and drink sales (AO3a).

Its 3.2 rating is visible to anyone choosing a cinema online, against 4.4 for the rival (AO2). Because potential customers see unanswered complaints, they assume Lantern does not care, so the poor reputation spreads to new customers as well as existing ones, reducing revenue further; replying and acting on feedback could show customers that things have changed (AO3a).
:::

::: example Justify - staff training or self-service
Crestway Supermarkets plc has 300 stores. Customer surveys show that 35% of shoppers are unhappy with queue times and 25% say staff could not answer product questions.
Option 1: spend £6 million training staff in product knowledge and customer service.
Option 2: spend £15 million installing self-service tills in every store, allowing 900 fewer checkout staff.

Justify which **one** of these two options Crestway should choose. (9)

Self-service tills tackle the biggest complaint, with 35% of shoppers unhappy about queues, because more tills can be open at once and customers buying a few items are served faster (AO2). With 900 fewer checkout staff, Crestway's wage costs fall every year, so the £15 million could be recovered and lower costs could support competitive prices in a price-focused market (AO3a).

However, self-service does nothing for the 25% who say staff cannot answer questions, and fewer staff on the shop floor could make this worse (AO2). Some shoppers, especially older customers, dislike self-service tills, and errors at the tills frustrate customers, which could lower satisfaction and send them to rivals; job losses may also harm morale and Crestway's reputation (AO3a).

Crestway should install self-service tills, because queues are the larger problem and the saving in wages makes the investment pay for itself over time, while training costs £6 million without cutting costs. This depends on Crestway keeping some staffed tills and redeploying some checkout staff to help customers on the shop floor, so that the product-knowledge problem is not made worse (AO3b).
:::

::: q
Snapfeast Ltd is an online takeaway delivery company in UK cities. Its average delivery time has risen from 30 to 48 minutes, and 18% of orders now arrive cold.

Analyse the impact on Snapfeast of its slower speed of service. (6)
---
Indicative content:
- AO2: online takeaway delivery; delivery time up from 30 to 48 minutes; 18% of orders cold.
- AO3a: hungry customers value speed, so they will switch to rival delivery apps, reducing orders and revenue; cold food means refunds or credits, adding costs; poor app reviews put off new customers; restaurants may leave Snapfeast if their food arrives cold and harms their reputation; Snapfeast may need more drivers, adding costs.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): developed chains applied to Snapfeast throughout.
- Level 2 (3–4): some developed reasoning with some use of the case.
- Level 1 (1–2): isolated points.
An answer with no reference to the context is capped at 3 marks.
:::

::: watch
- Use the spec's five elements **by name**. "Being nice to customers" is not an element.
- Always develop to a **business benefit**: loyalty → repeat custom → sales; good reviews → new customers at low cost; good service → a higher price.
- Customer feedback means **listening and acting**. Develop how acting on it changes the product or service.
- **Post-sales service** happens after the sale: warranties, repairs, returns, help lines. It is not promotion.
- Good service **costs money**. In a justify or evaluate, weigh training, staffing or technology costs against the gains.
- Technology can speed up service but may reduce personal engagement and product knowledge.
:::
