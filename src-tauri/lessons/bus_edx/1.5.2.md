## What it is

Technology is one of the main **external influences** on business: it changes what customers expect, what businesses can offer and what they spend. Edexcel names four **types of technology** and three areas of **business activity** they influence: **sales, costs and the marketing mix**.

### Types of technology used by business

| Type | What it is | Small-business examples |
|---|---|---|
| **E-commerce** | Buying and selling goods and services online | A website shop; selling through an online marketplace; a social-media shop; taking bookings and payment online |
| **Social media** | Online platforms where users create and share content and interact | Posting product photos and videos; running targeted adverts; replying to customers' comments and messages; working with local influencers |
| **Digital communication** | Electronic ways of communicating inside and outside the business | Email newsletters; messaging apps for orders and customer queries; video calls with suppliers or clients; shared online documents and calendars for staff rotas |
| **Payment systems** | Electronic ways for customers to pay | Card readers and contactless payment; mobile and app payments; online checkout; payment links sent by message |

### How technology influences sales

- **E-commerce** opens the business to customers far beyond its local area, 24 hours a day, so sales can rise without a bigger shop.
- **Social media** builds awareness and word of mouth: posts are shared, so more potential customers hear about the business, often for free.
- **Digital payment systems** make buying quicker and easier. Customers who carry no cash can still buy, impulse purchases are easier, and online checkout lets them buy the moment they decide. A business that only takes cash may lose sales.
- **Digital communication** lets businesses answer queries quickly and send offers to past customers, encouraging repeat sales.

Technology can also **threaten** sales: customers can compare prices instantly, and online rivals anywhere in the country compete for the same customers.

### How technology influences costs

**Technology can reduce costs:**
- An online-only business avoids the rent of a high-street shop (1.4.2).
- Social media promotion is cheaper than print or radio advertising, and can be targeted so less is wasted.
- Email and messaging replace post and phone calls; video calls save travel.
- Card payments reduce the time and risk of handling cash and banking it.
- Online booking systems reduce admin time.

**Technology also creates costs:**
- **Set-up costs**: building a website, buying card readers, software and equipment.
- **Running costs**: website hosting and maintenance, software subscriptions, marketplace and app commission.
- **Transaction fees**: card and online payment providers charge a fee on each payment.
- **Training**: staff need time and training to use new systems.
- **Security**: protecting customer data and payments from fraud and cyber attacks.
- **Keeping up**: technology changes quickly, so equipment and skills must be updated.

### How technology influences the marketing mix

| P | Influence of technology |
|---|---|
| **Product** | Customer feedback from reviews and social media helps improve products; digital products and services (online classes, downloadable patterns) become possible; personalisation is easier |
| **Price** | Customers compare prices instantly online, so prices must be competitive; lower costs of online selling can allow lower prices; prices can be changed quickly |
| **Promotion** | Social media and digital communication give cheap, targeted, measurable promotion; customer reviews and shares act as word of mouth |
| **Place** | E-commerce means products can be sold online, to more customers, at any time; marketplaces and delivery apps provide new places to sell |

**E-commerce is a way of selling (place), not a form of promotion.** Examiners reported in 2025 that answers describing e-commerce as advertising were not credited. Social media can be both: posts and adverts are promotion; a social-media shop is a place.

### Weighing technology for a small business

Technology is not automatically good. A small business should ask:
- Will it bring **enough extra sales** or **cost savings** to cover its set-up and running costs?
- Do the **target customers** use it? Older customers may prefer a phone call; young customers expect contactless and social media.
- Does the owner have the **skills and time** to use it well? A neglected social media account or a slow website can harm the business's image.
- What are the **risks**: fees, security, dependence on a platform's rules?

::: terms
- **Technology**: equipment, software and systems used to carry out business activities.
- **E-commerce**: buying and selling goods and services online.
- **Social media**: online platforms on which users create, share and interact with content.
- **Digital communication**: electronic ways of communicating, such as email, messaging apps and video calls.
- **Payment systems**: the methods customers use to pay, such as card readers, contactless, mobile and online payments.
- **Transaction fee**: a charge made by a payment provider or platform on each sale.
- **Set-up costs**: one-off costs of introducing technology, such as building a website or buying equipment.
- **Online marketplace**: a website on which many businesses sell their products to customers.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the type of technology** using the spec words: e-commerce, social media, digital communication, payment systems.
2. **Link it to sales, costs or a P** of the marketing mix, then develop the chain to revenue or profit.
3. **Keep e-commerce under place (selling).** Social media adverts and posts are promotion.
4. **Always consider the cost side**: set-up, fees, training, security. A strong answer weighs benefits against costs.
5. **Apply the case**: what the business sells, who its customers are, and how they like to buy and pay.
6. **Justify (9)** between two technologies: choose one; weigh the extra sales or savings against its cost; conclude with a "depends on" factor such as the customers' age or the owner's skills.
:::

::: example Explain - e-commerce and sales
Explain **one** way e-commerce could increase a small business's sales. (3)

E-commerce lets the business sell through a website to customers anywhere, not just those who visit (1). Customers in other towns, or who cannot get to the shop during opening hours, can now buy at any time of day (1), so the business reaches a much larger market and makes sales it would otherwise have missed (1).
:::

::: example Outline - drawback of payment systems
Bella runs Bella's Bagels, a stall at a market in Bristol. She has started accepting card and contactless payments. Outline **one** drawback to Bella of using digital payment systems. (2)

Bella pays a transaction fee to the card-payment provider on every sale (1), so on low-priced items such as a bagel, a small fee on each sale noticeably reduces her profit per item (1). Or: the card reader depends on a phone signal at the stall, developed.
:::

::: example Explain - social media and costs
Explain **one** way social media can reduce a small business's costs. (3)

It is a cheap form of promotion (1): posting on social media is free and paid adverts can be targeted to people of the right age and area (1), so the business spends less than on newspaper or radio adverts and wastes less money on people who will never buy (1).
:::

::: example Analyse - technology and the mix
Ling runs Stitch Studio, a sewing-class business in Cambridge. She has started live-streaming classes online and selling digital sewing patterns through her website. Analyse how technology has influenced Stitch Studio's marketing mix. (6)

Product: technology has allowed Ling to create new digital products, live online classes and downloadable patterns, alongside her in-person classes (AO2). These have almost no cost per extra customer, so each sale adds nearly its full price to profit, which raises her profit margin (AO3a).

Place: e-commerce means students no longer need to travel to Cambridge (AO2). Ling can sell to people across the UK and abroad, at any time, so her market is no longer limited by the size of her studio; however, she now competes with many free online sewing videos, so her promotion through social media must show why her classes are worth paying for, perhaps through clips of students' finished projects (AO3a).
:::

::: example Justify - website or social media
Pete runs Pete's Plants, a garden nursery near Doncaster. He has £2,500 to invest. Option 1: build a website with an online shop and local delivery. Option 2: spend the money on social-media marketing over six months. Justify which **one** of these options Pete should choose. (9)

A website with an online shop gives Pete a new place to sell, so customers who cannot visit the nursery, or who want to order heavy plants and compost for delivery, can buy at any time (AO2). This is an extra source of sales that lasts after the £2,500 is spent, so it could raise revenue for years (AO3a).

However, people may not find the website unless he also promotes it, and local delivery adds costs for a van, fuel and staff time. Plants are delicate, so some may be damaged in transit, leading to refunds (AO3a).

Pete should build the website, because it creates a lasting way of selling rather than six months of promotion that stops when the money runs out. This depends on his customers: if most are older gardeners who enjoy visiting the nursery and rarely shop online, social media to bring more of them to the site may be the better use of £2,500 (AO3b).
:::

::: q
Explain **one** cost to a small business of introducing e-commerce. (3)
---
1 mark plus up to 2. For example: building and maintaining a website (1) requires paying a designer and ongoing hosting fees (1), which raise fixed costs, so the business must make enough extra online sales to cover them (1).
:::

::: q
Explain **one** way digital communication can help a business work with its suppliers. (3)
---
1 mark plus up to 2. For example: email and messaging let orders be placed instantly (1), so stock arrives faster and mistakes are easier to correct in writing (1), which reduces the risk of running out of stock and losing sales (1).
:::

::: q
Explain **one** risk to a business of using social media. (3)
---
1 mark plus up to 2. For example: negative comments or reviews can spread quickly (1), so a single unhappy customer can reach thousands of potential customers (1), damaging the business's reputation and reducing sales (1).
:::

::: q
Analyse the impact on Tide Café, a seafront café in Whitby, of switching to card and contactless payments only.
(6)
---
Indicative content: tourists and younger customers increasingly do not carry cash, so more sales and quicker service at busy times; less time spent counting and banking cash, lower theft risk; but transaction fees on every sale reduce profit; older customers or those who prefer cash may go elsewhere; dependence on card reader and signal, so a breakdown stops sales.
Level 3 (5–6): developed chains applied to Tide Café. Level 2 (3–4): some development. Level 1 (1–2): isolated points. No context: maximum 3.

::: watch
- **E-commerce is selling (place), not promotion.** Do not call a website shop "advertising".
- Use the **four spec types** by name and say which one you mean.
- Link to the spec's **three effects**: sales, costs, marketing mix.
- Technology **costs money**: set-up, running costs, transaction fees, training and security. Include them for balance.
- Consider the **customers**: technology helps only if the target market uses it.
- A drawback of payment systems is usually **fees** or **dependence on the system**, not "people cannot pay".
:::
