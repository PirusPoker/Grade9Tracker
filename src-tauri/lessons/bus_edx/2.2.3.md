## What it is

**Promotion** is how a business **communicates** with customers to make them **aware** of its products, **persuade** them to buy and **remind** them to buy again. In Theme 1 (1.4.3) you met promotion as part of the marketing mix. Theme 2 asks two more detailed questions:
1. Which **promotion strategies** are **appropriate for different market segments**? The spec names five: **advertising, sponsorship, product trials, special offers and branding**.
2. How is **technology** used in promotion? The spec names three: **targeted advertising online, viral advertising via social media and e-newsletters**.

The key idea throughout is **fit**: the right method depends on the **target segment** (who they are, where they are and what media they use), the **product**, and the **budget**.

### Promotion strategies

**Advertising**
- Paying to put a message in the media: TV, radio, cinema, newspapers, magazines, billboards, posters and online.
- **Strengths**: reaches large audiences; builds awareness quickly; the business controls the message.
- **Weaknesses**: can be very **expensive** (national TV especially); hard to measure what sales it creates; many people ignore or skip adverts.
- **Fit**: choose media the segment uses. Older customers may read newspapers or watch TV; younger customers are more easily reached online; specialist magazines reach hobby segments.

**Sponsorship**
- A business **pays to be associated** with an event, team, TV programme or person, in return for its name and logo being shown.
- **Strengths**: builds brand awareness and a **positive image** by linking the brand with something customers like; reaches a defined audience (for example sports fans).
- **Weaknesses**: expensive; hard to measure the effect on sales; risk of bad publicity if the team or person is involved in a scandal.
- **Fit**: choose events the target segment cares about, such as a sports drink sponsoring a marathon.

**Product trials**
- Letting customers **try the product free or at low cost** before buying: free samples, tasters in shops, free trial periods for apps and subscriptions, test drives.
- **Strengths**: removes the **risk** for customers of buying something unfamiliar; if they like it they are likely to buy; especially useful for **new products**.
- **Weaknesses**: the cost of giving away products; some people take the sample and never buy.
- **Fit**: products customers need to experience: food, drink, cosmetics, software and subscription services.

**Special offers**
- Short-term incentives to buy: **discounts**, "buy one get one free", multi-buys, coupons, loyalty points, free gifts.
- **Strengths**: quick boost to sales; attracts **price-sensitive** customers; can encourage customers to try a brand or clear old stock.
- **Weaknesses**: reduces the **profit margin**; sales may fall back when the offer ends; overuse can cheapen a brand's image, and customers may wait for the next offer.
- **Fit**: price-conscious segments, such as families and students; less suitable for luxury brands.

**Branding**
- Creating a distinctive **identity** for a product or business: a name, logo, design, colours, slogan and personality, so customers recognise it and associate it with certain qualities.
- **Strengths**: builds **recognition**, **trust** and **loyalty**; allows a **higher price**; makes launching new products under the same brand easier.
- **Weaknesses**: takes time and money to build; a damaged brand affects all its products.
- **Fit**: every segment, but the brand's image must match the segment (luxury, value, ethical, youthful).

### Technology in promotion

**Targeted advertising online**
- Online platforms (search engines, social media, websites) collect **data** on users' age, location, interests and browsing, so a business can show adverts only to people in its **target segment**.
- **Strengths**: **less wasted spending**, as adverts reach the right people; businesses often pay only when someone clicks; results can be **measured** precisely (clicks, sales); suits small budgets.
- **Weaknesses**: users may find it intrusive and use ad-blockers; costs rise with competition for popular search terms; data privacy rules must be followed.

**Viral advertising via social media**
- Creating content (videos, images, challenges) so engaging that **users share it** with their friends, spreading it rapidly to millions at little cost.
- **Strengths**: **very low cost** for huge reach; sharing by friends feels more trustworthy than an advert.
- **Weaknesses**: **unpredictable**: most content does not go viral; the business **loses control** once people start sharing, and content can be mocked or attract negative comments.

**E-newsletters**
- Regular emails sent to customers who have **signed up**, with news, new products, offers and content.
- **Strengths**: **cheap**; reaches people already interested in the brand, so it encourages **repeat purchases** and loyalty; can be **personalised** using customers' purchase history.
- **Weaknesses**: only reaches existing customers or subscribers, not new ones; many emails go unread or to spam folders; customers unsubscribe if they receive too many.

### Measuring effectiveness

A business should check whether promotion is worth its cost by tracking **sales** before and after a campaign, **website visits and clicks**, discount code use, social media engagement, and asking new customers how they heard about the business. Online methods are much easier to measure than traditional ones such as sponsorship.

### Matching promotion to segments: summary

| Segment | Likely to suit |
|---|---|
| Teenagers and young adults | Viral social media content, targeted online adverts, influencer content, product trials |
| Families on a budget | Special offers, multi-buys, loyalty schemes, e-newsletters with offers |
| Older customers | Newspaper and TV advertising, direct mail, branding that signals trust |
| High-income, luxury buyers | Strong branding, premium magazine advertising, sponsorship of prestige events; few discounts |
| Business customers | Trade shows, specialist publications, targeted online adverts, product trials |

::: terms
- **Promotion**: communicating with customers to inform them about, persuade them to buy, and remind them of a product.
- **Advertising**: paying to place a message about a product in the media, such as TV, print or online.
- **Sponsorship**: paying to be associated with an event, team or person in return for publicity.
- **Product trial**: letting customers try a product free or cheaply before they buy it.
- **Special offer**: a short-term incentive to buy, such as a discount or "buy one get one free".
- **Branding**: creating a distinctive name, logo, design and image so customers recognise and trust a product.
- **Targeted advertising**: online adverts shown only to people whose data suggests they are in the business's target segment.
- **Viral advertising**: content designed to be shared rapidly from person to person on social media.
- **E-newsletter**: a regular email sent to customers who have signed up, with news, products and offers.
- **Market segment**: a group of customers with similar characteristics, such as age, income or lifestyle.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the method precisely** using the spec's terms: advertising, sponsorship, product trials, special offers, branding, targeted online advertising, viral advertising, e-newsletters.
2. **For an explain on a method**, say what it is or does, then develop how it reaches or persuades customers, then the effect on sales, loyalty or costs.
3. **Match the method to the segment**: who the customers are, which media they use, and whether they are price-sensitive.
4. **Weigh the cost against the reach**: national advertising and sponsorship are expensive; online targeted adverts and e-newsletters are cheap; viral content is cheap but unpredictable.
5. **Apply the case**: the budget, the product, the segment and any figures given.
6. **Justify or evaluate**: choose one method, give its benefits and drawbacks for this business and this budget, then conclude with a "depends on" factor, such as whether the aim is new customers or repeat sales.
:::

::: example Explain - sponsorship
Explain **one** reason why a business may use sponsorship. (3)

Sponsorship links the business's brand with an event or team that its target customers already like (1), so the brand is seen by a large audience in a positive setting (1), which builds brand awareness and a good image, encouraging fans to choose its products (1).
:::

::: example Explain - product trials
Explain **one** benefit to a business of offering product trials. (3)

Customers can try the product without the risk of paying for something they may not like (1), so more people are willing to test a new or unfamiliar product (1), and those who like it are likely to buy it, increasing sales (1).
:::

::: example Outline - e-newsletters in context
Harbour Pets plc sells pet food and accessories through its website and 50 shops. It has 200,000 customers signed up to receive emails. Outline **one** benefit to Harbour Pets of using e-newsletters. (2)

Harbour Pets can send cheap, personalised offers to its 200,000 signed-up customers, for example on the type of pet food they usually buy (1), encouraging them to make repeat purchases online or in its shops (1).
:::

::: example Analyse - social media promotion
Zest Drinks plc has launched a new energy drink aimed at 18–25-year-olds. Analyse the advantages to Zest Drinks of promoting the drink through social media. (6)

Most 18–25-year-olds spend a lot of time on social media, so Zest can reach its exact target segment where they already are (AO2). Targeted adverts can be shown only to users in this age group with interests such as gaming or sport, so less of Zest's budget is wasted on people who would never buy, and it can measure clicks and sales to see what works (AO3a).

If Zest creates content that young people enjoy and share, such as challenges or short videos, the drink could spread virally (AO2). Friends sharing content is more trusted than a paid advert, and each share reaches new potential customers for free, so awareness of the new drink could grow much faster and more cheaply than through TV advertising (AO3a).
:::

::: example Justify - choosing promotion with a budget
Ridgeway Boots plc makes premium walking boots for experienced hikers. It has a promotion budget of £200,000. Option 1: sponsor a well-known long-distance hiking event. Option 2: run a national special offer of 25% off all boots. Justify which **one** of these options Ridgeway Boots should choose. (9)

Sponsoring a hiking event puts Ridgeway's brand in front of experienced hikers, exactly its target segment, in a setting where boots matter (AO2). Linking the brand with a respected event supports its premium image, building recognition and trust, so hikers are more likely to choose Ridgeway and pay its higher prices (AO3a).

However, sponsorship is hard to measure, and £200,000 may be spent with no clear rise in sales. The audience at one event is limited, and if something goes wrong at the event, such as an accident, the brand could be linked to bad publicity (AO3a).

Ridgeway should choose sponsorship, because a 25% discount would cut its margin on every pair and could damage the premium image that justifies its prices. This depends on how many hikers the event reaches, including through coverage online: if the audience is small, targeted online advertising to hikers might give better value from the same budget (AO3b).
:::

::: watch
- **Match the method to the segment and the budget.** National TV advertising for a small, local or niche market is not a credible choice.
- Learn the spec's **eight** named methods and use their exact names: advertising, sponsorship, product trials, special offers, branding, targeted advertising online, viral advertising via social media, e-newsletters.
- An e-newsletter goes to people who **have signed up**, so it mainly drives **repeat** sales, not new customers.
- Viral advertising is **cheap but unpredictable**. Do not assume content will go viral.
- **Special offers** cut margins and can cheapen a premium brand. Consider the brand's image before recommending them.
- Promotion is not the same as **place**. Selling online (e-commerce) is a distribution channel, not a promotion method.
