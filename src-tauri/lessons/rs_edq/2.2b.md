## What it is

This lesson covers two linked Christian beliefs: **creation** and the **authority of the Bible**. You need: **Genesis 1-3**, the **nature and role of humans**, and **literal and non-literal** ways of interpreting it; the role of the **Word** and the **Spirit** in creation (**John 1:1-5**); and the Bible as **law** and the **Word of God**: **inspiration** and **revelation**, differing ways of **interpreting** biblical writings, and the Bible's place beside **other sources of authority**.

It is part of Question 1 (Beliefs) in Component 2. The June 2025 paper asked students to explain the different ways Christians interpret the creation account, so **diversity** of interpretation is the heart of this topic.

### Genesis 1-3: the accounts of creation and the Fall

**Genesis 1 (and 2:1-3), the six days**: "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." The earth was formless and empty, and **the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters** (1:2). God creates by **speaking**: "Let there be light". Over six days he creates light and darkness; the sky; land, seas and plants; sun, moon and stars; sea creatures and birds; land animals and finally **humans**, male and female, **in his own image** (1:27), giving them responsibility over the earth. He sees that everything is "very good" (1:31), and on the seventh day he **rests**, making it holy.

**Genesis 2, the garden**: God forms **Adam** from the **dust** of the ground and breathes into him the **breath of life** (2:7). He places him in the **Garden of Eden** "to work it and take care of it" (2:15). He commands him not to eat from the **tree of the knowledge of good and evil**. Saying it is not good for the man to be alone, God makes **Eve** from Adam's rib, as a partner.

**Genesis 3, the Fall**: a **serpent** tempts Eve to eat the forbidden fruit; she and Adam both eat. They realise they are naked and hide from God. God punishes the serpent, the woman (pain in childbirth) and the man (hard labour), and they are **expelled** from Eden. Sin, suffering and death have entered the world, and the relationship between humans and God is broken.

### The nature and role of humans

| Belief | Source | Meaning for Christians |
|---|---|---|
| Made in **God's image** (imago Dei) | Genesis 1:26-27 | Every person has dignity and value; humans can reason, love, create and know God. |
| Body and **soul** | Genesis 2:7 (dust + breath of life) | Humans are physical but also spiritual, with a soul that can relate to God. |
| **Stewards** of creation | Genesis 1:28; 2:15 | Humans are to care for the earth on God's behalf, not exploit it. |
| Made for **relationships** | Genesis 2:18 | Humans need each other; marriage and community are part of God's plan. |
| **Free will** | Genesis 2:16-17; 3 | Humans can choose to obey or disobey God; the Fall shows its misuse. |
| **Fallen** but redeemable | Genesis 3 | Humans have a tendency to sin (Original Sin, especially in Catholic and many Protestant teaching), which Jesus came to overcome. |

### Literal and non-literal interpretations

| | Literal (fundamentalist) | Non-literal (liberal and many mainstream Christians) |
|---|---|---|
| View | Genesis is a **factual, historical** account: God made the world in six 24-hour days, Adam and Eve were real individuals, and the Fall was a historical event. | Genesis teaches **religious truths** through symbolic, poetic or story form; it is not a scientific account. |
| Who | **Young Earth creationists**, often citing genealogies to argue the earth is only thousands of years old. | Most Catholics, Anglicans, Methodists and many evangelicals; the Catholic Church accepts that evolution is compatible with faith. |
| Science | The Big Bang and evolution are rejected or reinterpreted where they contradict Genesis. | Science explains **how** the world developed; Genesis explains **who** made it and **why**. |
| Key truths kept | God created everything from nothing; humans are special. | God created everything; creation is good; humans are made in his image and responsible for the world; sin damages the relationship with God. |

There are positions in between: some Christians take the "days" as long **ages** (**Old Earth creationism**); others accept the Big Bang as the way God began the universe and accept evolution guided by God (**theistic evolution**). **Non-literal Christians still believe God is the creator**: they differ only on how to read the text.

### The Word and the Spirit in creation

- **The Spirit**: in Genesis 1:2, the Spirit of God hovers over the waters before creation begins, giving life and order. Christians see the **Holy Spirit** as active in creation, bringing life (compare the "breath of life" in 2:7).
- **The Word**: **John 1:1-5** begins: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." Through the Word **all things were made**; in him was **life**, and that life was the **light** of all people; the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.
- Christians interpret the **Word** (Greek: **logos**) as **Jesus**, the Son, who existed with God before creation and later became human (John 1:14). God created by speaking ("And God said…"), and that creative Word is the Son.
- So Christians believe **all three persons of the Trinity** were present and active in creation: the Father as creator, the Son as the Word through whom all things were made, and the Spirit as the life-giving breath. This links creation to the Trinity and to salvation: the same Word that made the world came to rescue it.

### The Bible as the Word of God: inspiration and revelation

The **Bible** is the Christian holy book: the **Old Testament** (the Jewish scriptures, including the **Law** or Torah) and the **New Testament** (the Gospels, Acts, the letters and Revelation). Christians call it the **Word of God**.

- **Revelation** means God **making himself known**. Christians believe God reveals himself in the Bible, in creation, in human experience and conscience, and above all in **Jesus**.
- **Inspiration** means God **guiding** the human writers of the Bible. "All Scripture is God-breathed" (2 Timothy 3:16).

Christians differ on what inspiration means:

| View | Belief about the Bible | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Fundamentalist / literalist** | God dictated or directly controlled every word, so the Bible is **without error** in everything, including history and science. | Read literally where possible; Genesis is factual. |
| **Conservative** | The writers were inspired by God but wrote in their own styles and in different **genres** (history, poetry, law, parable). The Bible is fully true in what it intends to teach. | Read each book according to its genre; its moral and religious teaching is binding. |
| **Liberal** | The writers were inspired people who wrote about their experience of God, but their writing reflects their own times and cultures. | Some teachings (e.g. on slavery or women's roles) need reinterpreting today; the central message of love and justice is what matters. |

### The Bible and other sources of authority

- **Protestants** often hold to **sola scriptura** ("scripture alone"), a principle of the Reformation: the Bible is the **supreme** authority, above Church tradition.
- **Catholics** teach that the Bible and **Tradition** together form one source of revelation, interpreted with authority by the **Magisterium** (the Pope and bishops).
- **Orthodox** Christians see scripture as part of Holy Tradition, interpreted through the Church Fathers and the councils.
- **Anglicans** traditionally balance **scripture, tradition and reason**; Methodists add **experience**.
- **Quakers** emphasise the **inner light**, God's guidance within each person, alongside the Bible.
- Other sources include **conscience**, the **creeds**, the **example of Jesus**, and the guidance of the **Holy Spirit** in prayer.

Christians use the Bible in worship (readings, sermons), private prayer and study, and moral decisions. Its authority is high for all, but how it is weighed against other sources is a key point of difference.

::: terms
- **Creation**: the act of God bringing the universe and everything in it into existence.
- **Genesis**: the first book of the Bible, whose chapters 1-3 tell of creation and the Fall.
- **Imago Dei**: the belief that humans are made in the image of God, giving them unique dignity.
- **Literal interpretation**: reading a biblical text as a factual, historical account, word for word.
- **Non-literal interpretation**: reading a biblical text as symbolic or poetic, teaching religious truths rather than science.
- **Word (logos)**: in John 1, the Son of God, through whom all things were made and who became human in Jesus.
- **Inspiration**: the belief that God guided the human writers of the Bible.
- **Revelation**: God making himself and his will known to humans, above all in Jesus and the Bible.
- **Sola scriptura**: the Protestant principle that scripture alone is the supreme authority for Christians.
- **Magisterium**: the teaching authority of the Catholic Church, exercised by the Pope and bishops.
:::

::: example (b) Word and Spirit
Describe the role of the Word and Spirit in creation. (5)

At the very beginning of Genesis, "the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters" (1:2), so Christians believe the Holy Spirit was present before anything took shape, bringing life and order to creation. God created by speaking ("Let there be light"), and John's Gospel explains this: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God", and through him all things were made (John 1:1-3). Christians believe the Word is Jesus, the Son, who existed before creation and later became human (John 1:14). In him was life and light, which darkness cannot overcome (1:4-5). So Christians believe the whole Trinity was at work in creation, which they celebrate in worship and creeds.

::: example (c) interpreting Genesis
Explain the different ways in which Christians interpret the account of Creation in Genesis 1-3. (8)

Fundamentalist Christians read Genesis literally: God created the world in six 24-hour days, Adam was formed from dust and Eve from his rib, and the Fall really happened when they ate the forbidden fruit. Young Earth creationists use the Bible's genealogies to argue the earth is only thousands of years old, and they reject parts of evolution that contradict the text, because they believe the Bible is inspired word for word and without error. Other Christians read the "days" as long ages, so they accept an ancient universe while keeping a historical Adam and Eve. Most Catholics, Anglicans and Methodists read Genesis non-literally: it is a symbolic account teaching that God created everything, that creation is "very good", that humans are made in God's image and are stewards (2:15), and that sin breaks our relationship with God. They accept the Big Bang and evolution as how God created. All agree that God is the creator.

::: example (d) evaluative paragraph
'The Bible is the most important source of authority for Christians.' One evaluative paragraph. (15)

Many Protestants would agree, following sola scriptura: the Bible is "God-breathed" (2 Timothy 3:16), so it carries God's own authority and can judge every other source, including Church tradition. This is a strong argument for anyone who accepts that God inspired its writers. However, Catholics argue that scripture and Tradition belong together, and that the Magisterium is needed to interpret the Bible faithfully; otherwise, as the many Protestant denominations show, readers reach conflicting conclusions. Liberal Christians add that reason and conscience must be used, since some passages reflect ancient cultures. So the Bible may be the most important single source, but most Christians do not treat it as the only one.
:::
