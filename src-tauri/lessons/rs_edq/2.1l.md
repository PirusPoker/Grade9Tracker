## What it is

This part of **Theme 4: Issues of Human Rights** covers **prejudice and discrimination**, especially **racial** prejudice. You need: Christian teaching (**Galatians 3:27-29**) and **Martin Luther King's** teachings on equality; Muslim teaching (**Qur'an 5:8**, **49:13**) and the actions of the **Christian/Muslim Forum**. Questions ask you to describe how believers work against prejudice, explain attitudes from two religions, and evaluate whether religion helps or harms.

### Prejudice and discrimination

- **Prejudice** is an **attitude**: pre-judging a person or group, usually unfavourably, without good reason or knowledge, often because of race, religion, gender, disability, age or sexuality. It is a thought or feeling.
- **Discrimination** is an **action**: treating someone unfairly because of prejudice, e.g. refusing someone a job, housing or service because of their race.
- **Stereotyping** (assuming all members of a group are the same) often feeds prejudice; **scapegoating** blames a whole group for problems.
- **Positive discrimination** means treating a group more favourably to make up for past disadvantage (e.g. reserved places). Many people argue it is unfair to others; in the UK most forms are illegal, though "positive action" (encouraging under-represented groups to apply) is allowed.
- **Effects**: on individuals, prejudice and discrimination cause hurt, fear, low self-esteem and lost opportunities; on society, they cause division, resentment and sometimes violence.
- **The law**: the **Equality Act 2010** makes it illegal to discriminate in work, education and services on grounds including race, religion or belief, sex, disability, age and sexual orientation. Earlier race relations laws began in the 1960s.

### Christian teaching on prejudice and discrimination

- **Galatians 3:27-29**: all who have been **baptised into Christ** have "clothed" themselves with Christ; there is **neither Jew nor Gentile, slave nor free, male nor female**, for all are **one in Christ Jesus**; and if they belong to Christ, they are Abraham's descendants and heirs of God's promise. Christians interpret this as meaning that the divisions people use to rank one another (ethnicity, status, gender) have **no place** in the Church: all are equal before God.
- **Genesis 1:26-27**: every person is made in the **image of God**, so all have equal dignity.
- **Jesus' example**: he spoke with a **Samaritan** woman (John 4), a member of a group many Jews of his time looked down on; he told the parable of the **Good Samaritan** (Luke 10), making a despised outsider the hero; he touched lepers and ate with outcasts. Peter learned that "God does not show favouritism" (Acts 10:34).
- **Diversity and history**: Christians accept that the Bible was once wrongly used to defend **slavery** and, in South Africa, **apartheid** (by some in the Dutch Reformed Church). Other Christians led the fight against both: evangelical Christians such as **William Wilberforce** campaigned to abolish the slave trade, and Archbishop **Desmond Tutu** and others opposed apartheid. Today almost all churches teach that racism is a **sin**. Christians still discuss other forms of discrimination, such as the roles of women and attitudes to LGBT people, where views differ between traditions.

### Martin Luther King and racial equality

**Martin Luther King Jr** (1929-1968) was an American **Baptist minister** who led the **civil rights movement** against racial segregation and discrimination in the USA.

- **Actions**: he led the **Montgomery bus boycott** (1955-56) after **Rosa Parks** was arrested for refusing to give up her bus seat to a white passenger; he led marches and peaceful protests and was jailed several times; at the **March on Washington** in **1963** he gave his "**I have a dream**" speech, hoping his children would one day be judged by the content of their character, not the colour of their skin.
- **Teaching**: all people are **equal children of God**; segregation and racism are sinful because they deny that equality; unjust laws should be resisted, but only by **non-violence**, following Jesus' command to love enemies (Matthew 5:44) and inspired also by Gandhi. Love, not hatred, would change people's hearts.
- **Results**: his campaign helped bring about the **Civil Rights Act 1964** and the **Voting Rights Act 1965**. He received the **Nobel Peace Prize** in 1964 and was assassinated in **1968**.

### Muslim teaching on prejudice and discrimination

- **Qur'an 49:13**: Allah created humans from a **male and female** and made them into **nations and tribes** so that they may **know one another**; the most **honoured** in Allah's sight is the most **righteous**. Muslims interpret this as meaning that ethnic and national differences are part of Allah's design, intended to bring people together, not divide them; the only true distinction is **taqwa** (God-consciousness and good conduct), which only Allah can judge.
- **Qur'an 5:8**: believers must **stand firm for Allah** as witnesses to **justice**, and must not let hatred of a people lead them to be **unjust**; being just is nearest to righteousness. Muslims interpret this as forbidding discrimination even against people they dislike or who have wronged them.
- **The Last Sermon** of the Prophet taught that an Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab, nor a white person over a black person, nor a black person over a white person, except by piety and good action.
- **Bilal ibn Rabah**, a freed Abyssinian (Ethiopian) slave, was chosen by the Prophet as the first **mu'adhin** (caller to prayer), showing that status and race do not matter in Islam.
- **The ummah** is one community of every race; on **Hajj**, pilgrims of all nations wear simple white clothing (**ihram**) and stand together as equals before Allah.
- **Diversity**: Muslims accept that racism exists in some Muslim communities and among some Muslims, despite these teachings; many Muslim leaders challenge it. Muslims are themselves often victims of prejudice (Islamophobia).

### The Christian/Muslim Forum

The **Christian/Muslim Forum** was set up in England in **2006** to bring Christians and Muslims together. Its work includes:

- bringing together **Christian and Muslim leaders** (from several denominations and Muslim traditions) and local people for **dialogue**, so they understand each other's beliefs and build friendships;
- issuing **joint statements**, for example condemning terrorist attacks and attacks on mosques and churches, and standing against racism, Islamophobia and anti-Christian hostility;
- publishing guidance for respectful **sharing of faith**, so that Christians and Muslims can talk about their beliefs honestly without pressure or disrespect;
- supporting local projects where the two communities work together.

Its aim is to replace suspicion and stereotypes with **understanding and trust**, which directly tackles prejudice. For Christians this follows "love your neighbour"; for Muslims it follows 49:13 ("that you may know one another").

### How religious believers work against prejudice

- **Teaching and worship** that stress equality (Galatians 3:28; 49:13).
- **Campaigning** against racism and for fair laws (King; churches against apartheid).
- **Interfaith work** such as the Christian/Muslim Forum; mosque and church open days.
- **Supporting victims** of hate crime and refugees; community projects that bring different groups together.
- **Personal example**: treating everyone with respect at work and school.

::: terms
- **Prejudice**: an unfair attitude or opinion about a person or group formed without good reason or knowledge.
- **Discrimination**: treating a person or group unfairly because of prejudice, e.g. refusing them a job.
- **Racial prejudice**: an unfair attitude towards people because of their race, colour or ethnic origin.
- **Stereotype**: an oversimplified belief that all members of a group are the same.
- **Positive discrimination**: treating a group more favourably to make up for past disadvantage.
- **Equality**: the state of being equal in value, rights and opportunities, whatever a person's background.
- **Segregation**: the forced separation of people by race, as in the southern USA before the civil rights movement.
- **Taqwa**: God-consciousness and righteous conduct; in 49:13 the only true basis of honour before Allah.
- **Christian/Muslim Forum**: an English organisation bringing Christians and Muslims together for dialogue and joint action.
:::
