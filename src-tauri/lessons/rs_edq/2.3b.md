## What it is

This lesson covers the **foundations of faith** in Islam: the **six articles of faith** in **Sunni Islam**, the **five roots of Usul ad-Din** in **Shi'a Islam**, and Muslim attitudes to the **kutub** (holy books): the **Sahifah** (Scrolls), **Tawrat** (Torah), **Zabur** (Psalms), **Injil** (Gospel) and the **Qur'an**.

It is examined in Question 1 (Beliefs and Teachings) of Component 3: (a) 2, (b) 5, (c) 8, (d) 15, with **no SPaG**. Typical questions: "Describe the six articles of faith", "Describe the five roots of Usul ad-Din in Shi'a Islam", "Explain Muslim attitudes to the holy books". The 2025 examiners' report praised candidates who used the word **kutub** and the Arabic names of the books. Answers stay inside Islam; Sunni and Shi'a diversity is credited.

### Why "foundations"?

Muslims distinguish between **belief** (iman, faith in the heart) and **practice** (acting on that faith). The articles of faith and the roots of Usul ad-Din are the **beliefs** every Muslim must hold; the Five Pillars (Sunni) and the Ten Obligatory Acts (Shi'a) are the **practices** that grow from them (2.3f, 2.3g). Shi'a Muslims use the picture of a tree: **Usul ad-Din** means "roots of religion", and the practices are **Furu' ad-Din**, "branches of religion". Without roots, the branches cannot live.

### The six articles of faith (Sunni Islam)

The six articles come from the well-known **Hadith of Jibril** (recorded by Muslim), in which the angel Jibril, in human form, asked the Prophet what faith (iman) is. The Prophet answered: to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and al-Qadr, both its good and its bad. **Qur'an 2:285** names the first four: the Messenger and the believers all believe "in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers".

| Article | Arabic | What Sunni Muslims believe | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Allah | **Tawhid** | One God, unique, with no partners; the foundation of all belief | 2.3a |
| 2. Angels | **malaikah** | Beings created from light who serve Allah perfectly and carry His messages, e.g. Jibril | 2.3d |
| 3. Holy books | **kutub** | Allah revealed books to guide humanity; the Qur'an is the final and complete revelation | below |
| 4. Prophets | **Risalah** (rusul, messengers) | Allah sent prophets to every people, from Adam to Muhammad, the Seal of the Prophets | 2.3c |
| 5. The Last Day | **Akhirah** / Day of Judgement | Everyone will be resurrected and judged; the afterlife in heaven (Jannah) or hell (Jahannam) | 2.3e |
| 6. Predestination | **al-Qadr** | Allah knows, has written and wills everything that happens, yet humans remain responsible | 2.3e |

**Why the articles matter**:

- They give Sunni Muslims a clear, shared **creed**: anyone who sincerely accepts all six is a believer, which unites the **ummah**.
- Each article **shapes behaviour**: belief in angels who record deeds and in the Last Day encourages honesty and good actions; belief in the books and prophets gives guidance; belief in al-Qadr brings patience and trust in hard times ("insha'Allah").
- They are **linked**: Allah sends His message through **angels** (Jibril) to **prophets**, who receive **books**; humans are judged on the **Last Day** on how they responded; and all of this happens within Allah's **al-Qadr**.

### The five roots of Usul ad-Din (Shi'a Islam)

**Usul ad-Din** ("the roots of religion") are the five core beliefs of **Twelver Shi'a** Islam, the largest Shi'a group and the one most British Shi'a Muslims belong to. Shi'a scholars teach that a believer should accept these roots through their own understanding and reflection, not just by copying others.

| Root | Meaning | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Tawhid** | Oneness of Allah | As for Sunni Muslims: Allah is One, with no partners; the basis of everything |
| 2. **Adalat** ('Adl) | Divine justice | Allah is perfectly just and never acts wrongly; humans are free and responsible (2.3a) |
| 3. **Nubuwwah** | Prophethood | Allah sent prophets, protected from sin, from Adam to Muhammad, the last prophet |
| 4. **Imamah** | Leadership of the Imams | After Muhammad, Allah appointed **Imams** from the Prophet's family to lead and guide the community, beginning with **Ali** |
| 5. **Mi'ad** (also called Qiyamah) | The Day of Judgement and resurrection | All people will be raised and judged by Allah |

**Imamah** is the distinctively Shi'a root. Shi'a Muslims believe the Prophet named his cousin and son-in-law **Ali** as his successor, and that leadership passed through Ali and **Fatimah**'s descendants. Twelver Shi'a believe in **twelve Imams**, who were divinely chosen, protected from sin (ma'sum) and given special knowledge to interpret the Qur'an. The **twelfth Imam**, Muhammad al-Mahdi, is believed to be alive but hidden (in **occultation**) and will return before the Day of Judgement to bring justice. In his absence, senior scholars (**marja'**) guide the community. Imamah is why Shi'a Muslims honour the Imams' shrines and remember Imam Husayn at Ashura.

### Comparing the Sunni and Shi'a foundations

| | Six articles (Sunni) | Five roots (Shi'a) |
|---|---|---|
| Shared beliefs | Allah (Tawhid), prophets, Day of Judgement | Tawhid, Nubuwwah, Mi'ad |
| Only listed by Sunnis | Angels, holy books, al-Qadr | (Shi'a Muslims also believe in these, but count them within the other roots, e.g. books and angels under Nubuwwah) |
| Only listed by Shi'as | | **Adalat** (justice) and **Imamah** (leadership) |
| Emphasis | Allah's power and decree (al-Qadr) | Allah's justice and human responsibility (Adalat) |

Key point for (c) and (d): the two lists **overlap far more than they differ**. Both traditions believe in all six Sunni articles, and both believe Allah is just. The differences are about **emphasis** (justice and leadership are made separate roots in Shi'a Islam) and the real difference is **Imamah**, which Sunni Muslims do not accept in the Shi'a sense (they believe the first leaders, the caliphs, were chosen by the community).

### The kutub: the holy books

Muslims believe Allah has always guided humanity by revealing His message to prophets. Belief in the **kutub** (books; singular kitab) is the third article of faith. The Qur'an names five revealed scriptures:

| Book | Revealed to | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sahifah** (Scrolls, Suhuf) | **Ibrahim** (and Musa) | The Qur'an mentions "the scriptures of Ibrahim and Musa" (Qur'an 87:18-19); believed to be **lost** |
| **Tawrat** (Torah) | **Musa** (Moses) | Contained guidance and light; Muslims believe its original text was **altered** over time |
| **Zabur** (Psalms) | **Dawud** (David) | "To Dawud We gave the Zabur" (Qur'an 4:163); songs of praise |
| **Injil** (Gospel) | **Isa** (Jesus) | Confirmed the Tawrat, with guidance and light (Qur'an 5:46); Muslims believe the original Injil was a message given to Isa, not the four Gospels written later, and that it was **lost or changed** |
| **Qur'an** | **Muhammad** | The final, complete revelation, in Arabic, through Jibril over about 23 years; **preserved** without change |

**Muslim attitudes to the earlier books**:

- They were **genuinely revealed by Allah**, and Muslims must believe in them (Qur'an 2:285; 2:136 lists what was given to Ibrahim, Musa, Isa and the prophets). Muslims respect Jews and Christians as **People of the Book** (Ahl al-Kitab).
- But most Muslims believe the texts in use today are **not the original revelation**: over time they were lost, mixed with human words, or deliberately changed (**tahrif**, corruption or distortion). So where they disagree with the Qur'an, the Qur'an is followed. Some Muslims hold that the corruption is mainly in interpretation rather than the words; most believe the texts themselves changed.
- Their message was for **particular peoples and times**; the Qur'an is for all humanity and all time.

**Muslim attitudes to the Qur'an**:

- It is the **literal word of Allah**, revealed to Muhammad through **Jibril**, beginning on the **Night of Power** (Laylat al-Qadr) in 610 CE (see 2.3i).
- It is **final and complete**: it confirms what was true in the earlier books and corrects what was changed. Qur'an 5:3 says Allah has perfected the religion.
- It is **preserved**: "It is We who sent down the Reminder, and We will surely guard it" (Qur'an 15:9). It was memorised by companions and written down; Muslims believe the Arabic text has not changed.
- It is in **Arabic**: translations are seen as interpretations of the meaning, not the Qur'an itself, so prayer and recitation are in Arabic everywhere, uniting the ummah.
- It is the **highest authority**, followed by the **Sunnah** and **Hadith** of the Prophet. It is the first source of **shariah**.

**How the Qur'an is treated** (showing its importance):

- Many Muslims perform **wudu** (ritual washing) before touching it, following the view that "none touch it except the purified" (Qur'an 56:79).
- It is kept on a **high shelf**, nothing placed on top of it, often wrapped in cloth; it is read on a stand (**rehal**) and never put on the floor.
- It is **recited** beautifully with rules of pronunciation (**tajwid**); many children learn to read it at the mosque **madrasah**.
- Someone who memorises the whole Qur'an is a **hafiz** and is greatly respected.
- Many Muslims recite the whole Qur'an during **Ramadan**; old, damaged copies are buried or disposed of respectfully.

**Diversity**: Sunni and Shi'a Muslims accept the **same Qur'an**. Both value the Hadith, but they use **different collections**: Sunni Muslims especially trust collections such as those of Bukhari and Muslim; Shi'a Muslims give authority to traditions passed down through the Prophet's family and the Imams (e.g. al-Kafi), and to the teachings of the Imams themselves.

::: terms
- **Six articles of faith**: the six core beliefs of Sunni Islam: Allah, angels, holy books, prophets, the Last Day and al-Qadr.
- **Usul ad-Din**: the five roots of religion in Shi'a Islam: Tawhid, Adalat, Nubuwwah, Imamah and Mi'ad.
- **Furu' ad-Din**: the branches of religion; the Shi'a practices (Ten Obligatory Acts) that grow from the roots.
- **Nubuwwah**: prophethood; the Shi'a root of belief in the prophets from Adam to Muhammad.
- **Imamah**: the Shi'a belief that Allah appointed twelve Imams from the Prophet's family to lead the community.
- **Mi'ad**: the Shi'a root of belief in the resurrection and the Day of Judgement.
- **Kutub**: the holy books Muslims believe Allah revealed: Sahifah, Tawrat, Zabur, Injil and Qur'an.
- **Sahifah**: the Scrolls revealed to Ibrahim, now believed to be lost.
- **Tawrat**: the Torah, revealed to the prophet Musa.
- **Zabur**: the Psalms, revealed to the prophet Dawud.
- **Injil**: the Gospel, the message revealed to the prophet Isa.
- **Tahrif**: the belief that earlier scriptures were changed or corrupted over time.
- **Hafiz**: a person who has memorised the whole Qur'an.
:::

::: example (b) the six articles
Describe the six articles of faith in Sunni Islam. (5)

The six articles come from the Hadith of Jibril. First is belief in Allah and Tawhid, His oneness. Second is belief in angels (malaikah), such as Jibril who brought the Qur'an. Third is belief in the holy books (kutub), including the Tawrat, Zabur and Injil, and above all the Qur'an. Fourth is belief in the prophets (Risalah), from Adam to Muhammad, the Seal of the Prophets; Qur'an 2:285 says believers believe "in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers". Fifth is belief in the Last Day, when all will be resurrected and judged and sent to Jannah or Jahannam. Sixth is al-Qadr, that Allah knows and decrees everything. Together they unite Sunni Muslims and guide their lives, for example making them careful of their actions because the angels record them.

[Band 3, 5 marks: all six named accurately, sources, influence.]
:::

::: example (b) the five roots
Describe the five roots of Usul ad-Din in Shi'a Islam. (5)

Shi'a Muslims believe in five roots of religion. Tawhid is the oneness of Allah, the basis of everything. Adalat is Allah's perfect justice: He never does wrong, so humans have free will and are responsible for their actions. Nubuwwah is belief in the prophets from Adam to Muhammad, who are protected from sin. Imamah is the belief that Allah appointed twelve Imams from the Prophet's family, beginning with Ali, to guide the community; the twelfth Imam, al-Mahdi, is in hiding and will return. Mi'ad is the belief in the resurrection and the Day of Judgement. Shi'a Muslims see these as the roots from which the branches, the Ten Obligatory Acts, grow, and they are meant to accept them through their own reflection.

[Band 3, 5 marks: all five, accurate meaning, distinctive Shi'a detail.]
:::

::: example (c) attitudes to the kutub
Explain Muslim attitudes to the holy books. (8)

Belief in the kutub is one of the six articles of faith, because Allah has always guided humanity through revealed books. Muslims believe in the Sahifah of Ibrahim, the Tawrat given to Musa, the Zabur given to Dawud (Qur'an 4:163), the Injil given to Isa, and the Qur'an given to Muhammad. Qur'an 2:136 tells Muslims to believe in what was revealed to all these prophets and to make no distinction between them, so Muslims respect Jews and Christians as People of the Book.

However, most Muslims believe the earlier books were lost or changed over time (tahrif). The Sahifah has been lost, and the Tawrat and Injil in use today are believed to contain human additions; the Injil, for example, was a message given to Isa, not the Gospels written later. So where they disagree with the Qur'an, Muslims follow the Qur'an.

The Qur'an is seen as Allah's final, complete word, revealed in Arabic through Jibril. Muslims believe it is perfectly preserved, as Allah promised to guard it (Qur'an 15:9), and is meant for all people and all times. This attitude is shown in how it is treated: many Muslims perform wudu before touching it, keep it on a high shelf and read it on a stand, recite it in Arabic with tajwid, and honour anyone who becomes a hafiz. Sunni and Shi'a Muslims share the same Qur'an, though they rely on different Hadith collections to interpret it.

[Band 4, 8 marks: all books, contrasting attitudes, sources, practice and diversity.]
:::

::: example (d) evaluative paragraph
'The Qur'an is the only holy book Muslims need.' One evaluative paragraph. (15)

Many Muslims would agree, because they believe the Qur'an is Allah's final and complete revelation, protected from change (Qur'an 15:9), whereas the earlier kutub were lost or altered. It confirms everything true in the Tawrat and Injil, so a Muslim loses nothing by relying on it. This is a strong argument, because if the Qur'an corrects the earlier books, they cannot add anything it lacks. However, belief in all the kutub is itself an article of faith, and Qur'an 2:136 commands Muslims to believe in what was given to Ibrahim, Musa and Isa; rejecting them would contradict the Qur'an. Also, in practice Muslims need the Sunnah and Hadith to know how to pray or pay zakah, details the Qur'an does not give. Therefore the Qur'an may be the only book Muslims need as their final authority, but they still need to believe in the earlier books and to use the Hadith to live it out.
:::
