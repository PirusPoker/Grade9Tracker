## What it is

The last part of **Theme 1: Issues of Relationships** covers **same-sex relationships** and **gender equality**, especially the **roles of women and men in worship and authority**. The spec names: **Leviticus 20:13** and **1 Timothy 1:8-10** (same-sex relationships, Christian); **Qur'an 7:80-81** (same-sex relationships, Islam); **1 Timothy 2:11-12** and **Galatians 3:27-29** (women and men, Christian), with **Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican** views; and **Qur'an 2:228, 40:40 and 4:1** (women and men, Islam). It also covers **gender prejudice and discrimination**.

These are areas where sincere believers disagree deeply. Examiners reward answers that describe each view **accurately and fairly in its own terms** and penalise stereotypes. Never present one religion as simply "for" and the other as "against".

### The law in Britain (background)

- **Civil partnerships** for same-sex couples began under the Civil Partnership Act 2004. **Same-sex marriage** has been legal in England and Wales since **March 2014**. The law protects religious groups: none can be forced to conduct a same-sex wedding.
- The **Equality Act 2010** makes it illegal to discriminate on grounds including sex, sexual orientation and religion, with some exemptions for religious organisations (e.g. who may be a minister).

### Christian attitudes to same-sex relationships

**The key texts and how they are read**

- **Leviticus 20:13** forbids a man lying with a man "as one lies with a woman" and calls it detestable. **Conservative** Christians read it as a lasting moral law, confirmed in the New Testament. **Liberal** Christians note that it sits in the **Holiness Code**, alongside laws Christians no longer keep (food laws, mixed fabrics), so they see it as tied to ancient Israel's culture rather than binding today.
- **1 Timothy 1:8-10** says the law is made for the lawless, and lists wrongdoers including (in many translations) men who practise homosexuality. Conservatives read this as clear New Testament condemnation. Others argue the Greek word used is rare and uncertain, and may refer to **exploitative** relationships (such as the abuse of boys or slaves), not loving, faithful partnerships.

**Views across the churches**

| Church | Teaching |
|---|---|
| **Catholic** | People with same-sex attraction must be treated with **respect, compassion and sensitivity**, and every sign of unjust discrimination avoided. But sex belongs only in the marriage of a man and a woman, open to life, so same-sex sexual acts are not approved and gay Catholics are called to **chastity**. Since 2023 priests may give short, informal blessings to couples in irregular situations, including same-sex couples, without approving the relationship or treating it as a wedding. |
| **Orthodox** | Traditional: marriage is between a man and a woman; same-sex acts are sinful; people are to be loved and welcomed to repentance like all sinners. |
| **Church of England** | Its teaching on marriage remains that it is between one man and one woman, and it does not conduct same-sex weddings. In 2023 the **General Synod** backed **Prayers of Love and Faith**, prayers that clergy may use to bless same-sex couples. The Church remains deeply divided, and its wider Anglican Communion more so. |
| **Liberal Protestant** (Quakers, the Methodist Church in Britain, the United Reformed Church, the Scottish Episcopal Church) | Same-sex weddings are allowed. **Love, faithfulness and commitment** are what make a relationship holy; all are made in God's image (Genesis 1:27); "there is neither… male nor female" in Christ (Galatians 3:28); Jesus said nothing directly about same-sex relationships but always welcomed outsiders. |
| **Many evangelical churches** | Traditional teaching, often stated as "love the sinner, not the sin"; some offer pastoral support to gay members who choose celibacy. |

### Muslim attitudes to same-sex relationships

- **Qur'an 7:80-81** tells of the Prophet **Lut** (Lot), who rebuked his people for an immorality no one had done before: approaching men with desire instead of women. They are called a people who **transgress** limits, and the story ends with their destruction.
- **Mainstream Sunni and Shi'a teaching**: sex is lawful only within **nikah** between a man and a woman, so same-sex sexual acts are **haram** (forbidden). Many scholars distinguish between **feelings**, which a person may not choose, and **acts**, which they can; a Muslim who experiences same-sex attraction but does not act on it has not sinned.
- **Treatment of people**: Muslim leaders in Britain teach that no one should be abused or attacked, and that British Muslims accept that same-sex marriage is lawful for others in a free society, even though they do not accept it religiously.
- **A minority view**: a small number of Muslims, including support groups for LGBT Muslims such as **Imaan**, argue for acceptance, reading 7:80-81 as about assault and inhospitality rather than loving relationships, and stressing Allah's mercy. Most scholars reject this reading.

### Gender equality: Christianity

**Gender equality** means people of all genders having equal rights, status and opportunities. Christians start from **Genesis 1:27**: God created humans in his image, "male and female he created them", so both have **equal worth**. The disagreement is about **roles**.

**The two key texts**

- **Galatians 3:27-29**: all who are baptised have "clothed" themselves with Christ; "there is neither Jew nor Gentile, neither slave nor free, nor is there male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus." Liberal and many mainstream Christians read this as a principle of **full equality**, including in ministry.
- **1 Timothy 2:11-12**: a woman should learn quietly, and Paul does "not permit a woman to teach or to assume authority over a man". **Literal** readers (some evangelicals) apply it today: women may serve in many ways but not as senior pastors. Others read it as **advice to one church** in a particular culture, like the instructions about hair and jewellery in the same passage, and not a rule for all time.

**Roles in worship and authority**

| Church | Women's roles |
|---|---|
| **Catholic** | Only men can be ordained **priests** (and bishops). Reasons: Jesus chose twelve male apostles; the priest acts *in the person of Christ* at Mass; the unbroken **tradition** of the Church. Pope John Paul II taught (1994) that the Church has no authority to ordain women as priests. Women have many other roles: readers, extraordinary ministers of Holy Communion, catechists, chaplains, parish and diocesan leaders, and **religious sisters** and abbesses who lead their communities. Since 2021 women may be formally instituted as lectors and acolytes. Equal does not mean identical: Catholics see different roles as having equal dignity, and honour **Mary** above all saints. |
| **Orthodox** | Also a male priesthood, for similar reasons of **tradition** and the icon of Christ. Women serve as teachers, choir leaders, iconographers and nuns, and are honoured as saints; the Theotokos (Mary) is central in worship. |
| **Anglican (Church of England)** | Women have been ordained **priests** since **1994** and **bishops** since **2015**. In 2025 **Sarah Mullally** was named the first woman Archbishop of Canterbury. Parishes that cannot accept women's ministry for theological reasons can receive the oversight of male bishops, so diversity remains within the Church. |
| **Other Protestants** | Methodists, Baptists (many), the URC and the Salvation Army have long had women ministers; some evangelical churches keep senior leadership male (a **complementarian** view: equal worth, different roles). |

### Gender equality: Islam

- **Equal before Allah**: humans were created "from a single soul" and from it its mate (**4:1**), so men and women share one nature. Whoever does good, "whether male or female", while a believer, will enter Paradise (**40:40**). Spiritual worth and reward are the same.
- **Different but complementary roles**: **2:228** says women have rights similar to those over them, according to what is fair, "but men have a degree over them". Many scholars read this "degree" as men's **extra responsibility**, for example to provide financially and maintain the family, rather than superior worth. Others see it as giving men leadership in the family. Progressive Muslims stress that the verse is in a passage about divorce and protects women's rights.

**Roles in worship and authority**

- Men are required to attend **Jummah** (Friday prayer) at the mosque; women are **not required** to, largely because of family duties, but may attend, and many British mosques have women's facilities.
- Men and women pray in **separate rows or areas**, usually men in front, to keep concentration and modesty during the bowing and prostration of salah. Some see this as practical; critics say women's spaces are sometimes poor.
- A woman may **lead other women** in prayer. Most scholars, Sunni and Shi'a, hold that the imam of a **mixed congregation** must be a man; a small minority disagree, and a few women-led congregations exist.
- Women have long had **religious authority as scholars**: **Aisha**, the Prophet's wife, taught and transmitted many hadith; today women teach the Qur'an, serve as chaplains, sit on mosque committees and lead Muslim organisations.

### Gender prejudice and discrimination

**Prejudice** is an unfair attitude (pre-judging); **discrimination** is acting on it, e.g. paying women less or refusing them a role because of their sex. Christians and Muslims condemn unjust discrimination because all are equal before God. They disagree about whether a **male-only priesthood** or **male imams** for mixed congregations is discrimination: supporters say it is a difference of **role** grounded in revelation and tradition, not a judgement on worth; critics, including many believers, say it treats women as less capable of leadership.

::: terms
- **Gender equality**: people of all genders having the same rights, status and opportunities in all areas of life.
- **Roles**: the parts or functions people are expected to take on, e.g. in worship, the family or leadership.
- **Gender prejudice**: an unfair attitude or opinion about someone because of their sex or gender.
- **Gender discrimination**: treating someone unfairly because of their sex or gender.
- **Same-sex relationship**: a romantic and sexual relationship between two people of the same sex.
- **Civil partnership**: a legal union of two people giving similar legal rights to marriage.
- **Complementarian**: the view that men and women are equal in worth but have different God-given roles.
- **Ordination**: the ceremony by which a person is made a deacon, priest or bishop.
- **Haram**: forbidden in Islam.
- **Imam**: a person who leads prayer in a mosque; often also a teacher in the community.
:::

::: method
These topics appear in Question 1: (a) 2, (b) 5, (c) 8, (d) 15 + 6 SPaG.

2. **(b) "Describe… attitudes to same-sex relationships"**: describe **several** views fairly (Catholic, Church of England, liberal Protestant; or mainstream and minority Muslim), with a source each.
3. **(c) "Explain… beliefs about the role of women in worship"**: answer about **worship and authority**, not jobs at home. Two clear sections (Christianity and Islam, or Catholic and Anglican). Interpret a text in each (1 Timothy 2:11-12 vs Galatians 3:28; 2:228 vs 40:40).
4. **(d)**: give at least three viewpoints; explain the **reason** each holds (tradition, scripture read literally or in context, equality, love); say which reasoning is stronger and why; judge.
5. Use respectful, accurate language for every position. Words like "backward" or "sinful people" lose credibility and accuracy.

::: example (b) same-sex relationships, one religion
Describe Christian attitudes to same-sex relationships. (5)

Christian attitudes differ. The Catholic Church teaches that gay people must be treated with respect, compassion and sensitivity, but that sex belongs only within the marriage of a man and a woman, so it calls gay Catholics to chastity. Conservative Christians support this from Leviticus 20:13 and 1 Timothy 1:8-10, which they read as lasting moral teaching. The Church of England does not marry same-sex couples, but its General Synod backed Prayers of Love and Faith in 2023 so that clergy can bless them. This affects whether gay Christians can marry in their own church.

::: example (c) role of women in worship
Explain, from either two religions or two religious traditions, beliefs about the role of women in worship. (8)

**Christianity.** Catholics believe only men can be ordained priests, because Jesus chose twelve male apostles and the priest represents Christ at Mass. Some also read 1 Timothy 2:12, "I do not permit a woman to teach or to assume authority over a man", as supporting male leadership. But equal does not mean identical: women serve as readers, ministers of Holy Communion, catechists and religious sisters, and since 2021 can be instituted as lectors. The Church of England takes a different view, interpreting Galatians 3:28 — "there is neither… male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus" — as meaning that women can hold every role. It has ordained women priests since 1994 and bishops since 2015.

**Islam.** Muslims believe men and women are spiritually equal: whoever does good, "male or female", will enter Paradise (40:40). But they have some different roles in worship. Men must attend Friday prayer, while women are not obliged to, mainly because of family responsibilities. Men and women pray in separate rows or areas for concentration and modesty. Most scholars teach that a man leads a mixed congregation, while a woman may lead women. Many Muslims read Qur'an 2:228, "men have a degree over them", as a degree of responsibility rather than worth. Women such as Aisha have always taught the faith, and today women serve as teachers and chaplains.

[Band 4, 8 marks: worship-focused, two religions, diversity, sources interpreted.]

::: example (d) weighing, not listing
'Men and women will never have equal roles in worship.' One evaluative paragraph. (15)

Supporters of the statement point to the Catholic and Orthodox priesthoods, which are reserved for men because of the example of Jesus' twelve apostles and two thousand years of tradition; Pope John Paul II taught that the Church has no authority to change this. Because this argument rests on tradition and on the nature of the sacrament, not on public opinion, it is unlikely to change, which gives the statement real strength for those churches. However, the word "never" is undermined by what has already happened: the Church of England moved from an all-male priesthood to women bishops in about twenty years, reading Galatians 3:28 as the principle by which 1 Timothy 2:12 should be understood. So whether the statement is true depends on the tradition, and its "never" is too strong for Christianity as a whole.
:::
