## What it is

This lesson covers **Risalah** (prophethood): the **nature of prophethood** and why prophets are important (**Qur'an 2:136**); **Adam** as the first prophet; **Ibrahim** as father of **Isma'il** and **Ishaq** (Isaac) and his significance; **Isa** as a prophet for Muslims (**Qur'an 2:87**); and **Muhammad** as the **Seal of the Prophets**.

It is examined in Question 1 (Beliefs and Teachings) of Component 3: (a) 2, (b) 5, (c) 8, (d) 15, with **no SPaG**. **Prophethood** is one of the eight (a) concepts. The June 2025 (d) was "For Muslims, all of the prophets in Islam are equally important"; the report said the best answers weighed the prophets' **collective** importance (one message, no distinction) against their **individual** importance, while weaker answers wrote a pre-learnt account of one prophet and never evaluated. Stay inside Islam; Sunni and Shi'a views are credited.

### The nature of prophethood

**Risalah** means "message" or "messengership": the way Allah communicates His will to humanity through chosen human beings. Belief in the prophets is one of the **six articles of faith** (Sunni) and the root **Nubuwwah** (Shi'a).

- A **nabi** (prophet) is a person chosen by Allah to receive guidance and teach it; a **rasul** (messenger) is a prophet who was also given a **book** or a new law for a people. Every rasul is a nabi, but not every nabi is a rasul.
- **25 prophets** are named in the Qur'an, including Adam, Nuh (Noah), Ibrahim, Isma'il, Ishaq, Yaqub, Yusuf, Musa, Dawud, Sulayman, Yunus, Yahya (John), Isa and Muhammad. A well-known tradition says there were around **124,000** prophets in total, because Allah sent a messenger to **every nation**.
- Prophets are **fully human**: they ate, slept, married, worked and died. They are not divine and must never be worshipped, which would be **shirk**.
- They are **protected from sin** (**'ismah**). Sunni scholars generally teach that prophets were protected from major sins and from error in passing on the message; Shi'a scholars teach that prophets (and the Imams) were completely **sinless** and free from error.
- They all taught the **same core message**: Tawhid, the worship of the One God, doing good, and accountability on the Day of Judgement. The details of law differed for each people.
- Five are especially honoured as the prophets of great determination (**ulul 'azm**): Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa and Muhammad.

**Qur'an 2:136**: believers are told to say that they believe in Allah and what was revealed to them, and what was revealed to Ibrahim, Isma'il, Ishaq, Yaqub and the tribes, and what was given to Musa and Isa and to all the prophets from their Lord; "**we make no distinction between any of them**", and to Him they submit.

**Why prophets are important**:

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Guidance | Humans cannot know Allah's will by themselves; prophets bring His message and the kutub |
| Role models | Prophets show how to live the message; Muhammad's example (**Sunnah**) is followed in daily life |
| Allah's mercy and justice | Allah sent prophets to every people, so no one can say they were not warned |
| One message, one ummah | All prophets taught Tawhid, so Islam is the original religion of all the prophets, completed by Muhammad |
| Warning and good news | Prophets warn of judgement and give hope of paradise |

### Adam: the first prophet

- **Created by Allah** from clay (earth), shaped and given life when Allah breathed His spirit into him; Muslims see him as the **father of humanity**.
- Allah told the angels He was placing a **khalifah** (successor, steward) on earth (Qur'an 2:30), and **taught Adam the names** of all things, knowledge the angels did not have (2:31). The angels were told to bow to Adam; all did except **Iblis**, who refused out of pride (2:34).
- Adam and his wife **Hawwa** (Eve) lived in the Garden but ate from the forbidden tree when tempted by Shaytan. They **repented**, and Allah **accepted their repentance** (2:37). So Islam teaches there is **no original sin**: each person is born pure (fitrah) and responsible only for their own deeds.
- Adam was sent to earth as the **first prophet**, teaching his children to worship Allah alone.

**Significance**: shows the **dignity** of humans (taught by Allah, honoured by angels); the role of **khalifah**, caring for the earth; the pattern of **sin, repentance and forgiveness** that every Muslim follows; the **unity** of all humanity, descended from one father; and that Islam is as old as humanity itself.

### Ibrahim: the father of the prophets

- Ibrahim grew up among **idol-worshippers**; his father made idols. He reasoned that the stars, moon and sun could not be God because they set, and turned to the One Creator. He broke the idols of his people and was thrown into a fire, which Allah made **cool and safe** for him (Qur'an 21:69).
- He is called a **hanif**, one who naturally worships the One God, and the Qur'an says he was neither a Jew nor a Christian but a submitter to Allah (3:67). He is called **Khalilullah**, the Friend of Allah.
- **Father of Isma'il and Ishaq**: Isma'il was born to **Hajar**, and Ishaq to **Sarah**. Muslims trace the line of Isma'il to the Arabs and to **Muhammad**; the line of Ishaq to the prophets of the Israelites (Yaqub, Musa, Dawud, Isa). So Ibrahim is father of a family of prophets.
- **Hajar and Isma'il in Makkah**: by Allah's command Ibrahim left Hajar and baby Isma'il in the desert valley of Makkah. Hajar ran between the hills of **Safa** and **Marwah** searching for water, and the well of **Zamzam** sprang up.
- **The Ka'bah**: Ibrahim and Isma'il raised the foundations of the **Ka'bah** as a house for worshipping Allah (Qur'an 2:127) and prayed for a messenger to come from their descendants, which Muslims see fulfilled in Muhammad.
- **The sacrifice**: Ibrahim dreamt he was commanded to sacrifice his son; the son (believed by most Muslims to be Isma'il) agreed; as Ibrahim was about to obey, Allah stopped him and provided a ram instead (Qur'an 37:102-107).

**Significance**: the model of **Tawhid** against idolatry; the model of **total submission**, the meaning of "Islam"; his family's story is relived in **Hajj** (tawaf of the Ka'bah, sa'i between Safa and Marwah, stoning the pillars where Shaytan tempted him) and in **Id-ul-Adha** (the sacrifice); Muslims mention Ibrahim and his family in every salah, asking Allah to bless Muhammad as He blessed Ibrahim.

### Isa: a prophet for Muslims

Muslims love and honour **Isa** (Jesus) as one of the greatest prophets.

- **Qur'an 2:87**: Allah gave Musa the Book, sent messengers after him, and gave Isa son of Maryam **clear signs** and **supported him with the Holy Spirit** (Ruh al-Qudus), whom Muslims understand to be **Jibril**.
- **Virgin birth**: Isa was born to **Maryam** (Mary), a pure and devout woman, without a father, by Allah's command "Be". Maryam is greatly honoured and a whole surah (19) is named after her. Muslims compare his creation to Adam's, who had neither father nor mother.
- **Miracles**, by Allah's permission: speaking from the cradle, healing the blind and lepers, raising the dead, making a bird from clay that came to life (Qur'an 3:49).
- He received the **Injil**, confirming the Tawrat (5:46), and is called **al-Masih** (the Messiah).
- He is a **human prophet, not the Son of God**. Muslims believe calling him divine would break Tawhid (4:171; Surah 112).
- Most Muslims believe Isa was **not crucified**: it was made to appear so, and Allah raised him up to Himself (Qur'an 4:157-158). Many Muslims believe he will **return** before the Day of Judgement to defeat the false messiah (Dajjal) and establish justice.

**Significance**: a great prophet of mercy and miracles; a link between Muslims and Christians as People of the Book; a sign of Allah's power; and a part of the Muslim hope for the end of time. In an answer, state these as **Muslim** beliefs; do not compare with Christian views on this paper.

### Muhammad: the Seal of the Prophets

**Life in outline**:

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| c. 570 CE | Born in **Makkah**; orphaned young, raised by his grandfather and then his uncle Abu Talib; known as **al-Amin** (the trustworthy) |
| c. 595 | Married **Khadijah**, a businesswoman, who became the first to believe in his message |
| 610 | Aged about 40, while meditating in the cave of **Hira**, he received the first revelation from **Jibril** ("Read!"), the **Night of Power** |
| 610-622 | Preached Tawhid in Makkah; persecuted by the leaders of the Quraysh |
| 622 | The **Hijrah**: migration to **Madinah**, start of the Islamic calendar; built the first Muslim community and mosque |
| 630 | Returned to **Makkah** peacefully; cleared the Ka'bah of idols |
| 632 | Final pilgrimage and Farewell Sermon; died in Madinah |

**Seal of the Prophets** (**Khatam an-Nabiyyin**): Qur'an 33:40 says Muhammad is not the father of any of your men, but the Messenger of Allah and the **Seal of the Prophets**. A seal closes and completes a document, so:

- Muhammad is the **final** prophet; no prophet will come after him. (Isa's return is not a new prophethood.)
- He received the **Qur'an**, the final, complete and preserved revelation for **all humanity**, not one nation.
- He **completed** the message of all earlier prophets.
- His **Sunnah** (example) and **Hadith** (sayings) are the second source of guidance after the Qur'an, so Muslims copy his manner of prayer, washing, eating, kindness and honesty.
- He is named in the **Shahadah**, so every Muslim's declaration of faith includes him.
- Muslims show respect by saying "peace be upon him" after his name and never making pictures of him.

**Diversity**: Sunni and Shi'a Muslims agree that Muhammad is the Seal of the Prophets. Shi'a Muslims add that guidance continued through the **Imams** (not as prophets, but as divinely appointed leaders) beginning with **Ali**, whom they believe the Prophet named as his successor.

### Are all prophets equally important?

| "Equal" (collective importance) | "Not equal" (individual importance) |
|---|---|
| "We make no distinction between any of them" (2:136) | Some are given higher ranks: the Qur'an says Allah raised some messengers above others (2:253) |
| All chosen by Allah, all taught Tawhid, all protected from sin | Muhammad is the Seal, received the final book, and is in the Shahadah |
| Rejecting any prophet means rejecting Islam | The five ulul 'azm are especially honoured |
| All are role models in different ways | Adam was first; Ibrahim is the father of prophets; Isa will return |

A strong judgement: Muslims **believe in** all the prophets equally (no distinction in faith or respect), but they recognise **different roles**, and Muhammad has a unique place as the final messenger.

::: terms
- **Prophethood**: Risalah; the belief that Allah chose human messengers to bring His guidance to humanity.
- **Nabi**: a prophet chosen by Allah to receive and teach His guidance.
- **Rasul**: a messenger; a prophet who was also given a book or a law for a people.
- **'Ismah**: the belief that prophets were protected by Allah from sin and error.
- **Khalifah**: a steward or successor; Adam's role of caring for the earth on Allah's behalf.
- **Hanif**: a person who naturally worships the One God, as Ibrahim did among idol-worshippers.
- **Seal of the Prophets**: Khatam an-Nabiyyin; Muhammad as the last and final prophet (Qur'an 33:40).
- **Sunnah**: the example of the Prophet Muhammad's life, followed by Muslims as guidance.
- **Hadith**: the recorded sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad.
- **Hijrah**: Muhammad's migration from Makkah to Madinah in 622 CE, the start of the Islamic calendar.
- **Ulul 'azm**: the prophets of great determination: Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa and Muhammad.
:::

::: example (c) Isa
Explain why Isa is important to Muslims. (8)

Isa is one of the five prophets of great determination. Qur'an 2:87 says Allah gave him "clear signs" and supported him with the Holy Spirit, which Muslims understand to be the angel Jibril. This shows that Allah chose and strengthened him for his mission.

His birth was a miracle: he was born to Maryam, a pure and devout woman, without a father, by Allah's command "Be". This shows Allah's power, just as He created Adam without parents, and it is why Maryam is honoured with a surah named after her. By Allah's permission Isa spoke as a baby, healed the blind and lepers, and raised the dead (3:49), which confirmed he was a true prophet.

Isa received the Injil, which confirmed the Tawrat and brought guidance and light (5:46), so belief in him is part of belief in the kutub. However, Muslims believe he was a human prophet and not the Son of God, because Tawhid means Allah has no children (Surah 112); worshipping him would be shirk. Most Muslims believe he was not crucified but raised up to Allah (4:157-158), and many believe he will return before the Day of Judgement to defeat the Dajjal and bring justice. So Isa is important as a sign of Allah's power, a teacher of Tawhid, and a part of Muslim hope for the future.

[Band 4, 8 marks: detailed, sources, importance explained throughout.]
:::

::: example (d) evaluative paragraph
'For Muslims, all of the prophets in Islam are equally important.' One evaluative paragraph. (15)

Some Muslims would agree, because Qur'an 2:136 tells believers to make "no distinction between any of them": all prophets were chosen by Allah, protected from sin and taught the same message of Tawhid, so rejecting any one of them would mean rejecting Islam. This is a strong argument because it comes from the Qur'an itself and shows that the prophets are one family with one message. However, others argue that Muhammad has a unique place: he is the Seal of the Prophets (33:40), he received the final, preserved revelation, and his name is in the Shahadah, so his Sunnah shapes every part of Muslim life in a way no other prophet's does. The Qur'an also says Allah raised some messengers above others (2:253). Therefore the best judgement is that Muslims believe in and respect all prophets equally, but recognise that they had different roles, and Muhammad's role as the final messenger makes him uniquely important.
:::
