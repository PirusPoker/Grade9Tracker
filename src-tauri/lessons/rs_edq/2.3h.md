## What it is

This lesson covers **jihad**: **Greater Jihad**, the daily struggle to live as a good Muslim, including the issues of living as a Muslim and keeping a Muslim lifestyle in **Britain today**; and **Lesser Jihad**: its **origins**, **influence** and the **conditions for declaring it** in the Qur'an and Hadith (**Qur'an 2:190** and **22:39**).

It is examined in Question 2 (Practices) of Component 3: (a) 2, (b) 5, (c) 8, (d) 15, with **no SPaG**. **Greater/Lesser Jihad** is one of the eight (a) concepts in the spec, so learn a precise two-mark definition of each. The classic (d) is "Greater Jihad is more important than Lesser Jihad". Jihad is also one of the **Ten Obligatory Acts** of Shi'a Islam (2.3g).

### What jihad means

**Jihad** comes from an Arabic root meaning to **strive**, **struggle** or make an effort. The full phrase is jihad **"in the way of Allah"** (fi sabilillah): any struggle to please Allah and do His will.

- It does **not** mean "holy war". That phrase is not in the Qur'an, and translating jihad as "holy war" is a common **misconception**, often spread by media reports about extremists.
- Muslims usually speak of two kinds:

| | **Greater Jihad** (al-jihad al-akbar) | **Lesser Jihad** (al-jihad al-asghar) |
|---|---|---|
| What | The **inner**, personal struggle against one's own selfish desires (the **nafs**) to live as a good Muslim | The **outer** struggle to defend Islam, Muslims and the oppressed, which under **strict conditions** may include military force |
| Who | **Every** Muslim, **every** day, for life | Only when its conditions are met, and only declared by a legitimate authority |
| Examples | Praying on time, fasting, honesty, controlling anger, resisting temptation, learning the Qur'an | Defending a Muslim community under attack; protecting freedom of worship |
| Why "greater" | It never stops, it concerns the soul, and it is the basis of all other good deeds | It is rare, limited and only justified when Greater Jihad has made the person just |

### The hadith of the "greater jihad"

A well-known report tells that the Prophet, returning from a battle, said that the Muslims were returning from the **lesser jihad** to the **greater jihad**: the struggle against the self. Many hadith scholars regard the **chain of narration** of this report as **weak**, so some Muslims do not rely on it. However, the idea is supported elsewhere: another hadith teaches that the true **mujahid** (one who strives) is the person who **struggles against their own self** for Allah, and **Qur'an 29:69** promises that Allah will guide those who strive for Him to His ways. Good answers can mention both the hadith and the debate about it.

### Greater Jihad: the daily struggle to live as a good Muslim

Greater Jihad is the struggle to **submit** to Allah (the meaning of "Islam") when the nafs pulls the other way. Examples:

- **Worship**: getting up before dawn for Fajr; praying five times a day when busy or tired; going to Jummah.
- **Sawm**: fasting through Ramadan, controlling hunger, thirst and temper; this is often called a training in Greater Jihad.
- **Wealth**: paying zakah (and, for Shi'a Muslims, khums); giving sadaqah; resisting greed.
- **Character**: honesty, patience, forgiving others, controlling anger, avoiding gossip, lying and backbiting; respecting parents.
- **Knowledge**: learning and reciting the Qur'an; studying the faith; teaching children.
- **The tongue and pen**: speaking the truth, standing up for justice, explaining Islam peacefully to others.
- **Society**: amr-bil-maroof (encouraging good) and nahi anil munkar (discouraging evil); volunteering; caring for neighbours and the environment.

**Why it matters**: it purifies the soul and builds **taqwa** (God-consciousness); it is how Muslims carry out the Shahadah and the pillars; it prepares them for the **Day of Judgement**, when their deeds will be weighed; and it is open to everyone, every day.

### Issues for Muslims living in Britain today

Britain is a mainly secular and non-Muslim society, so keeping a Muslim lifestyle can itself be a form of Greater Jihad:

| Issue | Why it is a struggle | How British Muslims respond |
|---|---|---|
| **Prayer** | Prayer times fall in the school or working day; finding a clean, quiet place | Prayer rooms at many schools, universities and workplaces; apps; combining or praying in breaks; the Equality Act 2010 protects against religious discrimination |
| **Fasting** | Long summer fasts (18+ hours); exams; food everywhere | Community iftars, timetables, understanding employers; exemptions in 2:184 |
| **Food** | Not all food is **halal**; ingredients such as gelatine or alcohol may be hidden | Halal shops and restaurants; checking labels; vegetarian options |
| **Alcohol and gambling** | Drinking culture in pubs, parties and work events; gambling adverts | Avoiding these settings, choosing alcohol-free socialising |
| **Dress and modesty** | Wearing hijab or modest clothes may attract comments or discrimination | Support from family and community; legal protection |
| **Relationships** | Pressure from dating culture, social media and peers | Family guidance; Islamic teaching on modesty and marriage |
| **Money** | Most mortgages and loans charge **interest** (riba), which is haram | Islamic finance products from some British banks |
| **Islamophobia** | Prejudice, abuse, and negative media stereotypes, including about jihad | Responding with patience and good character; interfaith work; reporting hate crime |
| **Identity** | Balancing British and Muslim identity, especially for young people | Mosques, youth groups, Islamic societies at universities |

Many British Muslims see these challenges positively: the struggle brings **reward**, and living well in a diverse society is itself a way of showing what Islam teaches.

### Lesser Jihad: origins

- **In Makkah** (about 610-622 CE): the first Muslims were mocked, boycotted and persecuted for around thirteen years, and some were tortured or killed. The Qur'an at this stage told them to be **patient**; they were **not** permitted to fight back.
- **The Hijrah** (622 CE): the Muslims migrated to **Madinah**, where they formed a community, but their enemies in Makkah continued to attack them.
- **Permission to fight**: the first verses allowing fighting came in Madinah. **Qur'an 22:39** gives permission to those who are being fought, **because they have been wronged**, and promises Allah's help. The next verse explains that they had been driven from their homes only for saying "Our Lord is Allah", and that without such defence, **monasteries, churches, synagogues and mosques**, where Allah's name is remembered, would be destroyed. So the original purpose of Lesser Jihad was **self-defence** and protecting the **freedom to worship**.
- **Qur'an 2:190** tells Muslims to fight in the way of Allah **those who fight you**, but **do not transgress** (go beyond the limits), for Allah does not love transgressors. The following verses add that if the enemy **stops**, Muslims must stop too.
- **Early battles** such as **Badr** (624 CE) were fought by the small Muslim community against the much larger Makkan forces.

### Conditions for declaring Lesser Jihad

From the Qur'an, the Hadith and the teaching of scholars, Lesser Jihad is only lawful if **all** these conditions are met:

1. **A just cause**: **self-defence**, defending Muslims who are attacked or oppressed, or protecting freedom of worship (22:39). Never for land, wealth, revenge or glory.
2. **Declared by a legitimate authority**: a recognised Muslim leader or government after consulting scholars, **not** by individuals or self-appointed groups. Many Shi'a scholars teach that, while the twelfth Imam is hidden, only **defensive** jihad is permitted.
3. **A last resort**: peaceful solutions must be tried first.
4. **Proportionate**: use the **minimum force** needed; "do not transgress" (2:190).
5. **Protecting the innocent**: the Prophet **forbade killing women and children**; the first caliph, **Abu Bakr**, is reported to have told his army not to kill the elderly, children or women, not to destroy trees, crops or buildings, and to leave monks in their places of worship alone.
6. **Not to force anyone to convert**: "there is no compulsion in religion" (**2:256**).
7. **Ending when the enemy offers peace**: if they incline to peace, Muslims must incline to it too (**8:61**); prisoners must be treated humanely.
8. **The aim is to restore peace and justice**, not to destroy.

### Influence of Lesser Jihad

- **On early Islam**: it allowed the small Muslim community in Madinah to survive and to worship freely.
- **On Muslim attitudes to war**: the conditions shape Muslim thinking on when war can be justified and how it must be fought. Most Muslims see jihad as a set of strict **limits** on violence, not a licence for it. Muslims serve in the **British Armed Forces**, which have their own Muslim chaplains.
- **Misuse by extremists**: some terrorist groups have claimed to be fighting "jihad". The **vast majority** of Muslims and scholars reject this: such attacks are not declared by a legitimate authority, target civilians, and break 2:190 and the Prophet's commands. Qur'an 5:32 teaches that killing one innocent person is like killing all humanity. British Muslim organisations and scholars have repeatedly condemned terrorism; in 2010 the scholar **Muhammad Tahir-ul-Qadri** launched in London a long **fatwa** (ruling) condemning terrorism and suicide bombing as against Islam.
- **On British Muslims**: the misuse of the word has fed **Islamophobia**, so many British Muslims work to explain the true meaning of jihad, which is itself a form of Greater Jihad (striving with the tongue and pen).
- **For Shi'a Muslims**: jihad is one of the Ten Obligatory Acts, and **Imam Husayn's** stand against an unjust ruler at **Karbala** (680 CE) is remembered as the model of resisting oppression whatever the cost.

### Greater or Lesser: which is more important?

| Greater Jihad is more important | Lesser Jihad is (also) important |
|---|---|
| Called "greater" in the hadith; it is daily and lifelong for every Muslim | A real duty in the Qur'an (22:39, 2:190) and Hadith when the conditions are met |
| Purifies the soul, the basis of all good deeds | Protects the community, the oppressed and freedom of worship, including for other faiths (22:40) |
| Lesser Jihad is only legitimate for those who have first mastered themselves | The hadith of the "greater jihad" has a weak chain, so the ranking is debated |
| In Britain Lesser Jihad's conditions do not apply; Greater Jihad is constant | Standing against injustice (as Husayn did) can require outward struggle |
| Emphasising it corrects the "holy war" misconception | Both are "in the way of Allah"; one leads to the other |

::: terms
- **Jihad**: striving or struggling in the way of Allah; it does not mean "holy war".
- **Greater Jihad**: the inner, daily struggle against one's own selfish desires to live as a good Muslim.
- **Lesser Jihad**: the outer struggle to defend Islam and the oppressed, which may involve force under strict conditions.
- **Nafs**: the self or ego, with its selfish desires, which Greater Jihad struggles to control.
- **Mujahid**: a person who strives or struggles in the way of Allah.
- **Fi sabilillah**: "in the way of Allah": the purpose that makes a struggle a jihad.
- **Hijrah**: the migration of the Prophet and the Muslims from Makkah to Madinah in 622 CE.
- **Legitimate authority**: a recognised Muslim leader or government, the only one who may declare Lesser Jihad.
- **Transgress**: to go beyond the limits Allah has set, forbidden in war by Qur'an 2:190.
- **Fatwa**: a ruling on Islamic law given by a qualified scholar.
- **Islamophobia**: prejudice, fear or hostility towards Islam and Muslims.
:::

::: example (c) Greater Jihad in Britain
Explain the importance of Greater Jihad for Muslims in Britain. (8)

Jihad means striving in the way of Allah, and Greater Jihad is the struggle against one's own nafs, or selfish desires, to live as a good Muslim. A well-known hadith calls it the "greater jihad", although some scholars think its chain is weak; another hadith says the true mujahid is the one who struggles against himself, and Qur'an 29:69 promises guidance to those who strive. It is important because it is a daily duty for every Muslim, and it is how they fulfil the Five Pillars and prepare for the Day of Judgement.

In Britain Greater Jihad is especially important because keeping a Muslim lifestyle in a mainly non-Muslim society is hard. A Muslim student may need to find a quiet place to pray Zuhr at school, fast for eighteen hours during summer exams, and check food labels for non-halal ingredients. They may face pressure to drink alcohol at parties or work events, and most mortgages involve interest, which is haram. Some face Islamophobia and must respond with patience and good character rather than anger.

Greater Jihad strengthens the individual's taqwa and self-discipline, and it builds strong communities, as mosques run youth groups and iftars to support each other. By living well, British Muslims also correct the misconception that jihad means "holy war", which is itself a form of striving for Allah.

[Band 4, 8 marks: meaning, sources, specific British issues, influence on individual and community.]

::: example (d) evaluative paragraph
'Greater Jihad is more important than Lesser Jihad.' One evaluative paragraph. (15)

Many Muslims agree, because Greater Jihad is a duty for every Muslim every day, while Lesser Jihad only applies in rare circumstances and under strict conditions; for a Muslim in Britain, where no legitimate authority has declared war and Muslims live in peace, Greater Jihad is the only jihad they practise. However, the main evidence for calling it "greater", the hadith about returning from battle, has a chain that many scholars regard as weak, so the ranking is not certain. Qur'an 22:39 shows that Lesser Jihad was a real command, protecting the right to worship, including for churches and synagogues. Even so, Lesser Jihad depends on Greater Jihad: only a person who has controlled their anger and desires can fight justly without transgressing (2:190). So Greater Jihad is more important, because it is the foundation that makes any Lesser Jihad legitimate.
