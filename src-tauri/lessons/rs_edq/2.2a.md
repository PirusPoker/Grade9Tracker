## What it is

This lesson opens **Component 2: Study of Christianity** with Christian beliefs about **the nature of God**. You need: God as **omnipotent** (**Exodus 7-11**, **Exodus 14:21**) and **omnibenevolent** (**Psalm 86:15**, **John 3:16**, **Romans 8:37-39**); the challenge of **evil and suffering**, using the quotation on God and evil attributed to **Epicurus** and the **Book of Job** (**1:8-12**, **42:1-6**); and the **Trinity**, the oneness of God as Father, Son and Holy Spirit (**John 10:30**, **John 14:6-11**).

### God is omnipotent

**Omnipotent** means **all-powerful**: God can do anything that is logically possible. Christians believe he shows this in creation, in miracles and in history.

- **Exodus 7-11, the plagues**: when Pharaoh refused to free the Israelites from slavery in Egypt, God sent ten plagues through Moses, among them the Nile turned to blood, frogs, gnats, flies, the death of livestock, boils, hail, locusts, darkness, and finally the death of the firstborn. Christians see this as God's power over nature and over the mightiest ruler of the day, used to rescue the oppressed.
- **Exodus 14:21, the Red Sea**: Moses stretched his hand over the sea, and the Lord drove it back with a strong east wind all night and made it dry land, so the Israelites crossed. This shows God's power to save his people.
- Jesus' miracles (calming the storm, healing, raising the dead) and his resurrection are, for Christians, further signs of God's power.
- **Divergent views**: most Christians (following Aquinas and C. S. Lewis) say omnipotence does **not** mean God can do the logically impossible (such as make a square circle) or act against his own loving nature. Some liberal theologians suggest God chooses to limit his power to give humans real freedom, or that his power works through love rather than force.

### God is omnibenevolent

**Omnibenevolent** means **all-loving** and all-good: God's love is perfect, unlimited and for everyone.

- **Psalm 86:15**: God is compassionate and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in love and faithfulness. God's love is patient and steady even when people fail.
- **John 3:16**: "For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life." The greatest sign of God's love is the gift of **Jesus**, who died to save humanity.
- **Romans 8:37-39**: Paul teaches that nothing, neither death nor life, angels nor rulers, things present nor to come, can **separate** believers from the love of God in Christ Jesus. God's love is secure, even in suffering.
- **Influence on believers**: Christians try to imitate this love through **agape**: forgiveness, charity, caring for the poor and the sick. It gives comfort in hard times.
- **Divergent views**: Christians agree God is loving, but differ on what follows: some stress God's love alongside his **justice** and judgement (and so a real hell); others, more liberal, think a fully loving God will eventually save everyone.

### Evil and suffering: the challenge

If God is both all-powerful and all-loving, why is there evil and suffering? The philosopher **Epicurus** (ancient Greece) is credited with the classic dilemma, which runs like this:

- Is God **willing** to prevent evil but **not able**? Then he is not all-powerful.
- Is he **able** but **not willing**? Then he is not all-loving.
- Is he both able and willing? Then **where does evil come from**?
- Is he neither? Then why call him God?

The challenge is sharpest for **natural evil** (earthquakes, disease) that harms innocent people. Some people lose faith because of suffering; atheists argue it shows God does not exist.

### The Book of Job

The Book of Job explores why good people suffer.

- **Job 1:8-12**: God points out his servant **Job** as blameless and upright. **Satan** replies that Job only honours God because God has blessed and protected him; take everything away and Job will curse God. God allows Satan to take all Job has, but not to harm Job himself. Job then loses his wealth and his children, and later his health, but he refuses to curse God.
- Job's friends insist he must have sinned to deserve this. Job protests his innocence and demands an answer from God. God finally speaks out of a storm, asking Job where he was when the world was made, showing the vastness of creation that Job cannot understand.
- **Job 42:1-6**: Job admits that God can do all things and that no purpose of his can be stopped; he has spoken of things too wonderful for him to know. Before he had only heard of God, but now his eyes have **seen** him, so he repents in dust and ashes. God then restores Job's fortunes.
- **What Christians learn**: suffering is not always a punishment for sin; humans cannot fully understand God's purposes; faith means **trusting** God in suffering; it is acceptable to bring honest questions and anger to God; God is present in suffering.

### Christian responses to evil and suffering

| Response | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Free will** (Augustine) | God gave humans freedom so they could love him freely; moral evil comes from its misuse, beginning with the Fall (Genesis 3). |
| **Soul-making** (Irenaeus, John Hick) | Suffering gives the chance to grow in courage, compassion and faith; a world without pain would allow no moral growth. |
| **Trust** (Job) | God's wisdom is greater than ours; believers trust him even without an explanation. |
| **The cross** | In Jesus, God himself suffered; Christians believe God shares their pain and that the resurrection shows suffering does not have the last word. |
| **Heaven** | God will wipe every tear from their eyes (Revelation 21:4): suffering is not the end. |
| **Practical response** | Rather than only explaining suffering, Christians respond through **prayer** and **action**: hospitals, hospices, charities such as Christian Aid. |

### The Trinity

The **Trinity** is the Christian belief that there is **one God** in **three persons**: the **Father**, the **Son** (Jesus) and the **Holy Spirit**. The three are not three gods, nor three parts of God, nor one God wearing three masks: each person is fully God, they are **co-equal** and **co-eternal**, and they are distinct from one another.

- **The Father**: creator and sustainer of the universe, the loving parent of all.
- **The Son**: God **incarnate** in Jesus Christ, fully God and fully human, who died and rose to save humanity.
- **The Holy Spirit**: God present and active in the world and in believers today, giving guidance, strength and spiritual gifts.
- **John 10:30**: Jesus said, "I and the Father are one." Christians see this as Jesus claiming to share the Father's divine nature.
- **John 14:6-11**: Jesus said, "I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." When Philip asked to see the Father, Jesus replied that anyone who has seen him has seen the Father, and that he is in the Father and the Father is in him. This teaches the **oneness** of Father and Son, and that God is known through Jesus.
- **The creeds**: the **Nicene Creed** (4th century) states belief in one God, the Father almighty; in one Lord, Jesus Christ, "of one Being with the Father"; and in the Holy Spirit, who with the Father and the Son is worshipped and glorified. Christians say it in many services.
- **Why it matters**: it shows God is a **relationship of love** within himself; it explains how Jesus can be God and pray to the Father; it shapes worship (baptism "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit", Matthew 28:19) and prayer (to the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit).
- **Divergent views**: the Orthodox and Western churches differ over the **filioque**: Western churches say the Spirit proceeds from the Father "and the Son", while the Orthodox say from the Father. A few groups outside mainstream Christianity, such as Unitarians, do not accept the Trinity. Analogies such as water (ice, liquid, steam) or a shamrock help some believers, but every analogy is imperfect, and Christians accept the Trinity as a **mystery** revealed by God.

::: terms
- **Omnipotent**: all-powerful; God can do anything that is logically possible.
- **Omnibenevolent**: all-loving and all-good; God's love is perfect and extends to everyone.
- **Omniscient**: all-knowing; God knows everything past, present and future.
- **Evil**: that which is morally wrong or causes harm; moral evil comes from humans, natural evil from nature.
- **Suffering**: physical or mental pain and distress, which raises questions about a loving God.
- **Trinity**: the belief that there is one God in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
- **Holy Spirit**: the third person of the Trinity; God present and active in the world and in believers.
- **Incarnation**: God becoming human in Jesus Christ, fully God and fully human.
- **Nicene Creed**: a 4th-century statement of Christian belief in God as Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
- **Mystery**: a truth about God that humans cannot fully understand but believe because God has revealed it.
:::

::: example (b) the Trinity
Describe Christian beliefs about the Trinity. (5)

Christians believe in one God who exists as three persons: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. The three are co-equal and co-eternal; each is fully God, yet there is only one God. The Father is the creator; the Son is God incarnate in Jesus, who said, "I and the Father are one" (John 10:30) and "Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9); the Holy Spirit is God at work in believers today. ::: example (c) responses to suffering
Explain Christian responses to the problem of evil and suffering. (8)

Epicurus's dilemma asks why an all-powerful, all-loving God would allow evil; Christians answer in different ways. Many follow Augustine: God gave humans free will so their love would be real, and evil entered through its misuse at the Fall. Others follow Irenaeus and John Hick, who say suffering is part of a "vale of soul-making" where humans grow in courage and compassion. The Book of Job teaches that suffering is not always punishment for sin: God allowed Satan to test Job (1:8-12), and Job finally accepted that God's wisdom is beyond human understanding (42:1-6), so many Christians trust God without a full explanation. Christians also believe God shares suffering through Jesus on the cross, and that in heaven God will wipe away every tear (Revelation 21:4). Finally, most Christians stress a practical response, praying and helping those who suffer, for example through hospices and Christian Aid.

[Band 4, 8 marks: divergent responses, sources explained, coherent.]

::: example (d) evaluative paragraph
'The most important Christian belief is that God is omnipotent.' One evaluative paragraph. (15)

Some Christians would agree, because without God's power there would be no creation, no miracles and no resurrection: the God who parted the Red Sea (Exodus 14:21) is the God who can raise Jesus and save believers. This argument is strong because Christian hope depends on God being able to keep his promises. However, power on its own could be frightening; what makes God worth worshipping is his love. John 3:16 places God's love at the heart of the gospel, and Romans 8:37-39 says nothing can separate believers from it. Others argue that the Trinity is most important, because it explains who Jesus is and how God is love within himself. Overall, omnipotence matters most when it serves God's love, so the statement puts the emphasis in the wrong place.
:::
