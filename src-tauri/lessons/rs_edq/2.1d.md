## What it is

This topic opens **Theme 2: Issues of Life and Death**. You need: diverse Christian beliefs about the origin of the universe from **Genesis 1 and 2**; Muslim beliefs about the origin of the universe (**Qur'an 36:81**); the **relationship between religious and non-religious views of creation** and **how far they conflict**; **Brian Cox's view of the Big Bang**; and non-religious beliefs about **evolution**, from **Charles Darwin** and **Richard Dawkins**.

Theme 2 is different from the other three themes: its 15-mark (d) question says "You must refer to **religious and non-religious beliefs, such as those held by Humanists and Atheists**". So this lesson teaches three perspectives side by side: Christian, Muslim and non-religious.

### Christian beliefs: Genesis 1 and 2

**Genesis 1** describes God creating by his word over **six days** and resting on the seventh:

| Day | Created |
|---|---|
| 1 | Light, separated from darkness |
| 2 | The sky, separating the waters |
| 3 | Dry land, seas and plants |
| 4 | Sun, moon and stars |
| 5 | Sea creatures and birds |
| 6 | Land animals, then humans "in his own image… male and female" |
| 7 | God rests and blesses the day |

Key ideas: God created "in the beginning", from nothing (**creation ex nihilo**, as the Church has always taught); creation is ordered and deliberate ("And God said… and it was so"); everything is "**very good**"; humans are the high point, made in the **image of God**.

**Genesis 2** tells it differently: God forms the first man, **Adam**, "from the dust of the ground" and breathes into him the **breath of life**; plants him in the **Garden of Eden**; brings the animals for Adam to name; and makes **Eve** from Adam's side so he is not alone. Its emphasis is on the **closeness** of God to humans and on relationship.

**Diverse Christian interpretations**

| View | Who | Belief |
|---|---|---|
| **Literal (young-earth creationism)** | Some evangelical and fundamentalist Christians | Genesis is accurate history: six 24-hour days, a universe thousands of years old, each kind of creature made separately. The Bible is the inspired, error-free word of God. Many reject evolution. |
| **Old-earth / "day-age"** | Some evangelicals | A "day" can mean a long age; the order in Genesis 1 broadly matches science. |
| **Non-literal** | Catholic Church, Church of England, most mainstream Christians | Genesis is inspired, but written as poetic, symbolic narrative to teach **why** the world exists, not **how**: God is the creator, creation is good, humans are special and responsible. These truths fit with the Big Bang and evolution. |
| **Intelligent design** | Some Christians | Some features of living things are too complex to have arisen by chance; they point to a designer. |

The **Big Bang theory itself was first proposed by a Catholic priest**, **Georges Lemaître**, a Belgian physicist, in the late 1920s and early 1930s. Many Christians see this as showing that faith and science need not conflict. Catholic teaching accepts evolution as compatible with faith, provided that God is the creator of all and that each human **soul** is created directly by God.

### Muslim beliefs about the origin of the universe

- **Allah is al-Khaliq**, the Creator. He creates by command: when he wills a thing, he says "**Be**" (*kun*) and it is (36:82).
- **Qur'an 36:81** asks whether the One who created the heavens and the earth is not able to create the like of them again, and answers: yes, He is "the Knowing Creator". Muslims use it to show that Allah's power to **create** is also power to **recreate** (to raise the dead), and that the universe exists only by His will.
- The Qur'an says Allah created the heavens and earth in **six days** (e.g. 7:54). Most Muslims understand these as **periods** of time, not 24-hour days.
- Many Muslims point to verses that they see as fitting the Big Bang: the heavens and earth were once "joined" and Allah split them apart (21:30), and Allah is expanding the heaven (51:47). For them, science uncovers how Allah created.
- **Humans**: Adam was created from **clay** and Allah breathed His spirit into him; Hawwa (Eve) was created as his partner. Most Muslims believe Adam was created directly, so many accept evolution for other living things but **not for humans**; some accept a God-guided evolution; some reject evolution entirely.

### Non-religious views: the Big Bang and Brian Cox

**The Big Bang theory**: about **13.8 billion years ago** the universe began from an extremely hot, dense state and has been **expanding and cooling** ever since. Atoms formed, then stars and galaxies; the Earth formed about 4.5 billion years ago. The main evidence is **red shift** (galaxies are moving away from each other) and the **cosmic microwave background** radiation (the left-over heat of the early universe).

**Brian Cox** is a British particle physicist and well-known science broadcaster (for example the BBC *Wonders* series). In outline, his view is that:
- the Big Bang is the best scientific explanation of how the universe developed, supported by evidence and open to testing;
- the universe can be explained through the laws of physics, without needing God; Cox does not hold religious beliefs;
- science is a method for finding out, so questions such as what caused the Big Bang, or whether anything came "before", are still **open** questions for science, not proof that God exists.

### Non-religious views on evolution: Darwin and Dawkins

**Charles Darwin** (1809–1882) was a naturalist who travelled on HMS *Beagle* and studied wildlife, including on the Galápagos Islands. In ***On the Origin of Species*** (**1859**) he set out **evolution by natural selection**:
1. Individuals in a species **vary**.
2. More are born than can survive, so there is a **struggle for existence**.
3. Those with variations that suit their environment survive and reproduce more (**natural selection**, "survival of the fittest").
4. They pass those features on, so over very long periods species **change gradually**, and new species arise.
5. All living things share **common ancestors**; later, in *The Descent of Man* (1871), he applied this to humans.

Darwin trained for the Church of England as a young man but later called himself **agnostic**. His theory is "non-religious" in that it explains the variety of life without needing God to design each species.

**Richard Dawkins** (born 1941) is an evolutionary biologist and leading **atheist**.
- ***The Selfish Gene*** (1976): evolution is best understood at the level of **genes**, which are passed on by the organisms that carry them.
- ***The Blind Watchmaker*** (1986): natural selection explains the appearance of **design** in nature; it is a "blind" process with no purpose or designer, answering William Paley's design argument.
- ***The God Delusion*** (2006): belief in God is very probably false; science and religion are in **conflict**, and faith is not a good reason for belief.

**Humanists and atheists** generally accept the Big Bang and evolution as the best explanations of where the universe and life came from, based on evidence and reason; there is no need for a creator. Humanists stress that this does not make life meaningless: humans create meaning for themselves.

### How far do religious and non-religious views conflict?

| Position | Argument |
|---|---|
| **Strong conflict** | Young-earth creationists and some atheists (e.g. Dawkins) agree on one thing: Genesis read literally and evolution cannot both be true. Dawkins argues that science leaves no gap for God. |
| **No real conflict** | Science answers **how**; religion answers **why** and **who**. The Big Bang needs a cause, and many believers say that cause is God (a form of the **cosmological argument**). The "fine-tuning" of the universe suggests a designer. Lemaître was a priest and a scientist. |
| **Partial agreement (Islam)** | Many Muslims see the Big Bang as confirming the Qur'an (21:30) but hold that humans are a special creation through Adam. |
| **Non-religious, but open** | Some non-religious scientists say science cannot prove or disprove God; it simply does not need God to explain how nature works. |

::: terms
- **Evolution**: the gradual change of species over many generations through natural selection, leading to new species.
- **Natural selection**: the process by which individuals better suited to their environment survive and pass on their features.
- **Big Bang theory**: the scientific theory that the universe began about 13.8 billion years ago from a hot, dense state and has been expanding ever since.
- **Creation ex nihilo**: the belief that God created the universe out of nothing.
- **Creationism**: the belief that Genesis gives a literal account of how God made the world.
- **Literal interpretation**: reading a sacred text as accurate, word-for-word fact.
- **Non-literal interpretation**: reading a text as symbolic or poetic, teaching spiritual truths rather than scientific fact.
- **Intelligent design**: the view that some features of nature are so complex that they must have a designer.
- **Al-Khaliq**: the Creator, one of the names of Allah.
- **Atheist**: a person who believes there is no God.
- **Humanist**: a non-religious person who lives by reason, evidence and concern for human welfare, without belief in God.
:::

::: example (a) definition

The process by which living things change gradually over many generations (1), through natural selection, so that new species develop from common ancestors (1).

::: example (b) Darwin
Describe the beliefs of Charles Darwin about evolution. (5)

Darwin set out his theory in On the Origin of Species (1859), after studying wildlife on the voyage of HMS Beagle, including the Galápagos Islands. He believed that individuals within a species vary, and that more are born than can survive, so there is a struggle for existence. Those whose variations suit their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing those features on: this is natural selection. Over very long periods these small changes build up, so species change gradually and new species form. Darwin concluded that all living things, including humans, share common ancestors, which meant the variety of life could be explained without God designing each species separately.

[Band 3, 5 marks: accurate, coherent, specialist terms, book named.]

::: example (c) origin of the universe in two religions
Explain, from either two religions or two religious traditions, beliefs about the origin of the universe. (8)

**Christianity.** All Christians believe God created the universe out of nothing, as Genesis 1 begins "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth". But they interpret it differently. Young-earth creationists read Genesis 1 literally: God made everything in six 24-hour days only thousands of years ago, so they reject the scientific age of the universe. Most Catholics and Anglicans read Genesis 1 and 2 as inspired but symbolic, teaching that God is the creator and that creation is "very good", not how it happened. They accept the Big Bang as the way God created; the theory was first proposed by a Catholic priest, Georges Lemaître.

**Islam.** Muslims believe Allah is al-Khaliq, the Creator, who brings things into being by saying "Be". Qur'an 36:81 asks whether the One who created the heavens and the earth is not able to create the like of them, and answers that He is "the Knowing Creator", showing that the universe depends completely on Allah's power. The Qur'an says creation took six "days", which most Muslims read as long periods, and many see the verse that the heavens and earth were once joined and then split apart (21:30) as agreeing with the Big Bang.

[Band 4, 8 marks.]

::: example (d) paragraph with non-religious view
'Science has shown that God did not create the universe.' One evaluative paragraph using a non-religious view. (15)

Atheists such as Richard Dawkins would agree. He argues in The Blind Watchmaker that natural selection explains the appearance of design in living things without any designer, and Brian Cox explains the development of the universe from the Big Bang using physics alone. For them, if science explains how the universe and life developed, belief in a creator is unnecessary. This argument is strong against a literal reading of Genesis, because the evidence for an old universe and for evolution is overwhelming. However, it is weaker against non-literal believers. Science shows how the universe developed after the Big Bang, but, as Cox himself accepts, it has not explained what caused the Big Bang, and many Christians and Muslims say that cause is God. So science has shown that creationism is unlikely, not that God did not create.
