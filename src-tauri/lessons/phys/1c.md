## What it is

A **force** is a push or a pull. Forces can change an object's **speed**, its **direction** or its **shape**. They are measured in **newtons (N)** and always come in pairs between two objects — though the pair acts on *different* objects (see Newton's third law in 1g).

**Types of force** you need to name:

- **Weight** — gravity pulling a mass toward the Earth; acts from the centre of gravity, vertically downward.
- **Normal contact force (reaction)** — a surface pushing back on an object resting on it, at right angles to the surface.
- **Friction** — between surfaces sliding (or trying to slide) over each other; opposes the motion.
- **Air resistance / drag** — friction with a fluid; grows with speed.
- **Tension** — in a stretched rope, string or cable, pulling along it.
- **Thrust / driving force / lift / upthrust** — an engine or a fluid pushing an object.
- **Electrostatic and magnetic** forces — the non-contact forces alongside gravity.

**Scalars and vectors.** A **scalar** has size (magnitude) only: distance, speed, time, mass, energy, temperature. A **vector** has size **and direction**: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, weight, momentum. Force is a vector — an arrow on a diagram shows its direction, the arrow's length its size. Two forces of 5 N are only "the same" if they point the same way.

**Resultant force along a line.** When forces act along the same line, the **resultant** is their combined effect as a single force: **add** forces in the same direction, **subtract** forces in opposite directions, and give the direction of the larger. A resultant of **zero** means the forces are **balanced**: the object stays at rest or keeps moving at constant velocity. A **non-zero resultant** makes the object accelerate in the direction of the resultant.

::: terms
- **Force**: a push or pull that can change an object's speed, direction or shape; measured in newtons (N).
- **Scalar**: a quantity with size (magnitude) only — mass, speed, energy, time, distance.
- **Vector**: a quantity with both size and direction — force, velocity, displacement, acceleration, momentum, weight.
- **Resultant force**: the single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on an object.
- **Balanced forces**: forces whose resultant is zero, so the object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity.
- **Types of force**: gravitational (weight), electrostatic, magnetic, thrust, drag, air resistance, friction, upthrust, tension, normal reaction.
:::

::: method
1. **Draw a free-body diagram**: the object as a box, every force as an arrow *from* the object, labelled with its name and size.
2. Sort forces into **lines of action** (usually horizontal and vertical). Deal with each line separately.
3. **Same direction → add; opposite → subtract.** State the resultant's size *and* direction ("40 N to the left").
4. Resultant zero → *balanced*: stationary or constant velocity. Resultant non-zero → *accelerates* in that direction.
5. When asked to classify, ask "does it have a direction?" — if yes it is a vector.
:::

::: example Resultant force
A car engine provides a driving force of 1800 N. Air resistance is 600 N and friction 200 N. Find the resultant force and describe the car's motion. (3)

Resistive forces total 600 + 200 = 800 N backward (1). Resultant = 1800 − 800 = **1000 N forward** (1). The resultant is non-zero, so the car **accelerates** forward (1).
:::

::: example Balanced forces
A parachutist of weight 750 N is falling at a constant velocity. State the size of the air resistance and explain your answer. (2)

Air resistance = **750 N upward** (1); constant velocity means the resultant force is zero, so the forces must be balanced (1).
:::

::: example Vectors and scalars
Sort the following into scalars and vectors: mass, weight, velocity, energy, displacement, time. (3)

Scalars: mass, energy, time (1). Vectors: weight, velocity, displacement (1). Vectors have a direction as well as a magnitude; scalars have magnitude only (1).
:::

::: example Tug of war
Team A pulls with 2500 N to the left and team B with 2300 N to the right. Find the resultant and state what happens. (2)

Resultant = 2500 − 2300 = **200 N to the left** (1); the rope (and both teams) accelerate toward team A (1).
:::

::: q
State three things a force can do to an object. (3)
---
Change its speed (1), change its direction (1), change its shape (1).
:::

::: q
Name the force that (a) a table exerts on a book resting on it, (b) opposes a sliding object. (2)
---
(a) Normal contact force / reaction (1); (b) friction (1).
:::

::: q
Is force a scalar or a vector? Explain. (2)
---
Vector (1); it has a direction as well as a magnitude (1).
:::

::: q
Give two examples of scalars and two of vectors. (2)
---
Scalars e.g. speed, distance, mass, time, energy (1); vectors e.g. velocity, displacement, force, acceleration, momentum (1).
:::

::: q
Two forces of 30 N and 12 N act on an object in opposite directions. Find the resultant. (2)
---
30 − 12 = 18 N (1) in the direction of the 30 N force (1).
:::

::: q
Forces of 8 N and 6 N act on an object in the same direction. What is the resultant? (1)
---
14 N in that direction.
:::

::: q
A boat is pushed forward by its engine with 4000 N and experiences 4000 N of drag. Describe its motion. (2)
---
Resultant force is zero (1), so it moves at constant velocity (or stays at rest) (1).
:::

::: q
A book of weight 6 N rests on a table. State the size and direction of the normal contact force. (2)
---
6 N (1) upward (1).
:::

::: q
Why is friction useful when walking? (1)
---
It provides the backward push of the foot on the ground that gives the forward force on the walker (grip).
:::

::: q
Name two non-contact forces. (2)
---
Gravitational (weight) (1); magnetic or electrostatic (1).
:::

::: watch
- A resultant needs a **direction** as well as a number; "200 N" alone is one mark short.
- **Balanced forces ≠ stationary.** Zero resultant also means constant velocity — examiners set this deliberately.
- Weight and normal contact force are **not** a Newton-third-law pair (both act on the same object); they simply happen to balance.
- Speed is a scalar; **velocity** is the vector. Distance scalar; **displacement** vector.
- On free-body diagrams draw arrows **from the object**, with lengths roughly in proportion.
:::

::: test
A car's engine gives a forward force of 2,500 N. Friction is 400 N and air resistance is 900 N. Calculate the resultant force and describe the car's motion. (3)
---

::: test
Describe and explain the forces acting on an aeroplane as it takes off, flies at a steady speed and height, and lands. (6)
---
Any six: forces: thrust forward, drag backward, lift upward, weight downward; take-off: thrust greater than drag, so it accelerates; lift greater than weight, so it rises; steady flight: thrust equals drag, so constant speed; lift equals weight, so constant height; landing: thrust less than drag, so it slows; lift less than weight, so it descends; resultant force is zero when forces are balanced.
Marking: 1 mark for each correct point, up to 6; for full marks the answer must be in a logical order.
:::
