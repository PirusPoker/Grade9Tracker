## What it is

Visible light is one small part of the **electromagnetic spectrum** — a family of **transverse** waves that all travel at the **same speed in a vacuum, 3 × 10⁸ m/s**, need no medium, and transfer energy from a source to an absorber. They differ only in wavelength and frequency. In order of **increasing frequency** (and decreasing wavelength):

**Radio → Microwaves → Infrared → Visible → Ultraviolet → X-rays → Gamma**

Radio waves have the longest wavelength (metres to kilometres) and lowest frequency; gamma rays the shortest wavelength (smaller than an atom) and highest frequency. Higher frequency means **more energy** per wave — and more danger. Within the visible band, red has the longest wavelength and violet the shortest ("ROYGBIV"). A mnemonic for the order: *Raging Martians Invaded Venus Using X-ray Guns*.

**Uses of each part.**

| Region | Typical uses |
|---|---|
| Radio | Broadcasting radio and TV, communications |
| Microwaves | Cooking (water molecules absorb them), satellite communication, mobile phones |
| Infrared | Heaters and grills, thermal imaging and night-vision cameras, TV remote controls, optical fibres |
| Visible | Seeing, photography, illumination, optical fibres |
| Ultraviolet | Fluorescent lamps, sterilising water and equipment, detecting forged banknotes, sun tanning |
| X-rays | Medical imaging of bones, airport security scanners, checking welds |
| Gamma | Sterilising medical equipment and food, treating cancer (radiotherapy), medical tracers |

**Harmful effects of over-exposure.**

- **Microwaves**: internal heating of body tissue (water in cells absorbs them).
- **Infrared**: skin burns.
- **Ultraviolet**: sunburn, premature skin ageing, **skin cancer**, damage to the eyes (cataracts) — UV can damage the DNA in cells.
- **X-rays and gamma rays**: highly **ionising** — they can kill cells or cause **mutations** leading to cancer. Exposure is minimised by lead shielding, distance, short exposure times and limiting the number of X-rays a person has.

Radio waves and visible light at normal intensities are not considered harmful.

::: terms
- **Electromagnetic spectrum**: the family of transverse waves that all travel at the same speed in a vacuum: radio, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.
- **Order of the spectrum**: from radio waves (longest wavelength, lowest frequency) to gamma rays (shortest wavelength, highest frequency).
- **Uses**: radio — broadcasting; microwaves — cooking, satellites; infrared — heaters, remote controls; visible — fibre optics; ultraviolet — fluorescent lamps; X-rays — imaging bones; gamma — sterilising, cancer treatment.
- **Harmful effects**: microwaves — heating body cells; infrared — burns; ultraviolet — skin cancer and eye damage; X-rays and gamma — mutation and cancer.
:::

::: method
1. **Learn the order both ways**: radio (long λ, low f, low energy) → gamma (short λ, high f, high energy).
2. Same speed in a vacuum for all → from v = fλ, higher frequency **must** mean shorter wavelength.
3. Match uses to properties: microwaves are absorbed by water (cooking); infrared is emitted by warm objects (thermal imaging); X-rays pass through soft tissue but not bone; gamma penetrates and kills cells (sterilising).
4. Match harms to energy: heating for microwaves/infrared; DNA damage/cancer for UV; ionisation/mutation/cancer for X and gamma.
5. Use precise words: *ionising*, *mutation*, *absorbed*, *transmitted*.
:::

::: example Ordering and properties
Place infrared, X-rays and radio waves in order of increasing frequency and state which has the longest wavelength. (2)

Radio, infrared, X-rays (1). Radio waves have the longest wavelength (1).
:::

::: example Why microwaves cook food
Explain why microwaves are used in ovens and why an oven's door has a metal mesh. (3)

Microwaves are absorbed by water molecules in food, which heats the food from within (1). The same absorption would heat water in body tissue, so leaking microwaves are dangerous (1); the metal mesh reflects microwaves and keeps them inside the oven while letting shorter-wavelength visible light out so you can see in (1).
:::

::: example Dangers of ultraviolet
Describe the risks of over-exposure to ultraviolet radiation and one precaution. (3)

UV can damage DNA in skin cells, causing sunburn and skin cancer (1), and can damage the eyes, causing cataracts (1); precautions include sunscreen, covering up, sunglasses or avoiding midday sun (1).
:::

::: example Medical X-rays
Explain why X-rays are useful for imaging a broken bone and why the radiographer stands behind a lead screen. (3)

X-rays pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by dense bone, so bones show as shadows on the detector (1). X-rays are ionising and can damage or mutate cells (1); the radiographer takes many images a day, so the lead absorbs the X-rays and keeps their cumulative dose low (1).
:::

::: watch
- The order is asked in **both directions** — know whether the question wants increasing frequency or increasing wavelength.
- All EM waves share the **same speed in a vacuum**; "light is faster than radio" is a common wrong idea.
- Match the **harm to the mechanism**: microwaves and IR **heat**; UV damages skin cell DNA; X/gamma **ionise** → mutation → cancer.
- Visible light comes **between** infrared and ultraviolet — placing it at the end is a frequent slip.
- One use per region is expected; be specific ("thermal imaging cameras", not "cameras").
:::
