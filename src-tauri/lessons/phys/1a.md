## What it is

Motion is described with a small set of quantities, each with its own **SI unit**: distance in **metres (m)**, time in **seconds (s)**, speed and velocity in **metres per second (m/s)**, acceleration in **metres per second squared (m/s²)**, mass in **kilograms (kg)**, force in **newtons (N)** and gravitational field strength in **newtons per kilogram (N/kg)**. Physics-only quantities add **moment** in **newton metres (N m)** and **momentum** in **kilogram metres per second (kg m/s)**. Get the units right and half the marks in a calculation are already safe.

**Distance-time graphs** plot distance travelled (vertical) against time (horizontal).

- The **gradient** is the **speed**: steep = fast, horizontal = stationary.
- A **straight line** means constant speed; a **curve** means the speed is changing (getting steeper = accelerating).
- Speed over a section = rise ÷ run = distance ÷ time.

**Velocity-time graphs** plot velocity (vertical) against time (horizontal) and carry more information.

- The **gradient** is the **acceleration**: upward slope = speeding up, downward slope = slowing down (deceleration), horizontal = constant velocity.
- The **area under the graph** is the **distance travelled**. Split the shape into rectangles and triangles, work out each area and add them.
- A line **below** the time axis means velocity in the opposite direction.

**Practical: the motion of everyday objects.** Measure distance with a tape or metre rule and time with a stopwatch, or use **light gates** connected to a data logger (a card of known length breaks the beam; speed = card length ÷ time). Light gates remove **reaction-time error**, the main weakness of a hand-held stopwatch. Repeat and take a mean; a **ticker-timer** or video with a known frame rate also works.

::: terms
- **Distance**: how far an object moves, in metres (m); a scalar.
- **Displacement**: distance moved in a particular direction; a vector.
- **Speed**: distance travelled per unit time, in m/s; a scalar.
- **Velocity**: speed in a particular direction, in m/s; a vector.
- **Acceleration**: the rate of change of velocity, in m/s².
- **Distance-time graph**: its gradient gives the speed; a horizontal line means the object is stationary.
- **Velocity-time graph**: its gradient gives the acceleration, and the area under it gives the distance travelled.
:::

::: method
1. **Read the axes first.** Distance-time or velocity-time? The same shape means different things on each.
2. **Gradient** = change in the vertical quantity ÷ change in time. Choose two far-apart points on a straight section for accuracy.
3. **Area under a velocity-time graph** = distance. Cut into rectangles (width × height) and triangles (½ × base × height); add them.
4. Show the calculation for each piece, then the total, then the **unit**.
5. Describe motion in words using the right vocabulary: *stationary, constant velocity, accelerating uniformly, decelerating*.
:::

::: example Speed from a distance-time graph
A cyclist's distance-time graph is a straight line from (0 s, 0 m) to (25 s, 200 m), then horizontal until 40 s. Describe the motion and find the speed of the first section. (3)

Gradient = 200 ÷ 25 = **8 m/s** (1). For the first 25 s the cyclist travels at a constant speed of 8 m/s (1); from 25 s to 40 s the line is horizontal, so the cyclist is stationary (1).
:::

::: example Acceleration and distance from a velocity-time graph
A car's velocity rises uniformly from 0 to 24 m/s in 8 s, stays at 24 m/s for 12 s, then falls to rest in 4 s. Find the initial acceleration and the total distance. (4)

Acceleration = gradient = (24 − 0) ÷ 8 = **3 m/s²** (1). Area: triangle ½ × 8 × 24 = 96 m; rectangle 12 × 24 = 288 m; triangle ½ × 4 × 24 = 48 m (1 for the pieces). Total = 96 + 288 + 48 = **432 m** (1), unit m (1).
:::

::: example Choosing the right units
A student writes "the acceleration was 4 m/s". Explain the error and state the correct unit. Give the units of momentum and of a moment. (3)

m/s is a unit of speed; acceleration is change in velocity per second so its unit is **m/s²** (1). Momentum (mass × velocity): **kg m/s** (1). Moment (force × distance): **N m** (1).
:::

::: q
What does the gradient of a distance-time graph represent? (1)
---
Speed.
:::

::: q
What do (a) the gradient and (b) the area under a velocity-time graph represent? (2)
---
(a) Acceleration (1); (b) distance travelled (1).
:::

::: q
Describe the motion shown by a horizontal line on a velocity-time graph at 5 m/s. (1)
---
Constant velocity of 5 m/s (no acceleration).
:::

::: q
A velocity-time graph slopes downward from 30 m/s to 0 in 6 s. Calculate the acceleration. (2)
---
a = (0 − 30) ÷ 6 (1) = −5 m/s², i.e. a deceleration of 5 m/s² (1).
:::

::: q
A train accelerates uniformly from rest to 40 m/s in 20 s. Use the area under its velocity-time graph to find the distance travelled. (2)
---
Distance = ½ × 20 × 40 (1) = 400 m (1).
:::

::: q
Give the SI units of mass, force and gravitational field strength. (3)
---
kg (1); N (1); N/kg (1).
:::

::: q
On a distance-time graph, what does a curve of increasing steepness show? (1)
---
The object is accelerating (speed increasing).
:::

::: q
Explain why light gates give a more reliable speed than a stopwatch. (2)
---
Timing starts and stops automatically when the beam is broken (1), removing human reaction-time error (1).
:::

::: q
A velocity-time graph goes below the time axis. What does this mean? (1)
---
The object is moving in the opposite direction (negative velocity).
:::

::: q
An object's distance-time graph is a straight line from (0, 0) to (12 s, 60 m). A second object's is from (0, 0) to (12 s, 30 m). Compare their motion. (2)
---
Both move at constant speed (1); the first is twice as fast, 5 m/s compared with 2.5 m/s (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Mixing up the two graphs** is the classic error: a horizontal line is *stationary* on a distance-time graph but *constant velocity* on a velocity-time graph.
- Area under a v-t graph: **split into shapes and show each area** — a bare final number loses working marks if it is wrong.
- Units: acceleration is **m/s²**, never m/s. Momentum kg m/s, moment N m.
- "Describe the motion" wants words like *uniform acceleration*, *constant speed*, *decelerating* with values, not "it goes up".
- Take gradients from **large, clearly readable** triangles on the line, not two adjacent points.
:::

::: test
A cyclist's distance-time graph is a straight line from (0 s, 0 m) to (40 s, 240 m), then horizontal until 60 s. Describe the motion and calculate the speed in the first part. (3)
---
Constant speed for 40 s, then stationary for 20 s (1). Speed $= 240 \div 40$ (1) $= 6$ m/s (1).
:::

::: test
A velocity-time graph rises in a straight line from 0 to 24 m/s in 8 s, then stays at 24 m/s for 12 s. Calculate the acceleration in the first part and the total distance travelled. (4)
---
$a = 24 \div 8 = 3$ m/s$^2$ (1). Distance $=$ area: triangle $\frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 24 = 96$ m (1) plus rectangle $12 \times 24 = 288$ m (1); total 384 m (1).
:::

::: test
State what the gradient of a velocity-time graph represents, and what a negative gradient means. (2)
---
Acceleration (1); a negative gradient means deceleration — the object is slowing down (1).
:::

::: test
Give the SI units of (a) velocity, (b) acceleration and (c) time. (3)
---
(a) m/s (1); (b) m/s$^2$ (1); (c) s (1).
:::

::: test
Describe how you would measure the average speed of a trolley rolling down a ramp using a stopwatch and a metre rule, and give one way to improve the accuracy. (3)
---
Measure the distance along the ramp with the rule; time the trolley from release to the bottom with the stopwatch (1); speed $=$ distance $\div$ time (1). Improve: repeat and average, or use light gates to remove reaction-time error (1).
:::

::: test
A distance-time graph is a curve that gets less steep and finally becomes horizontal. Describe the motion. (2)
---
The object is slowing down (decelerating) (1) and then stops (1).
:::

::: test
A car's velocity-time graph shows a straight line from 30 m/s down to 0 in 5 s. Calculate its deceleration and the distance it travels while stopping. (3)
---
Deceleration $= 30 \div 5 = 6$ m/s$^2$ (1); distance $= \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 30$ (1) $= 75$ m (1).
:::

::: test
Explain the difference between a distance-time graph and a velocity-time graph that are both horizontal lines above the axis. (2)
---
Horizontal distance-time: stationary — distance is not changing (1). Horizontal velocity-time: moving at constant velocity — the velocity is not changing (1).
:::

::: test
The area under a velocity-time graph gives: A — acceleration; B — distance travelled; C — speed; D — force. (1)
---
B — the distance travelled.
:::

::: test
A horizontal line on a distance-time graph shows the object is: A — accelerating; B — moving at constant speed; C — stationary; D — decelerating. (1)
---
C — stationary.
:::

::: test
The unit of acceleration is: A — m/s; B — m/s²; C — N; D — m. (1)
---
B — m/s².
:::

::: test
A velocity-time graph shows a car accelerating uniformly from 0 to 20 m/s in 10 s. Calculate its acceleration and the distance it travels in this time. (4)
---
Acceleration = 20 ÷ 10 = 2 m/s² (2). Distance = area of triangle = ½ × 10 × 20 = 100 m (2).
:::

::: test
Explain how you can tell from a distance-time graph whether an object is moving faster in one part of its journey than another. (2)
---
The gradient of a distance-time graph gives the speed (1); the steeper the line, the faster the object is moving (1).
:::

::: test
A runner's velocity-time graph is curved, getting less steep until it becomes horizontal. Describe the runner's motion. (2)
---
The runner accelerates, but the acceleration decreases (1), until they reach a constant (maximum) velocity (1).
:::

::: test
Describe how to investigate how the speed of a trolley at the bottom of a ramp depends on the height of the ramp. (6)
---
Any six: set up a ramp at a measured height; release the trolley from the same point each time; measure the speed at the bottom using a light gate (length of card ÷ time) or measure distance and time with a stopwatch; repeat each height three times and take a mean; change the height in equal steps; keep the trolley mass and ramp surface the same; plot a graph of speed against height; describe the pattern.
Marking: 1 mark for each correct point, up to 6; for full marks the answer must be in a logical order.
:::
