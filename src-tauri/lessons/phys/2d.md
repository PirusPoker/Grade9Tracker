## What it is

When a current flows through a resistor, the moving electrons **collide with the ions** of the metal lattice, transferring energy that makes the ions vibrate more — the resistor **heats up**. This is how electric heaters, kettles, toasters and filament lamps work, and why wires and components must be rated for the current they carry.

**Power** is the rate of energy transfer:

$$P = I \times V \qquad \text{power (W) = current (A) × voltage (V)}$$

and the **energy transferred** in a time *t* is

$$E = I \times V \times t \qquad \text{energy (J) = current (A) × voltage (V) × time (s)}$$

(equivalently $E = P \times t$). Appliances are labelled with their power rating: a 2 kW kettle transfers 2000 J every second. Combined with V = IR you can also write $P = I^2R$ and $P = V^2/R$.

**Mains electricity** in the UK is **a.c. (alternating current)** at about **230 V and 50 Hz**: the current reverses direction 100 times a second (50 complete cycles) because the voltage alternates. A **battery** gives **d.c. (direct current)**: constant voltage, current in one direction only. On an oscilloscope a.c. is a wave; d.c. is a flat line.

**Safety.** Mains voltages are dangerous, so appliances and wiring use several layers of protection:

- **Insulation** — plastic coating on wires and plastic casings keep users from touching live parts.
- **Double insulation** — appliances with an all-plastic case (hairdryers, phone chargers) have no metal parts that could become live and need no earth wire; symbol: a square inside a square.
- **Earthing** — metal-cased appliances have an **earth wire** (green/yellow) connected to the case. If a fault makes the case live, a large current flows straight to earth through the low-resistance earth wire, blowing the fuse and stopping the user getting a shock.
- **Fuses** — a thin wire in the **live** side that **melts** if the current exceeds its rating, breaking the circuit. Choose a rating just above the appliance's normal current (3 A, 5 A or 13 A).
- **Circuit breakers** — electromagnetic switches that trip when the current is too high; faster than a fuse and can be reset. **RCDs** (residual current devices) detect a difference between live and neutral current and cut off in milliseconds — they protect people, not just wiring.

The three-pin plug: **live** (brown) carries the alternating voltage, **neutral** (blue) completes the circuit at about 0 V, **earth** (green/yellow) is the safety wire; the fuse sits in the live wire so that when it blows the appliance is disconnected from the live supply.

::: terms
- **Insulation**: a plastic coating on wires that stops people touching live conductors.
- **Double insulation**: an appliance with a plastic case, so no earth wire is needed.
- **Earth wire**: a wire connecting a metal case to earth, so a fault current flows to earth instead of through a person.
- **Fuse**: a thin wire that melts and breaks the circuit if the current is too large.
- **Circuit breaker**: an automatic switch that breaks the circuit when the current is too large, and can be reset.
- **Electrical power**: P = I × V.
- **Energy transferred**: E = I × V × t.
- **Alternating current (a.c.)**: current that repeatedly changes direction, like the mains supply.
- **Direct current (d.c.)**: current that flows in one direction only, like the supply from a battery.
- **Mains supply**: in the UK, 230 V a.c. at 50 Hz.
:::

::: method
1. **P = IV**, **E = IVt**, plus V = IR. Rearrange for the unknown: $I = P/V$ is the first step of most fuse questions.
2. Convert: kW × 1000 → W; minutes × 60 → s; kJ ÷ 1000 → J.
3. **Fuse rating**: find the normal current with $I = P/V$, then choose the next standard fuse **above** it.
4. Earthing explanation must include: fault → case live → large current through earth wire → fuse melts → circuit broken → user safe.
5. a.c. vs d.c.: a.c. changes direction (and the voltage alternates); d.c. flows one way. Quote 230 V, 50 Hz for UK mains.
:::

::: example Power and fuse choice
A kettle is rated 2300 W at 230 V. Calculate the current it draws and choose a suitable fuse from 3 A, 5 A and 13 A. (3)

$I = P/V = 2300 \div 230 = 10$ A (2). The fuse must be just above the normal current, so **13 A** (1).
:::

::: example Energy transferred
An electric heater draws 8 A from the 230 V mains for 30 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred. (3)

$t = 30 \times 60 = 1800$ s (1). $E = IVt = 8 \times 230 \times 1800$ (1) = **3 312 000 J** (3.3 MJ) (1).
:::

::: example Why a resistor gets hot
Explain why a current in a resistor causes it to heat up. (3)

Electrons moving through the resistor collide with the metal ions of the lattice (1), transferring energy to them so the ions vibrate more (1); the increased vibration is a rise in temperature (thermal energy) (1).
:::

::: example Earthing a metal-cased appliance
A washing machine has a metal case and an earth wire. Explain how the earth wire and fuse protect a user if the live wire touches the case. (4)

The case becomes live (1); the earth wire provides a low-resistance path to earth so a very large current flows (1); this current melts the fuse (1), breaking the circuit and isolating the appliance so the user cannot receive a shock (1).
:::

::: example Compare — a.c. and d.c.
Explain the difference between the current from a battery and the current from the mains. (3)

A battery gives direct current (d.c.), which flows in one direction only, with a constant voltage (1).

The mains gives alternating current (a.c.), which repeatedly reverses direction (1) — in the UK 50 times a second (50 Hz), with a voltage of about 230 V (1).
:::

::: watch
- Fuse rating is the standard value **just above** the normal current — never below it, and not "the biggest".
- In E = IVt the time is in **seconds**; "30 minutes" straight in gives an answer 60× too small.
- The earthing answer is a **chain**: live case → large current to earth → fuse melts → circuit broken. Missing links lose marks.
- The fuse goes in the **live** wire so the appliance is fully disconnected from the supply when it blows.
- Double insulation → **no earth wire needed**; the symbol is a square within a square.
:::
