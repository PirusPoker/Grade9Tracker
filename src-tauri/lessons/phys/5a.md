## What it is

Units for this section: temperature in **°C** or **kelvin (K)**, energy J, mass kg, density **kg/m³**, length m, area m², volume m³, acceleration m/s², force N, pressure **pascals (Pa)**.

**Density** is mass per unit volume:

$$\rho = \frac{m}{V} \qquad \text{density (kg/m³) = mass (kg) ÷ volume (m³)}$$

Water is 1000 kg/m³ (= 1 g/cm³); anything denser sinks in it, anything less dense floats. Gases have tiny densities (air ≈ 1.2 kg/m³) because their particles are far apart. Converting: 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³; 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³.

**Practical: measuring density.** For a **regular solid**, measure its dimensions with a ruler or vernier callipers and calculate the volume; find its mass on a balance. For an **irregular solid**, find the mass, then lower it into a **measuring cylinder** part-filled with water — the rise in level is its volume (or use a **displacement (eureka) can** filled to the spout and collect the overflow). For a **liquid**, weigh an empty measuring cylinder, add a measured volume, reweigh; density = mass of liquid ÷ volume. Read the meniscus at eye level; repeat and average; a larger sample reduces the percentage error.

**Pressure** is force per unit area:

$$p = \frac{F}{A} \qquad \text{pressure (Pa) = force (N) ÷ area (m²)}$$

One pascal is one newton per square metre. The same force on a smaller area gives a higher pressure — a drawing pin, a knife edge, stiletto heels; a larger area lowers it — snowshoes, wide tyres, foundations.

**Pressure in fluids.** A liquid or gas exerts pressure on **every surface it touches**, and at any point the pressure **acts equally in all directions** — that is why a submarine is squeezed from every side and a balloon inflates uniformly. Pressure in a fluid **increases with depth** because there is more fluid above pressing down:

$$\Delta p = h \times \rho \times g \qquad \text{pressure difference (Pa) = height (m) × density (kg/m³) × g (N/kg)}$$

Dam walls are thicker at the bottom; a diver 10 m down experiences an extra 100 000 Pa (1 atmosphere) from the water; atmospheric pressure at sea level is about 100 000 Pa (100 kPa) and falls with altitude.

::: terms
- **Density**: mass per unit volume: ρ = m ÷ V, in kg/m³ or g/cm³.
- **Pressure**: force per unit area: p = F ÷ A, in pascals (Pa); 1 Pa = 1 N/m².
- **Pressure in a fluid**: acts equally in all directions at a given depth.
- **Pressure difference in a fluid**: pressure difference = height × density × g.
- **Displacement method**: measuring the volume of an irregular object by the volume of water it pushes aside.
:::

::: method
1. **Density**: convert to kg and m³ *or* work consistently in g and cm³ and convert the answer (× 1000 for g/cm³ → kg/m³).
2. Volume of a cuboid = l × w × h; of a liquid or irregular solid from the measuring-cylinder rise.
3. **Pressure**: p = F/A with area in **m²** (cm² ÷ 10 000). Weight supplies the force for objects resting on a surface. Several feet/legs share the load — use the total contact area.
4. **Depth**: Δp = hρg with g = 10 N/kg; add atmospheric pressure (100 kPa) if the *total* pressure is asked.
5. "Explain" questions: link area to pressure (smaller area → larger pressure) and depth to weight of fluid above.
:::

::: example Density of a metal block
A metal block measures 5 cm × 4 cm × 2 cm and has a mass of 356 g. Calculate its density in g/cm³ and kg/m³. (3)

Volume = 5 × 4 × 2 = 40 cm³ (1). Density = 356 ÷ 40 = **8.9 g/cm³** (1) = **8900 kg/m³** (1).
:::

::: example Density of an irregular object
A stone of mass 54 g is placed in a measuring cylinder containing 40 cm³ of water; the level rises to 60 cm³. Find the stone's density. (3)

Volume = 60 − 40 = 20 cm³ (1). Density = 54 ÷ 20 (1) = **2.7 g/cm³** (2700 kg/m³) (1).
:::

::: example Pressure from a person
A 60 kg person stands on both feet; each shoe has a contact area of 150 cm². Calculate the pressure on the floor, and explain why it doubles if she stands on one foot. (4)

Weight = 600 N (1). Area = 2 × 150 = 300 cm² = 0.03 m² (1). p = 600 ÷ 0.03 = **20 000 Pa** (1). On one foot the same force acts on half the area, so the pressure doubles to 40 000 Pa (1).
:::

::: example Pressure under water
Calculate the pressure due to the water at a depth of 25 m in the sea (density 1030 kg/m³), and the total pressure if atmospheric pressure is 100 kPa. (3)

Δp = 25 × 1030 × 10 (1) = **257 500 Pa** (258 kPa) (1). Total = 258 + 100 = **358 kPa** (1).
:::

::: q
Write the equation for density and give its unit. (2)
---
Density = mass ÷ volume (1); kg/m³ (or g/cm³) (1).
:::

::: q
2.5 m³ of oil has a mass of 2000 kg. Calculate its density. (2)
---
2000 ÷ 2.5 (1) = 800 kg/m³ (1).
:::

::: q
Describe how to find the density of an irregular stone. (3)
---
Measure its mass on a balance (1); lower it into a measuring cylinder of water and record the rise in volume (1); density = mass ÷ volume (1).
:::

::: q
Why does ice float on water? (1)
---
Ice is less dense than liquid water.
:::

::: q
Write the equation for pressure and give its unit. (2)
---
Pressure = force ÷ area (1); pascal (N/m²) (1).
:::

::: q
A box of weight 400 N rests on an area of 0.5 m². Calculate the pressure. (2)
---
400 ÷ 0.5 (1) = 800 Pa (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a sharp knife cuts more easily than a blunt one. (2)
---
The sharp edge has a much smaller area (1), so the same force produces a much larger pressure (1).
:::

::: q
State how pressure in a liquid changes with depth, and why. (2)
---
It increases with depth (1) because there is a greater weight of liquid above pressing down (1).
:::

::: q
Calculate the pressure difference between the surface and the bottom of a 3 m deep swimming pool (water 1000 kg/m³). (2)
---
3 × 1000 × 10 (1) = 30 000 Pa (1).
:::

::: q
Why are dam walls built thicker at the bottom? (1)
---
Water pressure is greatest at the bottom, so the wall must withstand a larger force there.
:::

::: watch
- **Area in m²**: 150 cm² is 0.015 m². Dividing by cm² gives an answer 10 000× too small.
- Density unit conversion: g/cm³ **× 1000** → kg/m³.
- Force on a surface from a resting object is its **weight (mg)**, not its mass.
- Δp = hρg is the **extra** pressure due to the fluid; add atmospheric pressure only if the total is asked.
- Pressure in a fluid acts **in all directions**, not just downward.
:::

::: test

::: test

---

::: test

---
$h = p \div (\rho g) = 300\,000 \div 10\,300$ (1) $= 29.1$ m (1).
:::

::: test
Describe how to find the density of an irregular object, such as a small stone, and how to make the result as accurate as possible. (6)
---
Any six: measure the mass with a top-pan balance; partly fill a measuring cylinder (or use a eureka / displacement can) with water; read the starting volume at eye level from the bottom of the meniscus; lower the stone in on a thread so no water splashes out; read the new volume; volume of stone = difference; density = mass ÷ volume; repeat and take a mean; dry the stone before re-weighing; use a narrow cylinder with fine scale for better resolution.
Marking: 1 mark for each correct point, up to 6; for full marks the answer must be in a logical order.
:::
