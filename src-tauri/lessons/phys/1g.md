## What it is

**Momentum** is a measure of how hard it is to stop a moving object:

$$p = m \times v \qquad \text{momentum (kg m/s) = mass (kg) × velocity (m/s)}$$

It is a **vector**: it has the direction of the velocity. Choose one direction as positive and give the opposite direction a negative sign — this is essential in collisions.

**Conservation of momentum.** In any collision or explosion where no external force acts, the **total momentum before = total momentum after**. Two trolleys that stick together, a gun recoiling from a bullet, a rocket pushing out exhaust — in every case the momentum lost by one object is gained by the other. To use it: total *mv* before (with signs) = total *mv* after (with signs), then solve for the unknown velocity.

**Force and momentum.** Newton's second law in its more general form says a force changes momentum:

$$F = \frac{\text{change in momentum}}{\text{time}} = \frac{mv - mu}{t}$$

This is F = ma in disguise, since $(v-u)/t = a$. It explains **safety features**: for a given change in momentum (a passenger going from 15 m/s to rest), **increasing the time** over which it happens **reduces the force**. Crumple zones, airbags, seat belts that stretch slightly, cycle helmets, crash mats and bending your knees on landing all work by making the stop take longer.

**Newton's third law**: when object A exerts a force on object B, B exerts an **equal and opposite** force on A. The two forces are the same type, act on **different objects**, and exist for exactly as long as each other. A rocket pushes exhaust gas backward; the gas pushes the rocket forward. A swimmer pushes the water back; the water pushes her forward. Weight and the normal contact force on a resting book are *not* a third-law pair — both act on the book — the partner of the book's weight is the book pulling the Earth upward.

::: terms
- **Momentum**: mass × velocity, in kg m/s; a vector.
- **Conservation of momentum**: in a collision or explosion, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after, if no external forces act.
- **Force and momentum**: force = change in momentum ÷ time taken.
- **Safety features**: crumple zones, seat belts and airbags increase the time taken to stop, reducing the rate of change of momentum and so the force.
- **Newton's third law**: when body A exerts a force on body B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A.
:::

::: method
1. **Set a positive direction** (say, to the right). Any velocity the other way is negative.
2. **Momentum**: p = mv for each object; total before = total after. Solve; a negative answer means "moving in the negative direction".
3. **Force from momentum change**: F = (mv − mu) ÷ t. Keep the sign of the change; the size is what's usually asked.
4. **Safety features**: same momentum change, **longer time**, so **smaller force** — say all three parts.
5. **Third law pairs**: same size, opposite direction, same type of force, acting on **two different objects**. Check all four.
:::

::: example Momentum calculation
Calculate the momentum of a 1500 kg car travelling at 20 m/s, and of a 0.045 kg golf ball at 60 m/s. (2)

Car: 1500 × 20 = **30 000 kg m/s** (1). Ball: 0.045 × 60 = **2.7 kg m/s** (1).
:::

::: example Collision — sticking together
A 2 kg trolley moving at 3 m/s collides with a stationary 1 kg trolley and they stick together. Find their common velocity. (3)

Momentum before = 2 × 3 + 1 × 0 = 6 kg m/s (1). After: (2 + 1) × v = 3v (1). 3v = 6, so v = **2 m/s** in the original direction (1).
:::

::: example Explosion — recoil
A 4.0 kg rifle fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Calculate the recoil velocity of the rifle. (3)

Momentum before = 0 (1). After: 0.010 × 400 + 4.0 × v = 0 (1), so 4v = −4 and v = **−1 m/s**: the rifle recoils at 1 m/s in the opposite direction to the bullet (1).
:::

::: example Force during a crash
A 60 kg passenger travelling at 12 m/s is brought to rest by a seat belt in 0.4 s. Calculate the average force on the passenger, and explain why the force would be much larger without the belt. (4)

Change in momentum = 60 × 12 − 0 = 720 kg m/s (1). F = 720 ÷ 0.4 (1) = **1800 N** (1). Without the belt the passenger would hit the dashboard and stop in a much shorter time, so for the same momentum change the force would be far greater (1).
:::

::: example Explain — Newton's third law
A swimmer pushes backwards on the water with her hands. Use Newton's third law to explain why she moves forwards. (3)

Her hands exert a backward force on the water (1). By Newton's third law, the water exerts a force of equal size on her hands in the opposite direction — forwards (1). This forward force on her is the resultant that accelerates her forwards; the two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel (1).
:::

Write the equation for momentum and give its unit. (2)
---
p = mv (1); kg m/s (1).
:::

::: q
Calculate the momentum of a 70 kg runner moving at 8 m/s. (2)
---
70 × 8 (1) = 560 kg m/s (1).
:::

::: q
State the principle of conservation of momentum. (2)
---
In a collision (or explosion) with no external forces (1), the total momentum before equals the total momentum after (1).
:::

::: q
A 0.5 kg ball moving at 4 m/s hits a stationary 1.5 kg ball and stops. Find the velocity of the 1.5 kg ball. (3)
---
Before: 0.5 × 4 = 2 kg m/s (1); after: 1.5 × v = 2 (1); v = 1.33 m/s (1).
:::

::: q
Two ice skaters, 50 kg and 70 kg, push apart from rest. The 50 kg skater moves off at 1.4 m/s. Find the velocity of the other. (3)
---
0 = 50 × 1.4 + 70 × v (1); 70v = −70 (1); v = −1 m/s, i.e. 1 m/s in the opposite direction (1).
:::

::: q
Write the equation linking force, change in momentum and time. (1)
---
F = (mv − mu) ÷ t.
:::

::: q
A 0.16 kg cricket ball moving at 30 m/s is caught and stopped in 0.1 s. Find the average force on the hands. (3)
---
Change in momentum = 0.16 × 30 = 4.8 kg m/s (1); F = 4.8 ÷ 0.1 (1) = 48 N (1).
:::

::: q
Explain how a crumple zone reduces injury in a crash. (3)
---
It increases the time taken for the car (and occupants) to stop (1); the change in momentum is the same (1), so the force exerted is smaller (1).
:::

::: q
State Newton's third law. (2)
---
When one object exerts a force on a second (1), the second exerts an equal and opposite force of the same type on the first (1).
:::

::: q
A rocket takes off. Identify the third-law pair of forces involved. (2)
---
The rocket pushes the exhaust gases downward (1); the gases push the rocket upward with an equal force (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Signs.** Opposite directions get opposite signs; forgetting this turns an explosion answer into nonsense.
- Momentum before an explosion or when something is "at rest" is **zero** — write it down.
- Safety features: the answer must contain **longer time → smaller force** *and* "for the same change in momentum". One phrase alone is a partial answer.
- Third-law pairs act on **different objects**. Weight and normal reaction on the same book are *not* a pair.
- The unit is **kg m/s**; "kg/m/s" and "kg m/s²" are both wrong.
:::

::: test

---
It increases the time taken for the momentum to change (1); the same change in momentum over a longer time means a smaller force ($F = \Delta p / t$) (1).
