## What it is

**Speed** is how fast something moves; **velocity** is speed **in a stated direction**. Two cars doing 30 m/s in opposite directions have the same speed but different velocities. **Average speed** over a journey is

$$\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{distance moved}}{\text{time taken}} \qquad v = \frac{s}{t}$$

with distance in m, time in s, speed in m/s. Typical values worth knowing: walking ≈ 1.5 m/s, sprinting ≈ 10 m/s, a car on a motorway ≈ 30 m/s, sound in air ≈ 340 m/s, light 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

**Acceleration** is the rate at which velocity changes:

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} \qquad \text{(change in velocity ÷ time taken)}$$

where *u* is the initial velocity, *v* the final velocity, *t* the time, and *a* is in **m/s²**. An object slowing down has a **negative** acceleration (a deceleration). Because velocity has direction, an object moving at constant speed round a bend is still accelerating — its direction is changing.

When the acceleration is **uniform** (constant) and you know distances rather than times, use

$$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$

(final velocity squared = initial velocity squared + 2 × acceleration × distance). It links four quantities with no time in it, which is exactly when the *t* is unknown — e.g. a car braking over a measured distance, or an object falling a given height where *a* = *g* = 10 m/s².

::: terms
- **Average speed**: total distance travelled ÷ total time taken.
- **Acceleration**: change in velocity ÷ time taken: a = (v − u) ÷ t.
- **Deceleration**: a negative acceleration — slowing down.
- **Initial velocity (u)**: the velocity at the start of the motion.
- **Final velocity (v)**: the velocity at the end of the motion.
- **Equation of motion**: v² = u² + 2as, linking final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration and distance.
:::

::: method
1. Write down what you know using the symbols *u, v, a, s, t*; identify what is asked.
2. **Speed/distance/time**: $v = s/t$, rearranged as needed. Convert km to m and minutes or hours to seconds *before* substituting.
3. **Acceleration from velocities and time**: $a = (v-u)/t$. Keep the sign — slowing down gives a negative answer; say "deceleration of…" in words.
4. **No time given** (or not needed) → $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$. Substitute, then square-root at the end. Starting from rest: *u* = 0.
5. State the unit and, where direction matters, the direction.
:::

::: example Average speed with a unit conversion
A train covers 180 km in 1 hour 15 minutes. Calculate its average speed in m/s. (3)

Distance = 180 000 m; time = 75 × 60 = 4500 s (1). Speed = 180 000 ÷ 4500 (1) = **40 m/s** (1).
:::

::: example Acceleration
A cyclist travelling at 4 m/s speeds up to 10 m/s in 5 s. Later she brakes from 10 m/s to rest in 2.5 s. Calculate both accelerations. (4)

Speeding up: $a = (10 - 4) \div 5 = 6 \div 5 =$ **1.2 m/s²** (2). Braking: $a = (0 - 10) \div 2.5 =$ **−4 m/s²**, a deceleration of 4 m/s² (2).
:::

::: example Using v² = u² + 2as
A stone is dropped from a bridge 45 m above a river. Taking g = 10 m/s², find the speed at which it hits the water. (3)

*u* = 0, *a* = 10 m/s², *s* = 45 m. $v^2 = 0 + 2 \times 10 \times 45 = 900$ (2). $v = \sqrt{900} =$ **30 m/s** (1).
:::

::: example Braking distance from v² = u² + 2as
A car travelling at 20 m/s brakes with a deceleration of 5 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping? (3)

*v* = 0, *u* = 20, *a* = −5. $0 = 20^2 + 2 \times (-5) \times s$ (1) so $0 = 400 - 10s$ (1), giving $s = 400 \div 10 =$ **40 m** (1).
:::

::: q
State the difference between speed and velocity. (1)
---
Velocity has a direction as well as a size (it is a vector); speed is size only.
:::

::: q
A runner completes 400 m in 50 s. Calculate her average speed. (2)
---
400 ÷ 50 (1) = 8 m/s (1).
:::

::: q
How far does light travel in 2.0 ms? (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s) (2)
---
s = 3 × 10⁸ × 0.002 (1) = 6 × 10⁵ m (600 km) (1).
:::

::: q
Define acceleration and give its unit. (2)
---
The change in velocity per unit time (rate of change of velocity) (1); m/s² (1).
:::

::: q
A car goes from 15 m/s to 27 m/s in 4 s. Calculate its acceleration. (2)
---
(27 − 15) ÷ 4 (1) = 3 m/s² (1).
:::

::: q
A ball rolling at 6 m/s slows uniformly to 2 m/s in 8 s. Calculate its acceleration and state what the sign means. (3)
---
(2 − 6) ÷ 8 (1) = −0.5 m/s² (1); negative means it is decelerating (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a car travelling at a steady 20 m/s around a roundabout is accelerating. (2)
---
Its direction is changing (1), so its velocity is changing even though its speed is constant (1).
:::

::: q
A sprinter accelerates from rest at 3 m/s² over 24 m. Find her final speed. (3)
---
v² = 0 + 2 × 3 × 24 = 144 (2); v = 12 m/s (1).
:::

::: q
A lorry moving at 12 m/s accelerates at 0.5 m/s² until it reaches 18 m/s. What distance does it cover while accelerating? (3)
---
18² = 12² + 2 × 0.5 × s → 324 = 144 + s (2); s = 180 m (1).
:::

::: q
An object is thrown upward at 20 m/s. Using g = 10 m/s², how high does it rise? (3)
---
At the top v = 0: 0 = 20² + 2 × (−10) × s (1) → 400 = 20s (1) → s = 20 m (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Convert units first**: km → m (× 1000), minutes → s (× 60), hours → s (× 3600). Most lost marks here are unit slips, not physics.
- $a = (v - u)/t$: subtract **final minus initial**. Slowing down must come out negative.
- $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ gives **v²** — remember to square-root, and when the object is *decelerating* put *a* in as negative.
- "From rest" means *u* = 0; "comes to rest" means *v* = 0.
- Give **three significant figures** unless the data justifies fewer, and always the unit.
:::

::: test
A car travels 3.6 km in 4 minutes. Calculate its average speed in m/s. (2)
---
$3600$ m in $240$ s (1); $15$ m/s (1).
:::

::: test
Sound travels at 340 m/s. How long does thunder take to reach you from a lightning strike 5.1 km away? (2)
---
$t = d \div v = 5100 \div 340$ (1) $= 15$ s (1).
:::

::: test
A motorbike accelerates from 8 m/s to 26 m/s in 6 s. Calculate its acceleration. (2)
---
$a = (26 - 8) \div 6$ (1) $= 3$ m/s$^2$ (1).
:::

::: test
A train decelerates at 0.8 m/s$^2$ from 32 m/s to 12 m/s. How long does this take? (2)
---
$t = \Delta v \div a = 20 \div 0.8$ (1) $= 25$ s (1).
:::

::: test
A stone is dropped from rest and falls for 3 s. Ignoring air resistance, calculate its speed and the distance fallen. ($g = 10$ m/s$^2$) (3)
---
$v = at = 30$ m/s (1); $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$: $900 = 20s$ (1), $s = 45$ m (1).
:::

::: test
A car accelerates uniformly from 10 m/s to 30 m/s over a distance of 200 m. Calculate its acceleration. (3)
---
$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$: $900 = 100 + 400a$ (1); $400a = 800$ (1); $a = 2$ m/s$^2$ (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why a satellite in a circular orbit at constant speed is accelerating. (2)
---
Velocity is a vector, and its direction is constantly changing (1); a change in velocity is an acceleration, even at constant speed (1).
:::

::: test
A ball is thrown vertically upward at 15 m/s. Calculate the maximum height it reaches and the time taken to get there. ($g = 10$ m/s$^2$) (3)
---
At the top $v = 0$: $0 = 15^2 - 2 \times 10 \times s$, $s = 11.25$ m (2); $t = 15 \div 10 = 1.5$ s (1).
:::
