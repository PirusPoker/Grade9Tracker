## What it is

**Energy** is measured in **joules (J)**; **power**, the rate of energy transfer, in **watts (W)**. The other units in this section are the mechanics ones: mass kg, distance m, speed m/s, force N, time s.

Energy is described in terms of **stores** and **transfers**. An object or system holds energy in one or more stores:

- **Kinetic** — anything moving.
- **Gravitational potential** — anything raised above the ground.
- **Elastic potential** — a stretched or squashed spring, band or bow.
- **Thermal** — the vibration/movement of particles; hotter means more.
- **Chemical** — fuels, food, batteries; released in reactions.
- **Nuclear** — inside atomic nuclei; released in fission and fusion.
- **Electrostatic** and **magnetic** — charges or magnets held apart or together.

Energy is moved between stores by four kinds of **transfer**:

- **Mechanically** — a force doing work (pushing, lifting, stretching).
- **Electrically** — a current doing work in a circuit.
- **By heating** — conduction, convection or radiation from hot to cold.
- **By radiation** — light, sound and other waves carrying energy away.

A battery-powered torch: chemical store in the cell → transferred electrically → thermal store of the filament → transferred by radiation (light and infrared) → thermal store of the surroundings. A falling ball: gravitational potential store → kinetic store, transferred mechanically by the force of gravity.

**Conservation of energy**: energy **cannot be created or destroyed**, only transferred from one store to another. The total amount is always the same. In every real transfer some energy ends up **dissipated** — spread out into the thermal store of the surroundings by friction, air resistance or electrical heating — where it is no longer useful, but it has not vanished. "Wasted" energy is still energy.

::: method
1. Start with **where the energy is** (the store) and end with **where it finishes** (usually the thermal store of the surroundings).
2. Name the **transfer** between them: mechanically, electrically, by heating, by radiation.
3. Write it as a chain: *store → (transfer) → store*, one arrow per stage.
4. For "wasted" energy, say **dissipated to the thermal store of the surroundings** — and that the total is conserved.
5. Use the store names from the list; "movement energy" and "heat energy" are not the expected vocabulary.
:::

::: example Energy chain for a bungee jumper
Describe the energy transfers as a bungee jumper falls from the platform to the lowest point. (3)

At the top she has energy in her gravitational potential store (1); as she falls this is transferred mechanically (by the force of gravity) to her kinetic store (1); as the cord stretches, kinetic energy is transferred to the elastic potential store of the cord, with some dissipated to the thermal store of the cord and air (1).
:::

::: example An electric kettle
Describe the energy transfers when a mains kettle boils water, and explain what "wasted" energy means here. (3)

Energy is transferred electrically from the mains to the thermal store of the heating element (1), then by heating to the thermal store of the water (1); some is transferred by heating to the kettle body and the air — this is wasted because it is not heating the water, but it is not destroyed (1).
:::

::: example Conservation of energy
A pendulum is released from a height. Explain, using conservation of energy, why it does not quite return to its starting height. (3)

At release its energy is in the gravitational potential store; at the bottom this has all become kinetic, and it rises again as kinetic transfers back to gravitational potential (1). Air resistance and friction at the pivot transfer some energy to the thermal store of the surroundings on every swing (1); the total energy is conserved, but less is left in the pendulum's own stores each time, so it rises a little less (1).
:::

::: q
State the principle of conservation of energy. (1)
---
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one store to another.
:::

::: q
Name the energy store of (a) a compressed spring, (b) a moving car, (c) a book on a high shelf. (3)
---
(a) Elastic potential (1); (b) kinetic (1); (c) gravitational potential (1).
:::

::: q
Name the four ways energy can be transferred between stores. (4)
---
Mechanically (1); electrically (1); by heating (1); by radiation (light/sound) (1).
:::

::: q
Describe the energy transfers in a battery-powered fan. (2)
---
Chemical store of the battery → transferred electrically → kinetic store of the blades (1), with some transferred by heating and by sound to the surroundings (1).
:::

::: q
What is meant by energy being dissipated? (1)
---
It has spread out into the thermal store of the surroundings and is no longer useful.
:::

::: q
A ball is thrown upward. Describe the energy transfer on the way up. (1)
---
Kinetic store → gravitational potential store (transferred mechanically by gravity).
:::

::: q
Give the units of energy and power. (2)
---
Joule (1); watt (1).
:::

::: q
Describe the energy transfers in a coal-fired power station up to the generator. (3)
---
Chemical store of the coal → thermal store of the steam by heating (1) → kinetic store of the turbine, mechanically (1) → transferred electrically by the generator to the grid (1).
:::

::: q
Where does the energy of a car end up after it brakes to a stop? (1)
---
In the thermal store of the brakes, tyres and surroundings (dissipated as heat).
:::

::: q
Why is "energy is used up" a wrong description of a torch going flat? (2)
---
Energy is conserved; the chemical store of the battery has been transferred (1) to the thermal store of the surroundings via light and heat, where it is dissipated but not destroyed (1).
:::

::: watch
- Use **store** language: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, nuclear — and the **four transfer** words.
- Every chain should end in the **thermal store of the surroundings** for the dissipated part.
- Energy is never "lost" or "used up" — it is **transferred** and **dissipated**; the total is conserved.
- Light and sound are **transfers by radiation**, not stores.
- Name the store *and* the transfer pathway; "heat energy → light energy" earns little.
:::
