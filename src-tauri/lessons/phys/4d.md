## What it is

**Work** is done whenever a force moves something along the direction of the force:

$$W = F \times d \qquad \text{work done (J) = force (N) × distance moved in the direction of the force (m)}$$

Work done is **energy transferred**: lift a box and you do work against gravity, transferring energy to its gravitational potential store; push it along the floor against friction and the work is transferred to the thermal store of the floor and box. 1 J is the work done by a force of 1 N moving 1 m. If nothing moves, no work is done however hard you push.

**Gravitational potential energy (GPE)** — the energy an object has because of its height:

$$GPE = m \times g \times h \qquad \text{(kg × N/kg × m = J)}$$

with **g = 10 N/kg** on Earth and *h* the height gained or lost. It equals the work done against the weight to lift it.

**Kinetic energy (KE)** — the energy of a moving object:

$$KE = \tfrac{1}{2} \times m \times v^2 \qquad \text{(kg × (m/s)² = J)}$$

Note the **v²**: doubling the speed quadruples the kinetic energy.

**Conservation links them.** For a falling object (ignoring air resistance), the GPE lost equals the KE gained: $mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ regardless of mass. For a ball thrown upward, the KE at launch equals the GPE at the top: $h = v^2/2g$. Where friction acts, GPE lost = KE gained + energy dissipated, so the speed is lower than the ideal value.

**Power** is the **rate of doing work** (or transferring energy):

$$P = \frac{W}{t} \qquad \text{power (W) = work done (J) ÷ time (s)}$$

A 1 W machine does 1 J of work each second. A powerful car does the same work (accelerating to 30 m/s) in less time than a weak one. Human power output is about 100 W sustained; a kettle 2–3 kW; a car engine 100 kW.

::: method
1. **Work**: W = Fd, with *d* along the force. Lifting: F = weight = mg.
2. **GPE = mgh**: mass in kg, g = 10, height in m. It's the height *change* that matters.
3. **KE = ½mv²**: square the speed first, then halve and multiply by mass. Rearranging for *v*: $v = \sqrt{2KE/m}$.
4. **Energy conservation**: set GPE lost = KE gained (or work done = energy transferred) and solve. Mass usually cancels for speed.
5. **Power**: P = W/t, or P = E/t for any energy transfer. Time in seconds.
:::

::: example Work and power lifting
A crane lifts a 400 kg load through 15 m in 20 s. Calculate the work done and the power. (4)

Force = weight = 400 × 10 = 4000 N (1). Work = 4000 × 15 = **60 000 J** (1). Power = 60 000 ÷ 20 (1) = **3000 W** (1).
:::

::: example Kinetic energy
Calculate the kinetic energy of a 1200 kg car at 25 m/s, and state how it changes if the speed doubles. (3)

$KE = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1200 \times 25^2 = 600 \times 625$ (1) = **375 000 J** (1). Doubling the speed quadruples the KE to 1 500 000 J because KE depends on v² (1).
:::

::: example Falling object — GPE to KE
A 2 kg rock falls 20 m. Find the GPE lost and the speed just before impact, ignoring air resistance. (4)

GPE = 2 × 10 × 20 = **400 J** (1). KE gained = 400 J = ½ × 2 × v² (1), so v² = 400 (1) and v = **20 m/s** (1).
:::

::: example Roller coaster with friction
A 500 kg roller-coaster car starts at rest 30 m above the bottom of a dip and reaches the bottom at 22 m/s. How much energy was dissipated? (4)

GPE lost = 500 × 10 × 30 = 150 000 J (1). KE at bottom = ½ × 500 × 22² = 250 × 484 = 121 000 J (1). Dissipated = 150 000 − 121 000 = **29 000 J** (1), transferred to the thermal store of the track, wheels and air by friction and air resistance (1).
:::

::: q
Define work done and give its unit. (2)
---
Force × distance moved in the direction of the force (1); joule (1).
:::

::: q
How much work is done pushing a trolley 12 m with a force of 50 N? (2)
---
50 × 12 (1) = 600 J (1).
:::

::: q
Calculate the GPE gained by a 60 kg climber who ascends 250 m. (2)
---
60 × 10 × 250 (1) = 150 000 J (1).
:::

::: q
Calculate the KE of a 0.5 kg ball moving at 8 m/s. (2)
---
½ × 0.5 × 64 (1) = 16 J (1).
:::

::: q
A car's KE is 200 000 J and its mass is 1000 kg. Find its speed. (3)
---
v² = 2 × 200 000 ÷ 1000 = 400 (2); v = 20 m/s (1).
:::

::: q
Define power and give its unit. (2)
---
The rate of doing work / transferring energy (1); watt (1).
:::

::: q
A motor does 9000 J of work in 30 s. Calculate its power. (2)
---
9000 ÷ 30 (1) = 300 W (1).
:::

::: q
A ball is thrown straight up at 12 m/s. Ignoring air resistance, how high does it rise? (3)
---
½mv² = mgh → h = v²/2g = 144 ÷ 20 (2) = 7.2 m (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a heavy and a light stone dropped from the same height hit the ground at the same speed (no air resistance). (2)
---
mgh = ½mv², so the mass cancels (1) and v = √(2gh) depends only on g and h (1).
:::

::: q
A 75 kg student runs up a 4 m flight of stairs in 6 s. Calculate her power output. (3)
---
Work = 75 × 10 × 4 = 3000 J (1); P = 3000 ÷ 6 (1) = 500 W (1).
:::

::: watch
- **v² in KE.** Square before halving; and doubling speed **quadruples** KE — a favourite explain question.
- Use **g = 10 N/kg** and mass in **kg** (grams ÷ 1000).
- Work is force × distance **in the direction of the force** — carrying a bag horizontally at steady speed does no work against gravity.
- In conservation problems the **mass cancels** when finding speed or height — you may not even need it.
- Power is per **second**: convert minutes, and remember a kW is 1000 W.
:::
