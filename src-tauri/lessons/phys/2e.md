## What it is

**Current is a flow of charge.** In a metal the charge carriers are **electrons**, which are free to move between the fixed positive ions. Connect a cell and the electrons drift from the negative terminal toward the positive one — the opposite way to the arrow of *conventional current*, which is drawn from + to −. Insulators have no free electrons, so no current flows.

**Current is the rate of flow of charge**: how many coulombs pass a point per second.

$$Q = I \times t \qquad \text{charge (C) = current (A) × time (s)}$$

One ampere is one coulomb per second. The charge on a single electron is tiny (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C), so a coulomb is about 6 × 10¹⁸ electrons.

**Voltage is energy per unit charge.** The potential difference across a component is the energy transferred to (or by) each coulomb of charge that passes through it: **one volt is one joule per coulomb**. A 6 V lamp transfers 6 J of energy for every coulomb that flows through it; a 12 V battery gives each coulomb 12 J to spend around the circuit.

$$E = Q \times V \qquad \text{energy (J) = charge (C) × voltage (V)}$$

Put the two together and you recover $E = IVt$: the charge is *It*, and each coulomb carries *V* joules.

::: method
1. **Q = It**: time in seconds; answer in coulombs. Rearrange for *I* (= Q/t) or *t* (= Q/I).
2. **E = QV**: use when the charge is given or has just been found. If you have current and time instead, use E = IVt directly.
3. Define carefully: current = **rate of flow of charge**; voltage = **energy transferred per unit charge**; 1 V = 1 J/C.
4. In explanations of conduction: **free electrons** in metals; they move from − to +; conventional current is drawn + to −.
5. Keep track of large and small numbers with standard form.
:::

::: example Charge from current and time
A current of 2.5 A flows through a heater for 4 minutes. Calculate the charge that passes. (2)

$Q = It = 2.5 \times 240$ (1) = **600 C** (1).
:::

::: example Current from charge
A charge of 90 C passes through a lamp in 30 s. Find the current. (2)

$I = Q/t = 90 \div 30$ (1) = **3 A** (1).
:::

::: example Energy from charge and voltage
A 12 V car battery passes 500 C of charge through the starter motor. How much energy is transferred? (2)

$E = QV = 500 \times 12$ (1) = **6000 J** (1).
:::

::: example Meaning of the volt
A voltmeter across a resistor reads 4.5 V. Explain what this tells you about the energy transferred. (2)

Each coulomb of charge passing through the resistor transfers 4.5 J of energy (1) — the volt is a joule per coulomb, so voltage measures energy per unit charge (1).
:::

::: q
In which direction do electrons move in a circuit, and in which direction is conventional current drawn? (2)
---

---
V = 2400 ÷ 200 (1) = 12 V (1).
:::

::: watch
- Time in **seconds** for Q = It. Minutes must be converted.
- "Current is charge" is wrong: current is the **rate of flow** of charge — the "per second" matters.
- The volt is **joules per coulomb** — say "energy transferred per unit charge", not "the push" or "the amount of electricity".
- Electrons flow **negative to positive**; the conventional current arrow points the other way. Both are accepted only if you say which you mean.
- E = QV and E = IVt are the same relationship — pick the one that matches the data given.
:::

::: test

---
