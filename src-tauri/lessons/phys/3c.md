## What it is

**Light is a transverse wave** that travels in straight lines (rays) and, like every wave, can be **reflected** and **refracted**. Ray diagrams show its path; every angle is measured from the **normal** — the line drawn at right angles to the surface at the point where the ray meets it.

**Reflection.** When light hits a plane (flat) mirror:

$$\text{angle of incidence} = \text{angle of reflection}$$

(both measured from the normal), and the incident ray, reflected ray and normal all lie in the same plane. The image in a plane mirror is **virtual** (it cannot be formed on a screen — the rays only *appear* to come from behind the mirror), **upright**, the **same size** as the object, **laterally inverted** (left-right swapped) and **as far behind the mirror as the object is in front**. To locate it, draw two rays from the object reflecting off the mirror and extend the reflected rays backward until they meet.

**Refraction.** When light passes from one medium into another, its **speed changes**, and unless it hits along the normal its **direction changes**.

- Air → glass (or water): light **slows down** and bends **toward the normal**.
- Glass → air: light **speeds up** and bends **away from the normal**.
- Along the normal: no change of direction, only of speed.
- The **frequency** stays the same; the **wavelength** shortens in the slower medium.

Through a **rectangular block** a ray bends toward the normal on entry and away on exit, emerging **parallel** to its original direction but shifted sideways. Through a **triangular prism** the two refractions are in the same sense, so the ray is bent toward the prism's base; because the amount of refraction depends slightly on wavelength, white light spreads into a **spectrum** (dispersion) — violet is bent most, red least.

**Practical: refraction through blocks and prisms.** Place the block on paper and draw round it. Shine a **ray box** (single narrow slit) at one face at an angle; mark the incident ray and the emergent ray with pencil dots (two well-spaced dots per ray), remove the block and join the dots, adding the path inside the block. Draw the normal at each surface and measure the angles of incidence and refraction with a protractor. Repeat for several angles. Use a **dim room** and a narrow ray for sharp lines; mark points far apart to reduce angle error. Do the same with semicircular blocks (aim at the centre so the ray enters undeviated) and triangular prisms, where you can see the spectrum.

::: method
1. **Draw the normal first**, at 90° to the surface at the point of incidence. All angles are measured from it.
2. Reflection: mark *i* = *r* and draw the reflected ray. For images, extend reflected rays back with dashed lines.
3. Refraction: entering a denser medium → bend **toward** the normal; leaving → bend **away**. Along the normal → straight through.
4. Rectangular block: emergent ray **parallel** to the incident ray. Prism: ray bent **toward the base**; white light disperses, violet most.
5. State what changes: **speed** (and wavelength) change; **frequency** does not.
:::

::: example Law of reflection
A ray strikes a plane mirror at 35° to the mirror surface. Find the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection. (2)

Angle of incidence is measured from the normal: 90 − 35 = **55°** (1). Angle of reflection = angle of incidence = **55°** (1).
:::

::: example Image in a plane mirror
An object stands 40 cm in front of a plane mirror. Describe the image formed. (3)

Virtual and upright (1), the same size as the object and laterally inverted (1), 40 cm behind the mirror (1).
:::

::: example Refraction into glass
Describe and explain what happens to a ray of light entering a glass block at an angle to the normal. (3)

The light slows down as it enters the glass (1), so it changes direction, bending toward the normal (1); its frequency is unchanged but its wavelength is shorter in the glass (1).
:::

::: example Dispersion by a prism
Explain why a triangular prism splits white light into a spectrum. (3)

White light is a mixture of wavelengths (1); each wavelength is refracted by a slightly different amount — violet is slowed and bent most, red least (1); the two surfaces of the prism bend the light the same way, so the colours are spread apart rather than brought back parallel (1).
:::

::: q
What is the normal in a ray diagram? (1)
---
A line at right angles to the surface at the point where the ray meets it.
:::

::: q
State the law of reflection. (1)
---
Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection (measured from the normal).
:::

::: q
Give three properties of the image in a plane mirror. (3)
---
Any three: virtual; upright; same size as object; laterally inverted; same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.
:::

::: q
What is a virtual image? (1)
---
One that cannot be formed on a screen; the rays only appear to come from it.
:::

::: q
Why does light refract when it enters water? (1)
---
Its speed changes (it slows down).
:::

::: q
A ray passes from glass into air. Which way does it bend? (1)
---
Away from the normal.
:::

::: q
A ray hits a glass block along the normal. What happens to its direction and speed? (2)
---
Direction unchanged (1); speed decreases (1).
:::

::: q
Which colour is refracted most by a prism, and why? (2)
---
Violet (1); it has the shortest wavelength and is slowed most in the glass (1).
:::

::: q
In the ray-box practical, why are two widely spaced dots marked on each ray? (1)
---
So the line drawn through them is accurate — a small error in dot position gives a small error in angle.
:::

::: q
A ray enters a rectangular glass block and leaves through the opposite face. Describe the direction of the emergent ray compared with the incident ray. (1)
---
Parallel to it but displaced sideways.
:::

::: watch
- **Angles from the normal.** A ray "at 30° to the mirror" has an angle of incidence of 60°.
- Toward the normal going **in** to glass, away coming **out** — and no bending along the normal.
- The image in a plane mirror is **virtual, upright, same size, laterally inverted, equidistant** — five properties, learn all five.
- Refraction happens because the **speed changes**; the frequency is fixed by the source.
- In the practical, draw the block outline and normals in **pencil** and mark rays with **two spaced dots** — the examiner is looking for these details.
:::
