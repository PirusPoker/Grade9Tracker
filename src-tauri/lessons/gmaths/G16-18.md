## What it is

Mensuration means measuring shapes: perimeter (distance round), area (space covered, in square units) and volume (space filled, in cubic units), plus surface area (total area of every face of a solid). At GCSE Higher you must **know** the formulae for triangles, parallelograms, trapezia, prisms, cylinders and circles; the cone and sphere formulae are printed in the question when needed, but you must know how to use them, including for pyramids, frustums and composite solids.

### 2D areas (G16)

$$\text{Triangle} = \tfrac12 bh \qquad \text{Parallelogram} = bh \qquad \text{Trapezium} = \tfrac12(a + b)h$$

The height $h$ is always **perpendicular** to the base — never a slanted side. Composite shapes are split into these pieces, or found as a big shape minus a cut-out.

*Mini-example.* A trapezium has parallel sides 7 cm and 13 cm, 6 cm apart: area $= \frac12(7 + 13) \times 6 = 60\text{ cm}^2$.

### Prisms and cylinders (G16)

A prism has a constant cross-section, so
$$\text{Volume of a prism} = \text{area of cross-section} \times \text{length}.$$
A cuboid is $l \times w \times h$. A **cylinder** is a prism with a circular cross-section: $V = \pi r^2 h$. Its surface area is two circles plus the curved surface, which unrolls into a rectangle $2\pi r$ wide and $h$ tall:
$$\text{Total surface area of a cylinder} = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h.$$

### Circles (G17)

$$C = 2\pi r = \pi d \qquad A = \pi r^2$$

"Give your answer **in terms of $\pi$**" means leave $\pi$ as a symbol: a circle of radius 6 has area $36\pi$ and circumference $12\pi$. A **semicircle** has perimeter $\pi r + 2r$ (half the circumference plus the diameter) — forgetting the straight edge is the classic slip.

### Pyramids, cones and spheres (G17)

$$\text{Pyramid (any base)}: V = \tfrac13 \times \text{base area} \times h$$
$$\text{Cone}: V = \tfrac13\pi r^2 h, \quad \text{curved surface area} = \pi r l$$
$$\text{Sphere}: V = \tfrac43\pi r^3, \quad \text{surface area} = 4\pi r^2$$

Here $h$ is the **perpendicular height** and $l$ is the **slant height** of the cone; they are linked by Pythagoras, $l^2 = r^2 + h^2$. A cone's total surface area adds the circular base: $\pi r l + \pi r^2$. A **hemisphere** is half a sphere: volume $\frac23\pi r^3$, curved surface $2\pi r^2$, total surface (with the flat circle) $3\pi r^2$. The surface area of a pyramid is the base plus each triangular face.

*Mini-example.* A cone has radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Slant height $l = \sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$ cm. Volume $= \frac13\pi \times 25 \times 12 = 100\pi\text{ cm}^3$. Total surface area $= \pi \times 5 \times 13 + \pi \times 25 = 90\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

### Frustums and composite solids (G17)

A **frustum** is a cone (or pyramid) with the top sliced off parallel to the base.
$$\text{Volume of frustum} = \text{volume of large cone} - \text{volume of small cone removed}.$$
The small cone is **similar** to the large one, so its radius and height are in the same ratio (for example, if the removed cone has a third of the height it has a third of the radius). Composite solids — a cone on a hemisphere, a cylinder with a hemispherical end — are handled by adding volumes; for surface area add only the **outside** surfaces (the joins are hidden).

### Arcs and sectors (G18)

A **sector** with angle $\theta$ is the fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the full circle:
$$\text{Arc length} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r \qquad \text{Sector area} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$$
The **perimeter of a sector** is the arc plus two radii. You may be asked to work backwards — find the angle or radius from a given arc length or area — by forming an equation.

*Mini-example.* Sector of radius 9 cm, angle $80°$: arc $= \frac{80}{360} \times 18\pi = 4\pi$ cm; perimeter $= 4\pi + 18$ cm; area $= \frac{80}{360} \times 81\pi = 18\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

A **segment** (the region between a chord and its arc) is a sector minus the triangle: area $= \frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2 - \frac12 r^2 \sin\theta$.

Units: lengths in cm, areas in cm$^2$, volumes in cm$^3$ — check the units match before combining.

::: terms
- **Perpendicular height**: the height measured at right angles to the base, not along a sloping side.
- **Prism**: a solid with the same cross-section all the way through; volume is cross-section area × length.
- **Slant height**: the distance from the apex of a cone down its sloping side to the edge of the base, l² = r² + h².
- **Frustum**: the solid left when the top of a cone or pyramid is cut off parallel to the base.
- **Sector**: the region of a circle between two radii and an arc, like a slice of pizza.
- **Arc length**: the curved part of a sector's edge, the fraction θ/360 of the circumference.
- **In terms of π**: an exact answer with π left as a symbol, such as 36π cm².
:::

::: method
1. **Sketch and label** the shape with every length you know; mark the perpendicular height. Split composite shapes into standard pieces.
2. **Write the formula first**, then substitute — the formula line with correct values is usually the M1 even if the arithmetic later slips.
3. **Cone and sphere formulae** are given in the question when needed; everything else (trapezium, prism, cylinder, circle) you must know.
4. **Slant or perpendicular?** Volume of a cone uses $h$; curved surface uses $l$. If you are given one and need the other, use Pythagoras $l^2 = r^2 + h^2$.
5. **Paper 1 (non-calculator):** expect "give your answer in terms of $\pi$". Keep $\pi$ as a symbol, simplify fractions like $\frac{80}{360} = \frac29$, and collect $\pi$ terms. On calculator papers, keep full accuracy and round only at the end.
6. **Sectors:** find the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$ first, then multiply by $2\pi r$ or $\pi r^2$. Perimeter = arc + 2 radii. To find $\theta$ or $r$, set up an equation and solve it.
7. **Frustums:** big cone minus small cone; use similarity (ratio of heights = ratio of radii) to get the missing dimension.
8. **Composite surface area:** only surfaces you could paint count — the circle where a cone meets a hemisphere is inside and is not included.
9. **Units** in the answer: cm, cm$^2$ or cm$^3$. "Show that" answers need the unrounded value before rounding to the given figure.
:::

::: example Composite area and perimeter
A shape is a rectangle 10 cm by 6 cm with a semicircle on one of its 6 cm sides (on the outside). Find its area and perimeter to 1 decimal place.

Semicircle radius $= 3$ cm. Area $= 10 \times 6 + \frac12\pi \times 3^2 = 60 + 4.5\pi = 74.1\text{ cm}^2$.

Perimeter: three straight sides $10 + 6 + 10 = 26$ cm (the fourth side is replaced by the arc) plus half the circumference $\pi \times 3$: $26 + 3\pi = 35.4$ cm.
:::

::: example Cylinder in terms of π (non-calculator)
A closed cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume and total surface area in terms of $\pi$.

$V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 9 \times 10 = 90\pi\text{ cm}^3$.

Surface area $= 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 18\pi + 60\pi = 78\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: example Working backwards on a sector
A sector of radius 6 cm has area $15\pi\text{ cm}^2$. Find the angle of the sector and its perimeter, leaving $\pi$ in your answer.

$\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi \times 36 = 15\pi \Rightarrow \dfrac{\theta}{360} = \dfrac{15}{36} \Rightarrow \theta = 150°$.

Arc $= \dfrac{150}{360} \times 12\pi = 5\pi$. Perimeter $= 5\pi + 12$ cm.
:::

::: example Composite solid
A toy is a cone of height 6 cm on top of a hemisphere of radius 4 cm (same radius). Find its volume and surface area to 3 significant figures.

Volume $= \frac23\pi \times 4^3 + \frac13\pi \times 4^2 \times 6 = \frac{128}{3}\pi + 32\pi = \frac{224}{3}\pi = 235\text{ cm}^3$.

Slant height $l = \sqrt{4^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{52}$. Surface area $=$ curved hemisphere $+$ curved cone $= 2\pi \times 16 + \pi \times 4 \times \sqrt{52} = 100.5 + 90.6 = 191\text{ cm}^2$. The circle where they join is hidden, so it is not counted.
:::

::: example Frustum
A cone has base radius 9 cm and height 24 cm. The top 8 cm is cut off parallel to the base. Find the volume of the frustum in terms of $\pi$.

The removed cone is similar with height ratio $\frac{8}{24} = \frac13$, so its radius is $9 \times \frac13 = 3$ cm.

$V = \frac13\pi \times 9^2 \times 24 - \frac13\pi \times 3^2 \times 8 = 648\pi - 24\pi = 624\pi\text{ cm}^3$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: a sector folded into a cone
A sector of radius 12 cm and angle $150°$ is curled up so that its two straight edges meet, forming the curved surface of a cone. Find the exact volume of the cone.

The arc becomes the circumference of the base: arc $= \frac{150}{360} \times 24\pi = 10\pi$, so $2\pi r = 10\pi$ and $r = 5$ cm.

The radius of the sector becomes the slant height: $l = 12$. Height $h = \sqrt{12^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{119}$ cm.

$V = \frac13\pi \times 25 \times \sqrt{119} = \dfrac{25\sqrt{119}}{3}\pi\text{ cm}^3$ ($\approx 286\text{ cm}^3$).
:::

::: q
Find the area of (a) a triangle with base 12 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm (b) a parallelogram with base 9 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm.
---
(a) $\frac12 \times 12 \times 7 = 42\text{ cm}^2$. (b) $9 \times 4 = 36\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A circle has diameter 14 cm. Find its circumference and area in terms of $\pi$.
---
$C = \pi d = 14\pi$ cm. $r = 7$, so $A = 49\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A prism has a right-angled triangle cross-section with shorter sides 6 cm and 8 cm, and length 15 cm. Find its volume and surface area.
---
Hypotenuse $= 10$ cm. Cross-section $= \frac12 \times 6 \times 8 = 24\text{ cm}^2$. $V = 24 \times 15 = 360\text{ cm}^3$. Surface area $= 2 \times 24 + (6 + 8 + 10) \times 15 = 48 + 360 = 408\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A sector has radius 10 cm and angle $72°$. Find its arc length, area and perimeter to 3 significant figures.
---
Fraction $= \frac{72}{360} = \frac15$. Arc $= \frac15 \times 20\pi = 4\pi = 12.6$ cm. Area $= \frac15 \times 100\pi = 20\pi = 62.8\text{ cm}^2$. Perimeter $= 4\pi + 20 = 32.6$ cm.
:::

::: q
Find the volume and surface area of a sphere of radius 6 cm, in terms of $\pi$.
---
$V = \frac43\pi \times 216 = 288\pi\text{ cm}^3$. $SA = 4\pi \times 36 = 144\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A square-based pyramid has base edge 10 cm and perpendicular height 12 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.
---
$V = \frac13 \times 100 \times 12 = 400\text{ cm}^3$. Height of each triangular face $= \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = 13$ cm, so each face is $\frac12 \times 10 \times 13 = 65\text{ cm}^2$. Total $= 100 + 4 \times 65 = 360\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A cylinder has volume $500\text{ cm}^3$ and radius 5 cm. Find its height to 3 significant figures.
---
$25\pi h = 500 \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{500}{25\pi} = 6.37$ cm.
:::

::: q
A sector of radius 8 cm has arc length 15 cm. Find the angle of the sector to 1 decimal place.
---
$\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 16\pi = 15 \Rightarrow \theta = \dfrac{15 \times 360}{16\pi} = 107.4°$.
:::

::: q
A solid metal sphere of radius 3 cm is melted and recast as a cylinder of radius 2 cm. Find the height of the cylinder.
---
Sphere: $\frac43\pi \times 27 = 36\pi$. Cylinder: $\pi \times 4 \times h = 36\pi \Rightarrow h = 9$ cm.
:::

::: q
A chord cuts off a sector of angle $60°$ in a circle of radius 8 cm. Find the area of the minor segment to 3 significant figures.
---
Sector $= \frac{60}{360} \times 64\pi = 33.51$. Triangle $= \frac12 \times 8 \times 8 \times \sin 60° = 27.71$. Segment $= 33.51 - 27.71 = 5.80\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: watch
- **Slant height vs perpendicular height.** Volume of a cone or pyramid uses the vertical height; the curved surface of a cone uses the slant height. Mixing them up is the most common lost mark.
- **Perimeter of a sector or semicircle** must include the straight edges (the two radii, or the diameter).
- **Diameter given, radius needed.** Halve it before using $\pi r^2$, $\frac43\pi r^3$ or $\pi r l$.
- **Forgetting the $\frac13$** for cones and pyramids, or the $\frac12$ for a hemisphere.
- **Surface area of a composite solid** counts only the outside — don't add the hidden circle where two parts join, and do add the flat base of a cone if the question says "total".
- **"In terms of $\pi$"** means no decimal: write $624\pi$, not 1960.35. On a calculator paper, round only at the end.
:::

$2 \times \pi \times 7.5$ (M1) $= 47.1$ cm (A1).

::: test
A closed cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 9 cm. Find, in terms of $\pi$, (a) its volume (b) its total surface area. (4)
---
(a) $\pi \times 16 \times 9$ (M1) $= 144\pi\text{ cm}^3$ (A1). (b) $2 \times 16\pi + 2\pi \times 4 \times 9$ (M1) $= 104\pi\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A sector has radius 9 cm and angle $40°$. Find its perimeter, giving your answer in terms of $\pi$. (3)
---
Arc $= \frac{40}{360} \times 18\pi$ (M1) $= 2\pi$ (A1). Perimeter $= 2\pi + 18$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm with a hemisphere of radius 3 cm on top. Find its volume to 3 significant figures. (3)
---
Cylinder $= 90\pi$ (M1); hemisphere $= \frac23\pi \times 27 = 18\pi$ (M1); total $= 108\pi = 339\text{ cm}^3$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A cone has base radius 6 cm and slant height 10 cm. Find, in terms of $\pi$, (a) its volume (b) its total surface area. [Volume of a cone $= \frac13\pi r^2 h$; curved surface area $= \pi r l$] (5)
---
(a) $h = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8$ (M1 A1); $V = \frac13\pi \times 36 \times 8 = 96\pi\text{ cm}^3$ (A1). (b) $\pi \times 6 \times 10 + \pi \times 36$ (M1) $= 96\pi\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A sphere has volume $1000\text{ cm}^3$. Find its radius to 3 significant figures. [Volume of a sphere $= \frac43\pi r^3$] (3)
---
$\frac43\pi r^3 = 1000$ (M1); $r^3 = \dfrac{750}{\pi} = 238.7$ (M1); $r = 6.20$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
A cylinder of radius 6 cm contains water. A solid sphere of radius 3 cm is dropped in and is completely covered. By how much does the water level rise? (3)
---
Sphere volume $= \frac43\pi \times 27 = 36\pi$ (M1). Rise $h$: $\pi \times 36 \times h = 36\pi$ (M1) $\Rightarrow h = 1$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
A frustum has a circular base of radius 10 cm, a circular top of radius 4 cm and a vertical height of 9 cm. It was made by removing a small cone from a large cone. Find the volume of the frustum to 3 significant figures. (5)
---
Let the full cone have height $H$. Similar cones: $\dfrac{H - 9}{H} = \dfrac{4}{10}$ (M1) $\Rightarrow H = 15$, small cone height $6$ (A1). $V = \frac13\pi \times 100 \times 15 - \frac13\pi \times 16 \times 6$ (M1) $= 500\pi - 32\pi$ (M1) $= 468\pi = 1470\text{ cm}^3$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A sector has angle $120°$ and area $12\pi\text{ cm}^2$. Find its exact perimeter. (4)
---
$\frac{120}{360}\pi r^2 = 12\pi$ (M1) $\Rightarrow r^2 = 36$, $r = 6$ (A1). Arc $= \frac13 \times 12\pi = 4\pi$ (M1). Perimeter $= 4\pi + 12$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
A chord of length 12 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the area of the minor segment, to 3 significant figures. (5)
---
Half-angle at the centre: $\sin\frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{6}{10}$ (M1), $\theta = 73.74°$ (A1). Sector $= \frac{73.74}{360} \times 100\pi = 64.35$ (M1). Triangle: distance from centre to chord $= \sqrt{100 - 36} = 8$, area $= \frac12 \times 12 \times 8 = 48$ (M1). Segment $= 64.35 - 48 = 16.4\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::
