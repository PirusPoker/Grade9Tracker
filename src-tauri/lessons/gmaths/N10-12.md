## What it is

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same value: $\frac38 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$. This topic is about moving between them fluently — including **recurring decimals**, which Higher papers ask you to convert to fractions by an algebraic method — and using fractions and percentages as **operators** ("$\frac35$ of", "$\times 1.2$") and in **ratio** problems.

### Terminating decimals and fractions

A **terminating decimal** stops: $0.375$, $3.5$, $0.04$. Write it over a power of 10 and simplify:

$$0.375 = \frac{375}{1000} = \frac{3}{8} \qquad 3.5 = \frac{35}{10} = \frac72 \qquad 0.04 = \frac{4}{100} = \frac{1}{25}$$

To go from fraction to decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator (short division on Paper 1), or make the denominator a power of 10: $\frac{7}{20} = \frac{35}{100} = 0.35$, $\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8 = 0.375$.

**Which fractions terminate?** Write the fraction in its simplest form. It gives a terminating decimal **exactly when the denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5**, because only then can it be scaled up to a power of 10. So $\frac{7}{40}$ ($40 = 2^3 \times 5$) terminates ($0.175$), but $\frac{5}{12}$ ($12 = 2^2 \times 3$) recurs ($0.41\dot6$). Simplify first: $\frac{9}{30} = \frac{3}{10}$ terminates even though 30 has a factor 3.

### Recurring decimals

A **recurring decimal** repeats a block of digits for ever. Dot notation marks the first and last digits of the repeating block:

$$0.\dot{4} = 0.4444\ldots \qquad 0.\dot{2}\dot{7} = 0.272727\ldots \qquad 0.1\dot{3}\dot{6} = 0.1363636\ldots \qquad 0.\dot{1}4\dot{2} = 0.142142\ldots$$

**Fraction → recurring decimal:** divide, and stop when a remainder repeats. $\frac{5}{11}$: $5.000 \div 11$ gives $0.4545\ldots = 0.\dot4\dot5$.

**Recurring decimal → fraction (the algebraic method).** Let $x$ be the decimal. Multiply by the power of 10 that shifts one full repeating block ($10$ for one repeating digit, $100$ for two, $1000$ for three). Subtract so the recurring tails cancel, then solve.

$$x = 0.\dot2\dot7 = 0.272727\ldots$$
$$100x = 27.272727\ldots$$
$$100x - x = 27 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 99x = 27 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{27}{99} = \frac{3}{11}$$

If the repeat does not start straight after the point, use two multiples of $x$ whose decimal tails match:

$$x = 0.1\dot3\dot6 = 0.1363636\ldots$$
$$1000x = 136.363636\ldots \qquad 10x = 1.363636\ldots$$
$$990x = 135 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{135}{990} = \frac{3}{22}$$

A quick pattern to check your answer: a pure recurring decimal with an $n$-digit block is that block over $n$ nines: $0.\dot5 = \frac59$, $0.\dot0\dot7 = \frac{7}{99}$, $0.\dot1\dot2\dot3 = \frac{123}{999} = \frac{41}{333}$. In an exam, "show that" or "prove" needs the algebraic method, not just this pattern.

Also note $0.\dot9 = 1$: $x = 0.999\ldots$, $10x = 9.999\ldots$, $9x = 9$, $x = 1$.

### Fractions in ratio problems

A ratio compares parts; a fraction compares a part to the **whole**. If boys to girls is $3 : 5$, there are $3 + 5 = 8$ parts, so $\frac38$ are boys and $\frac58$ are girls. Going the other way, if $\frac27$ of the counters are red, the rest ($\frac57$) are blue, so red : blue $= 2 : 5$.

Fractions of a ratio's parts combine in multi-step problems: in a class where boys : girls $= 4 : 5$ and $\frac14$ of the boys and $\frac25$ of the girls wear glasses, the fraction of the class wearing glasses is

$$\frac49 \times \frac14 + \frac59 \times \frac25 = \frac19 + \frac29 = \frac13$$

### Fractions and percentages as operators

"Of" means multiply. A fraction or percentage acting on a quantity is an **operator**:

- $\frac{3}{5}$ of £240 $= \frac35 \times 240 = £144$.
- $35\%$ of 80 kg $= 0.35 \times 80 = 28$ kg.
- Finding the original from a part: if $\frac{2}{9}$ of a number is 36, the number is $36 \div \frac29 = 36 \times \frac92 = 162$.

A **multiplier** turns percentage change into one operation: increase by $15\%$ → $\times 1.15$; decrease by $15\%$ → $\times 0.85$; "is $\frac34$ of" → $\times 0.75$. Applying operators in turn means multiplying them: a price increased by 20% then decreased by 20% is $\times 1.2 \times 0.8 = \times 0.96$, a 4% decrease overall — not back to where it started. (Percentage change, reverse percentages and compound growth are developed fully in R9 and R16.)

Expressing one quantity as a fraction or percentage of another: 18 out of 45 is $\frac{18}{45} = \frac25 = 40\%$.

::: terms
- **Terminating decimal**: a decimal that stops after a finite number of digits, e.g. 0.375.
- **Recurring decimal**: a decimal with a block of digits that repeats for ever, e.g. 0.272727… written with dots over the 2 and the 7.
- **Dot notation**: dots over the first and last digits of the repeating block show which digits recur.
- **Operator**: a fraction, percentage or multiplier that acts on a quantity, e.g. 3/5 of 240 means 3/5 × 240.
- **Multiplier**: the single number you multiply by for a percentage change, e.g. 1.15 for a 15% increase.
- **Unit fraction**: a fraction with numerator 1, such as 1/8, whose decimal is found by dividing 1 by the denominator.
:::

::: method
1. **Decimal → fraction (terminating):** put it over 10, 100 or 1000 according to the number of decimal places, then cancel fully.
2. **Fraction → decimal:** on Paper 1 use short division (add zeros after the point) or scale the denominator to a power of 10. Stop when a remainder repeats, and write the answer with dot notation.
3. **Recurring → fraction ("show that" or "prove"):** write $x = $ the decimal with enough digits to show the pattern (e.g. $0.1363636\ldots$). Multiply by powers of 10 to get two equations with **identical** tails, write both out, subtract, solve for $x$ and simplify. Marks come for: the correct multiples written with their decimals (M1), the subtraction (M1), and the simplified fraction (A1).
4. **Terminating or not?** Simplify the fraction, then factorise the denominator: only 2s and 5s means it terminates.
5. **Ratio ↔ fraction:** total the parts to get the denominator. For multi-stage problems, multiply fractions along each branch and add.
6. **Operators:** "of" means $\times$. Use a decimal multiplier on calculator papers; on Paper 1 find $10\%$, $5\%$, $1\%$ and build up, or use fractions. To find the whole from a part, divide by the fraction.
:::

::: example Converting both ways
(a) Write $0.625$ as a fraction in its simplest form. (b) Write $\frac{7}{12}$ as a decimal.

(a) $\dfrac{625}{1000} = \dfrac{5}{8}$ (divide by 125).

(b) $7 \div 12$: $12$ into $70$ is 5 r 10; into $100$ is 8 r 4; into $40$ is 3 r 4; the remainder 4 repeats. So $0.58333\ldots = 0.58\dot3$.
:::

::: example Recurring decimal to fraction
Show that $0.\dot4\dot5 = \dfrac{5}{11}$.

Let $x = 0.454545\ldots$

$100x = 45.454545\ldots$

Subtract: $99x = 45$, so $x = \dfrac{45}{99} = \dfrac{5}{11}$.
:::

::: example Grade 8: the repeat starts later
Prove algebraically that $0.2\dot1\dot8 = \dfrac{12}{55}$.

Let $x = 0.2181818\ldots$

$1000x = 218.181818\ldots$ and $10x = 2.181818\ldots$

Subtract: $990x = 216$, so $x = \dfrac{216}{990} = \dfrac{12}{55}$ (dividing by 18).
:::

::: example Fractions in a ratio problem
In a car park the ratio of cars to vans is $7 : 2$. $\frac{3}{7}$ of the cars and $\frac12$ of the vans are white. What fraction of the vehicles are white?

Cars are $\frac79$ of the vehicles, vans $\frac29$.

White cars: $\frac79 \times \frac37 = \frac39 = \frac13$. White vans: $\frac29 \times \frac12 = \frac19$.

Total white: $\frac13 + \frac19 = \frac49$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: recurring decimals in a calculation
Write $0.\dot3 + 0.1\dot6$ as a fraction in its simplest form, and decide whether $0.\dot3 \times 0.1\dot6$ is a terminating decimal.

$0.\dot3 = \frac13$. For $y = 0.1666\ldots$: $100y = 16.666\ldots$, $10y = 1.666\ldots$, $90y = 15$, $y = \frac16$.

Sum: $\frac13 + \frac16 = \frac12$.

Product: $\frac13 \times \frac16 = \frac{1}{18}$, and $18 = 2 \times 3^2$ has a factor 3, so it does **not** terminate ($\frac{1}{18} = 0.0\dot5$).
:::

::: q
Write as fractions in their simplest form: (a) $0.85$, (b) $2.125$, (c) $0.064$.
---
(a) $\frac{85}{100} = \frac{17}{20}$. (b) $2\frac{125}{1000} = 2\frac18 = \frac{17}{8}$. (c) $\frac{64}{1000} = \frac{8}{125}$.
:::

::: q
Write as decimals: (a) $\frac{9}{40}$, (b) $\frac{2}{11}$, (c) $\frac{5}{6}$.
---
(a) $\frac{225}{1000} = 0.225$. (b) $0.1818\ldots = 0.\dot1\dot8$. (c) $0.8333\ldots = 0.8\dot3$.
:::

::: q
Which of these fractions give terminating decimals? $\frac{3}{16}$, $\frac{7}{15}$, $\frac{11}{50}$, $\frac{6}{75}$. Explain.
---
$16 = 2^4$ terminates ($0.1875$). $15 = 3 \times 5$ does not. $50 = 2 \times 5^2$ terminates ($0.22$). $\frac{6}{75} = \frac{2}{25}$, $25 = 5^2$, terminates ($0.08$).
:::

::: q
Convert $0.\dot7$ and $0.\dot3\dot6$ to fractions in their simplest form.
---
$x = 0.777\ldots$, $10x = 7.777\ldots$, $9x = 7$, $x = \frac79$. $y = 0.3636\ldots$, $100y = 36.3636\ldots$, $99y = 36$, $y = \frac{36}{99} = \frac{4}{11}$.
:::

::: q
Prove that $0.\dot2\dot1\dot6 = \dfrac{8}{37}$.
---
$x = 0.216216\ldots$, $1000x = 216.216216\ldots$, $999x = 216$, $x = \frac{216}{999} = \frac{8}{37}$ (dividing by 27).
:::

::: q
Convert $0.4\dot5$ to a fraction in its simplest form.
---
$x = 0.4555\ldots$, $100x = 45.555\ldots$, $10x = 4.555\ldots$, $90x = 41$, $x = \frac{41}{90}$.
:::

::: q
Write $1.2\dot3$ as a mixed number.
---
$x = 1.2333\ldots$, $100x = 123.333\ldots$, $10x = 12.333\ldots$, $90x = 111$, $x = \frac{111}{90} = \frac{37}{30} = 1\frac{7}{30}$.
:::

::: q
Blue and yellow paint are mixed in the ratio $3 : 8$. What fraction of the mixture is yellow? How much blue paint is in 5.5 litres of mixture?
---
$\frac{8}{11}$ is yellow. Blue: $\frac{3}{11} \times 5.5 = 1.5$ litres.
:::

::: q
$\frac{5}{8}$ of a number is 75. Find $\frac{3}{10}$ of the number.
---
Number $= 75 \div \frac58 = 120$. $\frac{3}{10} \times 120 = 36$.
:::

::: q
A coat costs £80. It is increased by 25% and then the new price is reduced by 20%. Find the final price, and the single multiplier for the two changes.
---
$80 \times 1.25 = 100$, then $100 \times 0.8 = £80$. Multiplier $1.25 \times 0.8 = 1$: no overall change.
:::

::: watch
- **Recurring decimal written as a rounded decimal.** $\frac{2}{3} = 0.\dot6$, not $0.67$, when an exact decimal is asked.
- **Wrong multiplier for the repeat.** Two repeating digits need $100x$; three need $1000x$. The tails must match exactly before you subtract.
- **Not showing the recurring digits.** Write $1000x = 136.3636\ldots$ with the dots — a line like "$1000x = 136.36$" loses the method mark.
- **Not simplifying.** $\frac{27}{99}$ is correct but the question usually wants $\frac{3}{11}$.
- **Dot notation misread.** $0.\dot1 4 \dot2$ repeats all three digits 142; $0.1\dot4\dot2$ repeats only 42.
- **Ratio turned into the wrong fraction.** In $3 : 5$ the fractions are $\frac38$ and $\frac58$, not $\frac35$.
- **Successive percentages added.** $+20\%$ then $-20\%$ is $\times 0.96$, not no change.
:::

::: test
A tank is $\frac{3}{8}$ full. After 55 litres are added it is $\frac{5}{6}$ full. Find the capacity of the tank. (3)
---
$\frac56 - \frac38 = \frac{20}{24} - \frac{9}{24} = \frac{11}{24}$ (M1). $\frac{11}{24}$ of the capacity is 55 litres (M1). Capacity $= 55 \times \frac{24}{11} = 120$ litres (A1).
:::

::: test
Given that $0.\dot1\dot8 = \frac{2}{11}$, write $0.0\dot1\dot8$ and $1.\dot8\dot1$ as fractions. Show your reasoning. (4)
---
$0.0\dot1\dot8 = \frac{1}{10} \times 0.\dot1\dot8 = \frac{2}{110} = \frac{1}{55}$ (M1 A1). $0.\dot8\dot1 = \frac{81}{99} = \frac{9}{11}$ (M1), so $1.\dot8\dot1 = 1\frac{9}{11} = \frac{20}{11}$ (A1).
:::
