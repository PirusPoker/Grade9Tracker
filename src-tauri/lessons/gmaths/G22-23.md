## What it is

SOH CAH TOA only works in right-angled triangles. For **any** triangle, GCSE Higher uses three results — the sine rule, the cosine rule and the area formula — to find unknown sides, angles and areas, in 2D and inside 3D solids. You must know all three; they are not printed in the question.

### Labelling

Label the angles with capitals $A$, $B$, $C$ and the side **opposite** each angle with the matching small letter $a$, $b$, $c$. Everything depends on getting these pairs right. In a question with other letters (triangle $PQR$), use the same idea: side $p$ is opposite angle $P$.

### The sine rule

$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} \qquad \text{or, for angles,} \qquad \frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$

Use it when you know a side and its **opposite angle** (a complete pair), plus one more side or angle.

*Mini-example (side).* $A = 40°$, $B = 75°$, $a = 8$ cm: $b = \dfrac{8\sin 75°}{\sin 40°} = 12.0$ cm.

**The ambiguous case.** Because $\sin(180° - x) = \sin x$, the equation $\sin B = 0.857$ has two solutions between $0°$ and $180°$: $B = 59.0°$ and $B = 180° - 59.0° = 121.0°$. Both can give a valid triangle when the known angle is opposite the shorter of the two given sides. The calculator gives only the acute one; the question usually tells you whether the angle is acute or obtuse, or asks for both.

### The cosine rule

$$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A \qquad \text{rearranged:} \qquad \cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$$

Use it when you know **two sides and the angle between them** (to find the third side), or **all three sides** (to find an angle). The angle $A$ in the formula is always the one opposite the side $a$ on the left.

*Mini-example (side).* $b = 7$, $c = 10$, $A = 65°$: $a^2 = 49 + 100 - 140\cos 65° = 89.83$, so $a = 9.48$.

*Mini-example (angle).* Sides 5, 7 and 10: the largest angle is opposite 10, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{25 + 49 - 100}{2 \times 5 \times 7} = -0.371$, so $\theta = 111.8°$. A **negative** cosine means an obtuse angle — the cosine rule never has the two-answer problem.

### The area of any triangle

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac12 ab\sin C$$

where $C$ is the angle **between** sides $a$ and $b$ (the included angle). It can be used backwards to find a side or an angle from a known area.

*Mini-example.* Sides 8 cm and $x$ cm with $30°$ between them, area $40\text{ cm}^2$: $\frac12 \times 8 \times x \times \frac12 = 40 \Rightarrow 2x = 40 \Rightarrow x = 20$ cm.

Note that finding an angle from the area has the same ambiguity as the sine rule: $\sin C = 0.6$ gives $36.9°$ or $143.1°$.

### Choosing the rule

| You know | You want | Use |
|---|---|---|
| a side and its opposite angle, plus one more | a side or an angle | sine rule |
| two sides and the angle between them | the third side | cosine rule |
| three sides | an angle | cosine rule (rearranged) |
| two sides and the angle between them | the area | $\frac12 ab\sin C$ |

Multi-step problems chain these: for example, the cosine rule for a missing side, then the sine rule for an angle, then the area. Once you know two angles, the third is $180°$ minus their sum.

### Problems in 3D

In a solid, find a **triangle** (not necessarily right-angled) in a flat plane that contains what you want. Find its sides using Pythagoras in the faces of the solid, then use the cosine rule for an angle and $\frac12 ab\sin C$ for its area. Bearings problems work the same way in 2D: the angle inside the triangle comes from the difference between bearings, or from parallel north lines.

::: terms
- **Sine rule**: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, used when you know a side and the angle opposite it.
- **Cosine rule**: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A, used with two sides and the included angle, or with three sides.
- **Included angle**: the angle between two given sides, where they meet.
- **Area of a triangle**: ½ab sin C, using two sides and the included angle C.
- **Ambiguous case**: when the sine rule gives an angle, both x and 180° − x may be possible because they have the same sine.
- **Obtuse angle**: an angle between 90° and 180°; its cosine is negative, its sine positive.
:::

::: method
1. **Sketch and label** the triangle: capital letters at the angles, the matching small letters on the opposite sides. Mark what you know and what you want.
2. **Choose the rule** from what you know: a complete side–angle pair → sine rule; two sides with the included angle or three sides → cosine rule; area with the included angle → $\frac12 ab\sin C$.
3. **Write the formula with the numbers substituted** before you touch the calculator — that line earns the method mark.
4. **Sine rule for an angle:** put the sines on top. After $\sin^{-1}$, ask whether $180° -$ the answer is possible (the question may say "obtuse").
5. **Cosine rule for an angle:** use the rearranged form; the side opposite the angle goes with the minus sign. A negative cosine means the angle is obtuse.
6. **Keep full accuracy** between steps (store values in the calculator memory) and round only the final answer: 3 s.f. for lengths and areas, 1 d.p. for angles.
7. **Non-calculator versions** use $30°$, $45°$, $60°$ or $120°$, $150°$ with exact values ($\cos 120° = -\frac12$, $\sin 150° = \frac12$) and want surd answers.
8. **3D and bearings:** first build the triangle — find its sides with Pythagoras in the faces, or its angle from bearings — then apply the rule.
:::

::: example Sine rule for a side
In triangle $PQR$, angle $P = 52°$, angle $Q = 71°$ and $p = 11$ cm. Find $q$.

$\dfrac{q}{\sin 71°} = \dfrac{11}{\sin 52°} \Rightarrow q = \dfrac{11\sin 71°}{\sin 52°} = 13.2$ cm (3 s.f.).
:::

::: example Sine rule: two possible angles
In triangle $ABC$, $a = 9$ cm, $b = 12$ cm and $A = 40°$. Find the two possible values of angle $B$.

$\dfrac{\sin B}{12} = \dfrac{\sin 40°}{9} \Rightarrow \sin B = \dfrac{12\sin 40°}{9} = 0.8571$.

$B = 59.0°$ or $B = 180° - 59.0° = 121.0°$. Both are valid, since $40° + 121.0° < 180°$.
:::

::: example Cosine rule with bearings
Boat $P$ is 12 km from a harbour $H$ on a bearing of $035°$. Boat $Q$ is 9 km from $H$ on a bearing of $110°$. How far apart are the boats?

Angle $PHQ = 110° - 35° = 75°$ (the included angle).

$PQ^2 = 12^2 + 9^2 - 2 \times 12 \times 9 \times \cos 75° = 225 - 55.90 = 169.1$, so $PQ = 13.0$ km.
:::

::: example Cosine rule for an angle
A triangle has sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 11 cm. Find its largest angle.

The largest angle is opposite the longest side: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{6^2 + 8^2 - 11^2}{2 \times 6 \times 8} = \dfrac{-21}{96} = -0.21875$, so $\theta = 102.6°$ (obtuse, as the cosine is negative).
:::

::: example Non-calculator: exact area and side
Triangle $ABC$ has $AB = 6$ cm, $AC = 10$ cm and angle $BAC = 60°$. Find the exact area and the exact length of $BC$.

Area $= \frac12 \times 6 \times 10 \times \sin 60° = 30 \times \frac{\sqrt3}{2} = 15\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2$.

$BC^2 = 36 + 100 - 2 \times 6 \times 10 \times \cos 60° = 136 - 60 = 76$, so $BC = \sqrt{76} = 2\sqrt{19}$ cm.
:::

::: example A multi-step problem
In quadrilateral $ABCD$, $AB = 7$ cm, $BC = 9$ cm and angle $ABC = 120°$. Also angle $CAD = 35°$ and angle $ADC = 80°$. Find $AC$, $CD$ and the area of $ABCD$.

$AC^2 = 49 + 81 - 2 \times 7 \times 9 \cos 120° = 130 + 63 = 193$, so $AC = \sqrt{193} = 13.89$ cm.

In triangle $ACD$, angle $ACD = 180° - 35° - 80° = 65°$. Sine rule: $CD = \dfrac{13.89\sin 35°}{\sin 80°} = 8.09$ cm.

Area $= \frac12 \times 7 \times 9 \sin 120° + \frac12 \times 13.89 \times 8.09 \sin 65° = 27.28 + 50.94 = 78.2\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: a triangle inside a cuboid
A cuboid $ABCDEFGH$ has base $ABCD$ with $AB = 8$ cm, $BC = 6$ cm, and vertical edges of 4 cm; $F$ is directly above $B$. Find angle $CAF$ and the area of triangle $ACF$.

Sides from Pythagoras in the faces: $AC = \sqrt{64 + 36} = 10$, $AF = \sqrt{64 + 16} = \sqrt{80}$, $CF = \sqrt{36 + 16} = \sqrt{52}$.

Cosine rule: $\cos CAF = \dfrac{100 + 80 - 52}{2 \times 10 \times \sqrt{80}} = \dfrac{128}{20\sqrt{80}} = 0.7155$, so angle $CAF = 44.3°$.

Area $= \frac12 \times 10 \times \sqrt{80} \times \sin 44.31° = 31.2\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $A = 38°$, $C = 85°$ and $c = 14$ cm. Find $a$.
---
$a = \dfrac{14\sin 38°}{\sin 85°} = 8.65$ cm.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $b = 9$ cm, $c = 6$ cm and $B = 72°$. Find angle $C$.
---
$\sin C = \dfrac{6\sin 72°}{9} = 0.6340 \Rightarrow C = 39.3°$. ($C$ is opposite the shorter side, so it must be acute.)
:::

::: q
Two sides of a triangle are 8 cm and 13 cm, with an angle of $47°$ between them. Find the third side.
---
$x^2 = 64 + 169 - 2 \times 8 \times 13\cos 47° = 91.14 \Rightarrow x = 9.55$ cm.
:::

::: q
A triangle has sides 4 cm, 6 cm and 9 cm. Find its smallest angle.
---
Opposite 4: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{36 + 81 - 16}{2 \times 6 \times 9} = \dfrac{101}{108} \Rightarrow \theta = 20.7°$.
:::

::: q
Find the area of a triangle with sides 11 cm and 15 cm and included angle $118°$.
---
$\frac12 \times 11 \times 15 \times \sin 118° = 72.8\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A triangle has area $50\text{ cm}^2$ and two sides of 12 cm and 10 cm. Find the two possible angles between these sides.
---
$\frac12 \times 12 \times 10 \sin\theta = 50 \Rightarrow \sin\theta = \frac{5}{6} \Rightarrow \theta = 56.4°$ or $123.6°$.
:::

::: q
*Non-calculator.* A triangle has sides 4 cm and 6 cm with an angle of $120°$ between them. Find the exact third side and the exact area.
---
$x^2 = 16 + 36 - 48\cos 120° = 52 + 24 = 76 \Rightarrow x = 2\sqrt{19}$ cm. Area $= \frac12 \times 4 \times 6 \times \frac{\sqrt3}{2} = 6\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A walker goes 20 km from $A$ to $B$ on a bearing of $070°$, then 15 km from $B$ to $C$ on a bearing of $190°$. Find the distance $AC$.
---
Bearing of $A$ from $B$ $= 250°$, so angle $ABC = 250° - 190° = 60°$. $AC^2 = 400 + 225 - 600\cos 60° = 325 \Rightarrow AC = 18.0$ km.
:::

::: q
A parallelogram has sides 7 cm and 12 cm with an angle of $65°$ between them. Find its area.
---
Two congruent triangles: $2 \times \frac12 \times 7 \times 12 \sin 65° = 84\sin 65° = 76.1\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: watch
- **Wrong pairs:** the sine rule only works with each side divided by the sine of the angle **opposite** it.
- **Only one answer from the sine rule** when two were possible (or using the acute one when the question said obtuse). Check $180° -$ your answer.
- **Cosine rule order of operations:** work out $b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ in one go. Typing $b^2 + c^2 - 2bc$ first and then multiplying by $\cos A$ is a classic wrong answer.
- **Non-included angle** in $\frac12 ab\sin C$ or in the cosine rule for a side: the angle must be between the two sides used.
- **Rounded intermediate values** — a side rounded to 2 s.f. can move the final angle by a whole degree.
- **Negative cosine panic:** $\cos\theta < 0$ is fine; it just means $\theta$ is obtuse.
:::

::: test
A triangle has area $35\text{ cm}^2$. Two of its sides are 10 cm and $x$ cm, and the angle between them is $44°$. Find $x$. (3)
---
$\frac12 \times 10 \times x \times \sin 44° = 35$ (M1); $x = \dfrac{70}{10\sin 44°}$ (M1) $= 10.1$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A ship sails 14 km from $P$ to $Q$ on a bearing of $050°$, then 10 km from $Q$ to $R$ on a bearing of $170°$. Find the distance $PR$ and the bearing of $R$ from $P$, to the nearest degree. (5)
---
Angle $PQR = 230° - 170° = 60°$ (B1). $PR^2 = 196 + 100 - 280\cos 60° = 156$ (M1), $PR = 12.5$ km (A1). $\sin QPR = \dfrac{10\sin 60°}{12.49}$ (M1), angle $QPR = 43.9°$; bearing $= 50 + 43.9 = 094°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $ABC$, $a = 8$ cm, $b = 11$ cm and $A = 42°$. Find the two possible sizes of angle $C$. (4)
---
$\sin B = \dfrac{11\sin 42°}{8} = 0.9201$ (M1); $B = 66.9°$ (A1) or $113.1°$ (A1). $C = 180 - 42 - 66.9 = 71.1°$ or $C = 180 - 42 - 113.1 = 24.9°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
*Non-calculator.* In triangle $ABC$, $AB = x$ cm, $AC = (x + 2)$ cm, $BC = 7$ cm and angle $BAC = 120°$. Find $x$ and the exact area of the triangle. (6)
---
$49 = x^2 + (x + 2)^2 - 2x(x + 2)\cos 120°$ (M1) $= x^2 + x^2 + 4x + 4 + x^2 + 2x$ (M1). $3x^2 + 6x - 45 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 + 2x - 15 = 0$ (A1) $\Rightarrow (x + 5)(x - 3) = 0$, $x = 3$ (A1). Area $= \frac12 \times 3 \times 5 \times \frac{\sqrt3}{2}$ (M1) $= \dfrac{15\sqrt3}{4}\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A pyramid has a square base of side 6 cm and four sloping edges, each 9 cm. Find the angle between two adjacent sloping edges at the apex, and the total surface area of the pyramid. (5)
---
Each face has sides 9, 9, 6: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{81 + 81 - 36}{2 \times 9 \times 9} = \dfrac{126}{162}$ (M1); $\theta = 38.9°$ (A1). Face area $= \frac12 \times 9 \times 9 \times \sin 38.94°$ (M1) $= 25.46$ (A1). Total $= 36 + 4 \times 25.46 = 138\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::
