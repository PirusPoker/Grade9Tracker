## What it is

Start with any graph $y = f(x)$ — a parabola, a cubic, a trig wave, or just a sketch with a few labelled points. Changing the equation slightly **moves** or **flips** the whole graph in a predictable way. At GCSE you need two kinds of change: **translations** (slides) and **reflections** (flips). (Stretches such as $y = 2f(x)$ are not in the GCSE statement.)

### The four rules

| New equation | What happens to the graph | A point $(x, y)$ moves to |
|---|---|---|
| $y = f(x) + a$ | translate **up** by $a$ | $(x,\ y + a)$ |
| $y = f(x + a)$ | translate **left** by $a$ | $(x - a,\ y)$ |
| $y = -f(x)$ | reflect in the **$x$-axis** | $(x,\ -y)$ |
| $y = f(-x)$ | reflect in the **$y$-axis** | $(-x,\ y)$ |

As column vectors: $y = f(x) + a$ is a translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ a \end{pmatrix}$ and $y = f(x + a)$ is a translation by $\begin{pmatrix} -a \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

A negative $a$ reverses the direction: $f(x) - 3$ is **down** 3; $f(x - 3)$ is **right** 3.

### Why "inside the bracket" goes the wrong way

Changes **outside** the function act on $y$ and do what they look like: $+a$ adds $a$ to every height.

Changes **inside** the bracket act on $x$ and do the opposite of what they look like. For $y = f(x - 3)$, the old value $f(0)$ now appears when $x - 3 = 0$, that is at $x = 3$. Everything happens 3 units later, so the graph has moved **right** 3.

Example with $f(x) = x^2$ (minimum at the origin):

- $y = x^2 + 4$: up 4, minimum $(0, 4)$.
- $y = (x + 4)^2$: left 4, minimum $(-4, 0)$.
- $y = (x - 2)^2 - 5$: right 2 and down 5, minimum $(2, -5)$ — the translation by vector $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}$.

That last line links to completing the square: any $y = x^2 + bx + c$ can be written $(x + p)^2 + q$, so **every** quadratic with $x^2$ coefficient 1 is a translation of $y = x^2$ by $\begin{pmatrix} -p \\ q \end{pmatrix}$.

### Reflections

- $y = -f(x)$ flips the graph over the $x$-axis: every $y$ changes sign. A minimum becomes a maximum. Points on the $x$-axis (the roots) stay where they are.
- $y = f(-x)$ flips the graph over the $y$-axis: every $x$ changes sign. The $y$-intercept stays where it is.

Example: $y = 2^x$ passes through $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 2)$. Then $y = 2^{-x}$ passes through $(0, 1)$ and $(-1, 2)$ — it is the decay curve $y = 0.5^x$.

A graph symmetrical about the $y$-axis, such as $y = x^2$ or $y = \cos x$, is unchanged by $f(-x)$.

### Tracking the key features

When you sketch a transformed graph, move the labelled features one by one and redraw the same shape through them:

- **Turning points** move like any other point.
- **Asymptotes** move too: $y = \dfrac1x$ has asymptotes $x = 0$ and $y = 0$; $y = \dfrac{1}{x - 2} + 3$ is the translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$, so its asymptotes are $x = 2$ and $y = 3$.
- **Intercepts do not simply move** — new ones must be recalculated. After a translation, find where the new graph crosses the axes by substituting $x = 0$ or $y = 0$ in the new equation.

### Trigonometric graphs

The trig graphs are the favourite "given function" in exams.

- $y = \sin x + 2$: the wave moves up 2; it now runs between 1 and 3.
- $y = \cos(x - 30°)$: the wave moves right $30°$; its maximum moves from $(0°, 1)$ to $(30°, 1)$.
- $y = \sin(x + 90°)$: $\sin$ moved left $90°$ — which is exactly $y = \cos x$.
- $y = -\cos x$: reflected in the $x$-axis; it starts at $(0°, -1)$ and has its maximum at $(180°, 1)$.

### Combining two transformations

For $y = f(x - 2) + 3$, do the inside change and the outside change separately — right 2, up 3. For $y = -f(x) + 3$, reflect first and then translate: $y = -f(x) + 3$ means "take the height, change its sign, then add 3". A point $(1, 4)$ goes to $(1, -4)$, then to $(1, -1)$.

::: terms
- **Transformation**: a change to a graph's equation that moves, flips or resizes the whole graph in a predictable way.
- **Translation**: a slide of the whole graph without turning or flipping it, described by a column vector.
- **Column vector**: two numbers stacked in brackets; the top is the move right (negative means left), the bottom the move up (negative means down).
- **Reflection in the x-axis**: the flip given by y = −f(x); every y-coordinate changes sign.
- **Reflection in the y-axis**: the flip given by y = f(−x); every x-coordinate changes sign.
- **Invariant point**: a point that does not move under a transformation, such as a root under y = −f(x).
:::

::: method
1. **Decide inside or outside.** A change outside $f$ (added to the whole function, or a minus in front) acts on $y$ and does what it looks like. A change inside the bracket acts on $x$ and does the opposite.
2. **Name it fully.** "Describe fully the single transformation" needs both the type and its details: "translation by $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$" or "reflection in the $x$-axis". "Moves left" alone usually gets no marks.
3. **Move the labelled points.** Transform each given point (turning point, intercepts, ends of a sketch) using the table, then draw the same shape through the new points. Label the new coordinates.
4. **Move the asymptotes** of reciprocal, exponential and tan graphs, and draw them as dashed lines.
5. **Recalculate new intercepts** from the new equation — they are not usually images of the old ones.
6. **Going backwards** ("find the equation of the translated graph"): write $y = f(x - p) + q$ for a translation by $\begin{pmatrix} p \\ q \end{pmatrix}$, then simplify if asked.
7. **Quadratics as translations:** complete the square, $(x + p)^2 + q$, to read the vector $\begin{pmatrix} -p \\ q \end{pmatrix}$. This is typical of a Paper 1 (non-calculator) question.
:::

::: example Moving one point four ways
The graph of $y = f(x)$ has a minimum point at $(2, -3)$. Write down the turning point of each graph and say whether it is a maximum or a minimum.

(a) $y = f(x) + 4$: up 4 → $(2, 1)$, minimum.

(b) $y = f(x + 4)$: left 4 → $(-2, -3)$, minimum.

(c) $y = -f(x)$: reflect in the $x$-axis → $(2, 3)$, now a **maximum**.

(d) $y = f(-x)$: reflect in the $y$-axis → $(-2, -3)$, still a minimum.
:::

::: example A quadratic as a translation of y = x²
The graph of $y = x^2 - 6x + 11$ is a translation of the graph of $y = x^2$. Find the vector of the translation.

$x^2 - 6x + 11 = (x - 3)^2 - 9 + 11 = (x - 3)^2 + 2$.

$(x - 3)$: right 3. $+2$: up 2. Translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. The minimum moves from $(0, 0)$ to $(3, 2)$.
:::

::: example Transforming a trig graph
(a) Sketch $y = \cos x + 1$ for $0° \le x \le 360°$ and give its maximum and minimum points.

$\cos x$ has maxima at $(0°, 1)$ and $(360°, 1)$, minimum at $(180°, -1)$. Up 1: maxima $(0°, 2)$ and $(360°, 2)$, minimum $(180°, 0)$ — the wave just touches the $x$-axis.

(b) The graph of $y = \sin x$ is translated by $\begin{pmatrix} -90 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Write down its equation and explain why it is the graph of $y = \cos x$.

Left $90°$: $y = \sin(x + 90°)$. The peak of $\sin$ at $(90°, 1)$ moves to $(0°, 1)$ and its root at $(0°, 0)$ moves to $(-90°, 0)$: exactly the shape and position of $\cos x$.
:::

::: example New intercepts after a translation
$f(x) = x^2 - 4x - 5$. Find the coordinates of the points where $y = f(x + 2)$ meets the axes.

Roots of $f$: $(x - 5)(x + 1) = 0$, so $x = 5$ or $x = -1$. Left 2: roots of $f(x + 2)$ are $x = 3$ and $x = -3$.

$y$-intercept: put $x = 0$ into $f(x + 2)$, giving $f(2) = 4 - 8 - 5 = -9$. So $(0, -9)$ — not the image of the old intercept $(0, -5)$.

Check by expanding: $f(x + 2) = (x + 2)^2 - 4(x + 2) - 5 = x^2 - 9$. ✓
:::

::: example Combined transformations and asymptotes (grade 9)
(a) The graph of $y = \dfrac1x$ is translated so that its asymptotes are $x = 3$ and $y = -2$. Write down its equation and find where it crosses the $y$-axis.

Right 3, down 2: $y = \dfrac{1}{x - 3} - 2$. At $x = 0$: $y = -\dfrac13 - 2 = -\dfrac73$. So $\left(0, -\dfrac73\right)$.

(b) $y = f(x)$ has a turning point at $(2, -5)$. Find the turning point of $y = -f(x + 1) + 3$.

Inside: left 1 → $(1, -5)$. Minus sign: reflect in the $x$-axis → $(1, 5)$. $+3$: up 3 → $(1, 8)$.
:::

::: q
The point $(3, -2)$ lies on $y = f(x)$. Write down its image on (a) $y = f(x) + 5$ (b) $y = f(x + 5)$ (c) $y = -f(x)$ (d) $y = f(-x)$.
---
(a) $(3, 3)$ (b) $(-2, -2)$ (c) $(3, 2)$ (d) $(-3, -2)$.
:::

::: q
Describe fully the single transformation that maps $y = x^3$ onto $y = x^3 - 7$.
---
Translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -7 \end{pmatrix}$ (down 7).
:::

::: q
The graph of $y = x^2$ is translated by $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$. Write down the equation of the new graph.
---
$y = (x + 2)^2 + 5$ (or $y = x^2 + 4x + 9$).
:::

::: q
The graph of $y = \sin x$ is reflected in the $x$-axis. Write down its equation and the coordinates of its maximum point for $0° \le x \le 360°$.
---
$y = -\sin x$. The minimum of $\sin x$ at $(270°, -1)$ becomes the maximum $(270°, 1)$.
:::

::: q
Find the vector of the translation that maps $y = x^2$ onto $y = x^2 + 6x + 4$.
---
$(x + 3)^2 - 9 + 4 = (x + 3)^2 - 5$: translation by $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}$.
:::

::: q
$f(x) = x^2 - 2x$. Find the roots of $y = f(x - 3)$.
---
Roots of $f$: $x(x - 2) = 0$, so $0$ and $2$. Right 3: $x = 3$ and $x = 5$.
:::

::: q
The graph of $y = 2^x$ is reflected in the $y$-axis. Write down its equation and one point it passes through.
---
$y = 2^{-x}$ (the same as $y = 0.5^x$). It still passes through $(0, 1)$; also $(-1, 2)$.
:::

::: q
Write down the equations of the asymptotes of $y = \dfrac{1}{x + 4}$.
---
It is $y = \dfrac1x$ moved left 4: $x = -4$ and $y = 0$.
:::

::: q
Explain why the graph of $y = f(-x)$ is the same as the graph of $y = f(x)$ when $f(x) = x^2 + 1$.
---
$f(-x) = (-x)^2 + 1 = x^2 + 1 = f(x)$. The graph is symmetrical about the $y$-axis, so reflecting it in the $y$-axis leaves it unchanged.
:::

::: q
$g(x) = \cos(x - 90°)$. Find the roots of $y = g(x)$ for $0° \le x \le 360°$.
---
$\cos x$ has roots at $-90°, 90°, 270°$. Right $90°$: $0°, 180°, 360°$. (Indeed $\cos(x - 90°) = \sin x$.)
:::

::: watch
- **Inside the bracket goes the opposite way.** $f(x + 3)$ moves the graph **left** 3; $f(x - 3)$ moves it **right** 3. This is the single most common error.
- **Mixing up the reflections.** $-f(x)$ changes $y$ (reflect in the $x$-axis); $f(-x)$ changes $x$ (reflect in the $y$-axis).
- **"Describe fully"** needs the word *translation* with a column vector, or *reflection* with the axis named.
- **Vector the wrong way up.** The top number is the horizontal move. $\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$ is up 4, not right 4.
- **Assuming intercepts move with the graph.** After a translation the $y$-intercept must be recalculated from the new equation.
- **Forgetting the asymptotes** when sketching a transformed reciprocal or tan graph.
:::
