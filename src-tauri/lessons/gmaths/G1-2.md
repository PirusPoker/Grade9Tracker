## What it is

Geometry starts with a shared vocabulary and a small set of exact drawings. This topic covers the words and labels every geometry question assumes, the four **ruler and compass constructions**, and **loci** — sets of points that obey a rule.

### The language of geometry

- A **point** has position but no size; points are named with capital letters, $A$, $B$, $C$.
- A **line** goes on for ever in both directions; a **line segment** $AB$ has two end points. A **plane** is a flat surface that extends for ever.
- **Parallel** lines never meet and stay the same distance apart (marked with matching arrows). **Perpendicular** lines meet at a **right angle**, $90°$ (marked with a small square).
- A **polygon** is a closed shape made of straight sides; the corners are **vertices** (singular *vertex*). On a 3D solid, the lines where faces meet are **edges**.
- A **regular polygon** has all sides equal **and** all angles equal. A rhombus has equal sides but is not regular (its angles differ); a rectangle has equal angles but is not regular.
- **Line symmetry**: the shape folds onto itself along a mirror line. **Rotational symmetry of order $n$**: it looks the same $n$ times in a full turn. A regular $n$-sided polygon has $n$ lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order $n$.

### Labelling triangles and angles

- Triangle $ABC$ has vertices $A$, $B$, $C$. The side **opposite** angle $A$ is labelled $a$ (it is $BC$), opposite $B$ is $b$ ($AC$), opposite $C$ is $c$ ($AB$). This convention is used by the sine and cosine rules.
- **Angle $ABC$** (written $\angle ABC$ or $A\hat{B}C$) is the angle **at the middle letter**, $B$, between the lines $BA$ and $BC$.
- Equal sides are marked with matching dashes; equal angles with matching arcs.

**Drawing from a written description** means turning words into a sketch: "$PQRS$ is a kite with $PQ = PS$ and angle $QPS = 70°$" — draw it, label the vertices in order round the shape, and mark the equal sides and the angle.

### The four standard constructions

Use only a ruler (for straight lines) and compasses. **Leave all construction arcs visible** — they are the evidence for the marks.

1. **Perpendicular bisector of a line segment $AB$.** Open the compasses to more than half of $AB$. With the point on $A$, draw arcs above and below the line; keep the same radius and repeat from $B$. Join the two points where the arcs cross. This line cuts $AB$ in half at right angles.
2. **Perpendicular from a point $P$ to a line.** With the point on $P$, draw an arc cutting the line twice, at $X$ and $Y$. Now construct the perpendicular bisector of $XY$ (arcs from $X$ and $Y$, same radius, crossing on the far side of the line). The line through $P$ and that crossing point is perpendicular to the line.
3. **Perpendicular at a point $P$ on a line.** With the point on $P$, mark two points $X$ and $Y$ on the line, equal distances either side. Bisect $XY$ as in construction 1: the bisector passes through $P$.
4. **Angle bisector.** With the point on the vertex, draw an arc cutting both arms. From each of those two points draw arcs of the same radius that cross inside the angle. Join the vertex to the crossing point.

**Other constructions built from these:** an equilateral triangle (arcs of radius equal to the side from each end) gives a $60°$ angle; bisect it for $30°$. A perpendicular gives $90°$; bisect it for $45°$. A triangle given three sides (SSS) is drawn with a ruler for one side and arcs for the other two.

### Why the constructions work

In construction 1, both arc points are the same distance from $A$ as from $B$. Joining $A$, the two arc points and $B$ gives a **rhombus** (all four sides equal the compass radius), and the diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles. The angle bisector works by congruent triangles (SSS): the vertex, the two arc points on the arms and the crossing point form two triangles with all three sides equal.

### Loci

A **locus** (plural *loci*) is the set of all points that satisfy a rule.

| Rule | Locus |
|---|---|
| a fixed distance $r$ from a point | a **circle**, radius $r$ |
| less than $r$ from a point | the region **inside** that circle |
| a fixed distance from a line segment | a "**racetrack**": two parallel lines joined by semicircles at the ends |
| equidistant from two points $A$ and $B$ | the **perpendicular bisector** of $AB$ |
| equidistant from two intersecting lines | the **angle bisector** |
| closer to $A$ than to $B$ | the side of the perpendicular bisector containing $A$ |

Region questions combine rules: "within 3 km of the mast **and** closer to road $X$ than road $Y$" — draw each locus accurately to scale, then shade (or clearly label) the region that satisfies **all** the conditions. Solid lines usually mean "on or within"; say so if the question asks.

### The shortest distance

The shortest distance from a point to a line is the **perpendicular distance**. If $N$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ and $Q$ is any other point on the line, triangle $PNQ$ is right-angled at $N$, so $PQ$ is its hypotenuse and $PQ > PN$. This is why "the shortest path from the house to the road" is the perpendicular from a point to a line (construction 2).

::: terms
- **Vertex**: a corner of a polygon or solid, where sides or edges meet (plural vertices).
- **Perpendicular**: at right angles (90°) to a line or plane.
- **Regular polygon**: a polygon with all sides equal and all interior angles equal.
- **Perpendicular bisector**: the line that cuts a line segment in half at right angles; every point on it is equidistant from the two ends.
- **Angle bisector**: the line that cuts an angle into two equal halves; every point on it is equidistant from the two arms.
- **Locus**: the set of all points that obey a given rule, e.g. all points 3 cm from a point form a circle.
- **Perpendicular distance**: the distance from a point to a line measured at right angles; it is the shortest distance to the line.
:::

::: method
1. **Read the rule and name the locus** before drawing: "equidistant from two points" → perpendicular bisector; "equidistant from two lines" → angle bisector; "fixed distance from a point" → circle; "fixed distance from a line" → parallel line (or racetrack round a segment).
2. **Use compasses and ruler only** unless a protractor is allowed. Construction questions are usually on the non-calculator Paper 1 but can appear on any paper.
3. **Leave every arc.** A correct line with no arcs scores at most a partial mark; arcs are how the examiner sees the method (typically M1 for correct arcs, A1 for an accurate line within 2 mm or 2°).
4. **Scale drawings:** convert real distances first (e.g. 1 cm to 2 m, so 5 m → 2.5 cm) and write the scaled length down.
5. **Region questions:** draw each locus, then shade or label **R** the region satisfying all the conditions. Check one point inside the region against every rule.
6. **Areas of loci:** split the region into rectangles, circles or fractions of circles. Leave "in terms of $\pi$" if asked; otherwise give 3 significant figures.
7. **Coordinates:** the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ passes through the **midpoint** with gradient $-\dfrac{1}{m_{AB}}$.
8. **Explain or "show that" a construction works** using congruent triangles (SSS) or rhombus properties — not "it looks right".
:::

::: example Constructing a 30° angle
Describe how to construct an angle of $30°$ at point $A$ on line $AB$ using only a ruler and compasses.

With the point on $A$, draw an arc cutting $AB$ at $X$. With the same radius and the point on $X$, draw an arc cutting the first arc at $Y$. Triangle $AXY$ has three sides equal to the radius, so it is **equilateral** and $\angle XAY = 60°$. Now bisect $\angle XAY$: arcs of equal radius from $X$ and $Y$ crossing at $Z$. Join $AZ$: $\angle ZAB = 30°$.
:::

::: example Describing a region
A garden is a rectangle $ABCD$ with $AB = 12$ m and $BC = 8$ m. A pond must be less than 5 m from corner $A$ and closer to side $AB$ than to side $AD$. Describe the region and how you would draw it at a scale of 1 cm to 2 m.

- "Less than 5 m from $A$": the inside of a circle centre $A$, radius $5 \div 2 = 2.5$ cm.
- "Closer to $AB$ than to $AD$": the side of the **bisector of angle $DAB$** nearest $AB$. Since angle $DAB = 90°$, the bisector is at $45°$.
- The region is a **sector** of radius 2.5 cm between $AB$ and the angle bisector — an eighth of a circle, real area $\dfrac{1}{8}\pi(5^2) = 9.82$ m².
:::

::: example Area of a locus round a shape
A flower bed is a rectangle 3 m by 2 m. A path is made from all points outside the bed within 1 m of it. Find the area of the path.

The locus is a racetrack round the rectangle: four strips and four quarter circles at the corners.

Strips: $2 \times (3 \times 1) + 2 \times (2 \times 1) = 10$ m². Corners: four quarter circles make one full circle, $\pi \times 1^2 = \pi$ m².

Area $= 10 + \pi = 13.1$ m² (3 s.f.).
:::

::: example Equidistant from two points on a grid (grade 7)
$A$ is $(0, 0)$ and $B$ is $(4, 2)$. Find the equation of the locus of points equidistant from $A$ and $B$.

The locus is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. Midpoint $(2, 1)$. Gradient of $AB = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac12$, so the perpendicular gradient is $-2$.

$y - 1 = -2(x - 2) \Rightarrow y = -2x + 5$.

Check: $(0, 5)$ is on the line; distance to $A$ is 5 and to $B$ is $\sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5$. ✓
:::

::: example Shortest distance to a line (grade 8)
Find the shortest distance from $P(0, 5)$ to the line $y = x$.

The shortest distance is along the perpendicular. The perpendicular to $y = x$ (gradient 1) has gradient $-1$; through $P$ it is $y = -x + 5$.

Meet: $x = -x + 5 \Rightarrow x = 2.5$, so the foot is $N(2.5, 2.5)$.

$PN = \sqrt{2.5^2 + 2.5^2} = 2.5\sqrt2 = 3.54$ (3 s.f.).
:::

::: example A goat and a shed (grade 9)
A shed is a rectangle 5 m by 3 m. A goat is tied to one outside corner of the shed with a 4 m rope. Find the area the goat can reach, in terms of $\pi$.

Around the corner the goat sweeps **three-quarters** of a circle of radius 4: $\dfrac34 \pi(4^2) = 12\pi$.

Along the 5 m wall the rope (4 m) does not reach the next corner. Along the 3 m wall it does, with $4 - 3 = 1$ m to spare, so the goat swings round that corner through a **quarter circle** of radius 1: $\dfrac14 \pi(1^2) = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

Total $= 12\pi + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{49\pi}{4}$ m² $(= 38.5$ m²$)$.
:::

::: q
Describe the locus of points exactly 4 cm from a point $P$, and the locus of points less than 4 cm from $P$.
---
Exactly 4 cm: a **circle** centre $P$, radius 4 cm. Less than 4 cm: the region **inside** that circle (boundary not included, usually drawn dashed).
:::

::: q
In triangle $PQR$, which side is labelled $q$? Which angle is $\angle PRQ$?
---
$q$ is the side opposite $Q$, which is **$PR$**. $\angle PRQ$ is the angle at **$R$** (the middle letter), between $RP$ and $RQ$.
:::

::: q
A house is near a straight road. Which construction gives the shortest path from the house to the road, and why is it shortest?
---
The **perpendicular from a point to a line**. Any other path is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose shorter side is the perpendicular, so it is longer.
:::

::: q
How many lines of symmetry, and what order of rotational symmetry, does a regular hexagon have? Is a rhombus a regular polygon?
---
6 lines, order 6. A rhombus is **not** regular: its sides are equal but its angles are not (unless it is a square).
:::

::: q
On a map with scale $1 : 25\,000$, a phone mast has a range of 2 km. What should you draw on the map?
---
$2 \text{ km} = 200\,000 \text{ cm}$; $200000 \div 25000 = 8$. A circle centre the mast, **radius 8 cm**.
:::

::: q
Find the equation of the locus of points equidistant from $A(2, 1)$ and $B(6, 5)$.
---
Midpoint $(4, 3)$; gradient $AB = 1$, so perpendicular gradient $-1$. $y - 3 = -(x - 4)$: **$y = -x + 7$**.
:::

::: q
A straight fence is 10 m long. Find the area of the region within 2 m of the fence (on both sides), to 3 s.f.
---
Racetrack: rectangle $10 \times 4 = 40$ plus two semicircles of radius 2 making one circle, $4\pi$. Total $40 + 4\pi = $ **52.6 m²**.
:::

::: q
A horse is tied to the middle of a long straight wall with a 3 m rope. Find the area it can graze, in terms of $\pi$.
---
A semicircle of radius 3: $\dfrac12 \pi (3^2) = $ **$\dfrac{9\pi}{2}$ m²**.
:::

::: q
Explain, using congruent triangles, why the angle bisector construction works.
---
Vertex $O$; arcs meet the arms at $X$ and $Y$ with $OX = OY$; the crossing point $Z$ has $XZ = YZ$ (same radius); $OZ$ is common. So triangles $OXZ$ and $OYZ$ are **congruent (SSS)**, and $\angle XOZ = \angle YOZ$.
:::

::: watch
- **Rubbing out the arcs** (or never drawing them) — the arcs are the method mark. Keep them faint but visible.
- **Compasses changing width** between the two ends of a perpendicular bisector: both sets of arcs must use the same radius.
- **Wrong locus for "equidistant from two lines"**: that is the angle bisector, not the perpendicular bisector.
- **Forgetting the rounded ends** of a locus round a line segment or a rectangle — the corners are quarter circles, not square corners.
- **Not converting with the scale**: 5 km at 1 cm to 2 km is 2.5 cm, not 5 cm.
- **Region not identified**: shade or label the final region, and make sure it satisfies every condition, not just the last one drawn.
- **Angle names**: $\angle ABC$ is at $B$ — the middle letter — not at $A$.
:::

::: test
In triangle $XYZ$, angle $XYZ = 90°$.

(a) Which side is the hypotenuse? (b) Using the standard convention, what letter labels that side? (2)
---
(a) The side opposite the right angle at $Y$: **$XZ$** (B1). (b) It is opposite $Y$, so it is labelled **$y$** (B1).
:::

::: test
A line segment $AB$ is 8 cm long. Describe the locus of points exactly 3 cm from $AB$ and work out the length of this locus, to 3 s.f. (3)
---
Two lines parallel to $AB$, 3 cm either side, joined by semicircles of radius 3 cm centred on $A$ and $B$ (B1). Length $= 2 \times 8 + 2\pi \times 3$ (M1) $= 16 + 6\pi = $ **34.8 cm** (A1).
:::

::: test
Describe how to construct the perpendicular bisector of a line segment $PQ$ using only a ruler and compasses. (2)
---
Open the compasses to more than half of $PQ$; draw arcs above and below $PQ$ from $P$, then from $Q$ with the same radius (B1). Join the two points where the arcs cross (B1).
:::

::: test
A boat must stay more than 5 km from a lighthouse $L$ and must be closer to port $P$ than to port $Q$. The map scale is 1 cm to 2 km. Describe the loci you would draw and the region the boat must be in. (3)
---
Circle centre $L$, radius $5 \div 2 = 2.5$ cm (B1). Perpendicular bisector of $PQ$ (B1). The region **outside** the circle and on the **same side** of the bisector as $P$ (B1).
:::

::: test
$A$ is the point $(-1, 2)$ and $B$ is the point $(3, 6)$. Point $C$ lies on the $x$-axis and is equidistant from $A$ and $B$. Find the coordinates of $C$. (4)
---
Midpoint of $AB = (1, 4)$ (M1). Gradient $AB = \dfrac{4}{4} = 1$, perpendicular gradient $-1$ (M1). Bisector: $y = -x + 5$ (A1). On the $x$-axis $y = 0$: **$C = (5, 0)$** (A1). Check: $CA = CB = \sqrt{40}$.
:::

::: test
A rectangular pool measures 8 m by 5 m. A safety zone is all points outside the pool within 2 m of its edge. Find the area of the safety zone, to 3 s.f. (3)
---
Strips: $2(8 \times 2) + 2(5 \times 2) = 52$ (M1). Corners: four quarter circles $= \pi \times 2^2 = 4\pi$ (M1). Total $52 + 4\pi = $ **64.6 m²** (A1).
:::

::: test
Find the shortest distance from the point $(7, 1)$ to the line $y = 2x$. Give your answer to 3 s.f. (4)
---
Perpendicular gradient $-\dfrac12$; line $y = -\dfrac12 x + 4.5$ (M1). Solve with $y = 2x$: $2.5x = 4.5$, $x = 1.8$, foot $(1.8, 3.6)$ (M1). Distance $= \sqrt{5.2^2 + 2.6^2} = \sqrt{33.8}$ (M1) $= $ **5.81** (A1).
:::

::: test
$M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $P$ is any point on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. Prove that $PA = PB$. (3)
---
$AM = MB$ ($M$ is the midpoint); $\angle PMA = \angle PMB = 90°$ (perpendicular); $PM$ is common (M1 for all three). So triangles $PMA$ and $PMB$ are congruent (**SAS**) (A1), and therefore $PA = PB$ (corresponding sides of congruent triangles) (A1).
:::

::: test
A dog is tied to one outside corner of a rectangular shed measuring 6 m by 2 m, with a rope 5 m long. Work out the area the dog can reach, in terms of $\pi$. (5)
---
Three-quarter circle, radius 5: $\dfrac34 \pi (25) = \dfrac{75\pi}{4}$ (M1 A1). Along the 6 m wall the rope does not reach the corner. Along the 2 m wall 3 m of rope is left, giving a quarter circle of radius 3: $\dfrac14 \pi(9) = \dfrac{9\pi}{4}$ (M1 A1). Total $= \dfrac{84\pi}{4} = $ **$21\pi$ m²** (A1).
:::
