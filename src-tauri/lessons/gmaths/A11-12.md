## What it is

You need to recognise, sketch and interpret seven families of graph, and read the key features of a quadratic both from a graph and from its equation. A **sketch** is not a plot: it shows the right shape and labels the important points (intercepts, turning points, asymptotes) — no table of values needed.

### Quadratic graphs: roots, intercepts and turning points

$y = ax^2 + bx + c$ is a **parabola**: a U-shape if $a > 0$ (a minimum point), an upside-down U if $a < 0$ (a maximum point).

- **$y$-intercept**: put $x = 0$, so it is $(0, c)$.
- **Roots**: where the curve crosses the $x$-axis — solve $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$. From a graph, read the $x$ values where $y = 0$; algebraically, factorise (or use the formula).
- **Line of symmetry**: every parabola is symmetrical about a vertical line through its turning point. It lies **midway between the roots** — and midway between any two points at the same height.
- **Turning point**: the minimum or maximum, on the line of symmetry.

Example: $y = x^2 - 6x + 5 = (x - 1)(x - 5)$. Roots $x = 1$ and $x = 5$; $y$-intercept $(0, 5)$; line of symmetry $x = 3$; at $x = 3$, $y = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4$, so the minimum is $(3, -4)$.

### Turning points by completing the square

Write the quadratic as $y = a(x + p)^2 + q$. A square is never negative, so:

$$y = (x + p)^2 + q \text{ has its minimum point at } (-p,\ q)$$

because $(x + p)^2$ is smallest (zero) when $x = -p$. Watch the sign: $(x - 3)^2 - 4$ gives $(3, -4)$.

To complete the square for $x^2 + bx + c$: halve $b$, write $\left(x + \frac b2\right)^2$, then subtract $\left(\frac b2\right)^2$ and add $c$.

- $x^2 - 6x + 5 = (x - 3)^2 - 9 + 5 = (x - 3)^2 - 4$: minimum $(3, -4)$ — matching the answer above.
- If $a \ne 1$, take $a$ out of the $x$ terms first: $2x^2 + 8x + 3 = 2(x^2 + 4x) + 3 = 2\big((x + 2)^2 - 4\big) + 3 = 2(x + 2)^2 - 5$. Minimum $(-2, -5)$.
- If $a < 0$ the turning point is a **maximum**: $-x^2 + 4x + 1 = -(x^2 - 4x) + 1 = -\big((x - 2)^2 - 4\big) + 1 = -(x - 2)^2 + 5$. Maximum $(2, 5)$.

The completed square also tells you how many roots there are. $y = (x + 2)^2 + 5$ has minimum $(-2, 5)$, which is **above** the $x$-axis, so the curve never crosses it: no real roots.

**Interpreting** a turning point in context: if the height of a ball is $h = 20t - 5t^2 = -5(t - 2)^2 + 20$, the maximum height is 20 m, reached at $t = 2$ s, and the roots $t = 0$ and $t = 4$ are when it leaves and returns to the ground.

### Linear and cubic graphs

- **Linear** $y = mx + c$: a straight line, gradient $m$, $y$-intercept $c$.
- **Simple cubic**: $y = x^3$ passes through the origin, rising from bottom-left to top-right with a flat "S" at $(0, 0)$. $y = -x^3$ falls from top-left to bottom-right.
- A factorised cubic such as $y = (x + 1)(x - 2)(x - 4)$ crosses the $x$-axis at $-1$, $2$ and $4$. With $+x^3$ it ends bottom-left and top-right; the $y$-intercept is $(1)(-2)(-4) = 8$. A **repeated** factor, as in $(x - 2)^2$, makes the curve **touch** the axis at $x = 2$ and turn back, instead of crossing.

### The reciprocal graph

$y = \dfrac1x$, with $x \ne 0$ (you cannot divide by zero). Two separate branches: top-right (both positive) and bottom-left (both negative). As $x$ grows, $y$ shrinks towards 0; as $x$ gets close to 0, $y$ becomes huge. Both **axes are asymptotes** — the curve gets ever closer but never touches them. It is symmetrical in the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$. $y = \dfrac kx$ with $k > 0$ has the same shape; with $k < 0$ the branches are top-left and bottom-right.

### Exponential graphs

$y = k^x$ for a positive number $k$:

- Always passes through $(0, 1)$, because $k^0 = 1$.
- $k > 1$ (e.g. $y = 2^x$): **growth** — rises ever more steeply to the right; to the left it flattens towards the $x$-axis.
- $0 < k < 1$ (e.g. $y = 0.5^x$): **decay** — the mirror image in the $y$-axis.
- $y$ is always positive: the $x$-axis is an asymptote.

If $y = k^x$ passes through $(3, 64)$ then $k^3 = 64$, so $k = 4$.

### Trigonometric graphs, for angles of any size

| Graph | Key points ($0°$ to $360°$) | Range | Repeats every |
|---|---|---|---|
| $y = \sin x$ | $(0, 0), (90, 1), (180, 0), (270, -1), (360, 0)$ | $-1$ to $1$ | $360°$ |
| $y = \cos x$ | $(0, 1), (90, 0), (180, -1), (270, 0), (360, 1)$ | $-1$ to $1$ | $360°$ |
| $y = \tan x$ | $(0, 0), (45, 1), (135, -1), (180, 0)$ | all values | $180°$ |

$y = \tan x$ has vertical asymptotes at $x = 90°, 270°, \dots$ (and $-90°$), where it is undefined. All three graphs carry on in both directions, so negative angles and angles beyond $360°$ are fine: $\sin(-30°) = -0.5$ and $\cos 420° = \cos 60° = 0.5$.

Solving $\sin x = k$ from the graph uses its symmetry. The calculator gives one answer $x_1 = \sin^{-1} k$:

- $\sin x = k$: $x_1$ and $180° - x_1$, then add or subtract $360°$.
- $\cos x = k$: $x_1$ and $360° - x_1$ (the graph is symmetrical about $x = 0$ and $x = 180°$), then $\pm 360°$.
- $\tan x = k$: $x_1$, then add or subtract $180°$.

Example: $\sin x = 0.5$ for $0° \le x \le 720°$ gives $30°$, $150°$, and adding $360°$: $390°$, $510°$.

::: terms
- **Root**: a value of x where a graph crosses (or touches) the x-axis, so y = 0 there.
- **Turning point**: the point where a curve changes direction — a minimum or a maximum.
- **Line of symmetry (of a parabola)**: the vertical line through the turning point, midway between the roots.
- **Completing the square**: rewriting ax² + bx + c as a(x + p)² + q, which shows the turning point (−p, q).
- **Asymptote**: a line that a curve approaches more and more closely but never meets.
- **Reciprocal function**: y = 1/x, defined for every x except 0; both axes are asymptotes.
- **Exponential function**: y = kˣ with k positive; it always passes through (0, 1) and stays above the x-axis.
- **Period**: the interval after which a graph repeats — 360° for sin and cos, 180° for tan.
:::

::: method
1. **Identify the family from the equation** before drawing anything: $x^2$ → parabola; $x^3$ → S-shape; $\frac kx$ → two branches; $k^x$ → exponential through $(0, 1)$; $\sin$, $\cos$, $\tan$ → waves or repeating branches.
2. **Quadratic sketch — three facts.** $y$-intercept (put $x = 0$); roots (factorise; this is usually the non-calculator Paper 1 route); turning point (midway between the roots, or by completing the square). Label all three with coordinates — unlabelled sketches lose the marks.
3. **Completing the square.** Take out $a$ if $a \ne 1$; halve the coefficient of $x$; subtract the square of that half; tidy the constant. The turning point is $(-p, q)$ — write the coordinates, not just the bracket. Questions saying "write in the form $(x + a)^2 + b$" award M1 for the bracket and A1 for the constant.
4. **Cubic sketch.** Mark the roots from the factors; the sign of $x^3$ fixes the ends; a squared factor touches the axis; put in the $y$-intercept.
5. **Reciprocal and exponential.** Show the asymptotes (the axes) clearly and do not let the curve touch them; for $k^x$ mark $(0, 1)$.
6. **Trig equations.** Get $x_1$ from the calculator (Papers 2 and 3), use the symmetry rule for the second solution, then add or subtract $360°$ ($180°$ for tan) until you have every solution in the given range — and none outside it.
7. **"Show that" or "explain"** questions about the number of roots: complete the square and say where the turning point is relative to the $x$-axis, in words.
:::

::: example Sketching a quadratic
Sketch $y = x^2 - 2x - 8$, labelling the intercepts and the turning point.

$y$-intercept: $(0, -8)$.

Roots: $(x + 2)(x - 4) = 0$, so $(-2, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$.

Line of symmetry midway: $x = \dfrac{-2 + 4}{2} = 1$. Then $y = 1 - 2 - 8 = -9$. Minimum point $(1, -9)$.

Sketch: a U-shape crossing the $x$-axis at $-2$ and $4$, through $(0, -8)$, lowest at $(1, -9)$.
:::

::: example Turning point with a negative coefficient
Write $y = 7 + 6x - x^2$ in the form $a - (x - b)^2$ and hence write down the coordinates of the turning point. Is it a maximum or a minimum?

$-x^2 + 6x + 7 = -(x^2 - 6x) + 7 = -\big((x - 3)^2 - 9\big) + 7 = 16 - (x - 3)^2$.

$a = 16$, $b = 3$. The square is subtracted, so $y$ is largest when $(x - 3)^2 = 0$: **maximum** at $(3, 16)$.

Check: at $x = 3$, $7 + 18 - 9 = 16$. ✓
:::

::: example Sketching a cubic with a repeated root
Sketch $y = (x + 1)(x - 2)^2$.

Roots: $x = -1$ (crosses) and $x = 2$ (repeated — touches and turns).

$y$-intercept: $(0 + 1)(0 - 2)^2 = 4$, so $(0, 4)$.

Expanding would start with $+x^3$, so it comes up from bottom-left, crosses at $-1$, rises through $(0, 4)$ to a hump, comes down to touch the axis at $(2, 0)$, then rises to the top-right.
:::

::: example Trig solutions beyond 360°
Solve $\sin x = -0.5$ for $0° \le x \le 720°$.

Calculator: $\sin^{-1}(-0.5) = -30°$, outside the range.

Symmetry: the other solution is $180° - (-30°) = 210°$. Also $-30° + 360° = 330°$.

So in $0°$–$360°$: $210°$, $330°$. Add $360°$: $570°$, $690°$.

Answer: $x = 210°, 330°, 570°, 690°$. (Check on the graph: $\sin$ is negative between $180°$ and $360°$. ✓)
:::

::: example Finding the equation from the turning point (grade 9)
A quadratic curve has a minimum point at $(3, -2)$ and passes through $(5, 6)$. Find its equation in the form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$.

Turning point $(3, -2)$ means $y = a(x - 3)^2 - 2$.

Substitute $(5, 6)$: $6 = a(2)^2 - 2$, so $4a = 8$ and $a = 2$.

$y = 2(x - 3)^2 - 2 = 2(x^2 - 6x + 9) - 2 = 2x^2 - 12x + 16$.

Check: roots of $2x^2 - 12x + 16 = 2(x - 2)(x - 4)$ are $2$ and $4$, midway $3$. ✓
:::

::: q
For $y = x^2 - 7x + 12$, write down the roots, the $y$-intercept, the line of symmetry and the turning point.
---
$(x - 3)(x - 4)$: roots $3$ and $4$. $y$-intercept $(0, 12)$. Line of symmetry $x = 3.5$. At $x = 3.5$: $y = (0.5)(-0.5) = -0.25$, so minimum $(3.5, -0.25)$.
:::

::: q
Write $x^2 + 8x + 11$ in the form $(x + a)^2 + b$ and give the turning point of $y = x^2 + 8x + 11$.
---
$(x + 4)^2 - 16 + 11 = (x + 4)^2 - 5$. Minimum $(-4, -5)$.
:::

::: q
Find the turning point of $y = x^2 - 5x + 2$.
---
$\left(x - \tfrac52\right)^2 - \tfrac{25}{4} + 2 = \left(x - \tfrac52\right)^2 - \tfrac{17}{4}$. Minimum $(2.5, -4.25)$.
:::

::: q
Write $3x^2 - 12x + 7$ in the form $a(x + b)^2 + c$ and state the turning point of its graph.
---
$3(x^2 - 4x) + 7 = 3\big((x - 2)^2 - 4\big) + 7 = 3(x - 2)^2 - 5$. Minimum $(2, -5)$.
:::

::: q
Sketch $y = x(x - 3)(x + 2)$, labelling where it meets the axes.
---
Roots $x = -2, 0, 3$; $y$-intercept $(0, 0)$. Positive $x^3$: up from bottom-left, crosses at $-2$, hump, down through the origin, dip, crosses at $3$, up to top-right.
:::

::: q
Explain, without solving, why $x^2 + 4x + 9 = 0$ has no real solutions.
---
$x^2 + 4x + 9 = (x + 2)^2 + 5$. The minimum of $y = x^2 + 4x + 9$ is $(-2, 5)$, above the $x$-axis, so the graph never meets it.
:::

::: q
Solve $\cos x = 0.5$ for $0° \le x \le 720°$.
---
$\cos^{-1} 0.5 = 60°$; $360° - 60° = 300°$; add $360°$: $420°$, $660°$. So $60°, 300°, 420°, 660°$.
:::

::: q
The graph of $y = k^x$ passes through $(3, 125)$. Find $k$, and the value of $y$ when $x = -1$.
---
$k^3 = 125 \Rightarrow k = 5$. $y = 5^{-1} = \dfrac15 = 0.2$.
:::

::: q
Solve $\tan x = -1$ for $0° \le x \le 360°$.
---
$\tan^{-1}(-1) = -45°$. Add $180°$: $135°$; add again: $315°$. So $x = 135°, 315°$.
:::

::: q
The points $(-1, 6)$ and $(5, 6)$ lie on a parabola. Write down the equation of its line of symmetry.
---
Same height, so the line of symmetry is midway: $x = \dfrac{-1 + 5}{2} = 2$.
:::

::: watch
- **Sign of the turning point.** $(x - 3)^2 - 4$ gives $(3, -4)$, not $(-3, -4)$. The $x$-coordinate has the opposite sign to the number in the bracket; the $y$-coordinate keeps its sign.
- **Forgetting to take out $a$.** $2x^2 + 8x + 3$ is not $(2x + 2)^2 - \dots$. Factor out the 2 from the $x$ terms first, and multiply the subtracted square by 2.
- **Unlabelled sketches.** A sketch without the intercepts and turning point marked as coordinates scores little even if the shape is right.
- **Asymptotes touched.** Reciprocal and exponential curves must not touch or cross their asymptotes; $\tan x$ must not be joined across $90°$.
- **Missing trig solutions.** Stopping at the calculator's answer loses marks — and so does including a negative angle when the range starts at $0°$.
- **Exponential through the origin.** $y = k^x$ passes through $(0, 1)$, never $(0, 0)$.
:::

A quadratic curve crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 1$ and $x = 7$ and passes through $(0, 14)$. Find the coordinates of its turning point. (4)
---
$y = a(x - 1)(x - 7)$ (M1). At $(0, 14)$: $14 = 7a$, so $a = 2$ (A1). Line of symmetry $x = 4$ (M1). $y = 2(3)(-3) = -18$: turning point $(4, -18)$ (A1).

::: test
The height, $h$ metres, of a ball $t$ seconds after it is thrown is $h = 2 + 12t - 3t^2$.
(a) By completing the square, find the maximum height and when it occurs. (b) Explain what the $h$-intercept of the graph represents. (4)
---
(a) $-3(t^2 - 4t) + 2 = -3\big((t - 2)^2 - 4\big) + 2$ (M1) $= 14 - 3(t - 2)^2$ (A1). Maximum height 14 m at $t = 2$ s (A1).
(b) At $t = 0$, $h = 2$: the ball is thrown from 2 m above the ground (B1).
:::
