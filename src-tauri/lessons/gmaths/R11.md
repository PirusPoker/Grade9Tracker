## What it is

A **compound unit** combines two measurements, usually as "one quantity **per** another": kilometres per hour, grams per cubic centimetre, pounds per hour, newtons per square metre, pence per gram. The unit itself tells you the formula — "per" means "divided by".

### The three formulas to learn

$$\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} \qquad \text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}} \qquad \text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$$

Learn them — do not count on them being printed in the exam. Each rearranges in the same way, so from speed: $\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}$ and $\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$. A formula triangle (the "top" quantity over the other two) is a fine memory aid.

| Quantity | Formula | Typical units |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | distance ÷ time | km/h, m/s, mph |
| Density | mass ÷ volume | g/cm³, kg/m³ |
| Pressure | force ÷ area | N/m² (pascals), N/cm² |

### Speed and time

Time must be in the unit the speed uses. **Minutes are not decimals of an hour**: $2$ h $15$ min $= 2.25$ h (divide the minutes by 60), not $2.15$ h. A car covering $162$ km in $2$ h $15$ min averages $162 \div 2.25 = 72$ km/h. Going the other way, $0.85$ h $= 0.85 \times 60 = 51$ minutes.

**Average speed** for a whole journey is always

$$\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}},$$

never the average of the separate speeds. $30$ km at $60$ km/h ($0.5$ h) then $30$ km at $40$ km/h ($0.75$ h) is $60$ km in $1.25$ h: $48$ km/h, not $50$.

### Density and pressure

A gold bar measuring $10$ cm by $4$ cm by $2.5$ cm has volume $100$ cm³; gold has density $19.3$ g/cm³, so its mass is $19.3 \times 100 = 1930$ g $= 1.93$ kg.

A crate with weight (force) $600$ N resting on a face of area $0.12$ m² exerts pressure $600 \div 0.12 = 5000$ N/m². The **smaller** the area, the **greater** the pressure — which is why the same box presses hardest on its smallest face.

### Other rates

- **Rates of pay**: pounds per hour. Overtime at "time and a half" is $1.5 \times$ the normal rate.
- **Unit pricing**: pence per gram (or grams per penny) to find the best buy.
- **Flow rate**: litres per minute. A $180$-litre tank filling at $7.5$ litres/min takes $180 \div 7.5 = 24$ minutes.
- **Population density**: people per km². $1.2$ million people in $480$ km² is $2500$ people/km².
- **Fuel consumption**: km per litre, or litres per 100 km.

### Converting compound units

Convert **each part** separately. For $72$ km/h to m/s: $72$ km $= 72\,000$ m and $1$ h $= 3600$ s, so

$$72 \text{ km/h} = \frac{72\,000}{3600} \text{ m/s} = 20 \text{ m/s}.$$

(Shortcut: km/h $\div 3.6$ = m/s.) For density, $1$ g/cm³ $= \dfrac{1\,000\,000 \text{ cm}^3 \text{ per m}^3}{1000 \text{ g per kg}} = 1000$ kg/m³, so steel at $7.8$ g/cm³ is $7800$ kg/m³. For pressure, $1$ m² $= 10\,000$ cm², so $6$ N/cm² $= 60\,000$ N/m².

::: terms
- **Compound unit**: a unit made from two others, such as km/h or g/cm³, read as "per".
- **Speed**: distance travelled divided by the time taken.
- **Average speed**: total distance divided by total time for the whole journey.
- **Density**: mass divided by volume, often in g/cm³ or kg/m³.
- **Pressure**: force divided by the area it acts on, in N/m² (pascals) or N/cm².
- **Rate**: how much of one quantity there is for each unit of another, such as litres per minute or pounds per hour.
:::

::: method
1. **Write the formula in words first**, then substitute: "density = mass ÷ volume". This line usually earns the first M1.
2. **Make the units match** before substituting: minutes to hours (divide by 60), cm³ to m³, grams to kilograms. Write the unit next to every number.
3. **Rearrange** the formula for the quantity you want (distance = speed × time, mass = density × volume, force = pressure × area).
4. **Whole journeys and mixtures:** add the totals (total distance and total time; total mass and total volume), then divide. Never average two speeds or two densities.
5. **Converting a compound unit:** convert the top unit and the bottom unit separately, then divide.
6. **Answer with units**, and convert a decimal time back to hours and minutes if asked ("give your answer in minutes").
7. **Non-calculator paper:** use friendly fractions of an hour — 20 min $= \frac13$ h, 45 min $= \frac34$ h. **Calculator paper:** do not round a time before using it again.
:::

::: example Speed with minutes
A coach leaves at 10:40 and arrives at 13:10, travelling 185 km. Work out its average speed.

Time $= 2$ h $30$ min $= 2.5$ h. Speed $= 185 \div 2.5 = 74$ km/h.
:::

::: example Converting compound units
Convert 15 m/s to km/h, and 0.92 g/cm³ to kg/m³.

$15$ m/s $= 15 \times 3600 = 54\,000$ m per hour $= 54$ km/h.

$0.92$ g/cm³: $1$ m³ $= 1\,000\,000$ cm³ has mass $920\,000$ g $= 920$ kg, so $920$ kg/m³.
:::

::: example Average speed for a whole journey
A cyclist rides 60 km to a town at 30 km/h and returns the same way at 20 km/h. Work out her average speed for the round trip.

Times: $60 \div 30 = 2$ h and $60 \div 20 = 3$ h. Total $5$ h for $120$ km.

Average speed $= 120 \div 5 = 24$ km/h — not $\frac{30 + 20}{2} = 25$ km/h, because she spends longer at the slower speed.
:::

::: example Pay with overtime
Sam is paid £11.80 per hour for a 37.5-hour week, and time and a half for overtime. One week he works 41.5 hours. Work out his pay.

Basic: $37.5 \times 11.80 = £442.50$. Overtime rate $= 1.5 \times 11.80 = £17.70$; $4 \times 17.70 = £70.80$.

Total $= £513.30$.
:::

::: example Mixing two metals (grade 8)
An alloy is made from $60$ cm³ of metal A, density $8.9$ g/cm³, and $270$ g of metal B, density $2.7$ g/cm³. Work out the density of the alloy.

Mass of A $= 8.9 \times 60 = 534$ g. Volume of B $= 270 \div 2.7 = 100$ cm³.

Totals: mass $534 + 270 = 804$ g, volume $60 + 100 = 160$ cm³. Density $= 804 \div 160 = 5.025$ g/cm³.
:::

::: example Pressure on different faces (grade 9)
A cuboid measuring 20 cm by 30 cm by 50 cm has weight 180 N. It rests on a table with a pressure of 1800 N/m². Which face is in contact with the table?

Face areas: $0.2 \times 0.3 = 0.06$ m², $0.2 \times 0.5 = 0.1$ m², $0.3 \times 0.5 = 0.15$ m².

Area needed $= \text{force} \div \text{pressure} = 180 \div 1800 = 0.1$ m², so the 20 cm by 50 cm face is on the table.
:::

::: q
A car travels 225 km in 2 hours 30 minutes. Work out its average speed.
---
$2$ h $30$ min $= 2.5$ h; $225 \div 2.5 = 90$ km/h.
:::

::: q
How long does it take to travel 34 km at 40 km/h? Give your answer in minutes.
---
$34 \div 40 = 0.85$ h; $0.85 \times 60 = 51$ minutes.
:::

::: q
Convert 25 m/s to km/h.
---
$25 \times 3600 = 90\,000$ m per hour $= 90$ km/h.
:::

::: q
A block has mass 450 g and volume 60 cm³. Work out its density.
---
$450 \div 60 = 7.5$ g/cm³.
:::

::: q
Concrete has a density of 2400 kg/m³. Work out the mass of 2.5 m³ of concrete.
---
Mass $=$ density $\times$ volume $= 2400 \times 2.5 = 6000$ kg.
:::

::: q
A force of 150 N produces a pressure of 25 N/cm². Work out the area it acts on.
---
Area $=$ force $\div$ pressure $= 150 \div 25 = 6$ cm².
:::

::: q
A county has a population of 1.2 million and an area of 480 km². Work out its population density.
---
$1\,200\,000 \div 480 = 2500$ people per km².
:::

::: q
A tap fills a 180-litre tank at 7.5 litres per minute. A second tap is opened, also at 7.5 litres per minute. How long does it now take to fill the empty tank?
---
Combined rate $15$ litres per minute; $180 \div 15 = 12$ minutes.
:::

::: q
Kai drives 50 km at 100 km/h and then 50 km at 50 km/h. Work out his average speed.
---
Times $0.5$ h and $1$ h, total $1.5$ h for $100$ km. $100 \div 1.5 = 66.7$ km/h (to 3 s.f.) — not 75 km/h.
:::

::: q
A force of 90 N acts on an area of 15 cm². Work out the pressure in N/m².
---
$90 \div 15 = 6$ N/cm². $1$ m² $= 10\,000$ cm², so $6 \times 10\,000 = 60\,000$ N/m². (Or $15$ cm² $= 0.0015$ m², $90 \div 0.0015 = 60\,000$.)
:::

::: watch
- **Hours and minutes are not decimals.** 1 h 45 min is 1.75 h, not 1.45 h. Convert decimal hours back by multiplying the decimal part by 60.
- **Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.** Averaging the two speeds is wrong whenever the times differ.
- **Units must match before you divide.** A force in N over an area in cm² gives N/cm², not N/m²; converting area units uses 10 000 (cm² to m²), not 100.
- For mixtures, add the **masses** and add the **volumes** separately, then divide — never average the densities.
- Leave units off and a correct number can still lose the final mark when the question asks "give the units of your answer".
:::

Change a speed of 30 m/s into km/h. (2)

::: test
A force of 80 N acts on an area of 0.25 m². (a) Work out the pressure. (2) (b) The same force acts on half the area. What happens to the pressure? (1)
---
(a) $80 \div 0.25$ (M1) $= 320$ N/m² (A1). (b) It doubles, to 640 N/m² (B1).

::: test
Toothpaste is sold in 75 ml tubes for £1.95 and 125 ml tubes for £3.10. Which is better value? (3)
---
$195 \div 75 = 2.6$p per ml (M1); $310 \div 125 = 2.48$p per ml (A1). The 125 ml tube is better value (A1).
:::

::: test
Lucy drives 48 km at an average speed of 64 km/h, then 40 km at an average speed of 32 km/h. Work out her average speed for the whole journey. (4)
---
$48 \div 64 = 0.75$ h (M1); $40 \div 32 = 1.25$ h (M1). Total distance $88$ km, total time $2$ h (M1). $88 \div 2 = 44$ km/h (A1). (Averaging the speeds gives a wrong 48 km/h.)
:::

::: test
400 cm³ of liquid A, density 0.8 g/cm³, is mixed with 600 g of liquid B, density 1.2 g/cm³. Work out the density of the mixture, to 3 significant figures. (4)
---
Mass of A $= 0.8 \times 400 = 320$ g (M1). Volume of B $= 600 \div 1.2 = 500$ cm³ (M1). Density $= \dfrac{320 + 600}{400 + 500} = \dfrac{920}{900}$ (M1) $= 1.02$ g/cm³ (A1).
:::

::: test
A cuboid measures 0.4 m by 0.5 m by 1.2 m. When it rests on its smallest face, the pressure on the ground is 3000 N/m². Work out the pressure when it rests on its largest face. (4)
---
Smallest face $0.4 \times 0.5 = 0.2$ m²; force $= 3000 \times 0.2 = 600$ N (M1, M1). Largest face $0.5 \times 1.2 = 0.6$ m² (M1). Pressure $= 600 \div 0.6 = 1000$ N/m² (A1).
:::
