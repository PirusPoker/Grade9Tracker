## What it is

A straight-line graph is the picture of a **linear** equation. At GCSE Higher you must plot one, read off its gradient and intercept, find its equation from given information, and use gradients to spot **parallel** and **perpendicular** lines. Many of the marks come from interpreting what the gradient and intercept **mean** in a real context.

### Coordinates in four quadrants

A point is written $(x, y)$: across first, then up. The axes split the plane into four quadrants, so either coordinate can be negative: $(-3, 2)$ is 3 left and 2 up. Points on the $x$-axis have $y = 0$; points on the $y$-axis have $x = 0$.

The **midpoint** of $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is the mean of each coordinate:

$$\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$$

The midpoint of $(-1, 2)$ and $(5, 6)$ is $(2, 4)$.

### Plotting a straight line

Make a table of values, plot the points, and join them with a **ruler** through the whole grid. For $y = 2x - 3$:

| $x$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | $-5$ | $-3$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $3$ |

If one point is out of line, recalculate it — straight-line points go up (or down) by the same amount each step.

Horizontal lines are $y = k$ and vertical lines are $x = k$. The line $y = x$ goes through the origin at 45°; $y = -x$ slopes the other way.

### Gradient and intercept: $y = mx + c$

In $y = mx + c$:

- $m$ is the **gradient** — how far the line goes **up for each 1 across**. Positive slopes up to the right; negative slopes down; zero is horizontal.
- $c$ is the **$y$-intercept** — where the line crosses the $y$-axis, at $(0, c)$.

$$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

The gradient between $(1, -3)$ and $(5, 9)$ is $\dfrac{9 - (-3)}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$.

**The equation must be rearranged into $y = \ldots$ before you read off $m$ and $c$.** For $3x + 2y = 12$:

$$2y = -3x + 12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = -\tfrac32 x + 6$$

so $m = -\frac32$ and $c = 6$. The **$x$-intercept** is where $y = 0$: $3x = 12$, so $(4, 0)$. Setting $x = 0$ then $y = 0$ is also the quickest way to plot a line given as $ax + by = c$.

To read the gradient from a graph, choose two points far apart where the line crosses grid corners, draw a right-angled triangle, and divide rise by run — **using the scales on the axes**, not the number of squares.

### Finding the equation of a line

**Given the gradient and one point:** substitute into $y = mx + c$ to find $c$.
Gradient 4 through $(2, 5)$: $\;5 = 4(2) + c \Rightarrow c = -3$, so $y = 4x - 3$.

**Given two points:** find the gradient first, then use either point.
Through $(-2, 7)$ and $(4, -5)$: $\;m = \dfrac{-5 - 7}{4 - (-2)} = \dfrac{-12}{6} = -2$. Then $7 = -2(-2) + c \Rightarrow c = 3$, so $y = -2x + 3$.

You may also use $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$, which gives the same line: $y - 7 = -2(x + 2)$.

### Parallel and perpendicular lines

**Parallel lines have equal gradients.** $y = 2x + 1$ and $4x - 2y = 3$ (which is $y = 2x - \frac32$) are parallel.

**Perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to $-1$.** If one gradient is $m$, the perpendicular gradient is $-\dfrac{1}{m}$: flip the fraction and change the sign.

| Gradient | $3$ | $-\frac14$ | $\frac23$ | $-5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perpendicular gradient | $-\frac13$ | $4$ | $-\frac32$ | $\frac15$ |

**Line perpendicular to $y = 3x - 1$ through $(6, 1)$:** gradient $-\frac13$; $\;1 = -\frac13(6) + c \Rightarrow c = 3$; $\;y = -\frac13 x + 3$, or $x + 3y = 9$.

A **perpendicular bisector** of $AB$ passes through the **midpoint** of $AB$ at right angles to it — find the midpoint, find the gradient of $AB$, take the negative reciprocal, then find $c$.

### Interpreting gradients and intercepts

In a real-life graph, the gradient is a **rate**: "(units of $y$) per (unit of $x$)", and the intercept is the **starting value** when $x = 0$.

A taxi fare is $C = 1.8d + 2.5$, with $C$ in pounds and $d$ in miles. The gradient $1.8$ means the fare rises by £1.80 for every mile; the intercept $2.5$ means there is a fixed charge of £2.50 before the taxi moves.

On a distance–time graph the gradient is the speed; on a graph of water volume against time, a negative gradient is the rate at which the tank empties.

::: terms
- **Gradient**: the steepness of a line, change in y divided by change in x; the m in y = mx + c.
- **y-intercept**: the point (0, c) where a line crosses the y-axis.
- **Linear equation**: an equation whose graph is a straight line, such as y = mx + c or ax + by = c.
- **Parallel lines**: lines that never meet; they have equal gradients.
- **Perpendicular lines**: lines meeting at right angles; their gradients multiply to give −1.
- **Midpoint**: the point halfway between two points, found by taking the mean of the x-coordinates and of the y-coordinates.
- **Perpendicular bisector**: the line through the midpoint of a segment at right angles to it.
:::

::: method
1. **Get $y$ on its own** before reading a gradient or intercept. $2y = 6x + 8$ has gradient 3, not 6.
2. **Gradient from two points:** $\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, with the $y$s on top and the points subtracted in the same order. Bracket negatives.
3. **Equation of a line:** find $m$, substitute one point into $y = mx + c$, solve for $c$, and write the full equation "$y = \ldots$". If asked for the form $ax + by = c$ or $ax + by + c = 0$ with integers, multiply out the fractions at the end.
4. **Parallel:** same $m$. **Perpendicular:** $m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{m_1}$. Write the new gradient down on its own line — it is usually worth a mark.
5. **Proving parallel, perpendicular or collinear:** calculate each gradient, show the product is $-1$ (or the gradients are equal) and write a sentence. For three points on a line, show $AB$ and $BC$ have equal gradients **and share point $B$**.
6. **Reading a graph:** pick two far-apart points on grid corners and use the axis scales. Plot with a ruler across the full range asked for.
7. **Interpreting:** give the gradient as a rate with both units ("£35 per day") and the intercept as the value when $x = 0$ ("a fixed charge of £40").
8. **Paper 1** often has fractional gradients — keep them as fractions. On calculator papers the numbers may be decimals, but the method is identical.
:::

::: example Gradient and intercepts from ax + by = c
Find the gradient of $5x - 2y = 10$ and where it crosses both axes.

$-2y = -5x + 10 \Rightarrow y = \frac52 x - 5$. Gradient $\frac52$, $y$-intercept $(0, -5)$.

$x$-intercept: $y = 0 \Rightarrow 5x = 10 \Rightarrow (2, 0)$.
:::

::: example Line through two points, integer form
Find the equation of the line through $(-3, 1)$ and $(5, 5)$. Give it in the form $ax + by = c$ where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are integers.

$m = \dfrac{5 - 1}{5 - (-3)} = \dfrac48 = \dfrac12$.

$1 = \frac12(-3) + c \Rightarrow c = \frac52$, so $y = \frac12 x + \frac52$.

Multiply by 2: $2y = x + 5$, so $x - 2y = -5$.
:::

::: example Perpendicular through a point
Find the equation of the line perpendicular to $2x + 5y = 7$ that passes through $(4, 3)$.

$5y = -2x + 7 \Rightarrow$ gradient $-\frac25$. Perpendicular gradient $= \frac52$.

$3 = \frac52(4) + c = 10 + c \Rightarrow c = -7$. $\;y = \frac52 x - 7$.
:::

::: example Interpreting a real-life line
A phone contract's monthly cost $C$ (£) is a straight-line function of the minutes used, $t$. The graph passes through $(0, 12)$ and $(400, 32)$. Find the equation and interpret the gradient and intercept.

Gradient $= \dfrac{32 - 12}{400 - 0} = \dfrac{20}{400} = 0.05$. Intercept 12. $\;C = 0.05t + 12$.

The gradient means each minute costs £0.05 (5p). The intercept means there is a fixed monthly charge of £12 even if no minutes are used.
:::

::: example Perpendicular bisector (grade 9)
$A$ is $(-1, 2)$ and $B$ is $(5, 6)$. Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, and where it crosses the $x$-axis.

Midpoint $= \left(\dfrac{-1 + 5}{2}, \dfrac{2 + 6}{2}\right) = (2, 4)$.

Gradient $AB = \dfrac{6 - 2}{5 - (-1)} = \dfrac46 = \dfrac23$, so the perpendicular gradient is $-\dfrac32$.

$4 = -\frac32(2) + c \Rightarrow c = 7$. $\;y = -\frac32 x + 7$, or $3x + 2y = 14$.

On the $x$-axis $y = 0$: $3x = 14$, so it crosses at $\left(\dfrac{14}{3}, 0\right)$.
:::

::: q
Complete a table of values for $y = 4 - 2x$ for $x = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2$, and give the midpoint of $(-2, 8)$ and $(2, 0)$.
---
$y = 8, 6, 4, 2, 0$. Midpoint $= \left(\frac{-2 + 2}{2}, \frac{8 + 0}{2}\right) = (0, 4)$ — which lies on the line, as it should.
:::

::: q
Write down the gradient and $y$-intercept of (a) $y = 5 - 3x$ (b) $2y - 6x = 8$.
---
(a) Gradient $-3$, intercept $(0, 5)$. (b) $y = 3x + 4$: gradient $3$, intercept $(0, 4)$.
:::

::: q
Find the gradient of the line through $(1, -3)$ and $(5, 9)$, and the equation of the line with gradient 4 through $(2, 5)$.
---
$\dfrac{9 + 3}{5 - 1} = 3$. $\quad 5 = 8 + c \Rightarrow c = -3$: $y = 4x - 3$.
:::

::: q
Find the equation of the line through $(-1, 4)$ and $(3, -4)$.
---
$m = \dfrac{-4 - 4}{3 - (-1)} = -2$. $\;4 = 2 + c \Rightarrow c = 2$. $\;y = -2x + 2$.
:::

::: q
Which two of these lines are parallel? $\;y = 2x + 1$, $\;4x - 2y = 3$, $\;y = -2x + 1$, $\;2y = x + 4$.
---
Gradients: $2$, $2$ (since $y = 2x - 1.5$), $-2$, $\frac12$. The first two are parallel.
:::

::: q
Find the equation of the line perpendicular to $y = -\frac14 x + 2$ through $(1, 3)$.
---
Perpendicular gradient $= 4$. $\;3 = 4 + c \Rightarrow c = -1$. $\;y = 4x - 1$.
:::

::: q
$A(0, 0)$, $B(4, 2)$ and $C(3, -6)$. Show that angle $BAC$ is a right angle.
---
Gradient $AB = \frac24 = \frac12$; gradient $AC = \frac{-6}{3} = -2$. $\;\frac12 \times (-2) = -1$, so $AB$ is perpendicular to $AC$ and angle $BAC = 90°$.
:::

::: q
A tank holds $V$ litres after $t$ minutes, where $V = 500 - 20t$. Interpret the gradient and the intercept, and find when the tank is empty.
---
Gradient $-20$: the tank loses 20 litres per minute. Intercept 500: it held 500 litres at the start. Empty when $500 - 20t = 0$, $t = 25$ minutes.
:::

::: q
The line $ax + 3y = 12$ is perpendicular to $2y = 3x + 1$. Find $a$.
---
$2y = 3x + 1$ has gradient $\frac32$, so the other line needs gradient $-\frac23$. $\;y = -\frac{a}{3}x + 4$, so $-\frac{a}{3} = -\frac23$ and $a = 2$.
:::

::: watch
- **Not rearranging first:** reading "gradient 6" from $2y = 6x + 8$. Divide everything by 2.
- **Upside-down gradient:** it is change in $y$ over change in $x$ — rise over run.
- **Sign slips with negative coordinates:** $5 - (-3) = 8$. Put negatives in brackets.
- **Perpendicular gradient:** flip **and** change sign. The perpendicular to gradient $\frac23$ is $-\frac32$, not $\frac32$ or $-\frac23$.
- **Counting squares:** when the axes have different scales, the gradient must use the axis values.
- **Stopping at $c$:** the question asks for the equation — write "$y = 4x - 3$", not "$c = -3$".
- **Interpreting without units:** "the gradient is 35" is not an interpretation; "£35 per day" is.
:::

::: test
The cost, £$C$, of hiring a van for $d$ days is given by a straight-line graph through $(0, 40)$ and $(5, 215)$.
(a) Find an equation for $C$ in terms of $d$. (b) Interpret the gradient in this context. (3)
---
(a) Gradient $= \dfrac{215 - 40}{5} = 35$ (M1); $C = 35d + 40$ (A1).
(b) The cost increases by £35 for each extra day of hire (B1).
:::

::: test
Find the equation of the line perpendicular to $y = 2x + 5$ that passes through $(4, -1)$. (3)
---
Gradient $-\frac12$ (M1). $-1 = -\frac12(4) + c$, so $c = 1$ (M1). $y = -\frac12 x + 1$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$A(-2, -7)$, $B(1, 2)$ and $C(4, 11)$. Show that $A$, $B$ and $C$ lie on a straight line. (3)
---
Gradient $AB = \dfrac{2 - (-7)}{1 - (-2)} = 3$ (M1). Gradient $BC = \dfrac{11 - 2}{4 - 1} = 3$ (M1). Equal gradients and the common point $B$, so $A$, $B$, $C$ are collinear (A1).
:::

::: test
$P$ is $(1, 7)$ and $Q$ is $(7, -1)$. Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$. Give your answer in the form $ax + by = c$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are integers. (4)
---
Midpoint $(4, 3)$ (B1). Gradient $PQ = \dfrac{-8}{6} = -\dfrac43$, so perpendicular gradient $\dfrac34$ (M1). $3 = \frac34(4) + c \Rightarrow c = 0$, so $y = \frac34 x$ (M1). $3x - 4y = 0$ (A1).
:::

::: test
The line $4x + ky = 7$ is perpendicular to the line $y = 2x + 3$. Find the value of $k$. (3)
---
Required gradient $-\frac12$ (M1). $ky = -4x + 7 \Rightarrow$ gradient $-\frac{4}{k}$ (M1). $-\frac{4}{k} = -\frac12 \Rightarrow k = 8$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Line $L_1$ passes through $A(0, 6)$ and $B(3, 0)$. Line $L_2$ is perpendicular to $L_1$ and passes through $B$. $L_2$ crosses the $y$-axis at $C$. Work out the area of triangle $ABC$. (5)
---
Gradient $L_1 = \dfrac{0 - 6}{3 - 0} = -2$ (M1). Gradient $L_2 = \frac12$ (M1). $0 = \frac12(3) + c \Rightarrow c = -\frac32$, so $C = (0, -1.5)$ (A1).
$AC$ lies on the $y$-axis with length $6 - (-1.5) = 7.5$; the height from $B$ to the $y$-axis is 3 (M1).
Area $= \frac12 \times 7.5 \times 3 = 11.25$ square units (A1).
:::
