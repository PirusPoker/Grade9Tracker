## What it is

A **linear equation** in one unknown has $x$ only to the power 1 — no $x^2$, no $\sqrt x$. It has exactly one solution (or, in special cases, none or infinitely many). You must solve them **algebraically** — including with brackets, fractions and the unknown on both sides — and find **approximate solutions from a graph**.

### The balance principle

An equation is a balance: whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Undo the operations in reverse order.

$4x + 7 = 31$: subtract 7 from both sides, $4x = 24$; divide by 4, $x = 6$.

### Unknown on both sides

Collect the $x$ terms on the side with **more** $x$ (so the $x$ coefficient stays positive), and the numbers on the other side.

$5x - 7 = 2x + 11$: subtract $2x$, $3x - 7 = 11$; add 7, $3x = 18$; $x = 6$.

$3 - 2x = 5x - 18$: add $2x$, $3 = 7x - 18$; add 18, $21 = 7x$; $x = 3$.

### Brackets

Expand first, taking care with a negative outside a bracket: $-2(x - 4) = -2x + 8$.

$3(2x - 5) - 2(x + 1) = 11$

$6x - 15 - 2x - 2 = 11$

$4x - 17 = 11$, so $4x = 28$ and $x = 7$.

### Fractions

Multiply **every term** on both sides by the lowest common multiple of the denominators. Put brackets round each numerator first, so that the whole numerator gets multiplied.

$\dfrac{x + 3}{4} - \dfrac{x - 2}{3} = 1$. Multiply by 12:

$3(x + 3) - 4(x - 2) = 12$

$3x + 9 - 4x + 8 = 12$ — note $-4 \times -2 = +8$

$-x + 17 = 12$, so $x = 5$.

If $x$ is in a denominator, multiply by that expression: $\dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3} = 5$ gives $2x + 1 = 5(x - 3) = 5x - 15$, so $16 = 3x$ and $x = \dfrac{16}{3}$. The equation is still linear once the fraction is cleared. (Here $x \ne 3$, and the answer is not 3, so it stands.)

A fraction equals a fraction: **cross-multiply**. $\dfrac{2x - 1}{5} = \dfrac{x + 4}{3}$ gives $3(2x - 1) = 5(x + 4)$, so $6x - 3 = 5x + 20$ and $x = 23$.

### Answers that are not whole numbers

Solutions can be negative or fractions. $6x = -4$ gives $x = -\dfrac23$. On Paper 1 leave it as a fraction; on a calculator paper follow the accuracy you are asked for.

### No solution, or every solution

- $2(x + 3) = 2x + 5$ becomes $2x + 6 = 2x + 5$, then $6 = 5$: impossible, so **no solution**.
- $3(2x - 4) = 6x - 12$ becomes $6x - 12 = 6x - 12$: true for **every** $x$. This is an **identity**, sometimes written with $\equiv$.

### Solving from a graph

The solution of $f(x) = g(x)$ is the $x$-coordinate where the graphs of $y = f(x)$ and $y = g(x)$ cross.

To solve $2x - 1 = 5 - x$, draw $y = 2x - 1$ and $y = 5 - x$. They meet at $(2, 3)$, so $x = 2$. (Check: $3x = 6$.)

Graph solutions are **approximate** — only as good as your drawing. The lines $y = 3x + 2$ and $y = 7 - 1.5x$ cross at about $x = 1.1$; algebra gives $4.5x = 5$, $x = \dfrac{10}{9} = 1.11\ldots$

To solve $ax + b = c$ from a single line $y = ax + b$, draw the horizontal line $y = c$ and read the $x$-coordinate of the crossing.

### Forming equations

Many exam questions set up the equation for you to find: angles in a shape, perimeters, ages, costs. Write an expression for each part, set up the equation from the fact you know (angles in a triangle sum to $180°$, perimeters are equal, and so on), solve, then **answer the question asked** — often the size of an angle or a length, not just $x$.

::: terms
- **Linear equation**: an equation in which the unknown appears only to the power 1, such as 3x + 4 = 2x − 1.
- **Solution**: the value of the unknown that makes both sides of the equation equal.
- **Balance method**: doing the same operation to both sides of an equation so that it stays true.
- **Lowest common multiple (LCM)**: the smallest number every denominator divides into; multiply through by it to clear fractions.
- **Cross-multiplying**: turning a/b = c/d into ad = bc by multiplying both sides by both denominators.
- **Identity**: a statement true for every value of the unknown, such as 2(x + 1) ≡ 2x + 2.
:::

::: method
1. **Clear fractions** first: multiply every term by the LCM of the denominators, with brackets round each numerator. If $x$ is in a denominator, multiply by that expression.
2. **Expand brackets**, watching signs: a minus outside a bracket changes every sign inside.
3. **Collect** $x$ terms on the side with more $x$, and numbers on the other.
4. **Divide** by the coefficient of $x$. Leave fractions as fractions on Paper 1; give decimals to the accuracy asked on Papers 2 and 3.
5. **Show each step on a new line.** Method marks go for a correct first step (e.g. correct expansion, or multiplying through correctly), so even if you slip later you keep them.
6. **Check** by substituting your answer into the original equation — both sides must match.
7. **From a graph:** find where the two lines cross and read the $x$-coordinate; say it is approximate. If asked, confirm algebraically.
8. **In context,** finish by answering the actual question (an angle, a length, an age) with units.
:::

::: example Unknown on both sides with brackets
Solve $5 - 2(x - 3) = 3x - 9$.

$5 - 2x + 6 = 3x - 9$ (the $-2$ multiplies both terms: $-2 \times -3 = +6$)

$11 - 2x = 3x - 9$

$20 = 5x$, so $x = 4$.

Check: left $5 - 2(1) = 3$; right $12 - 9 = 3$. ✓
:::

::: example Two fractions
Solve $\dfrac{x + 4}{3} - \dfrac{x - 1}{2} = 1$.

LCM 6: $2(x + 4) - 3(x - 1) = 6$

$2x + 8 - 3x + 3 = 6$

$-x + 11 = 6$, so $x = 5$.

Check: $\dfrac93 - \dfrac42 = 3 - 2 = 1$. ✓
:::

::: example Solving from a graph
The lines $y = 3x - 2$ and $y = 8 - 2x$ are drawn on the same grid. Use them to solve $3x - 2 = 8 - 2x$, and confirm algebraically.

The lines cross at $(2, 4)$, so $x = 2$.

Algebra: $5x = 10$, $x = 2$. ✓ (Both lines give $y = 4$ at $x = 2$.)
:::

::: example Forming an equation from angles
The angles of a quadrilateral are $2x$, $3x + 10$, $x + 40$ and $4x - 30$ degrees. Find the size of the largest angle.

Angles in a quadrilateral sum to $360°$: $10x + 20 = 360$, so $10x = 340$ and $x = 34$.

Angles: $68°$, $112°$, $74°$, $106°$ (sum $360°$ ✓). Largest angle $112°$.
:::

::: example Unknown in both denominators (grade 9)
Solve $\dfrac{x + 1}{2x - 1} = \dfrac{x + 3}{2x + 5}$.

Cross-multiply: $(x + 1)(2x + 5) = (x + 3)(2x - 1)$.

$2x^2 + 7x + 5 = 2x^2 + 5x - 3$

The $2x^2$ terms cancel — it is linear after all: $2x = -8$, so $x = -4$.

Check: $\dfrac{-3}{-9} = \dfrac13$ and $\dfrac{-1}{-3} = \dfrac13$. ✓
:::

::: q
Solve $7x + 4 = 3x - 16$.
---
$4x = -20$, $x = -5$.
:::

::: q
Solve $4(3 - x) = 2(x + 9)$.
---
$12 - 4x = 2x + 18 \Rightarrow -6 = 6x \Rightarrow x = -1$.
:::

::: q
Solve $\dfrac{2x - 1}{5} = \dfrac{x + 4}{3}$.
---
$3(2x - 1) = 5(x + 4) \Rightarrow 6x - 3 = 5x + 20 \Rightarrow x = 23$.
:::

::: q
Solve $\dfrac x2 + \dfrac x3 = 10$.
---
Multiply by 6: $3x + 2x = 60 \Rightarrow 5x = 60 \Rightarrow x = 12$.
:::

::: q
Solve $\dfrac{3x - 2}{x + 1} = 2$.
---
$3x - 2 = 2(x + 1) = 2x + 2 \Rightarrow x = 4$.
:::

::: q
Ann is three times as old as Ben. In 5 years' time their ages will add up to 50. How old is each now?
---
Ben $b$, Ann $3b$: $(3b + 5) + (b + 5) = 50 \Rightarrow 4b = 40 \Rightarrow b = 10$. Ben 10, Ann 30.
:::

::: q
The lines $y = 4 - x$ and $y = \dfrac12x + 1$ are drawn. Where do they cross, and what equation does that solve?
---
$4 - x = \dfrac12x + 1 \Rightarrow 3 = \dfrac32x \Rightarrow x = 2$; crossing at $(2, 2)$. It solves $4 - x = \dfrac12x + 1$.
:::

::: q
Show that $2(x + 3) = 2x + 5$ has no solution.
---
$2x + 6 = 2x + 5 \Rightarrow 6 = 5$, which is false for every $x$, so there is no solution.
:::

::: q
Solve $\dfrac{x}{4} - 3 = \dfrac{2x + 1}{6}$.
---
Multiply by 12: $3x - 36 = 2(2x + 1) = 4x + 2 \Rightarrow -38 = x$. So $x = -38$.
:::

::: watch
- **Not multiplying every term** when clearing fractions: in $\dfrac x4 - 3 = \dots$, the 3 must be multiplied by 12 too.
- **Losing the bracket round a numerator.** $-\dfrac{x - 2}{3}$ times 12 is $-4(x - 2) = -4x + 8$, not $-4x - 8$.
- **Sign errors when expanding** a negative bracket: $-2(x - 3) = -2x + 6$.
- **Moving terms and forgetting to change the sign.** Write the operation ("subtract $2x$ from both sides") rather than "moving" terms.
- **Stopping at $x$** in a context question when the question asked for an angle, length or age.
- **Graph answers stated as exact** when they are only read from a drawing — say "approximately".
:::

::: test
The angles of a triangle are $x + 10$, $2x$ and $3x - 40$ degrees. Work out the size of the largest angle. (4)
---
$x + 10 + 2x + 3x - 40 = 180$ (M1). $6x - 30 = 180$ (M1). $x = 35$ (A1). Angles $45°, 70°, 65°$: largest $70°$ (A1).

::: test
Sam is $x$ years old. His mum is four times as old as Sam. In 6 years' time, his mum will be two and a half times as old as Sam. Find their ages now. (4)
---
$4x + 6 = 2.5(x + 6)$ (M1). $4x + 6 = 2.5x + 15$ (M1). $1.5x = 9$, $x = 6$ (A1). Sam 6, mum 24 (A1).
:::

::: test
Solve $\dfrac{x + 3}{2x + 1} = \dfrac{x - 1}{2x - 5}$. (4)
---
$(x + 3)(2x - 5) = (x - 1)(2x + 1)$ (M1). $2x^2 + x - 15 = 2x^2 - x - 1$ (M1 for both expansions). $2x = 14$ (M1). $x = 7$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Is $3(2x - 4) = 6x - 12$ an equation with one solution, no solution or infinitely many solutions? Explain. (2)
---
Expanding: $6x - 12 = 6x - 12$ (M1). Both sides are identical for every $x$, so it is an identity with infinitely many solutions (A1).
:::
