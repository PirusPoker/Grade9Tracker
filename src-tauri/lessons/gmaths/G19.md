## What it is

Two shapes are **congruent** if they are exactly the same shape and size (one fits exactly on the other, possibly after turning it over). Two shapes are **similar** if one is an enlargement of the other: the same shape, with all angles equal and all lengths multiplied by the same **scale factor**. At Higher tier you must also use the fact that areas and volumes do **not** scale by the same factor as lengths.

### Congruent triangles

Two triangles are congruent if any one of these conditions holds:

| Condition | What matches |
|---|---|
| **SSS** | all three sides |
| **SAS** | two sides and the angle **between** them |
| **ASA** (or AAS) | two angles and a corresponding side |
| **RHS** | a right angle, the hypotenuse and one other side |

Note that AAA is **not** a congruence condition (it only proves similarity), and neither is SSA with the angle not between the sides.

A congruence proof lists three facts, each with a **reason**, then names the condition:

*Mini-example.* $ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Prove triangles $ABC$ and $CDA$ are congruent.
$AB = CD$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal); $BC = DA$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal); $AC$ is common to both. So triangle $ABC \cong$ triangle $CDA$ (SSS).

Once triangles are proved congruent, every corresponding side and angle is equal — that is how congruence is used to prove other results (for example that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other).

### Similar shapes and lengths

In similar shapes, corresponding angles are equal and corresponding lengths are in the same ratio:
$$\text{length scale factor } k = \frac{\text{length on the new shape}}{\text{corresponding length on the original}}.$$
Triangles are similar if their angles are equal (two pairs is enough, since the third follows), or if all three pairs of sides are in the same ratio.

Common layouts:
- **Nested triangles** ("A inside B"): a line parallel to one side cuts off a small triangle that shares an angle with the big one; the parallel line gives equal corresponding angles. Watch out — the side of the big triangle is the **whole** length, not just the extra piece.
- **Hourglass** (bow-tie): two triangles meet at a point where vertically opposite angles are equal, and parallel lines give equal alternate angles.

*Mini-example.* In triangle $ABC$, $DE$ is parallel to $BC$ with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$. $AD = 4$, $DB = 6$, $DE = 5$. Then $AB = 10$, so $k = \frac{10}{4} = 2.5$ and $BC = 5 \times 2.5 = 12.5$.

To match sides correctly, match **angles**: the side opposite the angle marked with one arc corresponds to the side opposite the matching angle in the other triangle. Redrawing the two triangles separately, the same way round, helps.

### Areas and volumes of similar shapes

If the length scale factor is $k$, then
$$\text{area scale factor} = k^2 \qquad \text{volume scale factor} = k^3.$$
This works for any similar shapes and solids — surface areas scale by $k^2$ and volumes (and capacities, and masses of the same material) by $k^3$.

*Mini-example.* Two similar cylinders have heights 4 cm and 12 cm, so $k = 3$. If the small one has surface area $50\text{ cm}^2$, the large one has $50 \times 9 = 450\text{ cm}^2$. If the small one has volume $30\text{ cm}^3$, the large one has $30 \times 27 = 810\text{ cm}^3$.

Working **backwards**: if you are given the areas, the length scale factor is the **square root** of the area ratio; if given volumes, it is the **cube root**. To go from areas to volumes, go via lengths:
$$\text{area ratio} \xrightarrow{\sqrt{\ }} \text{length ratio} \xrightarrow{(\ )^3} \text{volume ratio}.$$

*Mini-example.* Two similar jugs have capacities 250 ml and 2000 ml. Volume factor $= 8$, so $k = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$ and the area factor is $2^2 = 4$. If the small jug is 10 cm tall, the large one is 20 cm tall.

::: terms
- **Congruent**: exactly the same shape and size; one shape fits exactly on top of the other.
- **Similar**: the same shape but not necessarily the same size — equal angles and lengths in the same ratio.
- **Conditions for congruence**: SSS, SAS, ASA (or AAS) and RHS; AAA only proves similarity.
- **Length scale factor k**: the number every length is multiplied by to get from one similar shape to the other.
- **Area scale factor**: k², the factor that areas and surface areas are multiplied by.
- **Volume scale factor**: k³, the factor that volumes, capacities and masses of the same material are multiplied by.
- **Corresponding sides**: sides in matching positions, opposite equal angles in two similar or congruent shapes.
:::

::: method
1. **Congruence proof:** write three statements, each with a geometric reason ("common side", "given", "radii of the same circle", "alternate angles are equal"). Then name the condition: "so the triangles are congruent (SAS)". Full marks need every reason and the condition.
2. **Similarity check:** show two pairs of equal angles (with reasons), or three sides in the same ratio.
3. **Match corresponding sides by angles.** Redraw the two triangles separately, the same way round, and label them. For nested triangles use the **whole** side of the large triangle.
4. **Find $k$** from one pair of known corresponding lengths: $k = \dfrac{\text{new}}{\text{original}}$. Multiply to enlarge, divide to go back.
5. **Areas and volumes:** decide what you are given (length, area or volume ratio) and convert to the length ratio first — square root for areas, cube root for volumes — then square or cube as needed.
6. **Subtract at the end, not the start:** to find $DB$ in a nested triangle, find $AB$ first, then subtract $AD$.
7. **Non-calculator papers:** keep the scale factor as a fraction ($\frac32$, not 1.5 rounded), and use exact roots like $\sqrt{\frac{9}{4}} = \frac32$ and $\sqrt[3]{\frac{8}{27}} = \frac23$.
8. **Show the scale factor** as a separate line — it earns the first method mark even if later arithmetic slips.
:::

::: example Congruence proof (RHS)
Triangle $ABC$ is isosceles with $AB = AC$. $D$ is the point on $BC$ such that $AD$ is perpendicular to $BC$. Prove that triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$ are congruent, and hence that $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$.

Angle $ADB$ = angle $ADC = 90°$ ($AD$ is perpendicular to $BC$).
$AB = AC$ (given) — these are the hypotenuses.
$AD$ is common to both triangles.
So triangle $ABD \cong$ triangle $ACD$ (RHS). Hence $BD = CD$ (corresponding sides of congruent triangles), so $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$.
:::

::: example Nested triangles
In triangle $ABC$, $D$ is on $AB$ and $E$ is on $AC$, with $DE$ parallel to $BC$. $AD = 6$ cm, $DE = 8$ cm, $BC = 20$ cm and $AE = 7.5$ cm. Find $DB$ and $EC$.

Triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ are similar (angle $A$ is common; angle $ADE$ = angle $ABC$, corresponding angles). $k = \dfrac{20}{8} = 2.5$.

$AB = 6 \times 2.5 = 15$, so $DB = 15 - 6 = 9$ cm. $AC = 7.5 \times 2.5 = 18.75$, so $EC = 18.75 - 7.5 = 11.25$ cm.
:::

::: example Hourglass triangles
$AB$ is parallel to $DE$. The lines $AE$ and $BD$ cross at $C$. $AC = 4$ cm, $CE = 10$ cm, $AB = 6$ cm and $BC = 3.2$ cm. Find $DE$ and $CD$.

Angle $ACB$ = angle $ECD$ (vertically opposite); angle $BAC$ = angle $DEC$ (alternate angles). So the triangles are similar with $A \leftrightarrow E$, $B \leftrightarrow D$. $k = \dfrac{CE}{CA} = \dfrac{10}{4} = 2.5$.

$DE = 6 \times 2.5 = 15$ cm. $CD = 3.2 \times 2.5 = 8$ cm.
:::

::: example From areas to lengths
Two similar triangles have areas $20\text{ cm}^2$ and $45\text{ cm}^2$. A side of the smaller triangle is 6 cm. Find the corresponding side of the larger one.

Area factor $= \dfrac{45}{20} = \dfrac94$, so $k = \sqrt{\dfrac94} = \dfrac32$. Side $= 6 \times \dfrac32 = 9$ cm.
:::

::: example From surface areas to volumes
Two similar solids have surface areas $80\text{ cm}^2$ and $180\text{ cm}^2$. The larger has volume $540\text{ cm}^3$. Find the volume of the smaller.

Area factor (small to large) $= \dfrac{180}{80} = \dfrac94 \Rightarrow k = \dfrac32 \Rightarrow$ volume factor $= \dfrac{27}{8}$.

Small volume $= 540 \div \dfrac{27}{8} = 540 \times \dfrac{8}{27} = 160\text{ cm}^3$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: a partly filled cone
A cone-shaped container, vertex down, has depth 20 cm and holds 1.6 litres when full. Water is poured in to a depth of 15 cm. How much water is in it, in ml?

The water forms a cone similar to the container with $k = \dfrac{15}{20} = \dfrac34$. Volume factor $= \left(\dfrac34\right)^3 = \dfrac{27}{64}$.

$1.6$ litres $= 1600$ ml. Water $= 1600 \times \dfrac{27}{64} = 675$ ml.

(Note: three-quarters of the depth holds well under half the capacity.)
:::

::: q
Triangle $PQR$ has $PQ = 5$ cm, $QR = 7$ cm and angle $PQR = 40°$. Triangle $XYZ$ has $XY = 5$ cm, $YZ = 7$ cm and angle $XYZ = 40°$. Are they congruent? Give the condition.
---
Yes — two sides and the included angle are equal, so they are congruent (SAS).
:::

::: q
A rectangle measures 4 cm by 6 cm. A similar rectangle has shorter side 10 cm. Find its longer side.
---
$k = \frac{10}{4} = 2.5$; longer side $= 6 \times 2.5 = 15$ cm.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $DE$ is parallel to $BC$ with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$. $AD = x$ cm, $DB = 6$ cm, $DE = 4$ cm and $BC = 10$ cm. Find $x$.
---
$\dfrac{AB}{AD} = \dfrac{BC}{DE}$: $\dfrac{x + 6}{x} = \dfrac{10}{4} \Rightarrow 4x + 24 = 10x \Rightarrow x = 4$.
:::

::: q
Two similar shapes have lengths in the ratio $2 : 5$. The smaller has area $12\text{ cm}^2$. Find the area of the larger.
---
Area factor $= \left(\frac52\right)^2 = \frac{25}{4}$. Area $= 12 \times \frac{25}{4} = 75\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
Two similar cylinders have volumes $54\text{ cm}^3$ and $250\text{ cm}^3$. The smaller is 6 cm tall. How tall is the larger?
---
Volume factor $= \frac{250}{54} = \frac{125}{27}$, so $k = \frac53$. Height $= 6 \times \frac53 = 10$ cm.
:::

::: q
Two similar statues are made of the same metal. They are 12 cm and 30 cm tall. The small one has mass 400 g. Find the mass of the large one in kg.
---
$k = 2.5$, mass factor $= 2.5^3 = 15.625$. Mass $= 400 \times 15.625 = 6250$ g $= 6.25$ kg.
:::

::: q
Two similar boxes have heights in the ratio $1 : 3$. It takes 0.4 litres of paint to cover the small box. How much paint does the large box need?
---
Paint covers area, so use $k^2 = 9$: $0.4 \times 9 = 3.6$ litres.
:::

::: q
$ABCD$ is a parallelogram whose diagonals meet at $M$. Prove that triangles $AMB$ and $CMD$ are congruent, and deduce that the diagonals bisect each other.
---
$AB = CD$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram). Angle $MAB$ = angle $MCD$ (alternate angles, $AB \parallel DC$). Angle $MBA$ = angle $MDC$ (alternate angles). So the triangles are congruent (ASA). Hence $AM = CM$ and $BM = DM$, so $M$ is the midpoint of both diagonals.
:::

::: q
Two similar jugs have volumes $64\text{ cm}^3$ and $216\text{ cm}^3$. The larger has surface area $180\text{ cm}^2$. Find the surface area of the smaller.
---
$k^3 = \frac{216}{64} \Rightarrow k = \frac64 = \frac32$, so $k^2 = \frac94$. Smaller $= 180 \div \frac94 = 80\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
A solid cone has volume $400\text{ cm}^3$. It is cut parallel to the base halfway up its height. Find the volume of the frustum left at the bottom.
---
The small top cone is similar with $k = \frac12$, so its volume is $400 \times \frac18 = 50$. Frustum $= 400 - 50 = 350\text{ cm}^3$.
:::

::: watch
- **Using $k$ for areas or volumes.** If lengths double, areas multiply by 4 and volumes by 8. Paint, cloth and surface area use $k^2$; capacity and mass use $k^3$.
- **Going backwards from areas or volumes:** square-root or cube-root to get $k$ first. Many answers multiply a length by the area ratio.
- **Nested triangles:** the big triangle's side is the whole length $AB$, not the extra piece $DB$. Find $AB$ and subtract at the end.
- **Mismatched sides:** pair sides by the equal angles opposite them, not by where they sit on the page.
- **Congruence proofs without reasons,** or naming AAA as a condition. Every statement needs a reason and the proof must end with the condition (SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS).
:::

A rectangle is 3 cm wide and 5 cm long. A similar rectangle is 12 cm wide. Find its length. (2)
---
$k = \frac{12}{3} = 4$ (M1); length $= 20$ cm (A1).

::: test
Triangle $PQR$ has $PQ = 7$ cm, angle $Q = 50°$ and $QR = 9$ cm. Triangle $XYZ$ has $XY = 7$ cm, angle $Y = 50°$ and $YZ = 9$ cm. Explain why the triangles are congruent. (2)
---
Two pairs of equal sides with the equal angle between them (1); congruent by SAS (1).

::: test
Two similar shapes have length scale factor 3. The smaller has area $14\text{ cm}^2$. Find the area of the larger. (2)
---
Area factor $= 3^2 = 9$ (M1); $14 \times 9 = 126\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $ABC$, $DE$ is parallel to $BC$, with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$. $AD = 5$ cm, $AB = 12.5$ cm and $BC = 15$ cm. Find $DE$. (2)
---
$k = \frac{12.5}{5} = 2.5$ (M1); $DE = 15 \div 2.5 = 6$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
$AB$ is parallel to $DE$. $AE$ and $BD$ intersect at $C$. $AC = 6$ cm, $CE = 9$ cm, $AB = 8$ cm and $CD = 7.5$ cm. Find $DE$ and $BC$. (3)
---
Similar triangles (vertically opposite and alternate angles), $k = \frac{9}{6} = 1.5$ (M1). $DE = 8 \times 1.5 = 12$ cm (A1). $BC = 7.5 \div 1.5 = 5$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
Two similar bottles are 15 cm and 25 cm tall. The smaller holds 540 ml. How much does the larger hold? (3)
---
$k = \frac{25}{15} = \frac53$ (M1); volume factor $= \frac{125}{27}$ (M1); $540 \times \frac{125}{27} = 2500$ ml (A1).
:::

::: test
$ABCD$ is a kite with $AB = AD$ and $CB = CD$. Prove that triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$ are congruent, and hence that angle $ABC$ = angle $ADC$. (4)
---
$AB = AD$ (given) (1); $CB = CD$ (given) and $AC$ common (1); congruent by SSS (1); so angle $ABC$ = angle $ADC$, corresponding angles of congruent triangles (1).
:::

::: test
Two similar solids have surface areas $50\text{ cm}^2$ and $72\text{ cm}^2$. The smaller has volume $250\text{ cm}^3$. Find the volume of the larger. (4)
---
Area factor $= \frac{72}{50} = \frac{36}{25}$ (M1); $k = \frac65$ (A1); volume factor $= \frac{216}{125}$ (M1); $250 \times \frac{216}{125} = 432\text{ cm}^3$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Two similar cones have volumes in the ratio $8 : 27$. The sum of their surface areas is $390\text{ cm}^2$. Find the surface area of each cone. (4)
---
$k = \sqrt[3]{\frac{27}{8}} = \frac32$ (M1); area ratio $= 4 : 9$ (A1). $390 \div 13 = 30$ (M1); surface areas $120\text{ cm}^2$ and $270\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $ABC$, $DE$ is parallel to $BC$ with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$. $AD = x$ cm, $DB = 5$ cm, $DE = (x + 1)$ cm and $BC = 12$ cm. Given that $DE$ is longer than 4 cm, find $x$. (5)
---
Similar triangles: $\dfrac{x + 5}{x} = \dfrac{12}{x + 1}$ (M1). $(x + 5)(x + 1) = 12x$ (M1) $\Rightarrow x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0$ (A1) $\Rightarrow (x - 1)(x - 5) = 0$ (M1). $x = 1$ gives $DE = 2$, rejected; so $x = 5$ (A1).
:::
