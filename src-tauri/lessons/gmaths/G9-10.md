## What it is

The **circle theorems** are angle facts that hold for every circle. They are among the most reliable Higher-tier marks — if you can spot which theorem a diagram is built on and quote it in the standard words. At GCSE you must also be able to **prove** them and use them to prove other results.

### Circle vocabulary

- **Centre** — the fixed point; usually labelled $O$.
- **Radius** — a line from the centre to the circle. All radii of a circle are equal, so **two radii make an isosceles triangle**.
- **Diameter** — a chord through the centre; twice the radius.
- **Circumference** — the distance round the circle (also used for the curve itself: "a point on the circumference").
- **Chord** — a straight line joining two points on the circle.
- **Arc** — part of the circumference. The **minor arc** is the shorter one; the **major arc** is the longer.
- **Sector** — the region between two radii and an arc (a "pizza slice").
- **Segment** — the region between a chord and an arc. A chord splits a circle into a minor and a major segment.
- **Tangent** — a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point (the **point of contact**).
- **Cyclic quadrilateral** — a quadrilateral with all four vertices on the circle.

"Angle **subtended** by an arc at a point" means the angle made at that point by lines from the two ends of the arc.

### The theorems (with the wording examiners accept)

1. **The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference** (subtended by the same arc). If $\angle AOB = 130°$ at the centre, then $\angle ACB = 65°$ at any point $C$ on the major arc.
2. **The angle in a semicircle is a right angle.** If $AB$ is a diameter and $C$ is on the circle, $\angle ACB = 90°$. (This is theorem 1 with the centre angle $180°$.)
3. **Angles in the same segment are equal.** Angles subtended by the same chord (or arc), at points on the same side of it, are equal — look for a "bow-tie" shape.
4. **Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$.**
5. **The tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius** at the point of contact.
6. **Tangents from an external point are equal in length.** The line from that point to the centre bisects the angle between the tangents, so the diagram is a kite.
7. **The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord** (and conversely, the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it).
8. **The alternate segment theorem:** the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate (opposite) segment. If the tangent at $A$ makes angle $x$ with chord $AB$, then any angle $ACB$ in the segment on the other side of $AB$ is also $x$.

### Proving the theorems

**Angle at the centre.** Join $C$ through $O$ and extend to $D$. Triangles $OAC$ and $OBC$ are isosceles (radii). If $\angle OCA = x$ then $\angle OAC = x$, and the exterior angle $\angle AOD = 2x$ (exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the interior opposite angles). Similarly with $\angle OCB = y$, $\angle BOD = 2y$. So $\angle AOB = 2x + 2y = 2(x + y) = 2\angle ACB$.

**Angle in a semicircle.** With $AB$ a diameter, the angle at the centre is $180°$, so the angle at the circumference is $90°$. (Or directly: $OA = OB = OC$ gives two isosceles triangles with base angles $x$ and $y$; $2x + 2y = 180°$ so $x + y = 90°$.)

**Same segment.** Both angles are half of the same angle at the centre, so they are equal.

**Cyclic quadrilateral.** For $ABCD$, the angles at the centre subtended by the two arcs $BD$ are $2\angle A$ and $2\angle C$, and together they make $360°$ (angles around a point). So $\angle A + \angle C = 180°$.

**Tangents equal.** Tangents from $P$ touch at $A$ and $B$. $\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90°$ (tangent perpendicular to radius), $OP$ common, $OA = OB$ (radii). Triangles $OAP$ and $OBP$ are congruent (RHS), so $PA = PB$.

**Perpendicular bisects the chord.** For chord $AB$ with foot of perpendicular $M$: $OA = OB$ (radii), $OM$ common, $\angle OMA = \angle OMB = 90°$: congruent (RHS), so $AM = BM$.

**Alternate segment theorem.** Let the tangent at $A$ make angle $x$ with chord $AB$. Draw the diameter $AD$ and join $DB$. $\angle DAT = 90°$ (tangent ⟂ radius), so $\angle DAB = 90° - x$. $\angle ABD = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle), so $\angle ADB = 180° - 90° - (90° - x) = x$. Any angle $ACB$ in the same segment as $D$ equals $\angle ADB = x$ (angles in the same segment).

### Spotting the theorem

- A **diameter** with a triangle standing on it → angle in a semicircle.
- Lines from the **centre** and from the **circumference** to the same two points → angle at the centre is twice…
- A **bow-tie** (two triangles on the same chord, crossing) → angles in the same segment.
- Four points on the circle joined up → cyclic quadrilateral.
- A **tangent** → a right angle with the radius; two tangents → equal lengths and a kite; tangent **plus a chord from the point of contact** → alternate segment.
- **Radii** → isosceles triangles. This is the fact most often missing from a chain of reasoning.

::: terms
- **Chord**: a straight line joining two points on a circle; a diameter is the longest chord.
- **Tangent**: a straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius there.
- **Segment**: the region of a circle between a chord and the arc it cuts off.
- **Sector**: the region of a circle between two radii and the arc between them.
- **Cyclic quadrilateral**: a quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on the circumference of a circle.
- **Subtended angle**: the angle made at a point by lines drawn from the two ends of an arc or chord.
- **Alternate segment theorem**: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
:::

::: method
1. **Mark every radius as equal** and write the base angles of each isosceles triangle straight away.
2. **Find the theorem from the shape** (diameter, centre, bow-tie, cyclic quadrilateral, tangent, tangent plus chord) and write the angle on the diagram.
3. **One step per line with its reason**, in the standard words: "angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference", "angles in the same segment are equal", "opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$", "the tangent is perpendicular to the radius", "alternate segment theorem". A correct angle with a missing or vague reason usually loses the reasoning mark (often a whole B1 or C1 at the end).
4. **Mix in basic angle facts**: triangles, straight lines, isosceles triangles and parallel lines are usually needed as well.
5. **Algebra questions:** write each angle in terms of $x$ with reasons, then form and solve an equation.
6. **Proofs:** use letters (not numbers) for the angles, give a reason for every line, and end with a sentence stating the result. For "prove the angle at the centre is twice…", split with a line through the centre as shown.
7. **Lengths:** the perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord and a tangent meets a radius at $90°$ — both give right-angled triangles for Pythagoras and trigonometry (calculator papers).
:::

::: example Angle at the centre and cyclic quadrilateral
$A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ lie on a circle centre $O$, in that order. $\angle AOC = 124°$ (the angle facing arc $ABC$). Find $\angle ADC$ and $\angle ABC$, giving reasons.

$\angle ADC = 124° \div 2 = 62°$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference).

$\angle ABC = 180° - 62° = 118°$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$).
:::

::: example Angle in a semicircle
$AB$ is a diameter of a circle and $C$ is on the circle. $\angle CAB = 34°$. Find $\angle ABC$.

$\angle ACB = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle is a right angle).

$\angle ABC = 180° - 90° - 34° = 56°$ (angles in a triangle add up to $180°$).
:::

::: example Two tangents
$PA$ and $PB$ are tangents from $P$ to a circle centre $O$. $\angle APB = 50°$. Find $\angle AOB$ and $\angle PAB$.

$\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90°$ (tangent is perpendicular to the radius).

$\angle AOB = 360° - 90° - 90° - 50° = 130°$ (angles in a quadrilateral add up to $360°$).

$PA = PB$ (tangents from an external point are equal), so $\angle PAB = (180° - 50°) \div 2 = 65°$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle).
:::

::: example Cyclic quadrilateral with algebra
$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with $\angle A = (3x + 10)°$ and $\angle C = (2x - 5)°$. Find $\angle A$.

$3x + 10 + 2x - 5 = 180$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$).

$5x + 5 = 180 \Rightarrow x = 35$, so $\angle A = 115°$ (and $\angle C = 65°$).
:::

::: example Chord and radius (grade 7)
A chord of length 16 cm is in a circle of radius 10 cm. How far is the chord from the centre?

The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, giving a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 10 (radius) and one side 8 (half the chord).

Distance $= \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{36} = 6$ cm.
:::

::: example Three theorems in a chain (grade 8)
$A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ lie on a circle in that order. $TA$ is the tangent at $A$, and $T$ is on the side of $AB$ away from $C$ and $D$. $\angle TAB = 58°$ and $\angle ADC = 110°$. Find $\angle BAC$, giving reasons.

$\angle ACB = 58°$ (alternate segment theorem).

$\angle ABC = 180° - 110° = 70°$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$).

$\angle BAC = 180° - 58° - 70° = 52°$ (angles in a triangle add up to $180°$).
:::

::: example A proof with tangents (grade 9)
$A$, $B$ and $C$ lie on a circle centre $O$, with $C$ on the major arc $AB$. The tangents at $A$ and $B$ meet at $T$. $\angle ACB = x$. Prove that $\angle ATB = 180° - 2x$.

$\angle AOB = 2x$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference).

$\angle OAT = \angle OBT = 90°$ (tangent is perpendicular to the radius).

In quadrilateral $OATB$: $\angle ATB = 360° - 90° - 90° - 2x$ (angles in a quadrilateral add up to $360°$).

So $\angle ATB = 180° - 2x$. ∎
:::

::: q
$A$, $B$, $C$ are on a circle centre $O$, with $C$ on the major arc. $\angle AOB = 96°$. Find $\angle ACB$.
---
$48°$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference).
:::

::: q
$A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ are on a circle, with $C$ and $D$ on the same side of chord $AB$. $\angle ACB = 41°$. Find $\angle ADB$.
---
$41°$ (angles in the same segment are equal).
:::

::: q
One angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is $73°$. Find the angle opposite it.
---
$180° - 73° = 107°$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180°$).
:::

::: q
$PA$ is a tangent to a circle centre $O$ at $A$. The radius is 5 cm and $PA = 12$ cm. Find $OP$.
---
$\angle OAP = 90°$ (tangent ⟂ radius). $OP = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = 13$ cm.
:::

::: q
$A$ and $B$ lie on a circle centre $O$ with $\angle AOB = 110°$. Find $\angle OAB$.
---
$OA = OB$ (radii), so $\angle OAB = (180° - 110°) \div 2 = 35°$.
:::

::: q
The tangent at $A$ makes an angle of $64°$ with chord $AB$. $C$ is a point on the circle in the alternate segment. Find $\angle ACB$.
---
$64°$ (alternate segment theorem).
:::

::: q
$A$, $B$, $C$ are on a circle centre $O$, with $C$ on the minor arc $AB$, and $\angle ACB = 130°$. Find the reflex angle $AOB$.
---
Reflex $\angle AOB = 2 \times 130° = 260°$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference — the centre angle facing the same arc is the reflex one).
:::

::: q
A circle has radius 13 cm. A chord is 24 cm long. How far is the chord from the centre?
---
Half the chord is 12 (perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord). $\sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = 5$ cm.
:::

::: q
A quadrilateral has angles $80°$, $90°$, $110°$ and $80°$, in order. Could it be cyclic? Explain.
---
No: the opposite angles $80° + 110° = 190° \neq 180°$.
:::

::: watch
- **Reasons in non-standard words**: "angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference" — not "centre angle doubles" or "bow-tie rule". Write the full theorem name for every step.
- **Missing the isosceles triangle**: two radii always give equal base angles, and this is the step most often left out.
- **Using "angle at the centre" when the two angles are not subtended by the same arc** — check that both angles stand on the same two points, with the centre on the correct side.
- **Cyclic quadrilateral rule on a shape whose fourth vertex is the centre** — $O$ is not on the circle, so $OABC$ is not cyclic.
- **Alternate segment angle in the wrong segment**: the equal angle is on the **other side** of the chord from the tangent angle.
- **Assuming a line is a diameter** or passes through $O$ when the question does not say so.
:::

::: test
$A$, $B$ and $C$ lie on a circle centre $O$, with $C$ on the major arc. $\angle AOB = 140°$. Find $\angle ACB$, giving a reason. (2)
---
$70°$ (B1); angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference (B1).
:::

::: test
$AB$ is a diameter of a circle and $C$ is a point on the circle. $\angle ABC = 27°$. Find $\angle CAB$, giving reasons. (3)
---
$\angle ACB = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle) (B1). $\angle CAB = 180° - 90° - 27°$ (M1) $= 63°$ (angles in a triangle) (A1).
:::

::: test
$PA$ is a tangent from $P$ to a circle centre $O$, touching at $A$. $OA = 7$ cm and $PA = 24$ cm. Find $OP$ and angle $OPA$. (4)
---
$\angle OAP = 90°$ (tangent ⟂ radius) (B1). $OP = \sqrt{7^2 + 24^2} = 25$ cm (M1 A1). $\angle OPA = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{7}{24} = 16.3°$ (B1).
:::

::: test
$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with $\angle ABC = 2y$ and $\angle ADC = y + 30°$. Find $\angle ABC$. (3)
---
$2y + y + 30 = 180$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral) (M1). $y = 50$ (A1). $\angle ABC = 100°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$A$, $B$, $C$ lie on a circle and $CA = CB$. The tangent at $A$ makes an angle of $72°$ with $AB$, on the side away from $C$. Find $\angle ABC$, giving reasons. (4)
---
$\angle ACB = 72°$ (alternate segment theorem) (B1 B1). $CA = CB$, so $\angle CAB = \angle CBA$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle) (M1). $\angle ABC = (180° - 72°) \div 2 = 54°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$PA$ and $PB$ are tangents to a circle centre $O$. $\angle APB = 38°$. $C$ is a point on the major arc $AB$. Find $\angle ACB$, giving reasons. (4)
---
$\angle OAP = \angle OBP = 90°$ (tangent ⟂ radius) (B1). $\angle AOB = 360° - 180° - 38° = 142°$ (angles in a quadrilateral) (M1 A1). $\angle ACB = 71°$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference) (A1).
:::

::: test
$AB$ is a diameter of a circle centre $O$, and $C$ is any other point on the circle. Prove that $\angle ACB = 90°$. (4)
---
$OA = OB = OC$ (radii) (M1). Let $\angle OAC = x$ and $\angle OBC = y$; then $\angle OCA = x$ and $\angle OCB = y$ (base angles of isosceles triangles) (M1). In triangle $ABC$: $x + y + (x + y) = 180°$ (angles in a triangle) (M1), so $x + y = 90°$, i.e. $\angle ACB = 90°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$A$, $B$ and $C$ lie on a circle centre $O$, with $C$ on the major arc $AB$. $\angle OAB = x$. Prove that $\angle ACB = 90° - x$. (4)
---
$OA = OB$ (radii), so $\angle OBA = x$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle) (M1). $\angle AOB = 180° - 2x$ (angles in a triangle) (M1). $\angle ACB = \dfrac12(180° - 2x)$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference) (M1) $= 90° - x$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Prove the alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord is equal to the angle in the alternate segment. (5)
---
Let the tangent at $A$ make angle $x$ with chord $AB$. Draw the diameter $AD$ and join $DB$ (M1). $\angle DAT = 90°$ (tangent ⟂ radius), so $\angle DAB = 90° - x$ (M1). $\angle ABD = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle) (M1). $\angle ADB = 180° - 90° - (90° - x) = x$ (angles in a triangle) (A1). Any angle $ACB$ in the alternate segment equals $\angle ADB = x$ (angles in the same segment are equal) (A1).
:::
