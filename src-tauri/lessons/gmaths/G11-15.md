## What it is

This topic is the toolkit behind almost every geometry question: working with points on coordinate axes, knowing your 3D solids, drawing and reading plans and elevations, converting between units, and measuring with scales and bearings. None of it is hard on its own — marks go because one small fact (like $1\text{ m}^2 = 10\,000\text{ cm}^2$) is misremembered inside a bigger question.

### Geometry on coordinate axes (G11)

For two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$:

- **Midpoint** $= \left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$ — the average of the $x$s and the average of the $y$s.
- **Length** $AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$ — this is just Pythagoras on the horizontal and vertical steps.
- **Gradient** $= \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ = "change in $y$ over change in $x$". Parallel lines have equal gradients; perpendicular gradients multiply to $-1$.

*Mini-example.* $A(-2, 3)$ and $B(4, -5)$. Midpoint $= (1, -1)$. Steps are $6$ across and $-8$ up, so $AB = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10$. Gradient $= \dfrac{-8}{6} = -\dfrac43$.

Typical problems: find a missing vertex (in a parallelogram $ABCD$, the step from $A$ to $B$ equals the step from $D$ to $C$); decide what shape four points make (compare side lengths and gradients); find the area of a triangle or trapezium drawn on axes (use horizontal/vertical sides as base and height, or a surrounding rectangle minus corner triangles); work backwards from a midpoint (if $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, then $B = 2M - A$, coordinate by coordinate).

*Mini-example.* $M(3, 1)$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $A = (-1, 4)$. Then $B = (2 \times 3 - (-1), 2 \times 1 - 4) = (7, -2)$.

### Properties of 3D solids (G12)

A **face** is a flat surface, an **edge** is where two faces meet, a **vertex** is a corner. Curved solids have **surfaces** rather than faces.

| Solid | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube / cuboid | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Square-based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Tetrahedron (triangular pyramid) | 4 | 6 | 4 |
| Hexagonal prism | 8 | 18 | 12 |

A **prism** has the same cross-section all the way through: an $n$-sided prism has $n + 2$ faces, $3n$ edges and $2n$ vertices. A **pyramid** on an $n$-sided base has $n + 1$ faces, $2n$ edges and $n + 1$ vertices. For any solid with flat faces and no holes, **Euler's formula** $F + V - E = 2$ is a quick check. A **cylinder** has 2 flat faces and 1 curved surface (2 curved edges, no vertices); a **cone** has 1 flat face, 1 curved surface and 1 vertex (the apex); a **sphere** has one curved surface.

### Plans and elevations (G13)

- The **plan** is the view from directly above.
- The **front elevation** is the view from the front; the **side elevation** is the view from the side.

Each view shows the **true shape and size** of what you see from that direction, drawn as a 2D outline with no perspective. Draw a solid line wherever there is a change in depth or height (an edge you can see). At Higher tier you must **construct** these views accurately on a grid from an isometric (3D) drawing, and **interpret** them — rebuild the solid in your head, count cubes, or find a volume.

*Mini-example.* An L-shaped prism has an L-shaped front face and depth 3 cm. Its front elevation is the L; its side elevation and plan are rectangles, each 3 cm deep, with a line across where the step in height (or width) occurs.

### Units of measure (G14)

Length: $1\text{ km} = 1000\text{ m}$, $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$, $1\text{ cm} = 10\text{ mm}$. Mass: $1\text{ kg} = 1000\text{ g}$, $1\text{ tonne} = 1000\text{ kg}$. Capacity: $1\text{ litre} = 1000\text{ ml}$ and $1\text{ ml} = 1\text{ cm}^3$, so $1\text{ litre} = 1000\text{ cm}^3$ and $1\text{ m}^3 = 1000$ litres.

**Area and volume conversions square and cube the length factor:**
$$1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm} \Rightarrow 1\text{ m}^2 = 100^2 = 10\,000\text{ cm}^2 \Rightarrow 1\text{ m}^3 = 100^3 = 1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3$$
Likewise $1\text{ cm}^2 = 100\text{ mm}^2$, $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1000\text{ mm}^3$, $1\text{ km}^2 = 1\,000\,000\text{ m}^2$.

*Mini-example.* $3.5\text{ m}^2 = 3.5 \times 10\,000 = 35\,000\text{ cm}^2$; $84\,000\text{ cm}^3 = 84\,000 \div 1\,000\,000 = 0.084\text{ m}^3$.

**Time** is not decimal: $2.4$ hours $= 2$ hours $24$ minutes (because $0.4 \times 60 = 24$), and $1$ hour $45$ minutes $= 1.75$ hours. **Money** is given to 2 decimal places (£3.60, not £3.6). Related compound measures: speed $=$ distance $\div$ time, density $=$ mass $\div$ volume — convert units before dividing if they don't match.

### Measuring, scales, maps and bearings (G15)

Measure lengths to the nearest millimetre and angles with a protractor to the nearest degree. A **scale** links drawing to reality: "1 cm represents 5 km", or a **ratio scale** $1 : 25\,000$ meaning 1 unit on the map is 25 000 of the same units in real life. So 4 cm on a $1:25\,000$ map is $100\,000$ cm $= 1$ km.

A **bearing** is an angle measured **from north**, **clockwise**, written with **three figures**: east is $090°$, south is $180°$, south-west is $225°$, north-west is $315°$. "The bearing of $B$ from $A$" means stand at $A$, face north, turn clockwise until you face $B$. The **back bearing** (bearing of $A$ from $B$) differs by $180°$:
$$\text{bearing of } A \text{ from } B = \text{bearing of } B \text{ from } A \pm 180°$$
(add 180 if the first bearing is less than $180°$, subtract if it is more).

*Mini-example.* The bearing of $Q$ from $P$ is $064°$. The bearing of $P$ from $Q$ is $064° + 180° = 244°$.

Bearing questions often need the fact that the north lines at two points are **parallel**, so co-interior angles add to $180°$ and alternate angles are equal. Later topics combine bearings with Pythagoras, trigonometry and the sine and cosine rules.

::: terms
- **Midpoint**: the point halfway along a line segment — average the x-coordinates and average the y-coordinates.
- **Face, edge, vertex**: a flat surface of a solid, a line where two faces meet, and a corner where edges meet.
- **Plan**: the view of a 3D shape from directly above, drawn full size with no perspective.
- **Front and side elevation**: the views of a 3D shape from the front and from the side.
- **Scale**: the link between a drawing or map and real life, e.g. 1 : 50 000 or 1 cm represents 2 km.
- **Bearing**: a direction given as an angle measured clockwise from north, always written with three figures.
- **Back bearing**: the bearing for the return journey; it differs from the original bearing by 180°.
- **Area and volume conversion**: square the length factor for area (1 m² = 10 000 cm²) and cube it for volume (1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³).
:::

::: method
1. **Coordinates:** sketch the points roughly, even if no grid is given — it shows you which vertex is missing and stops sign errors. Work with the **steps** (change in $x$, change in $y$) between points.
2. **Proving a shape:** "show that" needs evidence written down — equal lengths from $\sqrt{\ }$ calculations, parallel sides from equal gradients, a right angle from gradients multiplying to $-1$ or from Pythagoras' converse. State the conclusion in words at the end.
3. **Solids:** list faces, edges and vertices systematically (top, bottom, sides) and check with $F + V - E = 2$.
4. **Plans and elevations:** decide which direction each view is from; draw only what that view sees, full size on the grid, with a line wherever the depth or height changes. Label the views if the question does not do it for you.
5. **Units:** convert **before** calculating so every length is in the same unit. For area square the length factor, for volume cube it. Remember $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ ml}$ and $1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1$ litre. On Paper 1 (non-calculator) these conversions are powers of 10 — show them.
6. **Scales:** convert the map distance with the scale, then change units at the end (100 000 cm = 1 km). For an area on a map, square the scale factor.
7. **Bearings:** draw a north line at **every** point you stand at. Measure clockwise from north and give three figures ($045°$, not $45°$). Use $\pm 180°$ for back bearings and parallel north lines (co-interior angles sum to $180°$) for angles inside the diagram.
8. **Method marks** come from written steps: the coordinate steps, the conversion factor, the angle facts with reasons. A correct final answer with no working may not score if the question says "show" or "give reasons".
:::

::: example Missing vertex of a parallelogram
$A(1, 2)$, $B(5, 4)$ and $C(6, 9)$ are three vertices of parallelogram $ABCD$. Find $D$.

In $ABCD$ the side $AB$ is parallel and equal to $DC$. The step from $A$ to $B$ is $(+4, +2)$, so the step from $D$ to $C$ is also $(+4, +2)$:
$$D = (6 - 4, 9 - 2) = (2, 7).$$
Check: $A \to D$ is $(+1, +5)$ and $B \to C$ is $(+1, +5)$ — equal, so $AD$ is parallel and equal to $BC$ as well.
:::

::: example Plan and elevation to volume
The front elevation of a prism is a trapezium with parallel sides 4 cm and 10 cm, 4 cm apart. The plan is a rectangle 10 cm by 6 cm. State the number of faces, edges and vertices, and find the volume.

The trapezium is the cross-section and 6 cm is the length, so this is a trapezoidal prism: $4 + 2 = 6$ faces, $3 \times 4 = 12$ edges, $2 \times 4 = 8$ vertices (check: $6 + 8 - 12 = 2$).

Volume $=$ area of cross-section $\times$ length $= \dfrac12(4 + 10) \times 4 \times 6 = 28 \times 6 = 168\text{ cm}^3$.
:::

::: example Units in a rate problem
A fish tank is a cuboid measuring 1.2 m by 80 cm by 50 cm. It is filled at 4 litres per minute. How long does it take to fill, in hours?

Convert first: $1.2\text{ m} = 120\text{ cm}$. Volume $= 120 \times 80 \times 50 = 480\,000\text{ cm}^3$.

$1000\text{ cm}^3 = 1$ litre, so the tank holds $480$ litres. Time $= 480 \div 4 = 120$ minutes $= 2$ hours.
:::

::: example Area on a map
On a map with scale $1 : 50\,000$, a lake has an area of $6\text{ cm}^2$. Find the real area in km$^2$.

$1\text{ cm}$ represents $50\,000\text{ cm} = 500\text{ m} = 0.5\text{ km}$.

So $1\text{ cm}^2$ represents $0.5^2 = 0.25\text{ km}^2$ — the length scale is squared for area.

Real area $= 6 \times 0.25 = 1.5\text{ km}^2$.
:::

::: example Bearings with angle facts
From $A$, the bearing of $B$ is $070°$ and the bearing of $C$ is $130°$. $C$ is due south of $B$. Find angle $ABC$, angle $ACB$ and the bearing of $A$ from $C$.

Bearing of $A$ from $B$ $= 070° + 180° = 250°$. From $B$, $C$ is due south, bearing $180°$. So angle $ABC = 250° - 180° = 70°$.

Angle $BAC = 130° - 70° = 60°$, so angle $ACB = 180° - 70° - 60° = 50°$ (angles in a triangle).

Bearing of $A$ from $C$ $= 130° + 180° = 310°$. Check: north at $C$ points towards $B$, and $360° - 310° = 50°$ = angle $ACB$. ✓
:::

::: example Grade 8–9: proving a right angle and finding an area
$A(-3, 1)$, $B(5, 5)$ and $C(-1, -3)$. Show that triangle $ABC$ is right-angled and find its exact area.

Steps: $A \to B = (8, 4)$, $A \to C = (2, -4)$, $B \to C = (-6, -8)$.

$AB^2 = 64 + 16 = 80$, $AC^2 = 4 + 16 = 20$, $BC^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$. Since $80 + 20 = 100$, $AB^2 + AC^2 = BC^2$, so by the converse of Pythagoras the angle at $A$ is $90°$. (Or: gradients $\frac12$ and $-2$ multiply to $-1$.)

The right angle is at $A$, so $AB$ and $AC$ are base and height: area $= \dfrac12 \times \sqrt{80} \times \sqrt{20} = \dfrac12\sqrt{1600} = \dfrac12 \times 40 = 20$ square units.
:::

::: q
Find the midpoint and the length of the line segment joining $P(-4, 7)$ and $Q(2, -1)$.
---
Midpoint $= \left(\dfrac{-4 + 2}{2}, \dfrac{7 + (-1)}{2}\right) = (-1, 3)$. Steps $6$ and $-8$: $PQ = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10$.
:::

::: q
$M(2, -3)$ is the midpoint of $PQ$, where $P = (7, 1)$. Find $Q$.
---
$Q = (2 \times 2 - 7, 2 \times (-3) - 1) = (-3, -7)$.
:::

::: q
Convert (a) $2.6\text{ m}^2$ to cm$^2$ (b) $45\,000\text{ cm}^3$ to litres and to m$^3$.
---
(a) $2.6 \times 10\,000 = 26\,000\text{ cm}^2$. (b) $45\,000 \div 1000 = 45$ litres; $45\,000 \div 1\,000\,000 = 0.045\text{ m}^3$.
:::

::: q
The bearing of $B$ from $A$ is $285°$. Find the bearing of $A$ from $B$.
---
$285° - 180° = 105°$.
:::

::: q
On a map with scale $1 : 25\,000$, a footpath measures 7.4 cm. How long is the real path in km?
---
$7.4 \times 25\,000 = 185\,000\text{ cm} = 1850\text{ m} = 1.85\text{ km}$.
:::

::: q
How many faces, edges and vertices do (a) a pentagonal prism (b) an octagon-based pyramid have?
---
(a) $5 + 2 = 7$ faces, $3 \times 5 = 15$ edges, $2 \times 5 = 10$ vertices. (b) $8 + 1 = 9$ faces, $2 \times 8 = 16$ edges, $8 + 1 = 9$ vertices. Both satisfy $F + V - E = 2$.
:::

::: q
Describe the plan and front elevation of (a) a cylinder standing on its circular end (b) a cone standing on its base.
---
(a) Plan: a circle. Front elevation: a rectangle (width = diameter, height = height of cylinder). (b) Plan: a circle with a dot at the centre (the apex). Front elevation: an isosceles triangle.
:::

::: q
A car travels for 2.4 hours at an average speed of 65 km/h. How far does it go, and how long is 2.4 hours in hours and minutes?
---
Distance $= 65 \times 2.4 = 156$ km. $0.4 \times 60 = 24$, so 2 hours 24 minutes.
:::

::: q
A metal block measures 20 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm and has a mass of 3.2 kg. Find its density in g/cm$^3$.
---
Volume $= 400\text{ cm}^3$. Mass $= 3200$ g. Density $= 3200 \div 400 = 8\text{ g/cm}^3$.
:::

::: q
Show that $A(0, 0)$, $B(4, 3)$, $C(7, -1)$ and $D(3, -4)$ are the vertices of a square.
---
Steps: $AB = (4, 3)$, $BC = (3, -4)$, $CD = (-4, -3)$, $DA = (-3, 4)$. Every side has length $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, so $ABCD$ is a rhombus. Gradient $AB = \frac34$, gradient $BC = -\frac43$; $\frac34 \times -\frac43 = -1$, so angle $ABC = 90°$. A rhombus with a right angle is a square.
:::

::: q
A 1 cm cube is cut from one top corner of a 3 cm by 2 cm by 2 cm cuboid. How many faces, edges and vertices does the new solid have?
---
Faces: the original 6 plus 3 new ones $= 9$. Vertices: lose 1 corner, gain the cube's other 7 corners: $8 - 1 + 7 = 14$. Edges: $12 + 9 = 21$ (3 edges meeting at the new inner corner and 6 new edges on the old faces). Check: $9 + 14 - 21 = 2$.
:::

::: watch
- **Area and volume conversions:** $1\text{ m}^2$ is $10\,000\text{ cm}^2$, not 100; $1\text{ m}^3$ is $1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3$, not 100 or 1000. Map areas use the **squared** scale.
- **Mixed units:** a cuboid given as 1.2 m by 80 cm by 50 cm must be converted to one unit **before** multiplying.
- **Time is base 60:** 1.25 hours is 1 hour 15 minutes, not 1 hour 25 minutes; 3 h 30 min is 3.5 h, not 3.3 h.
- **Bearings:** measured from **north**, **clockwise**, **three figures**. "From $A$" means the protractor sits at $A$. A north line is needed at every point used.
- **Coordinate slips:** subtracting a negative (the step from $-3$ to $5$ is $8$, not $2$), and writing a midpoint as the half-difference instead of the average.
- **"Show that" with no conclusion:** equal lengths and a right angle have to be followed by the sentence "so $ABCD$ is a square".
:::

Convert (a) 5.2 litres to cm$^3$ (b) 0.35 m to mm. (2)
---
(a) $5.2 \times 1000 = 5200\text{ cm}^3$ (1). (b) $0.35 \times 1000 = 350$ mm (1).
:::

::: test
The bearing of $Y$ from $X$ is $048°$. (a) Find the bearing of $X$ from $Y$. (b) The bearing of $Z$ from $X$ is $290°$. Find the angle $YXZ$ inside the triangle $XYZ$. (3)
---
(a) $048 + 180 = 228°$ (1). (b) Clockwise from $XZ$ to $XY$: $(360 - 290) + 48$ (M1) $= 118°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
On a map with scale $1 : 40\,000$, two villages are 6.5 cm apart. Find the real distance in kilometres. (2)
---
$6.5 \times 40\,000 = 260\,000$ cm (M1) $= 2.6$ km (A1).
:::

::: test
$A$ is the point $(-3, 8)$ and $B$ is the point $(5, 2)$. Find (a) the midpoint of $AB$ (b) the length of $AB$. (3)
---
(a) $(1, 5)$ (1). (b) $\sqrt{8^2 + 6^2}$ (M1) $= 10$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A solid has 7 faces and 10 vertices. (a) How many edges does it have? (b) Name a solid with these properties. (2)
---
(a) $E = F + V - 2 = 15$ (1). (b) A pentagonal prism (1).
:::

::: test
$A(-2, 1)$, $B(3, 3)$ and $C(5, -1)$ are three vertices of parallelogram $ABCD$. Find the coordinates of $D$. (2)
---
Step $B \to C = (2, -4)$ (M1), so $D = A + (2, -4) = (0, -3)$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A container holds $0.6\text{ m}^3$ of juice. How many 250 ml cups can be filled from it? (3)
---
$0.6 \times 1\,000\,000 = 600\,000\text{ cm}^3$ (M1) $= 600\,000$ ml (M1); $600\,000 \div 250 = 2400$ cups (A1).
:::

::: test
A swimming pool is a cuboid 25 m long, 10 m wide and 1.8 m deep. It is filled at 500 litres per minute. How many hours does it take to fill? (4)
---
Volume $= 25 \times 10 \times 1.8 = 450\text{ m}^3$ (M1) $= 450\,000$ litres (M1). $450\,000 \div 500 = 900$ minutes (M1) $= 15$ hours (A1).
:::

::: test
On a map with scale $1 : 25\,000$, a forest has an area of $12\text{ cm}^2$. Find the real area of the forest in km$^2$. (3)
---
$1\text{ cm}$ represents $25\,000\text{ cm} = 0.25$ km (M1); $1\text{ cm}^2$ represents $0.25^2 = 0.0625\text{ km}^2$ (M1); $12 \times 0.0625 = 0.75\text{ km}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A ship sails 8 km from $P$ to $Q$ on a bearing of $060°$, then 6 km from $Q$ to $R$ on a bearing of $150°$. (a) Show that angle $PQR = 90°$. (b) Find the distance $PR$ and the bearing of $R$ from $P$, to the nearest degree. (5)
---
(a) Bearing of $P$ from $Q$ $= 240°$ (M1); $240 - 150 = 90°$ (A1). (b) $PR = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$ km (B1). Angle $QPR = \tan^{-1}\frac{6}{8} = 36.9°$ (M1); bearing $= 60 + 36.9 = 097°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$A(1, 1)$, $B(7, 3)$ and $C(5, 9)$. Prove that triangle $ABC$ is a right-angled isosceles triangle, and find its area. (5)
---
$AB^2 = 6^2 + 2^2 = 40$, $BC^2 = (-2)^2 + 6^2 = 40$, $AC^2 = 4^2 + 8^2 = 80$ (M1 for any two). $AB = BC$ so isosceles (A1). $AB^2 + BC^2 = 80 = AC^2$ so the angle at $B$ is $90°$ by the converse of Pythagoras (A1). Area $= \frac12 \times \sqrt{40} \times \sqrt{40}$ (M1) $= 20$ square units (A1).
:::
