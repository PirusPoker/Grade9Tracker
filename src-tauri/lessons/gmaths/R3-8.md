## What it is

A **ratio** compares the sizes of two or more quantities; a **fraction** compares one part with a whole (or one quantity with another). This topic is about moving freely between the two, sharing amounts in a ratio, and using ratios as **proportion** — two ratios that are equal — in recipes, maps, mixtures, currency and graphs.

### One quantity as a fraction of another

Write the first quantity over the second, **in the same units**, and simplify:

- 45 minutes as a fraction of 2 hours: $\dfrac{45}{120} = \dfrac{3}{8}$.
- 18 kg as a fraction of 12 kg: $\dfrac{18}{12} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ — a fraction greater than 1, because the first quantity is bigger.

### Ratio notation and simplest form

$a : b$ is read "$a$ to $b$". Simplify by dividing every part by the highest common factor, just like a fraction: $24 : 36 = 2 : 3$.

- **Units** must match first: $40\text{ cm} : 2\text{ m} = 40 : 200 = 1 : 5$.
- **Decimals:** multiply up to whole numbers: $1.5 : 2.5 = 15 : 25 = 3 : 5$.
- **Fractions:** multiply by a common denominator: $\frac12 : \frac13 = 3 : 2$ (multiply both by 6).
- **The form $1 : n$:** divide both parts by the first. $5 : 8 = 1 : 1.6$. Here $n$ need not be whole.

Order matters: boys to girls $3 : 5$ is not the same as girls to boys.

### Sharing in a ratio

**Part : part.** Share £84 in the ratio $3 : 4$. Total parts $3 + 4 = 7$; one part is $84 \div 7 = £12$; the shares are $3 \times 12 = £36$ and $4 \times 12 = £48$. Check: $36 + 48 = 84$.

**Given one share or the difference.** If you know one share or the difference between shares, find what **one part** is worth from that, not from a total. Ali and Ben share money in the ratio $7 : 4$ and Ali gets £27 more: the difference is $3$ parts $= £27$, so one part is £9 and the total is $11 \times 9 = £99$.

**Part : whole.** "The ratio of boys to the whole class is $3 : 8$" means $\frac38$ of the class are boys, so $\frac58$ are girls and boys : girls $= 3 : 5$.

### Ratios and fractions

If $a : b = 2 : 5$ then:

- $a$ is $\dfrac{2}{7}$ of the **total**, and $b$ is $\dfrac57$ of the total;
- $a$ is $\dfrac25$ of $b$ (a **multiplicative relationship**: $a = \dfrac25 b$), and $b = \dfrac52 a$.

Going the other way, "Sam has $\frac34$ as many stickers as Tia" means Sam : Tia $= 3 : 4$.

### Proportion as equality of ratios

Two ratios are **in proportion** when they simplify to the same thing: $6 : 9 = 10 : 15$ because both are $2 : 3$. Writing ratios as fractions gives an equation you can solve:

$$x : 12 = 5 : 8 \Rightarrow \frac{x}{12} = \frac{5}{8} \Rightarrow x = \frac{60}{8} = 7.5$$

This is how you **scale** a recipe (flour : people stays constant), convert a **currency** (pounds : euros stays constant), and compare **concentrations** (squash : water).

### Real contexts

- **Scaling:** a recipe for 4 uses 300 g flour; for 10 people multiply by $\frac{10}{4} = 2.5$: 750 g.
- **Conversion:** £1 = €1.16, so £250 = €290 and €87 = $87 \div 1.16 = £75$.
- **Mixing:** concrete is cement : sand : gravel $= 1 : 2 : 4$. With 12 kg of sand, 2 parts = 12 kg, 1 part = 6 kg, total $7 \times 6 = 42$ kg of concrete.
- **Concentration and comparison:** paint A is red : yellow $3 : 5$ and paint B is $2 : 3$. Compare the **fractions** of red: $\frac38 = 0.375$ and $\frac25 = 0.4$, so B is redder.

### Ratios and linear functions

If $y : x$ is always $3 : 1$, then $y = 3x$: a straight-line graph **through the origin** with gradient 3. Any two quantities in a fixed ratio are in **direct proportion**, and their graph is a straight line through $(0, 0)$ whose gradient is the ratio. A currency conversion graph is one: pounds on one axis, euros on the other, gradient $1.16$.

Conversely, a straight line that does **not** pass through the origin (such as a taxi fare $C = 3 + 1.8m$) does **not** show a fixed ratio — doubling the miles does not double the cost.

### Combining ratios

To join $a : b = 2 : 3$ and $b : c = 4 : 5$, make the shared quantity $b$ the same in both. The LCM of 3 and 4 is 12: $a : b = 8 : 12$ and $b : c = 12 : 15$, so $a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15$.

::: terms
- **Ratio**: a comparison of two or more quantities in the form a : b, showing how many times one is as big as another.
- **Simplest form**: a ratio whose parts are whole numbers with no common factor other than 1.
- **Part : whole ratio**: a ratio comparing one part with the total, which is the same as a fraction of the total.
- **Proportion**: two ratios that are equal, such as 6 : 9 and 10 : 15; they can be written as equal fractions.
- **Unitary method**: finding the value of one part (or one unit) first, then scaling up to what is needed.
- **Direct proportion**: two quantities in a fixed ratio; the graph is a straight line through the origin.
:::

::: method
1. **Read which ratio you have.** Part : part (boys : girls) or part : whole (boys : class)? Is the total, one share, or the difference given? That decides what "one part" is.
2. **Find one part first** (M1): total ÷ total parts, or share ÷ its parts, or difference ÷ difference in parts. Then multiply for each share.
3. **Units the same before simplifying**, and simplest form means whole numbers with no common factor. "In the form $1 : n$" means divide by the first number, and $n$ may be a decimal.
4. **Proportion problems:** write the equal ratios as fractions and solve, or scale by a multiplier. Show the multiplier (e.g. $\times 2.5$) — it earns the method mark.
5. **Changing ratios** (grade 8–9): let the original amounts be $3k$ and $5k$, apply the change, write the new ratio as an equation and cross-multiply.
6. **Comparison questions:** convert each option to the same form (fraction of the total, or $1 : n$) and write a conclusion.
7. **Algebraic ratios:** if $x : y = 3 : 4$, write $x = 3k$, $y = 4k$ and substitute; the $k$ cancels.
8. **Paper 1** uses friendly numbers — look for a common factor rather than dividing messy decimals. On calculator papers, round only the final answer, and never round a number of people or objects in the wrong direction.
:::

::: example Fractions, including greater than 1
Write (a) 350 g as a fraction of 2 kg, (b) 2.5 hours as a fraction of 90 minutes.

(a) $\dfrac{350}{2000} = \dfrac{7}{40}$. (b) $\dfrac{150}{90} = \dfrac{5}{3}$.
:::

::: example Part : whole and multiplicative relationships
In a club the ratio of adults to children is $3 : 5$. (a) What fraction of members are children? (b) Write the number of adults as a fraction of the number of children. (c) There are 45 children. How many members are there?

(a) $\dfrac{5}{8}$. (b) $\dfrac35$, so adults $= \dfrac35 \times$ children.

(c) 5 parts $= 45$, one part $= 9$, total $8 \times 9 = 72$ members.
:::

::: example Scaling and conversion
A recipe for 6 people uses 450 g of flour and 3 eggs. Rosa has 1 kg of flour and 8 eggs. What is the greatest number of people she can make it for?

Flour: $1000 \div 450 = 2.22\ldots$ lots, enough for $6 \times 2.22\ldots = 13.3$ people. Eggs: $8 \div 3 = 2.66\ldots$ lots, enough for 16 people.

Flour runs out first: **13 people** (round down — 14 needs 1050 g).
:::

::: example Grade 7: combining two ratios
$a : b = 2 : 3$ and $b : c = 4 : 5$. Write $a : b : c$ in its simplest form. If $c = 60$, find $a$.

LCM of the $b$ parts, 3 and 4, is 12. $a : b = 8 : 12$ and $b : c = 12 : 15$, so $a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15$.

$15$ parts $= 60$, one part $= 4$, so $a = 32$.
:::

::: example Grade 8: a ratio that changes
At an animal shelter the ratio of cats to dogs is $4 : 7$. Then 10 more cats arrive, and the ratio becomes $6 : 7$. How many dogs are there?

Let there be $4k$ cats and $7k$ dogs. $(4k + 10) : 7k = 6 : 7$, so $7(4k + 10) = 6 \times 7k$.

$28k + 70 = 42k \Rightarrow 14k = 70 \Rightarrow k = 5$. There are $7 \times 5 = 35$ dogs. (Check: 30 cats : 35 dogs $= 6 : 7$ ✓.)
:::

::: example Grade 9: algebraic ratio
$x : y = 3 : 4$. Find $(2x + y) : (x + 3y)$ in its simplest form.

Let $x = 3k$, $y = 4k$. $2x + y = 10k$ and $x + 3y = 15k$. So the ratio is $10k : 15k = 2 : 3$.
:::

::: example Ratio and a linear graph
The cost of ribbon is in direct proportion to its length: 3 m costs £4.20. Find a formula for the cost $C$ (in £) of $L$ metres, describe its graph, and find the cost of 7.5 m.

$C : L = 4.20 : 3 = 1.4 : 1$, so $C = 1.4L$. The graph is a straight line through the origin with gradient $1.4$. For 7.5 m: $C = 1.4 \times 7.5 = 10.5$, so £10.50.
:::

---

::: watch
- **Dividing by the wrong number of parts.** In $3 : 4$ with a total of 84, divide by 7 — not by 3 or 4.
- **Treating a difference or one share as the total.** "Ali gets £27 more" is the difference in parts.
- **Part : whole confusion.** Boys : girls $= 3 : 5$ means boys are $\frac38$ of the class, not $\frac35$.
- **Order of the ratio.** Write it in the order the question names the quantities.
- **Units not matched** before simplifying: $40\text{ cm} : 2\text{ m}$ is not $20 : 1$.
- **Rounding people or items the wrong way.** If the flour only stretches to 13.3 people, the answer is 13.
- **Comparing ratios by eye.** $3 : 5$ versus $2 : 3$ — convert both to fractions or decimals of the total first.
:::
