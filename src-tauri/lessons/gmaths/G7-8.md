## What it is

A **transformation** moves a shape (the **object**) to a new position (the **image**). There are four at GCSE. Reflections, rotations and translations give an image **congruent** to the object; enlargements give an image **similar** to it. Image points are labelled with a dash: $A$ maps to $A'$.

### The four transformations — and what "fully describe" needs

| Transformation | To describe it fully, give… | Image is |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflection** | the equation of the **mirror line** | congruent (flipped) |
| **Rotation** | the **centre**, the **angle**, the **direction** (clockwise or anticlockwise) | congruent |
| **Translation** | the **column vector** $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ | congruent |
| **Enlargement** | the **scale factor** and the **centre** | similar |

A $180°$ rotation needs no direction. A single transformation must be described as **one** transformation — "reflection then translation" scores nothing when a single rotation was asked for.

### Reflection

Each image point is the same perpendicular distance from the mirror line as the object point, on the other side. Useful rules for a point $(x, y)$:

| Mirror line | Image |
|---|---|
| $x$-axis ($y = 0$) | $(x, -y)$ |
| $y$-axis ($x = 0$) | $(-x, y)$ |
| $y = x$ | $(y, x)$ |
| $y = -x$ | $(-y, -x)$ |
| $x = a$ | $(2a - x, y)$ |
| $y = b$ | $(x, 2b - y)$ |

Remember: $x = a$ is a **vertical** line and $y = b$ is **horizontal**.

### Rotation

About the origin:

| Rotation | $(x, y)$ maps to |
|---|---|
| $90°$ clockwise | $(y, -x)$ |
| $90°$ anticlockwise | $(-y, x)$ |
| $180°$ | $(-x, -y)$ |

About another centre $C$, use the **vector from $C$ to the point**, rotate that vector, and add it back to $C$. Rotate $(4, 3)$ by $90°$ clockwise about $(1, 2)$: the vector is $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$, rotated clockwise it becomes $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$, so the image is $(1 + 1, 2 - 3) = (2, -1)$. In the exam, tracing paper is allowed and is the reliable way to check.

To **find** the centre of a rotation, draw the perpendicular bisectors of $AA'$ and $BB'$: they meet at the centre (it is equidistant from each point and its image).

### Translation

Every point moves by the same **column vector**: $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$ means $a$ right (left if negative) and $b$ up (down if negative). $(2, 5)$ translated by $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$ goes to $(-1, 9)$.

### Enlargement

With centre $C$ and scale factor $k$, every image point satisfies
$$\overrightarrow{CP'} = k \, \overrightarrow{CP}.$$
Lengths are multiplied by $|k|$; angles are unchanged.

- $k > 1$: the image is bigger and further from the centre.
- $0 < k < 1$ (a **fractional** scale factor): the image is **smaller** and closer to the centre — it is still called an enlargement.
- $k < 0$ (a **negative** scale factor): the image is on the **opposite side** of the centre and **upside down** (rotated $180°$). Scale factor $-1$ is the same as a rotation of $180°$ about the centre.

Example: enlarge $(2, 3)$ by scale factor $-2$, centre $(1, 1)$. $\overrightarrow{CP} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$, times $-2$ is $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$, so $P' = (-1, -3)$.

To **find** the centre, draw lines through each pair of corresponding points $A$ and $A'$; they cross at the centre. Scale factor $= \dfrac{\text{image length}}{\text{object length}}$, negative if the image is inverted on the other side of the centre.

### Combinations of transformations

One transformation followed by another can often be replaced by a **single** transformation:

- Reflection in two **parallel** lines → a **translation**, perpendicular to the lines, of **twice** the distance between them.
- Reflection in two **intersecting** lines → a **rotation** about the crossing point through **twice** the angle between them. (Reflecting in the $x$-axis then the $y$-axis is a $180°$ rotation about the origin.)
- Two rotations about the same centre → one rotation (add the angles).
- Two translations → one translation (add the vectors).

**Order matters:** reflecting in $y = x$ then in the $x$-axis is **not** the same as the other way round. Track one or two labelled points through each step, or apply the coordinate rules one after another.

### Invariance

An **invariant point** is not moved by a transformation; an **invariant line** maps onto itself.

- Reflection: every point **on the mirror line** is invariant.
- Rotation: only the **centre** is invariant.
- Translation (by a non-zero vector): **no** invariant points (lines parallel to the vector are invariant lines).
- Enlargement (scale factor not 1): only the **centre** is invariant.

What always stays the same under reflections, rotations and translations: lengths, angles, area — the shape is congruent. What changes: position, and for reflections the **orientation** (the shape is flipped, so a clockwise labelling $ABC$ becomes anticlockwise).

::: terms
- **Object and image**: the shape before a transformation and the shape after it; image points are labelled A′, B′, C′.
- **Column vector**: a pair of numbers stacked vertically giving the horizontal then vertical movement of a translation.
- **Centre of rotation**: the fixed point a shape turns about; it is the only point a rotation does not move.
- **Scale factor**: the number every length is multiplied by in an enlargement; a fraction makes the image smaller and a negative value puts it on the other side of the centre, inverted.
- **Invariant point**: a point that is mapped onto itself by a transformation, such as any point on a mirror line.
- **Congruent**: the same shape and size; reflections, rotations and translations always give a congruent image.
- **Orientation**: the direction a shape faces; a reflection reverses it, so vertices labelled clockwise become anticlockwise.
:::

::: method
1. **Identify the type first.** Same size and same way round → translation or rotation. Same size, flipped → reflection. Different size → enlargement.
2. **Describe fully, in one transformation.** Reflection: "in the line $y = -x$". Rotation: centre, angle, direction. Translation: column vector. Enlargement: scale factor and centre. A missing detail usually loses a mark (commonly B1 for the type, B1 for each detail).
3. **Use tracing paper** for rotations and to check reflections — it is allowed on every paper. For enlargements, count squares from the centre to each vertex and multiply.
4. **Negative and fractional scale factors:** multiply the vector from the centre by $k$. For $k < 0$ you go the other way through the centre.
5. **Finding a centre:** for enlargement, join each point to its image and extend — the lines meet at the centre. For rotation, the perpendicular bisectors of $AA'$ and $BB'$ meet at the centre.
6. **Combinations:** apply the transformations **in the order given**, labelling the intermediate image (e.g. $B$, then $C$). Then look at the object and the final image together and describe the single transformation.
7. **Invariance questions:** test each point: which points end where they started? Reflections fix the mirror line; rotations and enlargements fix the centre; translations fix nothing.
8. **Label your image** and draw it accurately on the grid — the accuracy mark is for all vertices correct.
:::

::: example Reflection in y = x
Triangle $T$ has vertices $(1, 1)$, $(3, 1)$ and $(3, 2)$. Reflect $T$ in the line $y = x$ and state any invariant point.

Swap the coordinates: $(1, 1)$, $(1, 3)$, $(2, 3)$.

$(1, 1)$ lies on $y = x$, so it is **invariant**.
:::

::: example Describing a rotation
Triangle $A$ has vertices $(1, 2)$, $(3, 2)$, $(3, 3)$. Triangle $B$ has vertices $(-2, 1)$, $(-2, 3)$, $(-3, 3)$. Describe fully the single transformation that maps $A$ onto $B$.

Same size, same orientation, turned. Test $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$: $(1, 2) \to (-2, 1)$ ✓, $(3, 2) \to (-2, 3)$ ✓, $(3, 3) \to (-3, 3)$ ✓.

**Rotation, $90°$ anticlockwise, centre $(0, 0)$.**
:::

::: example Fractional scale factor
Enlarge the triangle with vertices $(4, 1)$, $(7, 1)$, $(7, 4)$ by scale factor $\dfrac13$, centre $(1, 1)$.

Vectors from the centre: $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$. Divide by 3: $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Image: $(2, 1)$, $(3, 1)$, $(3, 2)$ — a smaller triangle, closer to the centre.
:::

::: example Finding a negative scale factor and centre (grade 8)
Triangle $P$ has vertices $(6, 4)$, $(10, 4)$, $(10, 8)$. Triangle $Q$ has vertices $(0, 1)$, $(-2, 1)$, $(-2, -1)$. Describe fully the single transformation that maps $P$ onto $Q$.

$Q$ is smaller and upside down, so it is an enlargement with a negative scale factor. The side $(6, 4) \to (10, 4)$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$; its image $(0, 1) \to (-2, 1)$ is $\begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$. So $k = -\dfrac12$.

Centre $C$: $C + k(P - C) = P'$ gives $C(1 - k) = P' - kP$:
$1.5C = (0, 1) + 0.5(6, 4) = (3, 3)$, so $C = (2, 2)$.

Check $(10, 8)$: $(2, 2) - \frac12(8, 6) = (-2, -1)$ ✓.

**Enlargement, scale factor $-\dfrac12$, centre $(2, 2)$.**
:::

::: example Two reflections (grade 8)
Shape $S$ is reflected in the line $x = 1$, and the image is reflected in the line $x = 4$. Describe the single transformation that maps $S$ to the final image.

Follow a point: $(0, 0) \to (2, 0) \to (6, 0)$. Another: $(5, 3) \to (-3, 3) \to (11, 3)$. Both move 6 right.

**Translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$** — twice the distance (3) between the parallel mirror lines.
:::

::: example Combination and invariance (grade 9)
A shape is reflected in the line $y = x$ and then rotated $90°$ clockwise about the origin. (a) Show that this is equivalent to a single reflection, and give its mirror line. (b) Describe the invariant points.

(a) $(x, y) \xrightarrow{\;y = x\;} (y, x) \xrightarrow{\;90° \text{ cw}\;} (x, -y)$, using the rule $(a, b) \to (b, -a)$ with $a = y$, $b = x$.

$(x, y) \to (x, -y)$ is a **reflection in the $x$-axis**.

(b) The invariant points are those with $-y = y$, i.e. $y = 0$: **every point on the $x$-axis**.
:::

::: example Two half-turns (grade 9)
A shape is rotated $180°$ about $(0, 0)$ and then $180°$ about $(3, 0)$. Show that the result is a translation and find its vector.

$180°$ about $(0, 0)$: $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$.

$180°$ about $(3, 0)$: $(a, b) \to (6 - a, -b)$. With $a = -x$, $b = -y$: $(6 + x, y)$.

So $(x, y) \to (x + 6, y)$: a **translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$**, twice the vector from the first centre to the second. No point is invariant.
:::

::: q
Reflect the point $(3, -2)$ in (a) the line $x = 1$ (b) the line $y = -x$.
---
(a) $(2 \times 1 - 3, -2) = (-1, -2)$. (b) $(-y, -x) = (2, -3)$.
:::

::: q
Translate $(4, -1)$ by $\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$. Then find the vector that maps $(2, 3)$ to $(-1, 7)$.
---
$(4 - 5, -1 + 3) = (-1, 2)$. Vector $= \begin{pmatrix} -1 - 2 \\ 7 - 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$.
:::

::: q
Rotate $(2, 5)$ by (a) $90°$ clockwise about the origin (b) $180°$ about $(1, 1)$.
---
(a) $(y, -x) = (5, -2)$. (b) Vector from centre $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$ becomes $\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$: $(0, -3)$.
:::

::: q
Enlarge the point $(3, 1)$ by scale factor 3, centre $(1, 0)$.
---
Vector $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \times 3 = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$; image $(7, 3)$.
:::

::: q
Enlarge the point $(5, 4)$ by scale factor $-1$, centre $(2, 1)$. What single rotation has the same effect?
---
Vector $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \times (-1)$: image $(-1, -2)$. Same as a rotation of $180°$ about $(2, 1)$.
:::

::: q
Triangle $(1, 1)$, $(4, 1)$, $(1, 3)$ maps to $(-1, -1)$, $(-4, -1)$, $(-1, -3)$. Describe fully the single transformation.
---
$(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$: **rotation $180°$ about $(0, 0)$** (equivalently, enlargement scale factor $-1$, centre $(0, 0)$).
:::

::: q
A shape is reflected in the $x$-axis and then in the $y$-axis. Describe the single equivalent transformation.
---
$(x, y) \to (x, -y) \to (-x, -y)$: rotation $180°$ about the origin.
:::

::: q
Which points are invariant under (a) reflection in $y = 2$ (b) a translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ (c) an enlargement, scale factor 4, centre $(1, 5)$?
---
(a) Every point on the line $y = 2$. (b) None. (c) Only $(1, 5)$.
:::

::: q
A triangle of area 5 cm² is enlarged by scale factor $-3$. Find the area of the image.
---
Lengths are multiplied by $|{-3}| = 3$, so area by $3^2 = 9$: $45$ cm².
:::

::: watch
- **Incomplete descriptions**: a rotation without its centre, or an enlargement without its centre, loses marks. Use exactly one transformation when asked for "a single transformation".
- **Mixing up $x = a$ and $y = a$**: $x = 3$ is vertical, $y = 3$ is horizontal.
- **Column vectors upside down**: the top number is horizontal movement.
- **Clockwise vs anticlockwise** — check with tracing paper; $90°$ clockwise sends $(1, 0)$ to $(0, -1)$.
- **Fractional scale factors** make shapes smaller but are still called enlargements.
- **Negative scale factors**: measure from the centre and go the **other way**; the image is inverted.
- **Order of combined transformations**: do the first one first and label the intermediate image.
:::

::: test
Triangle $P$ has vertices $(1, 1)$, $(3, 1)$, $(3, 2)$. Triangle $Q$ has vertices $(-1, 1)$, $(5, 1)$, $(5, 4)$. Describe fully the single transformation that maps $P$ onto $Q$. (4)
---
Enlargement (B1). Side $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \to \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$, scale factor 3 (B1). Centre: $C - 3C = (-1, 1) - 3(1, 1) = (-4, -2)$, so $C = (2, 1)$ (M1 A1).
:::

::: test
Shape $A$ is reflected in the $y$-axis to give shape $B$. $B$ is rotated $90°$ clockwise about the origin to give shape $C$. Describe fully the single transformation that maps $A$ onto $C$. (4)
---
$(x, y) \to (-x, y)$ (M1) $\to (y, x)$ (M1, using $(a, b) \to (b, -a)$). This is a reflection (A1) in the line $y = x$ (A1).
:::

::: test
The point $P(a, b)$ is reflected in the line $x = 3$ and the image is then reflected in the line $y = 1$.

(a) Find the coordinates of the final image in terms of $a$ and $b$. (b) Describe fully the single transformation equivalent to the two reflections. (c) Write down its invariant point. (5)
---
(a) $(6 - a, b)$ (M1), then $(6 - a, 2 - b)$ (A1). (b) Rotation $180°$ (B1) about $(3, 1)$ (B1) — the vector from $(3, 1)$ to $(a, b)$ is reversed. (c) $(3, 1)$ (B1).
:::

::: test
A shape is enlarged by scale factor 2, centre $O$, and the image is then enlarged by scale factor $-1$, centre $O$. Describe the single transformation that maps the original shape to the final image. (2)
---
Enlargement, scale factor $2 \times (-1) = -2$ (B1), centre $O$ (B1).
:::
