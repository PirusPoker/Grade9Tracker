## What it is

**Growth and decay** problems are repeated percentage change: a bank balance earning interest year after year, a car losing value, a population rising, a radioactive sample halving. The key idea is that each change is applied to the **new** amount, not the original — so you multiply by the same **multiplier** again and again.

### Multipliers

A percentage increase of $r\%$ has multiplier $1 + \dfrac{r}{100}$; a decrease of $r\%$ has multiplier $1 - \dfrac{r}{100}$.

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| increase by 4% | $1.04$ |
| increase by 12.5% | $1.125$ |
| decrease by 15% | $0.85$ |
| decrease by 2.5% | $0.975$ |
| halves | $0.5$ |

### The growth and decay formula

Starting amount $P$, multiplier $m$, applied $n$ times:
$$\text{amount after } n \text{ periods} = P \times m^n$$

- $m > 1$ means **growth** (compound interest, population increase).
- $0 < m < 1$ means **decay** (depreciation, cooling, radioactive decay).

Example: £3000 invested at 2.5% per year **compound interest** for 4 years is worth $3000 \times 1.025^4 = £3311.44$.

### Simple interest versus compound interest

**Simple interest** is paid only on the original amount, so it is the same every year. **Compound interest** is paid on the original amount *plus* interest already earned. £4000 at 3.5% for 3 years:

- Simple: $4000 \times 0.035 \times 3 = £420$ interest, total £4420.
- Compound: $4000 \times 1.035^3 = £4434.87$, so £434.87 interest — £14.87 more.

"Depreciation" is the word for value going down: a car worth £18 000 that depreciates by 12% per year is worth $18000 \times 0.88^3 = £12\,266.50$ after 3 years.

### Working backwards

If you know the **final** amount, divide by the multiplier as many times as it was applied:
$$P = \frac{\text{final}}{m^n}$$
Never "take off the percentage" from the final amount — the percentage was of a different (earlier) number.

### Finding the rate or the time

- **Rate:** if an amount grows from 2000 to 2662 in 3 years, $m^3 = \dfrac{2662}{2000} = 1.331$, so $m = \sqrt[3]{1.331} = 1.1$: a 10% increase per year.
- **Time:** at GCSE, find $n$ by **trial and improvement** — try values of $n$ on the calculator until the amount first passes the target. To double at 4% a year, you need $1.04^n \geq 2$: $1.04^{17} = 1.948$, $1.04^{18} = 2.026$, so 18 years.

### Mixed periods

If the rate changes, apply each multiplier for its own number of years: £12 000 at 4% for one year and then 2% for three years gives $12000 \times 1.04 \times 1.02^3 = £13\,243.88$.

### General iterative processes

An **iterative process** produces each value from the one before by a rule, written with subscripts:
$$u_{n+1} = f(u_n)$$
You start from a given $u_0$ (or $u_1$) and apply the rule repeatedly. Growth is the special case $u_{n+1} = m \, u_n$, but the rule can be anything — for example, a fish farm grows by 20% each year and then 500 fish are sold:
$$P_{n+1} = 1.2P_n - 500, \quad P_0 = 3000$$
$P_1 = 1.2(3000) - 500 = 3100$, $P_2 = 1.2(3100) - 500 = 3220$, $P_3 = 1.2(3220) - 500 = 3364$.

On a calculator: type $3000$, press `=`, then type `1.2 × Ans − 500` and press `=` repeatedly — each press gives the next term.

**Interpreting** the answer matters: money is rounded to the nearest penny, but people, fish or cars must be whole numbers, and "after how many years" means the first whole year the condition holds.

### Half-life

A quantity with a **half-life** of $h$ hours halves every $h$ hours. 80 g with half-life 6 hours has $80 \times 0.5^3 = 10$ g left after 18 hours, because 18 hours is 3 half-lives.

::: terms
- **Multiplier**: the number you multiply by for a percentage change, e.g. 1.04 for a 4% increase or 0.85 for a 15% decrease.
- **Compound interest**: interest paid on the original amount plus all interest already earned, so the amount is multiplied by the same multiplier each year.
- **Simple interest**: interest paid only on the original amount, so the same sum is added every year.
- **Depreciation**: the loss in value of an item over time, usually a fixed percentage of its current value each year.
- **Exponential growth or decay**: change modelled by P × mⁿ, growth when m > 1 and decay when 0 < m < 1.
- **Half-life**: the time taken for a decaying quantity to fall to half of its value.
- **Iterative process**: a rule such as uₙ₊₁ = 1.2uₙ − 500 that produces each term from the one before.
:::

::: method
1. **Write the multiplier first** and show it: "increase of 3.2% → × 1.032". A method mark is often for the multiplier raised to the right power, e.g. $5000 \times 1.032^6$, even if you slip on the calculator.
2. **Use the power, not repeated sums.** $P \times m^n$ in one go on the calculator (Papers 2 and 3). On the non-calculator Paper 1 the numbers are chosen to be easy: 10%, 50%, 2 or 3 years — work year by year and set out each line.
3. **Read what is asked:** the *total* amount, or the *interest* (total minus original), or the *decrease* in value. A common final step is a subtraction people forget.
4. **Reverse problems:** divide by $m^n$. Check by multiplying back.
5. **Finding the rate:** $m = \sqrt[n]{\text{final} \div \text{start}}$, then convert $m$ to a percentage: $1.08 \to 8\%$, $0.94 \to 6\%$ decrease.
6. **Finding the time:** trial and improvement. Write down the values either side of the target (e.g. $n = 6$ gives 8278, $n = 7$ gives 7782) — those two lines are the method marks — then state the whole number of years.
7. **Iterations:** substitute the previous term into the rule, one line per term; use `Ans` on the calculator and write each value down (unrounded in the working, rounded in the answer).
8. **Interpret:** round money to the nearest penny, people and objects to whole numbers, and answer "which is better?" with both values and a sentence.
:::

::: example Compound interest
Sam invests £5000 at 3.2% per year compound interest. Work out the interest earned after 6 years.

Multiplier $1.032$. Amount $= 5000 \times 1.032^6 = 6040.156\ldots = £6040.16$.

Interest $= 6040.16 - 5000 = £1040.16$.
:::

::: example Reverse depreciation
A laptop loses 15% of its value each year. After 2 years it is worth £578. Work out its value when new.

Multiplier $0.85$, applied twice: new value $\times 0.85^2 = 578$.

New value $= \dfrac{578}{0.85^2} = \dfrac{578}{0.7225} = £800$.

Check: $800 \times 0.85 = 680$, $680 \times 0.85 = 578$. ✓
:::

::: example Finding the rate
A house was bought for £240 000. Two years later it was worth £279 936. The value increased by the same percentage each year. Find this percentage.

$240000 \times m^2 = 279936 \Rightarrow m^2 = 1.1664 \Rightarrow m = \sqrt{1.1664} = 1.08$.

The value increased by **8%** per year.
:::

::: example Finding the time
A town has 12 000 people. The population falls by 6% each year. After how many years will it first be below 8000?

$12000 \times 0.94^n < 8000$. Trial:

$n = 6$: $12000 \times 0.94^6 = 8278$ (still above).

$n = 7$: $12000 \times 0.94^7 = 7782$ (below).

**7 years.**
:::

::: example An iterative loan (grade 8)
Jo borrows £2000. At the end of each month 1.5% interest is added to the balance and then Jo repays £300. So $B_{n+1} = 1.015B_n - 300$ with $B_0 = 2000$.

(a) Find the balance after 3 months. (b) In which month is the loan paid off?

(a) $B_1 = 1.015(2000) - 300 = 1730$. $B_2 = 1.015(1730) - 300 = 1455.95$. $B_3 = 1.015(1455.95) - 300 = 1177.79$ (to the nearest penny). **£1177.79**

(b) Continue: $B_4 = 895.46$, $B_5 = 608.89$, $B_6 = 318.02$, $B_7 = 22.79$. In month 8 the balance with interest is only £23.13, less than £300, so the loan is paid off in **month 8**.
:::

::: example Finding the multiplier from an iteration (grade 9)
The number of bacteria follows $N_{t+1} = kN_t$, where $t$ is in hours. $N_0 = 500$ and $N_2 = 720$.

(a) Find $k$. (b) Find the number of bacteria after 4 hours. (c) Interpret $k$.

(a) $N_2 = k^2 N_0 \Rightarrow 500k^2 = 720 \Rightarrow k^2 = 1.44 \Rightarrow k = 1.2$ (positive, since the number grows).

(b) $N_4 = 500 \times 1.2^4 = 1036.8$, so about **1037 bacteria**.

(c) $k = 1.2$ means the population increases by **20% every hour**.
:::

::: example Comparing two accounts (grade 9)
Account A pays 3% per year compound interest. Account B pays 5% in the first year, then 2% per year after that. Which gives more on £10 000 after 5 years, and by how much?

A: $10000 \times 1.03^5 = £11\,592.74$.

B: $10000 \times 1.05 \times 1.02^4 = £11\,365.54$.

Account **A** gives £227.20 more.
:::

::: q
£800 is invested at 4% per year compound interest. Find the total after 3 years.
---
$800 \times 1.04^3 = 899.8912$: **£899.89**.
:::

::: q
(Non-calculator) A car worth £15 000 loses 20% of its value each year. Find its value after 2 years.
---
Year 1: $15000 \times 0.8 = 12000$. Year 2: $12000 \times 0.8 = 9600$. **£9600**.
:::

::: q
£2000 is invested for 2 years at 5% per year. How much more interest is earned with compound interest than with simple interest?
---
Simple: $2000 \times 0.05 \times 2 = £200$. Compound: $2000 \times 1.05^2 = 2205$, so £205. Difference **£5**.
:::

::: q
After 4 years of 2% per year compound interest, an account holds £6494.59. How much was invested?
---
$\dfrac{6494.59}{1.02^4} = 5999.997\ldots$: **£6000**.
:::

::: q
A 200 mg sample has a half-life of 5 hours. How much is left after 20 hours?
---
20 hours is 4 half-lives: $200 \times 0.5^4 = $ **12.5 mg**.
:::

::: q
A machine's value fell from £50 000 to £40 500 in 2 years, by the same percentage each year. Find the percentage.
---
$m^2 = \dfrac{40500}{50000} = 0.81 \Rightarrow m = 0.9$: a **10%** decrease per year.
:::

::: q
£1000 is invested at 5% per year compound interest. After how many whole years will it first exceed £1500?
---
$1000 \times 1.05^8 = 1477.46$ (not yet); $1000 \times 1.05^9 = 1551.33$. **9 years**.
:::

::: q
$u_{n+1} = 0.8u_n + 30$ and $u_0 = 100$. Find $u_1$, $u_2$ and $u_3$.
---
$u_1 = 80 + 30 = 110$; $u_2 = 88 + 30 = 118$; $u_3 = 94.4 + 30 = 124.4$.
:::

::: q
A herd of deer grows by 10% each year, then 40 deer are moved away, so $D_{n+1} = 1.1D_n - 40$. $D_0 = 300$. Find $D_3$ and comment on the trend.
---
$D_1 = 290$, $D_2 = 279$, $D_3 = 266.9 \approx 267$ deer. The herd is **falling**: 10% of the herd is less than 40 while the herd is under 400.
:::

::: q
A credit card charges 1.5% interest per month. What is the equivalent percentage per year?
---
$1.015^{12} = 1.1956\ldots$, so about **19.6%** per year (not $12 \times 1.5 = 18\%$).
:::

::: watch
- **Simple interest by mistake.** "Compound" means multiply by $m^n$; working out one year's interest and multiplying by $n$ loses every accuracy mark.
- **Wrong multiplier for decay.** A 15% decrease is $\times 0.85$, not $\times 0.15$ and not $\times 1.15$.
- **Reverse percentages:** dividing by $m^n$ is right; taking the percentage off the final amount is wrong.
- **Total versus interest.** If the question asks for the interest or the loss, subtract the original at the end.
- **"After how many years"** needs a whole number and the values either side written down — a bare "7" gets at most one mark.
- **Rounding too early** in an iteration drifts the answer; keep full values with `Ans` and round only the final answer.
- **Wrong 2% vs 0.2%:** $1.02$ is 2%, $1.002$ is 0.2%. Read decimals in the rate carefully.
:::

::: test
The mass, $M$ grams, of a radioactive substance after $n$ days is $M = 120 \times k^n$. After 3 days the mass is 61.44 g.

(a) Find $k$. (b) On which day does the mass first fall below 10 g? (5)
---
(a) $120k^3 = 61.44 \Rightarrow k^3 = 0.512$ (M1); $k = 0.8$ (A1).

(b) $120 \times 0.8^{11} = 10.31$ (M1); $120 \times 0.8^{12} = 8.25$ (M1); **day 12** (A1).
:::
