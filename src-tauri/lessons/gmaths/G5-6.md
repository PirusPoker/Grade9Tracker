## What it is

Two shapes are **congruent** if they are identical in shape and size — one fits exactly on top of the other, possibly after turning it over. For triangles you do not need to check all six measurements: three well-chosen facts are enough. This topic is about those **congruence criteria**, and about using them — with angle facts, similarity and quadrilateral properties — to **prove** geometric results.

### The four congruence criteria

Two triangles are congruent if they share one of these sets of equal measurements:

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **SSS** | all three sides equal |
| **SAS** | two sides and the angle **between them** (the included angle) equal |
| **ASA** | two angles and a **corresponding** side equal (AAS is the same case, since the third angle is then forced) |
| **RHS** | both have a **R**ight angle, equal **H**ypotenuses and one other equal **S**ide |

Two combinations do **not** prove congruence:

- **AAA** — three equal angles only proves the triangles are **similar** (same shape); one could be an enlargement of the other.
- **SSA** with the angle *not* between the two sides — two different triangles can often be drawn (the "ambiguous case"). RHS is the one exception, because the angle is $90°$.

When triangles are congruent, the **corresponding** sides and angles match. Always name them in matching order: if $\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle PQR$ then $A \leftrightarrow P$, $B \leftrightarrow Q$, $C \leftrightarrow R$, so $BC = QR$ and $\angle C = \angle R$.

### Congruent or similar?

**Similar** triangles have equal corresponding angles (AA is enough) and corresponding sides in the same ratio. Congruent triangles are the special case where the scale factor is 1. In a proof, "similar" lets you use equal angles and a ratio of sides; "congruent" lets you say sides are **equal**.

### Proving results

A geometric proof is a chain of statements, each with a reason, that shows a result is **always** true. The standard reasons are the angle facts (angles on a straight line, alternate angles, angles in a triangle …), properties of shapes, and congruence or similarity.

**Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.** In triangle $ABC$ with $AB = AC$, let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$. Then $AB = AC$ (given), $BM = CM$ ($M$ is the midpoint) and $AM$ is common. So triangles $ABM$ and $ACM$ are congruent (SSS), and $\angle ABC = \angle ACB$ (corresponding angles of congruent triangles). As a bonus, $\angle AMB = \angle AMC$ and they add to $180°$, so each is $90°$: the line from the apex to the midpoint of the base is perpendicular to it.

**The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.** In parallelogram $ABCD$ with diagonals meeting at $X$: $AB = DC$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram), $\angle XAB = \angle XCD$ and $\angle XBA = \angle XDC$ (alternate angles, $AB \parallel DC$). So triangles $ABX$ and $CDX$ are congruent (ASA), giving $AX = CX$ and $BX = DX$.

**Pythagoras' theorem.** Put four copies of a right-angled triangle (legs $a$, $b$, hypotenuse $c$) inside a square of side $a + b$, one in each corner, so that their hypotenuses make a tilted inner quadrilateral. Each side of the inner shape is $c$, and each of its angles is $180° - (\text{the two acute angles of the triangle}) = 180° - 90° = 90°$, so it is a square of area $c^2$. Then
$$(a + b)^2 = 4 \times \tfrac12 ab + c^2 \;\Rightarrow\; a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = 2ab + c^2 \;\Rightarrow\; a^2 + b^2 = c^2.$$

### Conjecture, then prove

"Conjecture" means make a sensible guess from examples; "derive" or "prove" means show it must be true. For example, drawing several kites suggests that the long diagonal cuts the short one in half at right angles; proving it uses SSS (to show the long diagonal bisects the angles) and then SAS.

### How to lay out a congruence proof

1. Name the two triangles.
2. List **three** pairs of equal sides or angles, each with its reason.
3. State the condition (SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS) and conclude "so the triangles are congruent".
4. If asked for more, use "corresponding sides (or angles) of congruent triangles are equal".

::: terms
- **Congruent**: exactly the same shape and size; one shape fits exactly on the other, possibly after reflection.
- **Similar**: the same shape but not necessarily the same size; corresponding angles are equal and sides are in the same ratio.
- **Included angle**: the angle between two given sides, as needed for the SAS condition.
- **Corresponding**: in matching positions in two congruent or similar shapes, e.g. side BC matches side QR if triangle ABC ≡ triangle PQR.
- **Hypotenuse**: the longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle.
- **Conjecture**: a statement believed to be true from examples, but not yet proved.
- **Proof**: a chain of statements, each justified by a known fact, showing that a result is always true.
:::

::: method
1. **Mark everything on the diagram** first: given equal sides (dashes), right angles, parallel lines, radii (always equal), shared sides.
2. **Pick the two triangles** that contain the sides or angles you need to prove equal.
3. **Write three pairs, each with a reason in brackets**: "$AB = AD$ (given)", "$AC$ is common", "$\angle ABC = \angle ADC = 90°$ (given)". Each correct pair with its reason typically earns a mark.
4. **Name the condition** — SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS — and conclude "triangles … are congruent". Check SAS really has the angle **between** the sides; never use AAA or SSA.
5. **Finish the job:** if the question asks you to prove a length or angle, add "so $\ldots = \ldots$ (corresponding sides of congruent triangles)". A proof that stops at "congruent" misses the last mark.
6. **Never measure** or say "it looks equal". Use only given facts and results you have proved.
7. **For a general result** (e.g. "prove the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal"), use letters, not numbers — a numerical example is not a proof. These appear on calculator and non-calculator papers alike, usually for 3–4 marks.
:::

::: example Choosing the condition
Which condition, if any, proves each pair of triangles congruent?

(a) Sides 5 cm and 7 cm with a $40°$ angle between them, in both triangles.
(b) Angles $50°$, $60°$, $70°$ in both.
(c) Right-angled, hypotenuse 10 cm, one other side 6 cm, in both.
(d) Angles $35°$ and $85°$ with the side between them 4 cm, in both.

(a) **SAS**. (b) **Not congruent necessarily** — AAA gives similar only. (c) **RHS**. (d) **ASA**.
:::

::: example Congruence in a square
$ABCD$ is a square and $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Prove that triangle $MCD$ is isosceles.

In triangles $AMD$ and $BMC$:

- $AM = BM$ ($M$ is the midpoint of $AB$)
- $\angle DAM = \angle CBM = 90°$ (angles of a square)
- $AD = BC$ (sides of a square)

So triangles $AMD$ and $BMC$ are congruent (**SAS**). Therefore $MD = MC$ (corresponding sides of congruent triangles), so triangle $MCD$ is isosceles. ∎
:::

::: example RHS
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = AC$. $D$ is the point on $BC$ with $AD$ perpendicular to $BC$. Prove that $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$.

In triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$:

- $\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90°$ ($AD$ is perpendicular to $BC$)
- $AB = AC$ (given) — the hypotenuses
- $AD$ is common

So the triangles are congruent (**RHS**), and $BD = CD$ (corresponding sides). So $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$. ∎
:::

::: example Two intersecting circles (grade 7)
Two circles, centres $A$ and $B$, meet at $P$ and $Q$. Prove that $AB$ bisects angle $PAQ$.

In triangles $APB$ and $AQB$:

- $AP = AQ$ (radii of the circle centre $A$)
- $BP = BQ$ (radii of the circle centre $B$)
- $AB$ is common

So the triangles are congruent (**SSS**), and $\angle PAB = \angle QAB$ (corresponding angles). So $AB$ bisects angle $PAQ$. ∎
:::

::: example Diagonals of a kite (grade 8)
$ABCD$ is a kite with $AB = AD$ and $CB = CD$. The diagonals meet at $X$. Prove that $BX = DX$ and that the diagonals are perpendicular.

Triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$: $AB = AD$, $CB = CD$ (given), $AC$ common, so congruent (**SSS**). Hence $\angle BAX = \angle DAX$ (corresponding angles).

Triangles $ABX$ and $ADX$: $AB = AD$ (given), $\angle BAX = \angle DAX$ (just proved), $AX$ common, so congruent (**SAS**).

Hence $BX = DX$, and $\angle AXB = \angle AXD$. These are on a straight line, so each is $180° \div 2 = 90°$: the diagonals are perpendicular. ∎
:::

::: example Equilateral triangles on two sides (grade 9)
$ABC$ is any triangle. Equilateral triangles $ABP$ and $ACQ$ are drawn outwards on sides $AB$ and $AC$. Prove that $PC = BQ$.

Consider triangles $APC$ and $ABQ$:

- $AP = AB$ (sides of equilateral triangle $ABP$)
- $AC = AQ$ (sides of equilateral triangle $ACQ$)
- $\angle PAC = 60° + \angle BAC$ and $\angle BAQ = \angle BAC + 60°$ (each angle of an equilateral triangle is $60°$), so $\angle PAC = \angle BAQ$ — the included angle in each case.

So triangles $APC$ and $ABQ$ are congruent (**SAS**), and $PC = BQ$ (corresponding sides of congruent triangles). ∎
:::

::: q
Two triangles both have angles $40°$, $60°$ and $80°$. Are they congruent? Explain.
---
Not necessarily. AAA only shows they are **similar** — one could be an enlargement of the other.
:::

::: q
Triangle $ABC$ is congruent to triangle $XYZ$ (in that order). $AB = 7$ cm and $\angle C = 52°$. Which side of $XYZ$ is 7 cm, and which angle is $52°$?
---
$A \leftrightarrow X$, $B \leftrightarrow Y$, $C \leftrightarrow Z$: $XY = 7$ cm and $\angle Z = 52°$.
:::

::: q
$ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Prove that triangles $ABC$ and $CDA$ are congruent.
---
$AB = CD$ and $BC = DA$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal); $AC$ is common. Congruent (**SSS**).
:::

::: q
$ABCD$ is a rectangle. Prove that its diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ are equal.
---
Triangles $ABC$ and $BAD$: $AB$ common; $BC = AD$ (opposite sides of a rectangle); $\angle ABC = \angle BAD = 90°$. Congruent (**SAS**), so $AC = BD$ (corresponding sides).
:::

::: q
Lines $AD$ and $BC$ cross at $M$, the midpoint of both. Prove that $AB$ is parallel to $CD$.
---
$AM = MD$, $BM = MC$ (given), $\angle AMB = \angle DMC$ (vertically opposite). Triangles $AMB$ and $DMC$ are congruent (**SAS**), so $\angle MAB = \angle MDC$. These are alternate angles, and they are equal, so $AB \parallel CD$.
:::

::: q
$ABCD$ is a rhombus. Prove that diagonal $AC$ bisects angle $BAD$.
---
$AB = AD$ and $CB = CD$ (all sides of a rhombus are equal), $AC$ common. Triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$ are congruent (**SSS**), so $\angle BAC = \angle DAC$.
:::

::: q
In triangle $ABC$, $D$ is on $AB$ and $E$ is on $AC$ with $DE$ parallel to $BC$. $AD = 4$ cm, $DB = 2$ cm and $DE = 5$ cm. Find $BC$.
---
Triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ are similar (corresponding angles equal). Scale factor $= \dfrac{AB}{AD} = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$. $BC = 5 \times 1.5 = 7.5$ cm.
:::

::: q
Is a triangle with sides 7 cm, 24 cm and 25 cm right-angled? Explain.
---
$7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$, so **yes** — by the converse of Pythagoras, the angle opposite the 25 cm side is $90°$.
:::

::: watch
- **Using AAA or SSA** as a congruence condition. AAA gives similarity only; SSA is not a condition (RHS is the only "side-side-angle" allowed).
- **SAS with the wrong angle.** The angle must be **between** the two sides.
- **Missing reasons.** "$AB = AD$" on its own scores less than "$AB = AD$ (given)" or "(radii)".
- **Stopping at "congruent".** If the question asks you to prove a side or angle equal, add the final line using corresponding parts.
- **Mismatched order.** Write the triangles with matching vertices in the same order so the corresponding sides are obvious.
- **Proof by example.** Measuring or trying numbers is not a proof; use letters and reasons.
:::

::: test
Write down the four conditions for two triangles to be congruent. (2)
---
SSS, SAS, ASA (or AAS), RHS (B2 for all four; B1 for three).
:::

::: test
Two triangles each have sides 8 cm and 6 cm, and an angle of $30°$ opposite the 6 cm side. Explain why they need not be congruent. (2)
---
The angle is not between the two sides (B1), so this is SSA, which is not a congruence condition — two different triangles can be drawn (B1).
:::

::: test
$ABCD$ is a parallelogram. Prove that triangles $ABD$ and $CDB$ are congruent. (3)
---
$AB = CD$ (opposite sides of a parallelogram) (B1); $AD = CB$ (opposite sides) (B1); $BD$ common, so congruent (SSS) (B1).
:::

::: test
$PQRS$ is a square. $T$ is a point inside the square such that $PQT$ is an equilateral triangle. Prove that triangle $TSR$ is isosceles. (4)
---
$\angle TPS = 90° - 60° = 30°$ and $\angle TQR = 90° - 60° = 30°$ (B1). $TP = TQ$ (equilateral) and $PS = QR$ (square) (B1). Triangles $TPS$ and $TQR$ are congruent (SAS) (B1). So $TS = TR$ (corresponding sides), and triangle $TSR$ is isosceles (B1).
:::

::: test
Lines $AD$ and $BC$ intersect at $M$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AD$ and of $BC$. Prove that $AB = CD$ and that $AB$ is parallel to $CD$. (4)
---
$AM = DM$, $BM = CM$ (given), $\angle AMB = \angle DMC$ (vertically opposite angles are equal) (M1). Triangles $AMB$ and $DMC$ are congruent (SAS) (A1). So $AB = DC$ (corresponding sides) (A1), and $\angle BAM = \angle CDM$; these are equal alternate angles, so $AB \parallel CD$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Triangle $ABC$ has $AB = AC$. Prove that $\angle ABC = \angle ACB$. (4)
---
Let the bisector of angle $A$ meet $BC$ at $D$ (M1). $AB = AC$ (given), $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$ (bisector), $AD$ common (M1). Triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$ are congruent (SAS) (A1). So $\angle ABD = \angle ACD$, i.e. $\angle ABC = \angle ACB$ (corresponding angles) (A1).
:::

::: test
$AB$ is a chord of a circle with centre $O$, and $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Prove that $OM$ is perpendicular to $AB$. (4)
---
$OA = OB$ (radii), $AM = BM$ ($M$ is the midpoint), $OM$ common (M1). Triangles $OAM$ and $OBM$ are congruent (SSS) (A1). So $\angle OMA = \angle OMB$ (M1). They lie on a straight line so each is $90°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $ABC$, $D$ is on $AB$ and $E$ is on $AC$ with $DE$ parallel to $BC$. $AD = 3$ cm, $AB = 5$ cm and $DE = 4.2$ cm. Prove that triangles $ADE$ and $ABC$ are similar, and find $BC$. (4)
---
$\angle ADE = \angle ABC$ (corresponding angles, $DE \parallel BC$) and $\angle A$ is common (B1), so the triangles are similar (B1). Scale factor $= \dfrac{5}{3}$ (M1); $BC = 4.2 \times \dfrac53 = 7$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
$ABCD$ is a square. $E$ is on $BC$ and $F$ is on $CD$ with $BE = CF$. $AE$ and $BF$ meet at $G$. Prove that $AE = BF$ and that $AE$ is perpendicular to $BF$. (5)
---
$AB = BC$ (square), $\angle ABE = \angle BCF = 90°$ (square), $BE = CF$ (given) (M1). Triangles $ABE$ and $BCF$ are congruent (SAS) (A1), so $AE = BF$ (A1). Also $\angle BAE = \angle CBF$ (corresponding angles). In triangle $ABG$: $\angle ABG = 90° - \angle CBF = 90° - \angle BAG$ (M1), so $\angle BAG + \angle ABG = 90°$ and $\angle AGB = 180° - 90° = 90°$ (angles in a triangle) (A1).
:::
