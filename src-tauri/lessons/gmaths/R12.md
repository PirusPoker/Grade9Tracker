## What it is

Ratio notation is a compact way to compare measurements. The key idea of this topic is that **lengths, areas and volumes scale differently**: when every length in a shape is multiplied by $k$, every area is multiplied by $k^2$ and every volume by $k^3$. That is the link between ratio, **scale factors** and **similarity**.

### Comparing lengths, areas and volumes

Take two cubes with edges $2$ cm and $5$ cm.

| | Small cube | Large cube | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edge (length) | $2$ | $5$ | $2 : 5$ |
| Surface area | $6 \times 2^2 = 24$ | $6 \times 5^2 = 150$ | $4 : 25$ |
| Volume | $2^3 = 8$ | $5^3 = 125$ | $8 : 125$ |

In general, for **similar** shapes (one is an exact enlargement of the other):

$$\text{length ratio } a : b \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{area ratio } a^2 : b^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{volume ratio } a^3 : b^3.$$

"Area" includes surface area and the area of any face; "length" includes perimeter, height, radius, diagonal — any one-dimensional measurement.

**Working backwards:** square-root an area ratio, or cube-root a volume ratio, to get the length ratio. Volumes in the ratio $8 : 27$ mean lengths $2 : 3$, so areas $4 : 9$.

Ratios can also be written as $1 : n$. Squares with sides $6$ cm and $15$ cm have areas $36 : 225 = 1 : 6.25$.

### Scale factors and maps

A **scale factor** is the length ratio written as a single multiplier. A model car on a scale of $1 : 20$ has length scale factor $20$, so real areas are $20^2 = 400$ times the model's and real volumes $20^3 = 8000$ times.

Map scales work the same way. On a $1 : 50\,000$ map, $1$ cm represents $50\,000$ cm $= 0.5$ km, so $1$ cm² represents $0.5^2 = 0.25$ km². A wood covering $4$ cm² on the map is $4 \times 0.25 = 1$ km² in real life. Convert the **length** first, then square — never multiply a map area by $50\,000$.

### Similarity and the trigonometric ratios

Two triangles with the same three angles are **similar**, so the ratios of their corresponding sides are equal. Every right-angled triangle with a $30°$ angle has angles $90°$, $30°$ and $60°$, so they are all similar: whatever its size, the side opposite $30°$ is always half the hypotenuse. That fixed ratio is what $\sin 30° = \dfrac12$ means.

This is why **sine, cosine and tangent** work at all: for a given angle $\theta$,

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$$

are the same in every right-angled triangle containing $\theta$, because all such triangles are similar. Trigonometry is similarity with the ratios already worked out for you.

### Comparing shapes that are not similar

Ratio notation compares any two measurements. A sphere of radius $r$ fits exactly inside a cylinder of radius $r$ and height $2r$:

$$\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 : \pi r^2 (2r) = \frac43 : 2 = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3.$$

The $\pi r^3$ cancels, so the ratio is the same for every size — a result worth recognising.

::: terms
- **Similar shapes**: shapes where one is an exact enlargement of the other, so all angles are equal and all lengths are in the same ratio.
- **Linear scale factor**: the number every length is multiplied by, k, when a shape is enlarged.
- **Area scale factor**: k², the number every area (including surface area) is multiplied by.
- **Volume scale factor**: k³, the number every volume (and the mass, for the same material) is multiplied by.
- **Map scale**: a ratio such as 1 : 50 000 comparing a length on the map with the real length.
- **Trigonometric ratio**: sin, cos or tan, a ratio of two sides that is fixed for a given angle because all right-angled triangles with that angle are similar.
:::

::: method
1. **Write the ratio line down:** length $a : b$, area $a^2 : b^2$, volume $a^3 : b^3$. Filling in whichever row the question gives you earns the first method mark.
2. **Get to the length ratio first.** From areas, square-root; from volumes (or masses of the same material), cube-root. Then go to the row you need.
3. **Simplify the ratio** before rooting: $180 : 405 = 4 : 9$, so lengths are $2 : 3$.
4. **Scale up or down with a fraction.** Larger volume $=$ smaller volume $\times \dfrac{b^3}{a^3}$. Check the answer is bigger or smaller, as it should be.
5. **Maps and models:** convert the map length to a real length in sensible units, then square or cube it. Never multiply an area by the plain scale number.
6. **"Show that" or "explain" with similarity:** name the equal angles (or equal ratios) that make the shapes similar.
7. **Non-calculator paper:** ratios are chosen to root nicely ($8 : 27$, $16 : 25$). **Calculator paper:** keep the exact scale factor, e.g. $\sqrt[3]{2}$, until the final step.
:::

::: example Areas and volumes from a length ratio
Two similar cylinders have heights 6 cm and 9 cm. The smaller has volume 160 cm³ and curved surface area 96 cm². Find the corresponding measurements of the larger.

Length ratio $6 : 9 = 2 : 3$; area ratio $4 : 9$; volume ratio $8 : 27$.

Volume $= 160 \times \dfrac{27}{8} = 540$ cm³. Curved surface area $= 96 \times \dfrac94 = 216$ cm².
:::

::: example Map area
A map has scale $1 : 25\,000$. A lake has an area of 6 cm² on the map. Work out its real area in km².

$1$ cm represents $25\,000$ cm $= 250$ m $= 0.25$ km, so $1$ cm² represents $0.25^2 = 0.0625$ km².

Real area $= 6 \times 0.0625 = 0.375$ km².
:::

::: example Similarity and trig
Triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ are right-angled at $B$ and $E$, with angle $A = $ angle $D = 30°$. $BC = 4$ cm, $AC = 8$ cm and $DF = 13$ cm. Find $EF$, and explain why $\sin 30° = \dfrac12$.

The triangles have the same angles ($90°$, $30°$, $60°$), so they are similar, with scale factor $\dfrac{13}{8}$. $EF = 4 \times \dfrac{13}{8} = 6.5$ cm.

In every such triangle, opposite $:$ hypotenuse $= 4 : 8 = 1 : 2$, so $\sin 30° = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

::: example From area ratio to volume (grade 8)
Two similar bottles have surface areas 180 cm² and 405 cm². The larger bottle holds 675 cm³. How much does the smaller hold?

Area ratio $180 : 405 = 4 : 9$, so length ratio $2 : 3$ and volume ratio $8 : 27$.

Smaller volume $= 675 \times \dfrac{8}{27} = 200$ cm³.
:::

::: example Masses of similar solids (grade 8)
Two similar statues are made from the same metal. The smaller is 12 cm tall with mass 2 kg; the larger has mass 16 kg. How tall is the larger statue?

Same material, so mass ratio $=$ volume ratio $= 2 : 16 = 1 : 8$. Length ratio $= 1 : \sqrt[3]{8} = 1 : 2$.

Height $= 12 \times 2 = 24$ cm.
:::

::: example Cutting a cone (grade 9)
A solid cone has height 12 cm. It is cut parallel to its base, 8 cm from the vertex, into a small cone and a frustum. Find the ratio of the volume of the small cone to the volume of the frustum.

The small cone is similar to the original, length ratio $8 : 12 = 2 : 3$, so volume ratio $8 : 27$.

Frustum $= 27 - 8 = 19$ parts. Small cone $:$ frustum $= 8 : 19$.
:::

::: q
Two similar shapes have lengths in the ratio $3 : 4$. Write down the ratio of their areas and the ratio of their volumes.
---
Areas $9 : 16$; volumes $27 : 64$.
:::

::: q
Two squares have sides 6 cm and 15 cm. Write the ratio of their areas in the form $1 : n$.
---
$36 : 225 = 1 : 6.25$.
:::

::: q
Two similar triangles have areas 12 cm² and 75 cm². A side of the smaller triangle is 4 cm. Find the corresponding side of the larger.
---
Area ratio $12 : 75 = 4 : 25$, so length ratio $2 : 5$. Side $= 4 \times \dfrac52 = 10$ cm.
:::

::: q
Two similar solids have volumes in the ratio $64 : 125$. Find the ratio of their surface areas.
---
Lengths $\sqrt[3]{64} : \sqrt[3]{125} = 4 : 5$, so surface areas $16 : 25$.
:::

::: q
Two similar cones have heights 10 cm and 15 cm. The smaller cone has volume 96 cm³. Find the volume of the larger.
---
Lengths $2 : 3$, volumes $8 : 27$. $96 \times \dfrac{27}{8} = 324$ cm³.
:::

::: q
A model car is made on a scale of $1 : 20$. The model's windscreen has area 60 cm². Find the area of the real windscreen in m².
---
Area factor $20^2 = 400$. $60 \times 400 = 24\,000$ cm² $= 2.4$ m² (since $1$ m² $= 10\,000$ cm²).
:::

::: q
Explain why $\tan 40°$ has the same value whichever right-angled triangle you measure it in.
---
Every right-angled triangle with a $40°$ angle has angles $90°$, $40°$ and $50°$, so they are all similar. Corresponding sides are in the same ratio, so opposite $\div$ adjacent is the same in all of them.
:::

::: q
Two cubes have volumes in the ratio $1 : 2$. Find the ratio of their edge lengths in the form $1 : n$, with $n$ to 3 significant figures.
---
$1 : \sqrt[3]{2} = 1 : 1.26$.
:::

::: q
A sphere of radius $r$ just fits inside a cylinder of radius $r$ and height $2r$. Show that the ratio of their volumes is $2 : 3$. (Volume of a sphere $= \frac43\pi r^3$.)
---
Cylinder $= \pi r^2 \times 2r = 2\pi r^3$. Ratio $\dfrac43\pi r^3 : 2\pi r^3 = \dfrac43 : 2 = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3$.
:::

::: watch
- **Areas use the square, volumes the cube.** Doubling the lengths multiplies the area by 4 and the volume by 8, not by 2.
- **Go back to lengths first.** From an area ratio to a volume ratio you must square-root and then cube; squaring or cubing the area ratio directly is wrong.
- **Mass follows volume** for objects made of the same material.
- **Map areas:** convert 1 cm to real length, then square. Multiplying a map area by 25 000 is a very common error.
- Simplify ratios before rooting, and check the direction: the larger shape must get the larger answer.
- Area unit conversions use squares too: $1$ m² $= 10\,000$ cm², not $100$ cm².
:::

::: test
A cube of side $x$ cm and a sphere of radius $x$ cm are compared. Show that the ratio of the volume of the cube to the volume of the sphere is $3 : 4\pi$. (Volume of a sphere $= \frac43\pi r^3$.) (2)
---
$x^3 : \dfrac43\pi x^3$ (M1) $= 1 : \dfrac43\pi = 3 : 4\pi$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A solid cone of height 15 cm is cut parallel to its base, 5 cm from the vertex, into a small cone and a frustum. The volume of the frustum is 520 cm³. Work out the volume of the small cone. (4)
---
Length ratio small $:$ whole $= 5 : 15 = 1 : 3$ (M1). Volume ratio $1 : 27$ (M1), so small cone $:$ frustum $= 1 : 26$ (M1). Small cone $= 520 \div 26 = 20$ cm³ (A1).
:::
