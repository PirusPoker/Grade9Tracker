## What it is

This topic is about **showing data** in the right diagram and **summarising** it with an average and a measure of spread, then using those to **compare** two sets of data.

### Choosing a diagram

| Data | Diagram |
|---|---|
| Categorical (colours, sports) | bar chart, pie chart, pictogram, frequency table |
| Ungrouped discrete numbers (goals, shoe size) | vertical line chart, bar chart |
| Values over time | time-series line graph |
| Grouped continuous data (heights, times) | histogram, cumulative frequency graph |
| Comparing spread and centre of two groups | box plots |

- **Pictogram:** a symbol stands for a stated number; part-symbols show fractions of it. Always give a key.
- **Pie chart:** each sector's angle is $\dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}} \times 360°$. Of 90 people, 30 choose football: $\frac{30}{90} \times 360° = 120°$. A pie chart shows **proportions**, not sizes — a bigger angle in one chart does not mean more people than a smaller angle in another chart with a larger total.
- **Vertical line chart:** a bar chart drawn with thin lines, for discrete values.
- **Time series:** plot points at equal time intervals and join them with straight lines. Describe the **trend** (the general rise or fall) and any **seasonal pattern** that repeats (sales high every December). Points between the plotted times have no meaning of their own.

### Averages and range from a frequency table

For a discrete frequency table:

| Goals $x$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency $f$ | 5 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 |

- $n = 20$. Mean $= \dfrac{\sum fx}{\sum f} = \dfrac{0 + 8 + 8 + 6 + 4}{20} = \dfrac{26}{20} = 1.3$.
- Median: the $\frac{20 + 1}{2} = 10.5$th value; running totals 5, 13, … so the 10th and 11th are both 1: median $= 1$.
- Mode $= 1$ (highest frequency). Range $= 4 - 0 = 4$.

**Grouped data:** you do not know the exact values, so use the **midpoint** of each class and call the mean an **estimate**. The class with the highest frequency is the **modal class**; the **median class** contains the $\frac{n}{2}$th value.

**Combining means:** the mean of 12 boys is 15 and of 18 girls is 20. Overall mean $= \dfrac{12 \times 15 + 18 \times 20}{30} = \dfrac{540}{30} = 18$ — work with totals, never average the averages.

### Quartiles, IQR and outliers

The **median** splits ordered data in half; the **lower quartile** (LQ) and **upper quartile** (UQ) split it into quarters. For a list of $n$ values, the LQ is the $\frac{n + 1}{4}$th value, the median the $\frac{n + 1}{2}$th and the UQ the $\frac{3(n + 1)}{4}$th.

3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 15, 18, 20, 41 ($n = 11$): LQ $= 7$ (3rd), median $= 12$ (6th), UQ $= 18$ (9th).

The **interquartile range** IQR $=$ UQ $-$ LQ $= 11$ measures the spread of the **middle half** of the data, so unlike the range it ignores extreme values.

An **outlier** is a value that does not fit the pattern of the rest. A common rule (the question will tell you if it is being used): an outlier is more than $1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ below the LQ or above the UQ. Here $1.5 \times 11 = 16.5$ and $18 + 16.5 = 34.5$, so 41 is an outlier. Outliers distort the **mean** and the **range** badly; the median and IQR hardly move. Check whether an outlier is an error before removing it.

### Box plots

A **box plot** shows five values on a scale: minimum, LQ, median, UQ, maximum. The box runs from LQ to UQ with a line at the median; the whiskers reach out to the extremes. (If outliers are marked separately, the whisker stops at the most extreme value that is not an outlier.) Box plots are ideal for comparing two groups side by side.

### Histograms

A **histogram** is for grouped continuous data. The **area** of each bar represents the frequency, so with unequal class widths the vertical axis is **frequency density**:
$$\text{frequency density} = \frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}}, \qquad \text{frequency} = \text{frequency density} \times \text{class width}.$$

| Height $h$ (cm) | $0 < h \le 10$ | $10 < h \le 15$ | $15 < h \le 20$ | $20 < h \le 30$ | $30 < h \le 50$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 12 | 18 | 24 | 30 | 16 |
| Freq. density | 1.2 | 3.6 | 4.8 | 3.0 | 0.8 |

Bars touch, there are no gaps, and each bar spans its class exactly. To **estimate** how many are between 12 and 25 cm, take the parts of bars: $\frac35 \times 18 + 24 + \frac{5}{10} \times 30 = 10.8 + 24 + 15 = 49.8 \approx 50$. This assumes the values are spread evenly within each class.

With **equal** class widths, bar heights can simply be frequencies; frequency density only matters when widths differ.

### Cumulative frequency

A **cumulative frequency** table gives running totals: "how many are less than or equal to each upper class boundary". Plot each running total at the **upper boundary** of its class and join with a smooth curve (or straight lines), starting from zero frequency at the lowest boundary.

| Mass $m$ (g) | $\le 20$ | $\le 30$ | $\le 40$ | $\le 50$ | $\le 60$ | $\le 70$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative freq. | 6 | 18 | 40 | 62 | 74 | 80 |

For $n = 80$ read across at $\frac{n}{2} = 40$ for the median ($40$ g), at $\frac{n}{4} = 20$ for the LQ (about 31 g) and at $\frac{3n}{4} = 60$ for the UQ (about 49 g), so IQR $\approx 18$ g. "How many weigh more than 55 g?" Read up from 55 to about 68, then $80 - 68 = 12$. For grouped data, use $\frac{n}{2}$ and $\frac{n}{4}$, not $\frac{n+1}{2}$.

### Comparing distributions

Compare **one average and one measure of spread**, each **in context**, using the actual numbers:

- "The median time for Group A (34 min) is lower than for Group B (38 min), so Group A were **generally faster**."
- "Group A's IQR (16 min) is bigger than Group B's (12 min), so Group A's times were **less consistent**."

Use median and IQR when there are outliers or the data is skewed; mean and range suit data without extreme values.

::: terms
- **Frequency density**: frequency divided by class width — the height of a histogram bar.
- **Histogram**: a chart for grouped continuous data in which the area of each bar represents frequency.
- **Cumulative frequency**: the running total of frequencies, plotted at the upper class boundary.
- **Modal class**: the class with the highest frequency (with unequal widths, not necessarily the tallest bar).
- **Estimated mean**: the mean of grouped data found using class midpoints, Σfx ÷ Σf.
- **Interquartile range (IQR)**: upper quartile minus lower quartile — the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
- **Outlier**: a value far from the rest, often defined as more than 1.5 × IQR beyond a quartile.
- **Box plot**: a diagram showing the minimum, lower quartile, median, upper quartile and maximum on a scale.
- **Time series**: data recorded at regular time intervals, plotted as a line graph to show trend and seasonal pattern.
:::

::: method
1. **Grouped mean:** add a midpoint column and an $f \times x$ column; divide $\sum fx$ by $\sum f$ (not by the number of classes). Call it an **estimate** and check it lies inside the data's range.
2. **Histogram from a table:** frequency density $=$ frequency $\div$ class width for every class; label the axis "Frequency density"; bars touch and cover each class exactly.
3. **Frequencies from a histogram:** frequency $=$ height $\times$ width. For part of a bar, use the fraction of the width. If the axis is unlabelled, use a bar with a known frequency to find what one square (or one unit of height) is worth.
4. **Cumulative frequency:** plot at **upper** boundaries; read at $\frac{n}{4}, \frac{n}{2}, \frac{3n}{4}$ and **draw the lines** on the graph — the construction lines earn method marks.
5. **"How many more than…"** from a cumulative frequency graph: read the value, then **subtract from the total**.
6. **Box plot:** plot min, LQ, median, UQ, max accurately against the scale.
7. **Compare** with one average and one measure of spread, quoting both values, **in context** ("generally taller", "more consistent"). Two comparisons usually earn two marks.
8. **Paper choice:** the calculator papers carry the big $\sum fx$ tables; on Paper 1 the numbers will be friendly, so organise the table neatly and add carefully.
:::

::: example Estimated mean from grouped data
The table shows the times, $t$ minutes, 40 students took to finish a puzzle.

| $t$ | $0 < t \le 10$ | $10 < t \le 20$ | $20 < t \le 30$ | $30 < t \le 40$ | $40 < t \le 60$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $f$ | 4 | 11 | 15 | 7 | 3 |

Estimate the mean, and write down the modal class and the class containing the median.

Midpoints 5, 15, 25, 35, 50. $\sum fx = 20 + 165 + 375 + 245 + 150 = 955$. Estimated mean $= \dfrac{955}{40} = 23.875 \approx 23.9$ minutes.

Modal class $20 < t \le 30$. The median is the 20th–21st value; running totals 4, 15, 30 put it in $20 < t \le 30$.
:::

::: example Reading a histogram
A histogram shows the speeds of cars, $v$ km/h, with these bars:

| Class | $0 < v \le 20$ | $20 < v \le 30$ | $30 < v \le 35$ | $35 < v \le 40$ | $40 < v \le 60$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency density | 0.5 | 2.4 | 5.2 | 4.0 | 0.9 |

(a) How many cars were recorded? (b) Estimate how many were faster than 38 km/h. (c) Estimate the mean speed.

(a) Frequencies $= $ density $\times$ width: $10, 24, 26, 20, 18$. Total $98$.

(b) $\frac{2}{5} \times 20 + 18 = 8 + 18 = 26$ cars.

(c) Midpoints 10, 25, 32.5, 37.5, 50: $\sum fx = 100 + 600 + 845 + 750 + 900 = 3195$; $\dfrac{3195}{98} = 32.6$ km/h (3 s.f.).
:::

::: example Cumulative frequency to box plot, then compare
Use the cumulative frequency table for the 80 masses in the notes. The smallest mass was 12 g and the largest 68 g. Compare with a second batch whose box plot shows median 44 g and IQR 10 g.

Median 40 g, LQ about 31 g, UQ about 49 g. Box plot: 12, 31, 40, 49, 68.

The second batch has a higher median (44 g against 40 g), so its items are generally heavier. Its IQR is smaller (10 g against about 18 g), so its masses are more consistent.
:::

::: example Quartiles and an outlier
The numbers of texts sent by 11 people in a day were: 20, 3, 13, 7, 41, 9, 15, 5, 12, 18, 8. (a) Find the median and IQR. (b) An outlier is more than $1.5 \times$ IQR above the UQ. Is 41 an outlier? (c) Which is the better average here, the mean or the median?

(a) Ordered: 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 15, 18, 20, 41. Median $= 12$, LQ $= 7$, UQ $= 18$, IQR $= 11$.

(b) $18 + 1.5 \times 11 = 34.5$; $41 > 34.5$, so yes.

(c) The median: the mean ($\frac{151}{11} \approx 13.7$) is pulled up by the outlier, while the median is not affected.
:::

::: example Grade 9: a histogram with no scale
On a histogram the vertical axis has no numbers. The bar for $10 < x \le 15$ is 8 cm tall and represents 20 people. The bar for $15 < x \le 25$ is 4.4 cm tall. How many people does it represent?

Frequency $= k \times \text{height} \times \text{width}$: $20 = k \times 8 \times 5$, so $k = 0.5$.

Second bar: $0.5 \times 4.4 \times 10 = 22$ people.

(Equivalently: the first bar has frequency density $20 \div 5 = 4$ and is 8 cm tall, so 1 cm $= 0.5$; the second has density $2.2$, and $2.2 \times 10 = 22$.)
:::

::: q
72 students were asked how they travel to school: 30 by bus, 18 by car, 24 walk. Work out the pie chart angles.
---
Bus $\frac{30}{72} \times 360° = 150°$; car $90°$; walk $120°$.
:::

::: q
Shoe sizes $x$ = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 have frequencies 3, 7, 6, 3, 1. Find the mean and the median.
---
$\sum fx = 3 + 14 + 18 + 12 + 5 = 52$; mean $= \dfrac{52}{20} = 2.6$. Median: 10th value is 2, 11th is 3, so median $= 2.5$.
:::

::: q
Masses $w$ kg: $0 < w \le 4$: 5; $4 < w \le 8$: 9; $8 < w \le 12$: 4; $12 < w \le 16$: 2. Estimate the mean.
---
Midpoints 2, 6, 10, 14: $\sum fx = 10 + 54 + 40 + 28 = 132$. Mean $\approx \dfrac{132}{20} = 6.6$ kg.
:::

::: q
The class $20 < x \le 35$ has frequency 45. What is the height of its histogram bar? Another bar, for $35 < x \le 60$, has frequency density 1.6. What is its frequency?
---
$45 \div 15 = 3$. $\quad 1.6 \times 25 = 40$.
:::

::: q
Find the median and interquartile range of 4, 6, 9, 10, 12, 15, 17.
---
$n = 7$: LQ is the 2nd value $= 6$, median the 4th $= 10$, UQ the 6th $= 15$. IQR $= 9$.
:::

::: q
A cumulative frequency graph shows 120 values. At what cumulative frequencies do you read the median and the quartiles?
---
Median at 60; LQ at 30; UQ at 90.
:::

::: q
The mean mark of 15 students is 62 and the mean mark of another 10 students is 70. Find the mean mark of all 25.
---
$\dfrac{15 \times 62 + 10 \times 70}{25} = \dfrac{1630}{25} = 65.2$.
:::

::: q
Boys' heights: median 1.62 m, IQR 0.12 m. Girls' heights: median 1.58 m, IQR 0.08 m. Make two comparisons.
---
The boys' median is higher, so the boys were generally taller. The girls' IQR is smaller, so the girls' heights were more consistent.
:::

::: q
Quarterly sales over two years were 20, 35, 28, 15, 24, 38, 31, 18. Describe the trend and the seasonal pattern.
---
Each quarter is higher than the same quarter a year before, so the trend is slightly upward. There is a seasonal pattern: sales peak in quarter 2 and are lowest in quarter 4 each year.
:::

::: watch
- **Histograms:** plotting **frequency** instead of frequency density with unequal widths is the most expensive mistake — the shape is wrong and every mark goes.
- **Grouped mean:** dividing by the number of classes (5) instead of the total frequency, or using class widths instead of midpoints.
- **Cumulative frequency:** plot at the **upper** boundary, not the midpoint; and "more than 55" means **total minus** the reading.
- **Comparisons** must be **in context** and quote the values: "Group B's median is higher (38 vs 34), so they were generally slower". "B is higher" alone gets nothing.
- Compare **like with like**: median with median, IQR with IQR. Do not compare the range of one group with the IQR of another.
- The modal class with unequal widths is the one with the highest **frequency**, not the tallest bar.
- Pie charts show proportions; a bigger sector does not mean a bigger number if the totals differ.
:::

::: test
In a survey of 120 people, 42 chose tea. (a) Work out the angle for tea on a pie chart. (b) In the same pie chart the sector for juice is $75°$. How many people chose juice? (3)
---
(a) $\dfrac{42}{120} \times 360$ (M1) $= 126°$ (A1).

(b) $\dfrac{75}{360} \times 120 = 25$ (B1).
:::

::: test
The table shows the number of children, $x$, in 30 households.

| $x$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $f$ | 4 | 9 | 12 | 3 | 2 |

Find (a) the mode, (b) the median, (c) the mean, to 3 significant figures. (4)
---
(a) 2 (B1).

(b) 15th/16th values: running totals 4, 13, 25, so median $= 2$ (B1).

(c) $\sum fx = 0 + 9 + 24 + 9 + 8 = 50$ (M1); $\dfrac{50}{30} = 1.67$ (A1).
:::

::: test
The heights, $h$ cm, of 40 plants are grouped: $150 < h \le 160$: 6; $160 < h \le 170$: 14; $170 < h \le 180$: 12; $180 < h \le 190$: 8. (a) Work out an estimate for the mean height. (b) Explain why your answer is only an estimate. (4)
---
(a) Midpoints 155, 165, 175, 185 (M1). $\sum fx = 930 + 2310 + 2100 + 1480 = 6820$ (M1). $\dfrac{6820}{40} = 170.5$ cm (A1).

(b) The exact heights are not known; midpoints were used for each class (B1).
:::

::: test
Times, $t$ seconds, are grouped: $0 < t \le 5$: 10; $5 < t \le 15$: 32; $15 < t \le 20$: 24; $20 < t \le 40$: 16. Work out the frequency density for each class, ready to draw a histogram. (3)
---
$10 \div 5 = 2$; $32 \div 10 = 3.2$; $24 \div 5 = 4.8$; $16 \div 20 = 0.8$ (M1 for frequency $\div$ width; A1 for three correct; A1 all four).
:::

::: test
A histogram has bars with these frequency densities: $0 < x \le 10$: 1.2; $10 < x \le 20$: 3.0; $20 < x \le 25$: 5.6; $25 < x \le 40$: 1.4. Work out an estimate for the number of values between 15 and 30. (3)
---
Frequencies 12, 30, 28, 21 (M1). Between 15 and 30: $\frac12 \times 30 + 28 + \frac{5}{15} \times 21$ (M1) $= 15 + 28 + 7 = 50$ (A1).
:::

::: test
The cumulative frequencies for 60 values of $x$ are: $\le 10$: 5; $\le 20$: 17; $\le 30$: 38; $\le 40$: 52; $\le 50$: 60. The points are joined with straight lines. (a) Estimate the median. (b) Estimate how many values are greater than 45. (4)
---
(a) Median at 30th value, between 17 (at 20) and 38 (at 30) (M1). $20 + \dfrac{13}{21} \times 10 \approx 26.2$ (A1).

(b) At 45, cumulative frequency $\approx 52 + \frac12 \times 8 = 56$ (M1). $60 - 56 = 4$ values (A1).
:::

::: test
Two classes sat a test. Class A: minimum 12, LQ 25, median 34, UQ 41, maximum 55. Class B: minimum 18, LQ 30, median 38, UQ 42, maximum 50. (a) Work out the IQR of each class. (b) Compare the marks of the two classes. (3)
---
(a) A: $41 - 25 = 16$; B: $42 - 30 = 12$ (B1).

(b) Class B's median is higher (38 vs 34), so class B generally scored higher (B1). Class B's IQR is smaller (12 vs 16), so its marks were more consistent (B1).
:::

::: test
Some grouped data have class midpoints 5, 15, 25, 35 with frequencies 6, $x$, 10, 4. The estimated mean is 19. Find $x$. (4)
---
$\sum fx = 30 + 15x + 250 + 140 = 420 + 15x$; $\sum f = 20 + x$ (M1). $\dfrac{420 + 15x}{20 + x} = 19$ (M1). $420 + 15x = 380 + 19x$ (M1), so $x = 10$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A histogram has no numbers on the vertical axis. The bar for $0 < x \le 10$ is 6 cm tall and represents 24 people. (a) The bar for $10 < x \le 15$ is 9 cm tall. How many people does it represent? (b) The class $15 < x \le 30$ contains 36 people. How tall should its bar be? (4)
---
$24 = k \times 6 \times 10$, so each cm of height per unit width is worth $k = 0.4$ (M1).

(a) $0.4 \times 9 \times 5 = 18$ people (A1).

(b) $36 = 0.4 \times h \times 15$ (M1), $h = 6$ cm (A1).
:::

::: test
For a data set, LQ $= 24$ and UQ $= 36$. An outlier is a value more than $1.5 \times$ IQR below the LQ or above the UQ. The smallest value is 5. Is it an outlier? Show your working. (2)
---
IQR $= 12$; $24 - 1.5 \times 12 = 6$ (M1). $5 < 6$, so yes, 5 is an outlier (A1).
:::
