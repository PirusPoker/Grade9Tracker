## What it is

This topic is the engine room of every GCSE paper: putting numbers in order, calculating accurately with all four operations (without a calculator on Paper 1), and using the order of operations and inverse operations to simplify work. Nobody gets a question titled "N2", but lost marks here leak into every other topic.

### Ordering numbers and the inequality symbols

The six symbols you must read and write:

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| $=$ | equal to | $0.25 = \frac14$ |
| $\ne$ | not equal to | $0.3 \ne \frac13$ |
| $<$ | less than | $-7 < -2$ |
| $>$ | greater than | $0.61 > 0.6$ |
| $\le$ | less than or equal to | $x \le 4$ |
| $\ge$ | greater than or equal to | $n \ge 0$ |

**Negative numbers:** the further left on the number line, the smaller. $-8 < -3$ because $-8$ is further below zero, even though 8 is bigger than 3.

**Decimals:** line up the decimal points and compare place by place, filling gaps with zeros: $0.608,\ 0.68,\ 0.6$ become $0.608,\ 0.680,\ 0.600$, so in ascending order they are $0.6,\ 0.608,\ 0.68$.

**Fractions:** either convert to decimals, or write over a common denominator. To order $\frac35, \frac23, \frac7{12}$: the LCM of 5, 3, 12 is 60, giving $\frac{36}{60}, \frac{40}{60}, \frac{35}{60}$, so ascending order is $\frac7{12}, \frac35, \frac23$. Cross-multiplying compares two fractions quickly: $\frac58$ vs $\frac{7}{11}$ — compare $5 \times 11 = 55$ with $7 \times 8 = 56$, so $\frac58 < \frac7{11}$.

**Mixed lists:** convert everything to decimals (to 3 or 4 decimal places) and keep a note of the original form so you write the answer in the form the question used.

### Place value

Each place is ten times the one to its right. Knowing one fact gives you many: if $47 \times 36 = 1692$, then

$$4.7 \times 3.6 = 16.92 \qquad 0.47 \times 360 = 169.2 \qquad 1692 \div 0.36 = 4700$$

Count the powers of ten you moved: $4.7 \times 3.6$ is $47 \times 36$ divided by $10 \times 10$. For $1692 \div 0.36$: $0.36$ is $36 \div 100$, so the answer is $47 \times 100$.

### The four operations

**Formal written methods** are expected on Paper 1: column addition and subtraction, long multiplication (or the grid method), and short or long division (bus stop). Examiners award method marks for a clear, complete method even if one digit slips.

- **Decimals — multiplying:** ignore the points, multiply the whole numbers, then put back the total number of decimal places. $0.07 \times 0.3$: $7 \times 3 = 21$, three decimal places in total, so $0.021$.
- **Decimals — dividing:** make the divisor a whole number by multiplying both numbers by the same power of 10. $4.56 \div 0.12 = 456 \div 12 = 38$.

**Negative numbers.** Adding a negative is subtracting: $5 + (-8) = -3$. Subtracting a negative is adding: $-4 - (-9) = 5$. For $\times$ and $\div$: same signs give positive, different signs give negative: $(-6) \times (-7) = 42$, $(-48) \div 6 = -8$.

**Fractions.**
- Add and subtract over a common denominator: $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{10}{12} - \frac{9}{12} = \frac{1}{12}$.
- Multiply tops and bottoms (cancel first to keep numbers small): $\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{15}{8} = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{5}{2} = \frac{5}{6}$.
- Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal: $\frac{2}{5} \div \frac{4}{7} = \frac{2}{5} \times \frac{7}{4} = \frac{14}{20} = \frac{7}{10}$.

**Mixed numbers.** For $\times$ and $\div$ always convert to improper fractions first: $2\frac13 \times 1\frac27 = \frac73 \times \frac97 = \frac{9}{3} = 3$. For $+$ and $-$ you may deal with whole parts separately, but improper fractions are safer when borrowing is needed: $3\frac14 - 1\frac56 = \frac{13}{4} - \frac{11}{6} = \frac{39}{12} - \frac{22}{12} = \frac{17}{12} = 1\frac{5}{12}$.

Negative mixed numbers work the same way: $-1\frac12 \times 2\frac23 = -\frac32 \times \frac83 = -4$.

### Order of operations

Work in this order (often remembered as BIDMAS):

1. **Brackets** (innermost first).
2. **Indices** — powers, roots and reciprocals.
3. **Division and Multiplication**, left to right.
4. **Addition and Subtraction**, left to right.

So $20 - 12 \div 4 \times 2 = 20 - 3 \times 2 = 14$ (divide then multiply, left to right). A fraction line acts as a bracket around the top and around the bottom: $\dfrac{6 + 3^2}{\sqrt{25} - 2} = \dfrac{15}{3} = 5$. A root sign covers everything under it: $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, not $3 + 4$.

Watch the power: $-3^2 = -9$ (the square applies to 3 only), but $(-3)^2 = 9$.

The **reciprocal** of $x$ is $\frac1x$ (for $x \ne 0$); the reciprocal of $\frac{a}{b}$ is $\frac{b}{a}$. A number times its reciprocal is 1. The reciprocal of $0.4$ is $\frac{1}{0.4} = \frac{10}{4} = 2.5$.

### Inverse operations and cancellation

Addition undoes subtraction, multiplication undoes division, squaring undoes square-rooting, and each operation is its own reciprocal partner: dividing by $\frac{a}{b}$ is multiplying by $\frac{b}{a}$. Use inverses to:

- **Check** an answer: if $864 \div 24 = 36$, then $36 \times 24$ should be 864.
- **Work backwards** ("I think of a number, multiply by 3, subtract 7, get 38": undo in reverse order, $38 + 7 = 45$, $45 \div 3 = 15$).
- **Cancel** before calculating: $\dfrac{36 \times 25}{15 \times 12} = \dfrac{3 \times 5}{3 \times 1} = 5$ (cancel 36 with 12 to leave 3, and 25 with 15 to leave $\frac53$). The same idea simplifies expressions: $\dfrac{8x^2 \times 3}{6x} = 4x$.

::: terms
- **Integer**: a whole number, positive, negative or zero, e.g. −4, 0, 17.
- **Inequality symbol**: one of <, >, ≤, ≥ or ≠, used to compare two values that need not be equal.
- **Place value**: the value a digit has because of its position, each place ten times the one to its right.
- **Improper fraction**: a fraction whose numerator is at least as big as its denominator, e.g. 11/4.
- **Mixed number**: a whole number and a proper fraction together, e.g. 2¾ = 11/4.
- **Reciprocal**: 1 divided by the number; the reciprocal of a/b is b/a, and a number times its reciprocal is 1.
- **Inverse operation**: the operation that undoes another, e.g. division undoes multiplication.
- **Order of operations**: brackets, then indices (powers, roots, reciprocals), then × and ÷, then + and −, each pair left to right.
:::

::: method
1. **Read which paper.** On Paper 1 (non-calculator) every multiplication, division and fraction calculation must be done by a written method — show it, because a full method earns M1 even if an arithmetic slip costs the A1.
2. **Ordering:** convert everything to one form (decimals to the same number of places, or fractions over one common denominator), order those, then **write the answer using the original numbers** in the order asked (ascending = smallest first).
3. **Place value questions** ("Given $23 \times 57 = 1311$, work out…"): count how many powers of 10 each number has moved and apply the same shifts to the answer. Check with a rough estimate.
4. **Fractions:** convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing; cancel before multiplying; flip the second fraction to divide. Give the answer in its simplest form, and as a mixed number if the question's numbers were mixed.
5. **"Show that" with fractions:** every step must be written, including the common denominator and the equivalent fractions. Ending with the given answer without the step that produces it scores nothing.
6. **Order of operations:** rewrite the calculation line by line, doing one layer at a time. Treat a fraction line and a root sign as brackets.
7. **Inverse operations:** to check, reverse the calculation; to find a starting value, undo each step in reverse order; to simplify, cancel common factors before multiplying anything out.
:::

::: example Ordering a mixed list
Write in ascending order: $\frac{5}{8},\ 0.6,\ 62\%,\ \frac{7}{11},\ 0.6\overline{1}$ (the last number is 0.6111…).

Convert: $\frac58 = 0.625$, $0.6 = 0.600$, $62\% = 0.620$, $\frac{7}{11} = 0.6363\ldots$, $0.6111\ldots$

Ascending: $0.6,\ 0.6\overline{1},\ 62\%,\ \frac58,\ \frac7{11}$.
:::

::: example Place value from a given fact
Given that $286 \times 43 = 12\,298$, write down the value of (a) $2.86 \times 4.3$, (b) $12.298 \div 0.43$, (c) $0.286 \times 0.043$.

(a) $2.86$ is $286 \div 100$ and $4.3$ is $43 \div 10$, so divide $12\,298$ by $1000$: $12.298$.

(b) $12\,298 \div 43 = 286$. Here $12.298$ is $12\,298 \div 1000$ and $0.43$ is $43 \div 100$, so the answer is $286 \div 10 = 28.6$.

(c) Divide by $1000 \times 1000$: $0.012298$.

Check (b) by estimating: $12 \div 0.4 = 30$. ✓
:::

::: example Mixed numbers, non-calculator
Work out $2\frac{2}{5} \div 1\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{4}$, giving your answer as a fraction in its simplest form.

Division first: $\frac{12}{5} \div \frac32 = \frac{12}{5} \times \frac23 = \frac{24}{15} = \frac85$.

Then $\frac85 - \frac34 = \frac{32}{20} - \frac{15}{20} = \frac{17}{20}$.
:::

::: example Order of operations with powers, roots and reciprocals
Work out $\dfrac{(-2)^3 + \sqrt{7^2 - 13}}{5^{-1} \times 10}$.

Top: $(-2)^3 = -8$; $\sqrt{49 - 13} = \sqrt{36} = 6$; so the top is $-8 + 6 = -2$.

Bottom: $5^{-1} = \frac15$, and $\frac15 \times 10 = 2$.

Answer: $\dfrac{-2}{2} = -1$.
:::

::: example Grade 7–8: a "show that" with mixed numbers
Show that $\dfrac{2\frac{1}{4} \times 1\frac{7}{9}}{\left(1\frac{1}{3}\right)^2} = 2\frac14$.

Convert: $2\frac14 = \frac94$, $1\frac79 = \frac{16}{9}$, $1\frac13 = \frac43$.

Top: $\frac94 \times \frac{16}{9} = \frac{16}{4} = 4$ (the 9s cancel).

Bottom: $\left(\frac43\right)^2 = \frac{16}{9}$.

$4 \div \frac{16}{9} = 4 \times \frac{9}{16} = \frac{36}{16} = \frac94 = 2\frac14$ as required.
:::

::: example Grade 8–9: inverse operations in a problem
A number is multiplied by $\frac34$, then $2\frac12$ is subtracted, then the result is divided by $-\frac{5}{6}$. The answer is $-1\frac15$. Find the starting number.

Undo in reverse order. Multiply by $-\frac56$: $-\frac65 \times -\frac56 = 1$.

Add $2\frac12$: $1 + 2\frac12 = \frac72$.

Divide by $\frac34$: $\frac72 \times \frac43 = \frac{28}{6} = \frac{14}{3} = 4\frac23$.

Check forwards: $\frac{14}{3} \times \frac34 = \frac72$; $\frac72 - \frac52 = 1$; $1 \div (-\frac56) = -\frac65 = -1\frac15$. ✓
:::

::: watch
- **Negative ordering:** $-2.52$ is smaller than $-2.5$. The bigger the digits after the minus sign, the smaller the number.
- **Decimals with different lengths:** $0.68 > 0.608$. Pad with zeros before comparing; do not think "608 is bigger than 68".
- **Mixed numbers multiplied part by part:** $2\frac12 \times 3\frac12$ is not $6\frac14$. Convert to improper fractions: $\frac52 \times \frac72 = \frac{35}{4} = 8\frac34$.
- **Left to right for × and ÷:** $24 \div 4 \times 2 = 12$, not 3. BIDMAS does not mean division before multiplication.
- **$-3^2 = -9$:** the power binds to the 3 only. Calculators agree; many students do not.
- **Place value answers not sense-checked:** a quick estimate catches a decimal point in the wrong place.
- **"Show that" skipped steps:** writing the target answer after one line loses all the marks. Show the common denominator and each conversion.
:::
