## What it is

Most of GCSE algebra is **rewriting an expression in a more useful form without changing its value**. Expanding takes brackets away; factorising puts them back. Algebraic fractions use both: factorise, cancel, combine. Every later topic — solving quadratics, proof, completing the square, functions — depends on doing these quickly and accurately.

### Collecting like terms and single brackets

**Like terms** have exactly the same letters and powers: $3x^2$ and $-5x^2$ are like terms; $3x^2$ and $3x$ are not.

$$4a + 3b - a + 2b^2 - 5b = 3a - 2b + 2b^2$$

To expand a single bracket, multiply **every** term inside by the term outside, sign included:

$$3(2x - 5) - 4(x - 2) = 6x - 15 - 4x + 8 = 2x - 7$$

The $-4$ multiplies the $-2$ to give $+8$ — the commonest slip in this topic.

### Taking out common factors

Factorising is the reverse. Take out the **highest common factor** of the numbers and of each letter:

$$12x^2y - 18xy^2 = 6xy(2x - 3y)$$

Check by expanding back. "Factorise fully" means nothing more can come out: $4x^2 + 8x = 4x(x + 2)$, not $2x(2x + 4)$. A bracket can itself be the common factor: $x(y + 3) - 2(y + 3) = (y + 3)(x - 2)$.

### Expanding two or more brackets

Multiply each term in the first bracket by each term in the second (a grid helps):

$$(2x - 3)(x + 4) = 2x^2 + 8x - 3x - 12 = 2x^2 + 5x - 12$$

Squares: $(x - 5)^2 = (x - 5)(x - 5) = x^2 - 10x + 25$ — **not** $x^2 + 25$.

For **three brackets**, expand two, then multiply the result by the third, one term at a time:

$$(x + 2)(x - 3)(2x + 1) = (x^2 - x - 6)(2x + 1) = 2x^3 + x^2 - 2x^2 - x - 12x - 6 = 2x^3 - x^2 - 13x - 6$$

A quick check: the constant term is $2 \times (-3) \times 1 = -6$, and the $x^3$ coefficient is $1 \times 1 \times 2 = 2$.

### Factorising quadratics

**$x^2 + bx + c$:** find two numbers that **multiply to $c$** and **add to $b$**.

$x^2 - 10x + 21$: $-3 \times -7 = 21$, $-3 + (-7) = -10$, so $(x - 3)(x - 7)$.

**Difference of two squares:** $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$.

$x^2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)$, $\quad 4x^2 - 25y^2 = (2x - 5y)(2x + 5y)$, $\quad 12x^2 - 27 = 3(4x^2 - 9) = 3(2x - 3)(2x + 3)$.

Always take out a common factor first.

**$ax^2 + bx + c$ with $a \ne 1$:** find two numbers that multiply to $ac$ and add to $b$, split the middle term, then factorise in pairs.

$6x^2 - 7x - 20$: $ac = -120$; $-15 \times 8 = -120$ and $-15 + 8 = -7$.

$$6x^2 + 8x - 15x - 20 = 2x(3x + 4) - 5(3x + 4) = (3x + 4)(2x - 5)$$

### Laws of indices in algebra

$$x^a \times x^b = x^{a+b} \qquad x^a \div x^b = x^{a-b} \qquad (x^a)^b = x^{ab} \qquad x^0 = 1 \qquad x^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n}$$

Apply them to numbers and letters separately:

$$\frac{(3x^2y)^3}{9x^4y} = \frac{27x^6y^3}{9x^4y} = 3x^2y^2$$

In $(3x^2y)^3$, the 3 is **cubed** too — $27$, not $9$ or $3$.

### Expressions with surds

Surds follow the same rules as letters, with $\sqrt a \times \sqrt a = a$:

$$(2 + \sqrt3)^2 = 4 + 2\sqrt3 + 2\sqrt3 + 3 = 7 + 4\sqrt3$$

$$(3 + \sqrt2)(1 - \sqrt2) = 3 - 3\sqrt2 + \sqrt2 - 2 = 1 - 2\sqrt2$$

### Algebraic fractions

**Simplifying:** factorise top and bottom completely, then cancel **whole factors** (brackets), never individual terms.

$$\frac{x^2 - 9}{2x^2 + 5x - 3} = \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(2x - 1)(x + 3)} = \frac{x - 3}{2x - 1}$$

**Multiplying and dividing:** factorise everything, flip the second fraction if dividing, then cancel and multiply.

**Adding and subtracting:** use a common denominator, just like $\frac23 - \frac14 = \frac{8 - 3}{12}$.

$$\frac{3}{x + 2} - \frac{2}{x - 1} = \frac{3(x - 1) - 2(x + 2)}{(x + 2)(x - 1)} = \frac{3x - 3 - 2x - 4}{(x + 2)(x - 1)} = \frac{x - 7}{(x + 2)(x - 1)}$$

Bracket the whole second numerator before subtracting: $-2(x + 2) = -2x - 4$. If the denominators share a factor (for example $x - 3$ and $x^2 - 9$), factorise them first and use the **lowest** common denominator — it keeps the numbers small and makes cancelling possible at the end.

::: terms
- **Like terms**: terms with exactly the same letters raised to the same powers, such as 3x² and −5x².
- **Expand**: multiply out brackets so the expression is written as a sum of terms.
- **Factorise**: write an expression as a product of factors, the reverse of expanding.
- **Binomial**: an expression with two terms, such as (2x − 3); expanding two binomials gives a quadratic.
- **Difference of two squares**: the identity a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b).
- **Algebraic fraction**: a fraction whose numerator or denominator contains a letter, such as 3 ÷ (x + 2).
- **Common denominator**: a denominator that each fraction divides into, needed before adding or subtracting fractions.
:::

::: method
1. **Read the command word.** "Expand" → remove brackets; "expand and simplify" → also collect like terms; "factorise fully" → take out the HCF first, then look for a quadratic or difference of two squares inside.
2. **Expanding:** multiply every term by every term, carrying signs. For two brackets expect four terms before simplifying; for three brackets, expand two first and write that line down — it earns M1. Check the constant term and the leading coefficient.
3. **Factorising a quadratic:** HCF first. Then: two terms with a minus and both square → difference of two squares; $x^2 + bx + c$ → product $c$, sum $b$; $ax^2 + bx + c$ → product $ac$, sum $b$, split the middle term, factorise in pairs. **Expand your answer back** to check.
4. **Indices:** deal with the numbers, then each letter in turn. Powers of brackets apply to every factor inside, including the number.
5. **Simplifying algebraic fractions:** factorise numerator and denominator fully, cancel identical brackets, and leave the answer factorised. You can never cancel a single term out of a sum.
6. **Adding/subtracting:** factorise denominators, find the lowest common denominator, multiply each numerator up, put the second numerator in brackets when subtracting, simplify the top, then try to factorise and cancel.
7. **These appear on all three papers.** On Paper 1 there is no calculator check, so the expand-back check matters more. "Show that … can be written as …" questions require every line: examiners want the unsimplified form, the combined form and the final answer.
:::

::: example Three brackets
Expand and simplify $(x - 4)^2(x + 1)$.

$(x - 4)^2 = x^2 - 8x + 16$.

$(x^2 - 8x + 16)(x + 1) = x^3 + x^2 - 8x^2 - 8x + 16x + 16 = x^3 - 7x^2 + 8x + 16$.

Check: constant $(-4)(-4)(1) = 16$. ✓
:::

::: example Factorising fully
Factorise (a) $2x^2y - 8xy^2$ (b) $10x^2 - 11x - 6$ (c) $3x^2 - 75$.

(a) HCF $2xy$: $2xy(x - 4y)$.

(b) $ac = -60$; numbers $4$ and $-15$ (product $-60$, sum $-11$).
$10x^2 + 4x - 15x - 6 = 2x(5x + 2) - 3(5x + 2) = (5x + 2)(2x - 3)$.

(c) $3(x^2 - 25) = 3(x - 5)(x + 5)$.
:::

::: example Simplifying an algebraic fraction
Simplify fully $\dfrac{6x^2 - x - 2}{9x^2 - 4}$.

Top: $ac = -12$; $-4$ and $3$. $6x^2 - 4x + 3x - 2 = 2x(3x - 2) + 1(3x - 2) = (3x - 2)(2x + 1)$.

Bottom: difference of two squares $(3x - 2)(3x + 2)$.

$$\frac{(3x - 2)(2x + 1)}{(3x - 2)(3x + 2)} = \frac{2x + 1}{3x + 2}$$
:::

::: example Dividing algebraic fractions
Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 - x - 6} \div \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 3}$.

Factorise: $\dfrac{(x + 2)(x + 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)} \div \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 3}$.

Flip and multiply: $\dfrac{(x + 2)(x + 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)} \times \dfrac{x - 3}{x + 3}$.

Every bracket cancels: the answer is $1$ (for all $x$ where the fractions are defined).
:::

::: example Subtracting with a shared factor (grade 9)
Write $\dfrac{x}{x - 3} - \dfrac{x + 15}{x^2 - 9}$ as a single fraction in its simplest form.

$x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$, so the lowest common denominator is $(x - 3)(x + 3)$.

$$\frac{x(x + 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} - \frac{(x + 15)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} = \frac{x^2 + 3x - x - 15}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} = \frac{x^2 + 2x - 15}{(x - 3)(x + 3)}$$

Factorise the top: $x^2 + 2x - 15 = (x + 5)(x - 3)$.

$$\frac{(x + 5)(x - 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} = \frac{x + 5}{x + 3}$$
:::

Expand and simplify $5(x + 3) - 2(3x - 4)$.
---
$5x + 15 - 6x + 8 = 23 - x$.
:::

::: q
Expand and simplify (a) $(3x - 2)(2x + 5)$ (b) $(2x - 3)^3$.
---
(a) $6x^2 + 15x - 4x - 10 = 6x^2 + 11x - 10$.
(b) $(2x - 3)^2 = 4x^2 - 12x + 9$; $(4x^2 - 12x + 9)(2x - 3) = 8x^3 - 12x^2 - 24x^2 + 36x + 18x - 27 = 8x^3 - 36x^2 + 54x - 27$.
:::

::: q
Factorise (a) $x^2 + x - 30$ (b) $x^2 - 81$ (c) $3x^2 + 10x - 8$.
---
(a) $6 \times -5 = -30$, $6 - 5 = 1$: $(x + 6)(x - 5)$. (b) $(x - 9)(x + 9)$.
(c) $ac = -24$: $12$ and $-2$. $3x^2 + 12x - 2x - 8 = 3x(x + 4) - 2(x + 4) = (x + 4)(3x - 2)$.
:::

::: q
Simplify $(2x^4y^3)^2 \times 3xy^{-2}$.
---
$(2x^4y^3)^2 = 4x^8y^6$. $\;4x^8y^6 \times 3xy^{-2} = 12x^9y^4$.
:::

::: q
Expand and simplify $(5 - \sqrt3)(2 + \sqrt3)$.
---
$10 + 5\sqrt3 - 2\sqrt3 - 3 = 7 + 3\sqrt3$.
:::

::: q
Simplify fully $\dfrac{2x^2 + 3x - 2}{x^2 - 4}$.
---
$\dfrac{(2x - 1)(x + 2)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} = \dfrac{2x - 1}{x - 2}$.
:::

::: q
Write $\dfrac{4}{x + 3} + \dfrac{3}{2x - 1}$ as a single fraction.
---
$\dfrac{4(2x - 1) + 3(x + 3)}{(x + 3)(2x - 1)} = \dfrac{8x - 4 + 3x + 9}{(x + 3)(2x - 1)} = \dfrac{11x + 5}{(x + 3)(2x - 1)}$.
:::

::: q
Write $\dfrac{2}{x^2 - 1} + \dfrac{1}{x + 1}$ as a single fraction in its simplest form.
---
Common denominator $(x - 1)(x + 1)$: $\dfrac{2 + (x - 1)}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} = \dfrac{x + 1}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} = \dfrac{1}{x - 1}$.
:::

::: q
Factorise fully $12x^2 - 27$ and $x(y + 3) - 2(y + 3)$.
---
$3(4x^2 - 9) = 3(2x - 3)(2x + 3)$. $\quad (y + 3)(x - 2)$ — the bracket is the common factor.
:::

::: watch
- **Sign on the second bracket:** $-2(3x - 4) = -6x + 8$, not $-6x - 8$.
- **$(x - 5)^2 \ne x^2 + 25$.** Write it as two brackets and expand; the middle term $-10x$ is where the marks are.
- **"Fully":** $4x^2 + 8x = 2x(2x + 4)$ is only partly factorised. Look inside the bracket for anything else that comes out, and look for a difference of two squares after taking out the HCF.
- **Cancelling terms, not factors:** $\dfrac{x + 3}{x + 5}$ cannot be simplified to $\dfrac35$. Only whole brackets that multiply can cancel.
- **Forgetting to bracket the numerator** when subtracting fractions: $-(x + 15)$ is $-x - 15$.
- **Power of a product:** $(4x^3)^3 = 64x^9$ — cube the 4 as well as the $x^3$, and multiply the powers, don't add them.
- Leaving an algebraic fraction answer un-factorised when it could cancel loses the final mark.
:::

Show that $(x + 3)(x - 1)(2x + 5)$ can be written in the form $ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d$ where $a$, $b$, $c$ and $d$ are integers. (3)

::: test
Simplify fully $\dfrac{x^2 - 16}{x^2 + x - 12} \div \dfrac{x^2 - 3x - 4}{2x^2 - 5x - 3}$. (4)
---
Factorise: $(x - 4)(x + 4)$, $(x + 4)(x - 3)$, $(x - 4)(x + 1)$, $(2x + 1)(x - 3)$ (M1 for any two, M1 for all four).
$\dfrac{(x - 4)(x + 4)}{(x + 4)(x - 3)} \times \dfrac{(2x + 1)(x - 3)}{(x - 4)(x + 1)}$ (M1) $= \dfrac{2x + 1}{x + 1}$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Show that $\dfrac{2x + 1}{x + 3} - \dfrac{x - 2}{x + 1}$ can be written as $\dfrac{x^2 + 2x + 7}{(x + 3)(x + 1)}$. (4)
---
$\dfrac{(2x + 1)(x + 1) - (x - 2)(x + 3)}{(x + 3)(x + 1)}$ (M1).
$(2x + 1)(x + 1) = 2x^2 + 3x + 1$ and $(x - 2)(x + 3) = x^2 + x - 6$ (M1).
$2x^2 + 3x + 1 - x^2 - x + 6$ (M1) $= x^2 + 2x + 7$, giving $\dfrac{x^2 + 2x + 7}{(x + 3)(x + 1)}$ (A1).
:::
