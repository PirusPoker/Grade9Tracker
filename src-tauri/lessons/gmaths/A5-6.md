## What it is

Two skills that both come down to **doing the same thing to both sides** and **writing a convincing chain of algebra**. Rearranging a formula means solving for a letter instead of a number. Proof means showing, with algebra rather than examples, that something is true for every value.

### Standard formulae

You should know common formulae without looking them up: area of a circle $A = \pi r^2$, circumference $C = 2\pi r$ (or $\pi d$), Pythagoras $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, area of a trapezium $A = \frac12(a + b)h$, speed $= \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$, density $= \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$, and the equations of motion $v = u + at$, $s = ut + \frac12 at^2$, $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$. "Use the formula" questions give a formula and expect you to substitute or rearrange it.

### Changing the subject

The **subject** is the letter on its own on one side: in $v = u + at$, $v$ is the subject. To make another letter the subject, undo what has been done to it in **reverse order**, doing the same to both sides — exactly as when solving an equation.

**Make $t$ the subject of $v = u + at$.**
$t$ has been multiplied by $a$, then had $u$ added. Undo in reverse: subtract $u$, then divide by $a$.

$$v - u = at \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = \frac{v - u}{a}$$

**Fractions:** multiply out first. Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{3x - 2}{5}$: $5y = 3x - 2$, $5y + 2 = 3x$, $x = \dfrac{5y + 2}{3}$.

**Powers and roots:** the inverse of squaring is square-rooting (and vice versa).

$V = \frac13\pi r^2h$: $\;3V = \pi r^2 h$, $\;r^2 = \dfrac{3V}{\pi h}$, $\;r = \sqrt{\dfrac{3V}{\pi h}}$.

$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$: divide by $2\pi$, then square: $\dfrac{T^2}{4\pi^2} = \dfrac{L}{g}$, so $L = \dfrac{gT^2}{4\pi^2}$.

Square both sides only once the root is **on its own**. When you square-root to find a length, take the positive root; in general algebra write $\pm$.

### When the subject appears twice (Higher)

If the new subject appears in more than one term, you cannot "undo" it directly. Instead:

1. clear any fractions (multiply both sides by the denominator);
2. expand any brackets;
3. collect every term containing the subject on **one side** and everything else on the other;
4. **factorise** the subject out;
5. divide by the bracket.

**Make $a$ the subject of $3a + b = ac - 4$.**

$$3a - ac = -4 - b \quad\Rightarrow\quad a(3 - c) = -4 - b \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = \frac{-4 - b}{3 - c} = \frac{b + 4}{c - 3}$$

(The last step multiplies top and bottom by $-1$; either form scores full marks.)

**Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}$.**

$$y(x - 3) = 2x + 1 \Rightarrow xy - 3y = 2x + 1 \Rightarrow xy - 2x = 3y + 1 \Rightarrow x(y - 2) = 3y + 1 \Rightarrow x = \frac{3y + 1}{y - 2}$$

This is exactly the method for finding an **inverse function** in A7.

### Equations and identities

An **equation** is true only for some values ($2x + 1 = 7$ only when $x = 3$). An **identity** is true for all values and uses $\equiv$: $(x + 5)^2 - (x + 1)^2 \equiv 8(x + 3)$.

To **show an identity**, start with one side (usually the more complicated) and simplify until it becomes the other side:

$$(x + 5)^2 - (x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 10x + 25 - x^2 - 2x - 1 = 8x + 24 = 8(x + 3)$$

Do not work on both sides at once as if solving an equation, and do not "check with a number" — that is not a proof.

### Algebraic proof

A proof must work for **every** case, so it uses letters. Standard building blocks, where $n$ is any integer:

| Idea | Algebra |
|---|---|
| any integer | $n$ |
| consecutive integers | $n,\ n + 1,\ n + 2$ |
| an even number | $2n$ |
| an odd number | $2n + 1$ (or $2n - 1$) |
| consecutive even / odd numbers | $2n,\ 2n + 2$ / $2n + 1,\ 2n + 3$ |
| a multiple of 5 | $5n$ |
| two different odd numbers | $2m + 1,\ 2n + 1$ (different letters!) |

Then simplify and **factorise to show the property**:

- A multiple of 8: the expression can be written as $8 \times (\text{an integer})$.
- Even: $2 \times (\text{integer})$. Odd: $2 \times (\text{integer}) + 1$.
- "One less than a multiple of 4": $4 \times (\text{integer}) - 1$.
- Always positive: complete the square, e.g. $n^2 - 4n + 7 = (n - 2)^2 + 3$, and a square is never negative, so the expression is at least 3.

**Prove that the difference between the squares of two consecutive odd numbers is a multiple of 8.**

$$(2n + 1)^2 - (2n - 1)^2 = (4n^2 + 4n + 1) - (4n^2 - 4n + 1) = 8n$$

$8n = 8 \times n$ and $n$ is an integer, so the difference is a multiple of 8.

**Disproving** a statement needs just **one counter-example**. "$n^2 + n + 41$ is prime for every positive integer $n$" is false: when $n = 40$, $1600 + 40 + 41 = 1681 = 41^2$.

### Arguing mathematically

Proof questions are marked on communication: define your letters ("let $n$ be an integer"), show every line, and finish with a **concluding sentence** that states what you have proved and why ("… $= 3(n + 1)$, which is a multiple of 3"). The final mark often depends on that sentence.

::: terms
- **Subject of a formula**: the single letter on its own on one side of the formula, such as v in v = u + at.
- **Rearrange**: change the subject by doing the same inverse operation to both sides, step by step.
- **Identity**: a statement true for every value of its letters, written with ≡ and proved with algebra.
- **Algebraic proof**: a chain of algebra using general letters, such as 2n + 1 for any odd number, that shows a result holds in every case.
- **Counter-example**: one specific case where a statement fails, which is enough to disprove it.
- **Consecutive**: following one after another, such as n, n + 1, n + 2 or the odd numbers 2n + 1, 2n + 3.
:::

::: method
1. **Rearranging:** write each step on a new line and do **one** operation to both sides each time — the M marks are for correct steps.
2. **Order of undoing:** remove fractions (multiply by the denominator), expand brackets, isolate the term with the subject, then undo roots or powers last. Square both sides only when the square root is alone on one side.
3. **Subject appears twice:** gather all subject terms on one side, **factorise** the subject out, divide by the bracket. This is the grade 7–9 skill and is often worth 4 marks.
4. **Showing an identity:** start from one side, expand and simplify until it matches the other. Never substitute numbers as your "proof".
5. **Proof:** define the letters ($n$ is an integer; odd $= 2n + 1$; use **different letters** for numbers that need not be related). Expand fully, collect terms, then factorise to reveal the property: $8n$, $2(\ldots)$, $2(\ldots) + 1$, $4(\ldots) - 1$.
6. **Conclude in words:** "$= 8n$, which is a multiple of 8 because $n$ is an integer." Without this sentence the final A1 is often withheld.
7. **Disproving:** one counter-example, with the arithmetic shown and a statement that it fails.
8. These questions appear on calculator and non-calculator papers alike; a calculator gives no help, so neat algebra matters more than speed.
:::

::: example Rearranging a trapezium formula
Make $b$ the subject of $A = \dfrac{(a + b)h}{2}$.

$$2A = (a + b)h \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{2A}{h} = a + b \quad\Rightarrow\quad b = \frac{2A}{h} - a$$

(Also correct: $b = \dfrac{2A - ah}{h}$.)
:::

::: example Subject in two terms, with fractions (grade 8)
The lens formula is $\dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f}$. Make $u$ the subject.

Multiply every term by $uvf$: $\;vf + uf = uv$.

Gather the $u$ terms: $\;vf = uv - uf$.

Factorise: $\;vf = u(v - f)$.

Divide: $\;u = \dfrac{vf}{v - f}$.
:::

::: example Proving a result is odd
Prove that the sum of the squares of any two consecutive integers is odd.

Let the integers be $n$ and $n + 1$.

$$n^2 + (n + 1)^2 = n^2 + n^2 + 2n + 1 = 2n^2 + 2n + 1 = 2(n^2 + n) + 1$$

$n^2 + n$ is an integer, so $2(n^2 + n)$ is even, and one more than an even number is odd.
:::

::: example Proving a multiple
Prove that $(3n + 1)^2 - (3n - 1)^2$ is a multiple of 12 for all positive integer values of $n$.

$$(9n^2 + 6n + 1) - (9n^2 - 6n + 1) = 12n$$

$12n = 12 \times n$ and $n$ is an integer, so the expression is a multiple of 12.
:::

::: example Proving always positive (grade 9)
Prove that $n^2 - 4n + 7 > 0$ for all values of $n$.

Complete the square: $n^2 - 4n + 7 = (n - 2)^2 - 4 + 7 = (n - 2)^2 + 3$.

$(n - 2)^2 \ge 0$ for every value of $n$, because a square is never negative. So $(n - 2)^2 + 3 \ge 3 > 0$.

The expression is always positive (its least value is 3, when $n = 2$).
:::

::: q
Make $a$ the subject of $v = u + at$.
---
$v - u = at \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$.
:::

::: q
Make $v$ the subject of $E = \frac12 mv^2$, where $v > 0$.
---
$2E = mv^2 \Rightarrow v^2 = \dfrac{2E}{m} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2E}{m}}$.
:::

::: q
Make $a$ the subject of $s = ut + \frac12 at^2$.
---
$s - ut = \frac12 at^2 \Rightarrow 2(s - ut) = at^2 \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{2(s - ut)}{t^2}$.
:::

::: q
Make $x$ the subject of $5(x - y) = 2x + 7$.
---
$5x - 5y = 2x + 7 \Rightarrow 3x = 5y + 7 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{5y + 7}{3}$.
:::

::: q
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \sqrt{\dfrac{x + 4}{3}}$.
---
Square: $y^2 = \dfrac{x + 4}{3}$. $\;3y^2 = x + 4$. $\;x = 3y^2 - 4$.
:::

::: q
Make $x$ the subject of $ax + 3 = bx - c$.
---
$ax - bx = -c - 3 \Rightarrow x(a - b) = -c - 3 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{-c - 3}{a - b} = \dfrac{c + 3}{b - a}$.
:::

::: q
Show that $(x + 5)^2 - (x + 1)^2 \equiv 8(x + 3)$.
---
LHS $= x^2 + 10x + 25 - (x^2 + 2x + 1) = 8x + 24 = 8(x + 3) =$ RHS.
:::

::: q
Prove that the difference between the squares of any two consecutive integers is equal to their sum.
---
$(n + 1)^2 - n^2 = n^2 + 2n + 1 - n^2 = 2n + 1$, and the sum is $n + (n + 1) = 2n + 1$. They are equal.
:::

::: q
Show that "$2^n - 1$ is prime for every positive integer $n$" is false.
---
Counter-example: $n = 4$ gives $2^4 - 1 = 15 = 3 \times 5$, which is not prime. (Also $n = 1$ gives 1, not prime.)
:::

::: watch
- **Subject on both sides:** candidates who never factorise end up with $x$ on both sides of their "answer" — that scores only the first method mark.
- **Square rooting too early:** in $y = \sqrt{\dfrac{x + 4}{3}}$ you must square the whole of both sides; you cannot square-root or square term by term.
- **Dividing only one term:** $\dfrac{2A}{h} - a$ is not $\dfrac{2A - a}{h}$.
- **Proof by examples:** showing a result works for $n = 1, 2, 3$ scores 0 — use $n$.
- **Same letter for unrelated numbers:** "two odd numbers" are $2m + 1$ and $2n + 1$; writing $2n + 1$ twice proves it only for two equal numbers.
- **No conclusion:** "$= 8n$" on its own often loses the final mark. Say "which is a multiple of 8 since $n$ is an integer".
- **Bracketing the subtraction:** $(2n + 1)^2 - (2n - 1)^2$ — the second square must be subtracted in full, so $-1$ and $+1$ cancel.
:::

::: test
Leah says "$n^2 + n + 41$ is a prime number for every positive integer $n$." Show that Leah is wrong. (2)
---
Take $n = 40$ (or $n = 41$): $40^2 + 40 + 41 = 1681$ (M1). $1681 = 41 \times 41$, so it is not prime (A1).
:::

::: test
Prove that the sum of any three consecutive integers is a multiple of 3. (3)
---
Let the integers be $n$, $n + 1$, $n + 2$ (M1). Sum $= 3n + 3$ (M1) $= 3(n + 1)$, which is a multiple of 3 because $n + 1$ is an integer (A1).
:::

::: test
Make $x$ the subject of $w = \sqrt{\dfrac{x - a}{b}}$. (3)
---
$w^2 = \dfrac{x - a}{b}$ (M1). $bw^2 = x - a$ (M1). $x = bw^2 + a$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Show that $(x + 5)^2 - (x - 1)^2 \equiv 12(x + 2)$. (3)
---
$(x^2 + 10x + 25) - (x^2 - 2x + 1)$ (M1 for both expansions) $= 12x + 24$ (M1) $= 12(x + 2)$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Make $x$ the subject of $3x + a = bx + 7$. (3)
---
$3x - bx = 7 - a$ (M1). $x(3 - b) = 7 - a$ (M1). $x = \dfrac{7 - a}{3 - b}$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \dfrac{3x - 2}{x + 4}$. (4)
---
$y(x + 4) = 3x - 2$ (M1). $xy + 4y = 3x - 2$, so $xy - 3x = -2 - 4y$ (M1). $x(y - 3) = -2 - 4y$ (M1). $x = \dfrac{-2 - 4y}{y - 3} = \dfrac{4y + 2}{3 - y}$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Prove that $(2n + 3)^2 - (2n - 3)^2$ is a multiple of 24 for all positive integers $n$. (3)
---
$(4n^2 + 12n + 9) - (4n^2 - 12n + 9)$ (M1) $= 24n$ (M1). $24n = 24 \times n$, a multiple of 24 as $n$ is an integer (A1).
:::

::: test
Prove that the product of two consecutive odd numbers is always one less than a multiple of 4. (4)
---
Odd numbers $2n - 1$ and $2n + 1$ (M1). Product $= 4n^2 - 1$ (M1, A1). $4n^2$ is $4 \times n^2$, a multiple of 4, so the product is one less than a multiple of 4 (A1).
:::

::: test
Prove that the sum of the squares of any two consecutive odd numbers is 2 more than a multiple of 8. (4)
---
$(2n - 1)^2 + (2n + 1)^2$ (M1) $= 4n^2 - 4n + 1 + 4n^2 + 4n + 1$ (M1) $= 8n^2 + 2$ (A1). $8n^2$ is a multiple of 8 since $n^2$ is an integer, so the sum is 2 more than a multiple of 8 (A1).
:::
