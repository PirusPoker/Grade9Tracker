## What it is

A **vector** has both size (magnitude) and direction. At GCSE you meet vectors in two ways: as **column vectors** describing movements on a grid, and as **letters** ($\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$) on a diagram, used to prove geometric facts such as "these lines are parallel" or "these three points lie on a straight line". Vector proof is one of the most reliable grade 8–9 questions on the Higher papers.

### Column vectors and translations (G24)

The column vector $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ means move $x$ right and $y$ up (negative $x$ is left, negative $y$ is down). A **translation** is described exactly by a column vector.

*Mini-example.* The translation taking $A(2, 5)$ to $A'(-1, 9)$ is $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$. The reverse translation is $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$.

In print a vector is bold ($\mathbf{a}$); by hand write it underlined ($\underline{a}$). $\overrightarrow{AB}$ means the vector from $A$ to $B$, and $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB}$ (same length, opposite direction). The **magnitude** (length) of $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ by Pythagoras, so $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$ has magnitude 5.

### Arithmetic with vectors (G25)

With column vectors, work component by component:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \qquad 3\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} \qquad \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}$$

On a diagram:
- **Adding** vectors means following one then the other ("nose to tail"). The result is the direct route: $\overrightarrow{AB} + \overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{AC}$.
- **Subtracting** means adding the reverse: $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a} + (-\mathbf{b})$.
- **Multiplying by a scalar** $k$ stretches the vector by factor $k$; a negative $k$ also reverses it. $2\mathbf{a}$ is parallel to $\mathbf{a}$ and twice as long; $-\frac12\mathbf{a}$ is parallel, half as long and in the opposite direction.

Vector algebra follows normal algebra rules: $2(\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}) - \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{a} + 6\mathbf{b}$.

### Finding a route

To write $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ in terms of given vectors, find **any** route from $P$ to $Q$ along vectors you know, adding them and reversing signs when you travel against an arrow.

*Mini-example.* $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. Then
$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{AO} + \overrightarrow{OB} = -\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}.$$
This "end minus start" result is used in almost every question.

**Midpoints and ratios.** If $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, then $\overrightarrow{AM} = \frac12\overrightarrow{AB}$. If $P$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $m : n$, then $\overrightarrow{AP} = \dfrac{m}{m + n}\overrightarrow{AB}$ — the ratio $2 : 3$ means $P$ is $\frac25$ of the way from $A$.

*Mini-example.* $P$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $1 : 3$. Then $\overrightarrow{OP} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \frac14\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a} + \frac14(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \frac34\mathbf{a} + \frac14\mathbf{b}$.

In regular shapes, equal and parallel sides are the **same vector**: in a parallelogram $OABC$ with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$, also $\overrightarrow{CB} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{c}$. In a regular hexagon the long diagonal through the centre is twice a side.

### Vector proof

**Parallel:** two vectors are parallel if one is a scalar multiple of the other. $\overrightarrow{PQ} = 2\mathbf{a} - 4\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{RS} = \mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b}$ are parallel because $\overrightarrow{PQ} = 2\overrightarrow{RS}$ — and $PQ$ is twice as long as $RS$.

**Collinear (on one straight line):** $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear if $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}$ (or $\overrightarrow{AC}$) are parallel **and** share a point. For example, if $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = 3\mathbf{a} + 6\mathbf{b} = 3\overrightarrow{AB}$, then $AB$ and $AC$ are parallel and both pass through $A$, so $A$, $B$, $C$ are collinear. The multiple also gives the ratio: $AB : BC = 1 : 2$.

**Unknown scalars (grade 9).** If two expressions for the same vector are equal, and $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel, the coefficients of $\mathbf{a}$ must match and the coefficients of $\mathbf{b}$ must match. This lets you find where two lines cross, or the value of $k$ that makes points collinear.

::: terms
- **Vector**: a quantity with both size and direction, written in bold (a) in print or underlined by hand.
- **Column vector**: a vector written as a column of two numbers — the move right and the move up.
- **Scalar**: an ordinary number that multiplies a vector, changing its length and possibly reversing it.
- **Resultant**: the single vector equal to following two or more vectors in turn, nose to tail.
- **Parallel vectors**: vectors where one is a scalar multiple of the other, such as 2a − b and 6a − 3b.
- **Collinear**: lying on the same straight line — shown by parallel vectors that share a point.
- **Magnitude**: the length of a vector; for a column vector with parts x and y it is √(x² + y²).
:::

::: method
1. **Copy the key vectors onto a sketch** and mark arrows. Note any midpoints and ratios as fractions of whole lines ($AP : PB = 2 : 3$ means $\overrightarrow{AP} = \frac25\overrightarrow{AB}$).
2. **Build each vector you need by a route** through points you know: add vectors along arrows, subtract against arrows. Write the route first, e.g. $\overrightarrow{MN} = \overrightarrow{MA} + \overrightarrow{AO} + \overrightarrow{ON}$ — this line gets the method mark.
3. **Simplify** by collecting $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ terms, then factorise to reveal a multiple: $3\mathbf{a} - 6\mathbf{b} = 3(\mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b})$.
4. **To prove parallel:** show one vector equals a number times the other, and say so in words: "$\overrightarrow{PQ} = 3\overrightarrow{RS}$, so $PQ$ is parallel to $RS$".
5. **To prove collinear:** show two vectors are multiples **and** share a point, and state both: "parallel with the common point $B$, so $A$, $B$, $C$ lie on a straight line".
6. **Ratios of lengths:** a multiple gives the ratio — if $\overrightarrow{AC} = 3\overrightarrow{AB}$ then $AB : BC = 1 : 2$.
7. **Unknown scalars:** write the same vector two ways, then equate the coefficients of $\mathbf{a}$ and of $\mathbf{b}$ and solve the simultaneous equations.
8. Vectors appear on both calculator and non-calculator papers; they rarely need a calculator, so keep fractions exact.
:::

::: example Column vectors
$\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $2\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b}$ and the magnitude of $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$.

$2\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ -16 \end{pmatrix}$.

$\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$, magnitude $= \sqrt{4 + 4} = \sqrt8 = 2\sqrt2$.
:::

::: example A point dividing a line in a ratio
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $P$ lies on $AB$ with $AP : PB = 2 : 1$. Find $\overrightarrow{OP}$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ and $P$ is $\frac23$ of the way along it.

$\overrightarrow{OP} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \frac23\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a} + \frac23(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \frac13\mathbf{a} + \frac23\mathbf{b}$.
:::

::: example Proving lines parallel (the midpoint theorem)
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $P$ is the midpoint of $OA$ and $Q$ is the midpoint of $OB$. Prove that $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$ and half its length.

$\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{PO} + \overrightarrow{OQ} = -\frac12\mathbf{a} + \frac12\mathbf{b} = \frac12(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \frac12\overrightarrow{AB}$. Therefore $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$ and half its length.
:::

::: example Proving points collinear
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = 6\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = 6\mathbf{b}$. $P$ is the midpoint of $OA$. $Q$ lies on $AB$ with $AQ : QB = 2 : 1$. $R$ is the point such that $B$ is the midpoint of $OR$. Prove that $P$, $Q$ and $R$ lie on a straight line, and find $PQ : QR$.

$\overrightarrow{OP} = 3\mathbf{a}$. $\overrightarrow{AQ} = \frac23(6\mathbf{b} - 6\mathbf{a}) = 4\mathbf{b} - 4\mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{OQ} = 6\mathbf{a} + 4\mathbf{b} - 4\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{a} + 4\mathbf{b}$. $\overrightarrow{OR} = 12\mathbf{b}$.

$\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{OQ} - \overrightarrow{OP} = -\mathbf{a} + 4\mathbf{b}$.
$\overrightarrow{PR} = \overrightarrow{OR} - \overrightarrow{OP} = -3\mathbf{a} + 12\mathbf{b} = 3(-\mathbf{a} + 4\mathbf{b}) = 3\overrightarrow{PQ}$.

$PR$ is parallel to $PQ$ and they share the point $P$, so $P$, $Q$, $R$ are collinear. $PR = 3PQ$, so $PQ : QR = 1 : 2$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: where two lines cross
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. The lines $OM$ and $AC$ meet at $X$. Find the ratio $OX : XM$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{c}$, so $\overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf{a} + \frac12\mathbf{c}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}$.

$X$ is on $OM$: $\overrightarrow{OX} = \lambda\left(\mathbf{a} + \frac12\mathbf{c}\right)$. $X$ is on $AC$: $\overrightarrow{OX} = \mathbf{a} + \mu(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}) = (1 - \mu)\mathbf{a} + \mu\mathbf{c}$.

Equate coefficients: $\lambda = 1 - \mu$ and $\frac12\lambda = \mu$. So $\lambda = 1 - \frac12\lambda$, giving $\lambda = \frac23$.

$\overrightarrow{OX} = \frac23\overrightarrow{OM}$, so $OX : XM = 2 : 1$.
:::

::: q
$\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$.
---
$3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 15 - 4 \\ 3 + 6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 11 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix}$. $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$.
:::

::: q
Find $k$ and $m$ such that $k\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ m \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$.
---
Top: $2k + 1 = 7 \Rightarrow k = 3$. Bottom: $9 + m = 5 \Rightarrow m = -4$.
:::

::: q
Find the magnitude of $\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix}$.
---
$\sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.
:::

::: q
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $\overrightarrow{OM}$.
---
$\overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf{a} + \frac12(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \frac12\mathbf{a} + \frac12\mathbf{b}$.
:::

::: q
Is $4\mathbf{a} - 6\mathbf{b}$ parallel to $-2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$? Explain.
---
Yes: $4\mathbf{a} - 6\mathbf{b} = -2(-2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b})$, a scalar multiple. (They point in opposite directions and one is twice as long.)
:::

::: q
$ABCDEF$ is a regular hexagon with centre $O$. $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\overrightarrow{OC}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}$.
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$. $OABC$ is a rhombus, so $\overrightarrow{OC} = \overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$. $D$ is opposite $A$ through $O$, so $\overrightarrow{AD} = -2\mathbf{a}$.
:::

::: q
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. $N$ lies on $CB$ with $CN : NB = 1 : 3$. Find $\overrightarrow{ON}$.
---
$\overrightarrow{CB} = \mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{CN} = \frac14\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{ON} = \mathbf{c} + \frac14\mathbf{a}$.
:::

::: q
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = 3\mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b}$. Show that $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear.
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$. $\overrightarrow{AC} = 2\mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b} = -2(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = -2\overrightarrow{AB}$. Parallel with the common point $A$, so collinear.
:::

::: q
$\overrightarrow{OP} = 2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$, $\overrightarrow{OQ} = \mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{OR} = k\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b}$. $P$, $Q$ and $R$ are collinear. Find $k$.
---
$\overrightarrow{PQ} = -\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}$. $\overrightarrow{PR} = (k - 2)\mathbf{a} - 4\mathbf{b}$ must be a multiple of $\overrightarrow{PQ}$: the $\mathbf{b}$ part gives the multiple $-2$, so $k - 2 = 2$ and $k = 4$.
:::

::: watch
- **Wrong direction:** $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ ("end minus start"), not $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$. Going against an arrow changes the sign.
- **Ratios as fractions:** $AP : PB = 2 : 3$ means $\frac25$ of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, not $\frac23$.
- **Incomplete proofs.** "Parallel" needs the words "is a multiple of"; "collinear" needs parallel **and** a common point, both stated.
- **Not factorising:** $3\mathbf{a} - 6\mathbf{b}$ only reveals itself as $3(\mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b})$ when you take the factor out.
- **Column vector signs:** subtracting a negative component, e.g. $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix}$.
- **Leaving the answer unsimplified:** $\mathbf{a} + \frac23(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$ usually needs to become $\frac13\mathbf{a} + \frac23\mathbf{b}$ for the final mark.
:::

::: test
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $P$ lies on $AB$ with $AP : PB = 1 : 3$. Find $\overrightarrow{OP}$ in its simplest form. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ (M1); $\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{a} + \frac14(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$ (M1) $= \frac34\mathbf{a} + \frac14\mathbf{b}$ (A1).

::: test
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. $P$ is the midpoint of $OA$ and $Q$ is the midpoint of $CB$. Prove that $PQ$ is parallel to $OC$ and equal to it in length. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{OQ} = \mathbf{c} + \frac12\mathbf{a}$ (M1). $\overrightarrow{PQ} = -\frac12\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c} + \frac12\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{c}$ (A1). $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{OC}$, so $PQ$ is parallel to $OC$ and the same length (A1).
:::

::: test
$\overrightarrow{OA} = 2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = 4\mathbf{a} + 7\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = k\mathbf{a} + 13\mathbf{b}$. $A$, $B$ and $C$ lie on a straight line. Find $k$. (3)
---
$\overrightarrow{AB} = 2\mathbf{a} + 4\mathbf{b}$ (M1). $\overrightarrow{AC} = (k - 2)\mathbf{a} + 10\mathbf{b} = 2.5\overrightarrow{AB}$ (M1), so $k - 2 = 5$, $k = 7$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. $D$ is the point such that $A$ is the midpoint of $OD$. $N$ lies on $OB$ with $ON : NB = 2 : 1$. Prove that $C$, $N$ and $D$ lie on a straight line. (5)
---
$\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}$, so $\overrightarrow{ON} = \frac23(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c})$ (M1). $\overrightarrow{CN} = \frac23\mathbf{a} + \frac23\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{c} = \frac23\mathbf{a} - \frac13\mathbf{c} = \frac13(2\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{c})$ (M1 A1). $\overrightarrow{CD} = 2\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{c}$ (B1). $\overrightarrow{CD} = 3\overrightarrow{CN}$, parallel with common point $C$, so $C$, $N$, $D$ are collinear (A1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $OAB$, $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $P$ lies on $OA$ with $OP : PA = 1 : 2$, and $Q$ is the midpoint of $AB$. The lines $BP$ and $OQ$ meet at $X$. Find the ratio $OX : XQ$. (6)
---
$\overrightarrow{OQ} = \frac12\mathbf{a} + \frac12\mathbf{b}$ (B1). On $OQ$: $\overrightarrow{OX} = \lambda\left(\frac12\mathbf{a} + \frac12\mathbf{b}\right)$ (M1). On $BP$: $\overrightarrow{OX} = \mathbf{b} + \mu\left(\frac13\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\right) = \frac{\mu}{3}\mathbf{a} + (1 - \mu)\mathbf{b}$ (M1). Equate: $\frac{\lambda}{2} = \frac{\mu}{3}$ and $\frac{\lambda}{2} = 1 - \mu$ (M1), so $\frac{\mu}{3} = 1 - \mu$, $\mu = \frac34$, $\lambda = \frac12$ (A1). $\overrightarrow{OX} = \frac12\overrightarrow{OQ}$, so $OX : XQ = 1 : 1$ (A1).
:::
