## What it is

A **percentage** is a number of parts per hundred: $37\%$ means $\dfrac{37}{100}$, which is $0.37$. Every percentage question is really a fraction or decimal question, and the fastest way through almost all of them is a **multiplier** — the single decimal you multiply by.

### Percentages as fractions, decimals and multipliers

| Percentage | Fraction | Decimal |
|---|---|---|
| $12.5\%$ | $\dfrac{1}{8}$ | $0.125$ |
| $0.4\%$ | $\dfrac{1}{250}$ | $0.004$ |
| $150\%$ | $\dfrac{3}{2}$ | $1.5$ |

**Percentages greater than 100%** are perfectly normal: $250\%$ of $40$ is $2.5 \times 40 = 100$. "This year's crop is $140\%$ of last year's" means it is $1.4$ times as big — a $40\%$ increase.

**Finding a percentage of an amount.** On a calculator paper: $17.5\%$ of $£360 = 0.175 \times 360 = £63$. On the non-calculator paper, build it from $10\%$, $5\%$ and $1\%$: $10\% = 36$, $5\% = 18$, $2.5\% = 9$, so $17.5\% = 36 + 18 + 9 = £63$.

### Percentage increase and decrease with multipliers

Increasing by $r\%$ leaves you with $(100 + r)\%$ of the original; decreasing by $r\%$ leaves $(100 - r)\%$.

- Increase $£240$ by $15\%$: multiplier $1.15$, so $240 \times 1.15 = £276$.
- Decrease $68$ kg by $35\%$: multiplier $0.65$, so $68 \times 0.65 = 44.2$ kg.

Multipliers **combine by multiplying**, which is how you handle successive changes: a $20\%$ rise followed by a $20\%$ fall is $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$ — an overall $4\%$ **decrease**, not "back to the start". (Repeating the same change many times, as in compound interest, is the next topic.)

### One quantity as a percentage of another, and comparing

To write $A$ as a percentage of $B$: $\dfrac{A}{B} \times 100$, with **both in the same units**. $18$ minutes as a percentage of $2$ hours is $\dfrac{18}{120} \times 100 = 15\%$.

Percentages let you compare things with different totals: $34$ out of $40$ is $85\%$; $52$ out of $60$ is $86.7\%$ — the second is the better score even though it "lost" more marks.

### Percentage change

$$\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{change}}{\text{original}} \times 100$$

A price rising from $£48$ to $£57.60$ changes by $£9.60$; $\dfrac{9.60}{48} \times 100 = 20\%$ increase. Profit and loss work the same way, with the **cost price** as the original. The denominator is always the **original** value, never the new one.

### Reverse percentages (finding the original value)

If an amount has already been changed, divide by the multiplier to undo it. After a $20\%$ reduction a coat costs $£84$: the sale price is $80\%$ of the original, so the original is $84 \div 0.8 = £105$.

The classic wrong answer is to take $20\%$ of $£84$ and add it back ($£100.80$) — $20\%$ of the **new** price is not $20\%$ of the old one.

### Simple interest

With **simple interest** the same amount of interest is paid every year, because it is always worked out on the original sum (the **principal**):

$$\text{interest} = \text{principal} \times \frac{\text{rate}}{100} \times \text{number of years}$$

$£3500$ at $2.4\%$ per year simple interest for $5$ years earns $3500 \times 0.024 \times 5 = £420$, so the account holds $£3920$. (Compound interest, where interest earns interest, is covered with growth and decay.)

::: terms
- **Percentage**: a number of parts per hundred, so 23% = 23/100 = 0.23.
- **Multiplier**: the decimal you multiply by to apply a percentage change, e.g. 1.15 for a 15% increase or 0.85 for a 15% decrease.
- **Percentage change**: the change divided by the original value, multiplied by 100.
- **Reverse percentage**: finding the original value by dividing the new value by the multiplier.
- **Simple interest**: interest worked out on the original principal only, so the same amount is added each year.
- **Principal**: the amount of money first invested or borrowed.
- **Percentage profit**: profit as a percentage of the cost price, not the selling price.
:::

::: method
1. **Decide which type of question it is.** Percentage *of* an amount; increase/decrease; percentage *change* (two values given); or *original value* (the change has already happened — words like "after", "sale price", "including VAT", "now").
2. **Write the multiplier down.** Increase by $r\%$: $1 + \frac{r}{100}$. Decrease by $r\%$: $1 - \frac{r}{100}$. Successive changes: multiply the multipliers.
3. **Percentage change:** change $\div$ **original** $\times 100$. Say "increase" or "decrease" in the answer.
4. **Original value:** new value $\div$ multiplier. Check by applying the change forwards to your answer.
5. **Simple interest:** interest for one year $\times$ number of years; add the principal only if the question asks for the total.
6. **Non-calculator paper (Paper 1):** build percentages from $10\%$, $5\%$ and $1\%$, and show each line — each correct part earns a method mark even if the final addition slips.
7. **Calculator papers:** write the calculation you type (e.g. $61.60 \div 0.7$) before the answer — that line is the M1. Round money to 2 decimal places at the **end**, and give percentages to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
8. **Comparisons** ("which is the better score/deal?") need both values worked out *and* a conclusion sentence — the final mark is for the decision.
:::

::: example Percentage of an amount without a calculator
Work out $35\%$ of $£260$ and $2.5\%$ of $£260$.

$10\% = 26$, so $30\% = 78$; $5\% = 13$. $35\% = 78 + 13 = £91$.

$2.5\%$ is half of $5\%$: $13 \div 2 = £6.50$.
:::

::: example Percentage change and profit
A dealer buys a car for £8400 and sells it for £9870. Work out the percentage profit.

Profit $= 9870 - 8400 = £1470$. Percentage profit $= \dfrac{1470}{8400} \times 100 = 17.5\%$.

The denominator is the **cost price** (the original), not £9870.
:::

::: example Original value
A TV costs £474 including VAT at $20\%$. A sofa, reduced by $35\%$ in a sale, now costs £643.50. Find the prices before VAT and before the sale.

TV: $474 = 1.2 \times \text{price}$, so price $= 474 \div 1.2 = £395$.

Sofa: multiplier $0.65$, so original $= 643.50 \div 0.65 = £990$.

Check: $990 \times 0.65 = 643.5$. ✓
:::

::: example Simple interest backwards
Priya invests £6000 at $x\%$ per year simple interest. After 4 years the investment is worth £6780. Find $x$.

Interest $= 6780 - 6000 = £780$ over 4 years, so $£195$ per year.

$x = \dfrac{195}{6000} \times 100 = 3.25$.
:::

::: example Successive changes, reversed (grade 7–8)
The value of a car fell by $12\%$ in its first year and by $9\%$ in its second year. It is now worth £12 012. Work out its value when new, and the overall percentage decrease.

Overall multiplier $= 0.88 \times 0.91 = 0.8008$.

Value when new $= 12\,012 \div 0.8008 = £15\,000$.

Overall decrease: $1 - 0.8008 = 0.1992$, so $19.92\%$ — not $12 + 9 = 21\%$.
:::

::: example Percentage algebra (grade 9)
$x$ is increased by $30\%$ to give $y$. Then $y$ is decreased by $p\%$ to give $x$ again. Find $p$ to 1 decimal place.

$y = 1.3x$ and $x = y\left(1 - \dfrac{p}{100}\right)$, so $1 - \dfrac{p}{100} = \dfrac{x}{y} = \dfrac{1}{1.3}$.

$\dfrac{p}{100} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{1.3} = \dfrac{0.3}{1.3} = 0.23077\ldots$, so $p = 23.1$.

A $30\%$ rise is undone by a fall of only about $23\%$, because the fall is taken from the larger amount.
:::

---
$0.0035$, $0.075$ and $1.45$ (divide each by 100).

Express 18 minutes as a percentage of 2 hours.
---
Same units: $\dfrac{18}{120} \times 100 = 15\%$.

::: q
Decrease 3.2 kg by $17.5\%$.
---
Multiplier $0.825$: $3.2 \times 0.825 = 2.64$ kg.
:::

::: q
A house bought for £180 000 is sold for £207 000. Work out the percentage profit.
---
Profit $£27\,000$; $\dfrac{27\,000}{180\,000} \times 100 = 15\%$.
:::

::: q
After a $15\%$ increase, the monthly rent on a flat is £920. What was it before the increase?
---
$920 \div 1.15 = £800$.
:::

::: q
In 2025, 3240 people went to a festival. This was $35\%$ more than in 2024. How many went in 2024?
---
$3240 \div 1.35 = 2400$ people.
:::

::: q
Shop A sells a jacket for £45 with $\frac13$ off. Shop B sells the same jacket for £40 with $20\%$ off. Which is cheaper, and by how much?
---
A: $45 \times \frac23 = £30$. B: $40 \times 0.8 = £32$. Shop A is cheaper by £2.
:::

::: q
A number is increased by $40\%$ and the result is then decreased by $40\%$. What is the overall percentage change?
---
$1.4 \times 0.6 = 0.84$: a $16\%$ decrease.
:::

::: q
The length of a rectangle is increased by $20\%$ and its width is decreased by $15\%$. Find the percentage change in its area.
---
$1.2 \times 0.85 = 1.02$: the area increases by $2\%$.
:::

::: q
A shop's price is £64 before VAT at $20\%$. What does the customer pay? Another item costs £64 including VAT — what is its price before VAT?
---
$64 \times 1.2 = £76.80$. $\quad 64 \div 1.2 = £53.33$ (to the nearest penny).
:::

::: watch
- **Percentage change divides by the original.** Using the new value as the denominator is the single most common lost mark.
- **Reverse percentages divide by the multiplier.** For "after a $20\%$ cut it costs £84", $84 \div 0.8 = £105$; adding $20\%$ of £84 gives the wrong £100.80.
- **Successive percentages do not add.** $+20\%$ then $-20\%$ is $0.96$, a $4\%$ fall. Multiply the multipliers.
- **Simple is not compound.** Simple interest is the same every year — multiply one year's interest by the number of years. Do not use a power.
- Read whether the question wants the **interest** or the **total amount**, and the **increase** or the **new value**.
- Money to 2 decimal places: £76.8 should be written £76.80; do not round midway through a multi-step problem.
:::
