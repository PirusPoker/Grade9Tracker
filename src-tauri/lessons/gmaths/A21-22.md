## What it is

This topic has two halves. **Forming equations** (A21): turning words, diagrams and procedures into algebra, solving, and saying what the answer means. **Inequalities** (A22): solving linear inequalities in one variable, quadratic inequalities, and inequalities in two variables shown as regions on a graph — and writing answers on a number line or in set notation.

### Forming expressions, formulae and equations

Choose a letter for the unknown, write each quantity in terms of it, then use the fact the question gives you (a total, a perimeter, an angle sum, "is equal to") to make an equation.

- "Think of a number, multiply by 3 and subtract 4": $3n - 4$.
- A taxi charges £3 plus £1.80 per mile: $C = 3 + 1.8m$ — a **formula**, because it links two variables.
- A rectangle is $2x + 3$ long and $x$ wide with perimeter 36 cm: $2(2x + 3) + 2x = 36 \Rightarrow 6x + 6 = 36 \Rightarrow x = 5$. The rectangle is 13 cm by 5 cm.

Useful facts to reach for: angles in a triangle sum to $180°$, on a straight line $180°$, around a point $360°$; consecutive integers are $n$, $n + 1$, $n + 2$; consecutive even numbers $2n$, $2n + 2$; "Ben is 3 years older than Amy" is $b = a + 3$.

When the facts give **two** unknowns, form **two** equations and solve them simultaneously (A19). When the equation turns out to be quadratic, solve it and **interpret**: a length, a price or a speed cannot be negative, so you reject that root and say why.

### Linear inequalities

The symbols: $<$ less than, $\le$ less than or equal to, $>$ greater than, $\ge$ greater than or equal to. Solve exactly as you would an equation, with one extra rule:

> **Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.**

$7 - 3x > 16 \Rightarrow -3x > 9 \Rightarrow x < -3$. (Check: $x = -4$ gives $7 + 12 = 19 > 16$ ✓.) A safer route is to move the $x$ term to the side where it is positive: $7 - 16 > 3x \Rightarrow -9 > 3x \Rightarrow x < -3$.

A **double inequality** is solved by doing the same thing to all three parts:

$$-3 < 2x + 5 \le 11 \Rightarrow -8 < 2x \le 6 \Rightarrow -4 < x \le 3$$

The **integer** values that satisfy it are $-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3$ ($-4$ is excluded, $3$ is included).

### Showing solutions

- **Number line:** an **open** circle ○ for $<$ or $>$ (value not included), a **filled** circle ● for $\le$ or $\ge$ (included), joined by a line over the values that work. For $-4 < x \le 3$: ○ at $-4$, ● at 3, line between.
- **Set notation:** $\{x : -4 < x \le 3\}$, read "the set of $x$ such that...". Two separate pieces are joined with the union symbol: $\{x : x < -3\} \cup \{x : x > 5\}$.

### Quadratic inequalities

Solve the matching equation to find the **critical values**, then sketch the parabola to see which part you need.

$x^2 - x - 12 \le 0$: $(x - 4)(x + 3) = 0$ gives $x = -3$ and $x = 4$. The graph of $y = x^2 - x - 12$ is a U-shape crossing the $x$-axis at $-3$ and $4$; it is **below** the axis (negative) **between** the roots. So $-3 \le x \le 4$.

$x^2 - 2x - 15 > 0$: roots $-3$ and $5$; the curve is **above** the axis **outside** the roots, so $x < -3$ or $x > 5$. This is two separate intervals — never write it as $5 < x < -3$.

Quick rule for a positive $x^2$ term: "$< 0$" → between the roots (one inequality); "$> 0$" → outside the roots (two inequalities). Always rearrange to $\dots > 0$ or $\dots < 0$ first. And **never** square-root an inequality blindly: $x^2 < 16$ means $-4 < x < 4$, not just $x < 4$.

### Inequalities in two variables: regions

A linear inequality in $x$ and $y$, such as $x + y < 6$, is satisfied by a whole **region** of the coordinate grid on one side of the line $x + y = 6$.

1. Draw the boundary line: **solid** for $\le$ or $\ge$ (points on the line count), **dashed** for $<$ or $>$ (they do not).
2. Pick a test point not on the line, usually $(0, 0)$: $0 + 0 < 6$ is true, so the origin's side is the region you want.
3. With several inequalities, the required region satisfies all of them. Label it $R$ (shade only as the question instructs — some ask you to shade the region *not* wanted).

For $y \ge 1$, $y \le 2x$ and $x + y < 6$, the points with **integer** coordinates in the region are $(1,1)$, $(2,1)$, $(3,1)$, $(4,1)$, $(1,2)$, $(2,2)$, $(3,2)$ and $(2,3)$. Points on the dashed line $x + y = 6$, such as $(5, 1)$, are excluded.

::: terms
- **Formula**: a rule linking two or more variables, such as C = 3 + 1.8m, where one is the subject.
- **Inequality**: a statement that one quantity is less than, greater than, or not equal to another, using <, ≤, >, ≥.
- **Critical values**: the roots of the matching equation; they mark where a quadratic inequality changes from true to false.
- **Set notation**: writing a solution as a set, e.g. {x : −4 < x ≤ 3}; the symbol ∪ joins two separate intervals.
- **Integer solutions**: the whole numbers (positive, negative or zero) that satisfy an inequality.
- **Region**: the part of a coordinate grid whose points satisfy every inequality given; its boundaries are straight lines.
:::

::: method
1. **Forming equations:** state what your letter stands for, write each quantity in terms of it (B1 or M1 for a correct expression), then write the equation using the fact given — perimeter, total, angle sum. "Show that" questions need every step leading exactly to the printed equation.
2. **Interpret the solution:** answer the question actually asked (often "find the area", not "find $x$"), with units. Reject a negative length, a fractional number of people, and say why.
3. **Linear inequalities:** use the same steps as an equation. If you divide or multiply by a negative, reverse the sign — or avoid it by collecting the $x$ terms where they are positive.
4. **Double inequalities:** do the same operation to all three parts. List integer solutions carefully: check whether the ends are included.
5. **Quadratic inequalities:** rearrange to $\dots < 0$ or $\dots > 0$, factorise (or use the formula on a calculator paper), find the critical values (M1), **sketch** the parabola (it earns method credit), then write "between" or "outside" in the form asked: inequalities or set notation.
6. **Regions:** draw each boundary line accurately over the full grid, solid or dashed as required, and label the region $R$. To *find* the inequalities for a drawn region, write the equation of each boundary line and use a point inside $R$ to choose the direction of each sign.
7. **Non-calculator papers** favour factorisable quadratics and integer answers; a calculator question may give critical values like $x = 1.62$ that you should round only at the end.
:::

::: example Forming and solving from a diagram
A triangle has angles $2x + 10$, $3x - 5$ and $x + 25$ degrees. Find the largest angle.

$(2x + 10) + (3x - 5) + (x + 25) = 180 \Rightarrow 6x + 30 = 180 \Rightarrow x = 25$.

Angles: $60°$, $70°$, $50°$. The largest angle is $70°$.
:::

::: example Grade 7: a quadratic from a context
A rectangular garden is 4 m longer than it is wide. Its area is $60\text{ m}^2$. Find its perimeter.

Width $x$, length $x + 4$: $x(x + 4) = 60 \Rightarrow x^2 + 4x - 60 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 10)(x - 6) = 0$.

$x = -10$ is impossible for a width, so $x = 6$. The garden is 6 m by 10 m, perimeter $2(6 + 10) = 32$ m.
:::

::: example A double inequality with integers
Find the integers $n$ such that $-7 \le 3n - 1 < 8$.

Add 1: $-6 \le 3n < 9$. Divide by 3: $-2 \le n < 3$. Integers: $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2$.

In set notation: $\{n : -2 \le n < 3\}$; on a number line, a filled circle at $-2$, an open circle at 3.
:::

::: example Quadratic inequality with set notation
Solve $2x^2 + 5x - 3 < 0$, giving your answer in set notation.

$(2x - 1)(x + 3) = 0$: critical values $x = \dfrac12$ and $x = -3$. The U-shaped graph is below the axis between them.

$\left\{x : -3 < x < \dfrac12\right\}$.
:::

::: example Grade 8: rearranging first, answer in two parts
Solve $x^2 > 2x + 15$.

$x^2 - 2x - 15 > 0 \Rightarrow (x - 5)(x + 3) > 0$. Critical values $-3$ and $5$. We need the curve **above** the axis: outside the roots.

$x < -3$ or $x > 5$, i.e. $\{x : x < -3\} \cup \{x : x > 5\}$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: forming an equation with algebraic fractions
A cyclist rides 60 km at an average speed of $x$ km/h. Riding 5 km/h faster would take 1 hour less. Find $x$.

Time $= \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$: $\dfrac{60}{x} - \dfrac{60}{x + 5} = 1$.

Multiply by $x(x + 5)$: $60(x + 5) - 60x = x(x + 5) \Rightarrow 300 = x^2 + 5x \Rightarrow x^2 + 5x - 300 = 0$.

$(x + 20)(x - 15) = 0$. Speed cannot be negative, so $x = 15$ km/h. Check: 4 hours at 15 km/h, 3 hours at 20 km/h ✓.
:::

::: example Grade 8: integer points in a region
List the points with integer coordinates that satisfy $y \ge 1$, $y \le 2x$ and $x + y < 6$.

Row by row. $y = 1$: need $x \ge \frac12$ and $x < 5$, so $x = 1, 2, 3, 4$. $y = 2$: $x \ge 1$ and $x < 4$, so $x = 1, 2, 3$. $y = 3$: $x \ge 1.5$ and $x < 3$, so $x = 2$. $y = 4$: $x \ge 2$ and $x < 2$ — none.

$(1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (4,1), (1,2), (2,2), (3,2), (2,3)$ — eight points.
:::

::: q
The sum of three consecutive integers is 72. Find them.
---
$n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 72$, $3n + 3 = 72$, $n = 23$: the integers are 23, 24, 25.
:::

::: q
Amir is twice as old as Beth. In 5 years' time the sum of their ages will be 52. How old is Amir now?
---
Beth $b$, Amir $2b$. $(b + 5) + (2b + 5) = 52$, $3b = 42$, $b = 14$. Amir is 28.
:::

::: q
Three coffees and two teas cost £9.60. One coffee and three teas cost £6.70. Find the cost of a coffee.
---
$3c + 2t = 9.6$, $c + 3t = 6.7$. $c = 6.7 - 3t$: $20.1 - 9t + 2t = 9.6$, $7t = 10.5$, $t = 1.5$. $c = 6.7 - 4.5 = 2.2$: a coffee costs £2.20.
:::

::: q
Solve $5x - 3 \ge 2x + 9$ and $4 - 5x < 19$.
---
$3x \ge 12$, so $x \ge 4$. $\quad -5x < 15$, dividing by $-5$ reverses the sign: $x > -3$.
:::

::: q
List the integers $x$ such that $-5 < 2x - 1 \le 7$.
---
$-4 < 2x \le 8$, $-2 < x \le 4$: $-1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$.
:::

::: q
Solve $x^2 - 9x + 20 \le 0$.
---
$(x - 4)(x - 5) \le 0$. Between the roots: $4 \le x \le 5$.
:::

::: q
Solve $x^2 \ge 25$, giving your answer in set notation.
---
$(x - 5)(x + 5) \ge 0$: outside the roots. $\{x : x \le -5\} \cup \{x : x \ge 5\}$.
:::

::: q
Solve $3x^2 + x - 2 > 0$.
---
$(3x - 2)(x + 1) > 0$, critical values $-1$ and $\dfrac23$. Outside: $x < -1$ or $x > \dfrac23$.
:::

::: q
A region $R$ lies to the right of the line $x = 1$, above the line $y = x$ and below the line $x + y = 8$, with all boundaries included. Write down the three inequalities that define $R$.
---
$x \ge 1$, $y \ge x$, $x + y \le 8$ (check with a point inside, e.g. $(2, 4)$: $2 \ge 1$, $4 \ge 2$, $6 \le 8$ ✓).
:::

::: watch
- **Not reversing the sign** when dividing by a negative: $-5x < 15$ gives $x > -3$, not $x < -3$.
- **Replacing the inequality with "=" halfway through** and never putting it back. The final answer must be an inequality, and the M1 is often for a correct inequality.
- **Quadratic inequalities answered as "$x < 4$ or $x < 5$"** — find both critical values, sketch, and decide between or outside. Outside is always two separate statements, never $5 < x < -3$.
- **$x^2 < 16$ written as $x < 4$** — it is $-4 < x < 4$.
- **Circles on the number line:** open for $<$, $>$; filled for $\le$, $\ge$.
- **Forming equations:** answering "$x = 6$" when the question asked for the perimeter, or keeping a negative length.
- **Regions:** dashed versus solid lines, and not labelling the region — lose the final mark.
:::

::: test
The length of a rectangle is 3 cm more than twice its width. The perimeter is 54 cm. Work out the area of the rectangle. (4)
---
Width $w$, length $2w + 3$: $2w + 2(2w + 3) = 54$ (M1), $6w + 6 = 54$, $w = 8$ (A1). Length 19 (M1). Area $= 8 \times 19 = 152\text{ cm}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Solve $x^2 - 3x - 10 < 0$. (3)
---
$(x - 5)(x + 2)$ (M1); critical values $5$ and $-2$ (A1); $-2 < x < 5$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Solve $x^2 + 4x \ge 12$. Give your answer using set notation. (3)
---
$x^2 + 4x - 12 \ge 0$, $(x + 6)(x - 2) \ge 0$ (M1), critical values $-6$ and $2$ (A1). $\{x : x \le -6\} \cup \{x : x \ge 2\}$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A right-angled triangle has shorter sides $x$ cm and $(x + 7)$ cm and hypotenuse $(x + 8)$ cm. Find the perimeter of the triangle. (5)
---
$x^2 + (x + 7)^2 = (x + 8)^2$ (M1). $2x^2 + 14x + 49 = x^2 + 16x + 64$, so $x^2 - 2x - 15 = 0$ (A1). $(x - 5)(x + 3) = 0$ (M1), $x = 5$ since a length cannot be $-3$ (A1). Sides 5, 12, 13: perimeter 30 cm (A1).
:::

::: test
The region $R$ satisfies $x \ge 0$, $y > x$ and $x + 2y \le 8$. (a) Which boundary line should be dashed? (b) List all the points with integer coordinates in $R$. (4)
---
(a) $y = x$, because the inequality $y > x$ is strict (B1).
(b) $x = 0$: $y = 1, 2, 3, 4$; $x = 1$: $y = 2, 3$; $x = 2$: $y = 3$ (M1 for a systematic check). $(0,1), (0,2), (0,3), (0,4), (1,2), (1,3), (2,3)$ (A2, A1 for five or six correct with none wrong).
:::

::: test
Find all the integers that satisfy both $x^2 < 20$ and $2x + 3 > 0$. (4)
---
$x^2 < 20$: $-\sqrt{20} < x < \sqrt{20}$, i.e. $-4.47\ldots < x < 4.47\ldots$ (M1 A1). $2x > -3$, $x > -1.5$ (B1). Integers: $-1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Pens cost $x$ pence each. When the price falls by 3p, £6 buys 10 more pens than before. Show that $x^2 - 3x - 180 = 0$ and hence find the original price. (5)
---
$\dfrac{600}{x - 3} - \dfrac{600}{x} = 10$ (M1). $600x - 600(x - 3) = 10x(x - 3)$ (M1), $1800 = 10x^2 - 30x$, so $x^2 - 3x - 180 = 0$ (A1). $(x - 15)(x + 12) = 0$ (M1); price cannot be negative, so the pens cost 15p (A1).
:::
