## What it is

At GCSE you need the equation of a circle whose **centre is the origin**, and the equation of the **tangent** to that circle at a given point. It joins three older ideas: Pythagoras, gradients of straight lines, and the circle theorem "a tangent is perpendicular to the radius".

### The equation of a circle centred at the origin

Take any point $(x, y)$ on a circle with centre $O(0, 0)$ and radius $r$. Drop a vertical line from it to the $x$-axis: you get a right-angled triangle with legs $x$ and $y$ and hypotenuse $r$. By Pythagoras:

$$x^2 + y^2 = r^2$$

Every point on the circle satisfies this equation, and every point that satisfies it is on the circle.

- $x^2 + y^2 = 49$: centre $(0, 0)$, radius $\sqrt{49} = 7$.
- $x^2 + y^2 = 20$: radius $\sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt5$ — leave it as a surd unless told otherwise.
- The circle centre $O$ with radius 6 is $x^2 + y^2 = 36$ — the right-hand side is $r^2$, not $r$.

**Where it meets the axes**: put $y = 0$ to get $x = \pm r$, and $x = 0$ to get $y = \pm r$. So $x^2 + y^2 = 49$ passes through $(7, 0)$, $(-7, 0)$, $(0, 7)$ and $(0, -7)$.

**Finding the equation from a point**: the circle centre $O$ through $(5, -12)$ has $r^2 = 5^2 + (-12)^2 = 169$, so it is $x^2 + y^2 = 169$ (radius 13).

**On, inside or outside?** Substitute the point into $x^2 + y^2$ and compare with $r^2$:

- equal → on the circle: $(3, -4)$ gives $9 + 16 = 25$, on $x^2 + y^2 = 25$;
- less → inside: $(1, 4)$ gives $17 < 25$;
- more → outside: $(4, 4)$ gives $32 > 25$.

**Finding a missing coordinate**: if $(k, -2)$ lies on $x^2 + y^2 = 29$, then $k^2 + 4 = 29$, $k^2 = 25$, $k = \pm 5$. Use any extra information (such as "$k < 0$") to choose.

### The tangent at a given point

A **tangent** touches the circle at exactly one point. The circle theorem you need:

> The tangent at a point is **perpendicular** to the radius at that point.

So the tangent's gradient comes from the radius's gradient. Perpendicular gradients multiply to $-1$: if one is $m$, the other is $-\dfrac1m$ (flip it and change the sign).

For the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ at $P(3, 4)$:

1. Gradient of the radius $OP$: $\dfrac{4 - 0}{3 - 0} = \dfrac43$.
2. Gradient of the tangent: $-\dfrac34$.
3. Line through $(3, 4)$ with gradient $-\dfrac34$: $y - 4 = -\dfrac34(x - 3)$, so $y = -\dfrac34x + \dfrac{25}{4}$, or $3x + 4y = 25$.

Check: $(3, 4)$ satisfies $3(3) + 4(4) = 25$. ✓

**Special cases**: at $(r, 0)$ or $(-r, 0)$ the radius is horizontal, so the tangent is vertical, $x = \pm r$. At $(0, r)$ or $(0, -r)$ the tangent is horizontal, $y = \pm r$.

**A useful check**: for the circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, the tangent at $(a, b)$ always simplifies to $ax + by = r^2$. Above: $3x + 4y = 25$. Use it to check your answer — but show the gradient method, because that is what the marks are for.

### Lines meeting circles

To find where a line meets the circle, substitute the line into $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ and solve the quadratic (this is the linear/quadratic simultaneous equations of A19). Two solutions: the line cuts the circle twice. One repeated solution: the line is a tangent. No solutions: the line misses.

::: terms
- **Equation of a circle**: x² + y² = r² for a circle with centre the origin and radius r.
- **Radius**: the distance from the centre to any point on the circle; here it is √(x² + y²).
- **Tangent**: a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
- **Perpendicular gradients**: two lines at right angles have gradients that multiply to −1, such as 2 and −½.
- **Negative reciprocal**: the gradient −1/m of a line perpendicular to a line of gradient m.
- **Point of contact**: the single point where a tangent touches the circle.
:::

::: method
1. **Read the circle.** $x^2 + y^2 = k$ has centre $(0, 0)$ and radius $\sqrt k$. Simplify surds: $\sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt5$.
2. **Circle through a point:** $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$ at that point; write $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ with the number $r^2$ on the right.
3. **Tangent at a point $P(a, b)$ — earn the marks in order:**
   - M1: gradient of $OP = \dfrac ba$;
   - M1: tangent gradient $= -\dfrac ab$ (negative reciprocal);
   - M1: substitute $P$ into $y = mx + c$ (or use $y - b = m(x - a)$);
   - A1: the equation, simplified.
4. **Give the form asked for.** "In the form $y = mx + c$" needs fractions for $m$ and $c$ if they are not whole; "$ax + by = c$ with integers" means clear the fractions.
5. **Check** that $P$ satisfies your tangent equation, and that $aX + bY = r^2$ gives the same line.
6. **Paper 1 (non-calculator)** favourites: surd radii and fractional gradients — keep everything exact; decimals such as 0.33 lose the accuracy mark.
7. **Follow-on parts** (where the tangent meets the axes, the area of a triangle): substitute $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ into your tangent, then sketch to see the triangle.
:::

::: example Reading and writing circle equations
(a) Write down the radius of $x^2 + y^2 = 72$. (b) Find the equation of the circle with centre $O$ passing through $(-7, 24)$. (c) Does $(6, 5)$ lie inside, on or outside $x^2 + y^2 = 60$?

(a) $\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = 6\sqrt2$.

(b) $r^2 = 49 + 576 = 625$, so $x^2 + y^2 = 625$ (radius 25).

(c) $36 + 25 = 61 > 60$: just outside.
:::

::: example Tangent at a point
Find the equation of the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 100$ at the point $(6, -8)$. Give your answer in the form $y = mx + c$.

Check the point: $36 + 64 = 100$. ✓

Gradient of the radius: $\dfrac{-8}{6} = -\dfrac43$. Tangent gradient: $\dfrac34$.

$y = \dfrac34x + c$ through $(6, -8)$: $-8 = \dfrac92 + c$, so $c = -\dfrac{25}{2}$.

$y = \dfrac34x - \dfrac{25}{2}$. (Check: $6x - 8y = 100$ gives $y = \dfrac34x - 12.5$. ✓)
:::

::: example A point with an unknown coordinate
The point $P(p, 2)$, with $p > 0$, lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 13$. Find $p$ and the equation of the tangent at $P$, giving it in the form $ax + by = c$ where $a$, $b$, $c$ are integers.

$p^2 + 4 = 13$, so $p^2 = 9$ and $p = 3$ (positive).

Radius gradient $\dfrac23$; tangent gradient $-\dfrac32$.

$y - 2 = -\dfrac32(x - 3)$. Multiply by 2: $2y - 4 = -3x + 9$, so $3x + 2y = 13$.
:::

::: example Where a line meets a circle
Find where the line $y = x + 1$ meets the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.

Substitute: $x^2 + (x + 1)^2 = 25 \Rightarrow 2x^2 + 2x + 1 = 25 \Rightarrow x^2 + x - 12 = 0$.

$(x + 4)(x - 3) = 0$: $x = -4$ or $x = 3$. Then $y = x + 1$: points $(-4, -3)$ and $(3, 4)$.
:::

::: example Tangent and a triangle (grade 9)
The tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 20$ at $(-2, 4)$ meets the axes at $A$ and $B$. Find the area of triangle $OAB$.

Radius gradient $\dfrac{4}{-2} = -2$. Tangent gradient $\dfrac12$.

$y - 4 = \dfrac12(x + 2)$, so $y = \dfrac12x + 5$.

$x = 0$: $B(0, 5)$. $y = 0$: $\dfrac12x = -5$, $A(-10, 0)$.

Area $= \dfrac12 \times 10 \times 5 = 25$ square units.

(Check: $-2x + 4y = 20$ is $y = \dfrac12x + 5$. ✓)
:::

Does the point $(2, -5)$ lie inside, on or outside the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 30$?
---
$4 + 25 = 29 < 30$: inside.

::: q
Find the equation of the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ at $(4, 3)$.
---
Radius gradient $\dfrac34$; tangent gradient $-\dfrac43$. $y - 3 = -\dfrac43(x - 4)$: $y = -\dfrac43x + \dfrac{25}{3}$, i.e. $4x + 3y = 25$.
:::

::: q
Find the equation of the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 10$ at $(-1, 3)$.
---
Radius gradient $\dfrac{3}{-1} = -3$; tangent gradient $\dfrac13$. $y - 3 = \dfrac13(x + 1)$: $y = \dfrac13x + \dfrac{10}{3}$, i.e. $x - 3y = -10$.
:::

::: q
Write down the equation of the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 36$ at $(0, -6)$.
---
The radius is vertical, so the tangent is horizontal: $y = -6$.
:::

::: q
The point $(k, -2)$ lies on $x^2 + y^2 = 29$ and $k < 0$. Find $k$.
---
$k^2 + 4 = 29 \Rightarrow k^2 = 25 \Rightarrow k = -5$.
:::

::: q
The tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 20$ at $(2, -4)$ meets the $x$-axis at $Q$. Find the coordinates of $Q$.
---
Radius gradient $-2$; tangent gradient $\dfrac12$. $y + 4 = \dfrac12(x - 2) \Rightarrow y = \dfrac12x - 5$. $y = 0$: $x = 10$, so $Q(10, 0)$.
:::

::: watch
- **$r$ versus $r^2$.** A circle of radius 6 is $x^2 + y^2 = 36$, not $= 6$; and $x^2 + y^2 = 20$ has radius $\sqrt{20}$, not 20 or 10.
- **Using the radius gradient for the tangent.** The tangent is perpendicular to the radius: flip the fraction **and** change the sign.
- **Sign slips with negative coordinates.** The radius to $(-2, 4)$ has gradient $\dfrac{4}{-2} = -2$; write the division out rather than doing it in your head.
- **Stopping at the gradient.** A tangent question wants a full equation — substitute the point to find $c$.
- **Decimals on Paper 1.** $-\dfrac43$ is exact; $-1.33$ is not and loses the final mark.
:::
