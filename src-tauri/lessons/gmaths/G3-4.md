## What it is

Angle facts are the building blocks of nearly every geometry question, and in the exam each fact's **name is the reason** you must write next to the working. This topic covers angles at points and on lines, parallel lines, angle sums of triangles and polygons, and the properties of special triangles and quadrilaterals — including **deriving** those results, not just quoting them.

### Angles at a point and on a line

- **Angles on a straight line add up to $180°$.**
- **Angles around a point add up to $360°$.**
- **Vertically opposite angles are equal** — where two straight lines cross, the opposite angles match.

Example: two lines cross; one angle is $64°$. The angle next to it is $180° - 64° = 116°$ (angles on a straight line), and the angle opposite is $64°$ (vertically opposite angles).

### Angles and parallel lines

A line crossing two parallel lines is a **transversal**. It makes three pairs to know:

- **Alternate angles are equal** — on opposite sides of the transversal, inside the parallels (a "Z" shape).
- **Corresponding angles are equal** — in matching positions at each crossing (an "F" shape).
- **Co-interior angles add up to $180°$** — on the same side, inside the parallels (a "C" or "U" shape). Some books call them allied angles.

Never call them "Z angles" or "F angles" in an answer — those words score no reasoning marks.

### Deriving the angle sum of a triangle

Draw triangle $ABC$ and a line through $A$ parallel to $BC$. The angles at $A$ on either side of the triangle are **alternate** to the angles at $B$ and $C$, so they equal $\angle B$ and $\angle C$. Those two angles and $\angle A$ lie on a straight line, so
$$\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180°.$$

The same diagram proves the **exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles**: extend $BC$ to $D$; $\angle ACD = 180° - \angle C = \angle A + \angle B$.

### Special triangles

| Triangle | Sides | Angles |
|---|---|---|
| equilateral | all 3 equal | all $60°$ |
| isosceles | 2 equal | the 2 **base angles** (opposite the equal sides) are equal |
| scalene | none equal | none equal |
| right-angled | — | one angle $90°$ |

### Angles in polygons

Split an $n$-sided polygon into triangles from one vertex: you get $n - 2$ triangles, so
$$\text{sum of interior angles} = (n - 2) \times 180°.$$
A pentagon: $3 \times 180° = 540°$; a hexagon: $720°$; a decagon: $1440°$.

At each vertex, **interior + exterior $= 180°$** (straight line). Walking round any convex polygon you turn through one full turn, so
$$\text{sum of exterior angles} = 360°.$$

For a **regular** polygon (all sides and angles equal):
$$\text{exterior angle} = \frac{360°}{n}, \qquad \text{interior angle} = 180° - \frac{360°}{n}, \qquad n = \frac{360°}{\text{exterior angle}}.$$
A regular 12-sided polygon has exterior angle $30°$ and interior angle $150°$. If a regular polygon has interior angle $156°$, its exterior angle is $24°$ and it has $360 \div 24 = 15$ sides. The number of sides must be a whole number — a regular polygon with interior angle $130°$ cannot exist, because $360 \div 50 = 7.2$.

### Special quadrilaterals

The angles of any quadrilateral add up to $360°$ (two triangles).

| Shape | Sides | Angles | Diagonals | Symmetry (lines, rotation order) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| square | 4 equal, opposite pairs parallel | all $90°$ | equal, bisect each other at $90°$ | 4, 4 |
| rectangle | opposite pairs equal and parallel | all $90°$ | equal, bisect each other | 2, 2 |
| rhombus | 4 equal, opposite pairs parallel | opposite angles equal | bisect each other at $90°$, bisect the angles | 2, 2 |
| parallelogram | opposite pairs equal and parallel | opposite angles equal | bisect each other | 0, 2 |
| kite | two pairs of **adjacent** sides equal | one pair of opposite angles equal | cross at $90°$; one bisects the other | 1, 1 |
| trapezium | exactly one pair of parallel sides | co-interior pairs add to $180°$ | — | isosceles trapezium: 1, 1 |

Each shape is a special case of the ones above it where the definitions allow: every square is a rectangle and a rhombus; every rhombus is a parallelogram and a kite.

### Deriving properties

Properties can be **proved** from definitions using angle facts. For example, in parallelogram $ABCD$, $\angle A + \angle B = 180°$ (co-interior angles, $AD \parallel BC$) and $\angle B + \angle C = 180°$ (co-interior, $AB \parallel DC$), so $\angle A = \angle C$: **opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal**.

::: terms
- **Vertically opposite angles**: the pairs of opposite angles formed where two straight lines cross; they are equal.
- **Alternate angles**: angles on opposite sides of a transversal between two parallel lines; they are equal.
- **Corresponding angles**: angles in the same position at each place a transversal crosses parallel lines; they are equal.
- **Co-interior angles**: angles on the same side of a transversal between two parallel lines; they add up to 180°.
- **Exterior angle**: the angle between one side of a polygon and the extension of the next side; interior + exterior = 180°.
- **Regular polygon**: a polygon with all sides equal and all angles equal; each exterior angle is 360° ÷ n.
- **Kite**: a quadrilateral with two pairs of equal adjacent sides; its diagonals cross at right angles.
:::

::: method
1. **Mark the diagram.** Write every angle you find on the diagram as you go — it often reveals the next step.
2. **One angle per line, reason in words.** "$\angle GHD = 58°$ (alternate angles are equal)." Use the full standard wording; "Z angles" or "because they're parallel" earns no reasoning mark. When a question says "give reasons", the reason marks are separate from the angle marks.
3. **Isosceles triangles hide everywhere** — look for equal-side dashes, two radii of a circle, or two sides of a regular polygon.
4. **Polygons:** go through the **exterior** angle whenever the polygon is regular: exterior $= 360° \div n$, interior $= 180° -$ exterior. Use $(n - 2) \times 180°$ for the total when it is not regular.
5. **Algebra in angles:** form an equation from a fact (straight line, triangle, polygon sum), solve it, then answer the actual question (often an angle, not $x$).
6. **"Show that" or "prove":** start from given facts only, give a reason for every step, and end with a sentence that restates what was to be proved. Never measure — diagrams are "not drawn accurately".
7. This topic appears on all three papers; on Paper 1 the arithmetic is designed to be done by hand.
:::

::: example Parallel lines with reasons
Line $EF$ crosses parallel lines $AB$ and $CD$ at $G$ and $H$ respectively, with $A$ and $C$ on the same side of $EF$. $\angle AGH = 58°$. Find $\angle GHD$ and $\angle GHC$, giving reasons.

$\angle GHD = 58°$ (alternate angles are equal).

$\angle GHC = 180° - 58° = 122°$ (co-interior angles add up to $180°$).
:::

::: example Isosceles triangle and exterior angle
In triangle $PQR$, $PQ = PR$ and $\angle QPR = 36°$. $QR$ is extended to $S$. Find $\angle PRS$, giving reasons.

$\angle PQR = \angle PRQ = (180° - 36°) \div 2 = 72°$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal; angles in a triangle add up to $180°$).

$\angle PRS = 180° - 72° = 108°$ (angles on a straight line add up to $180°$). Check: exterior angle $= 36° + 72° = 108°$. ✓
:::

::: example Algebra in a pentagon
The angles of a pentagon are $x$, $x + 20$, $2x$, $2x - 10$ and $110$ degrees. Find the largest angle.

Sum of interior angles $= 3 \times 180° = 540°$.

$x + x + 20 + 2x + 2x - 10 + 110 = 540 \Rightarrow 6x + 120 = 540 \Rightarrow x = 70$.

Angles: $70°, 90°, 140°, 130°, 110°$. Largest: $140°$.
:::

::: example Interior to exterior ratio (grade 7)
The interior and exterior angles of a regular polygon are in the ratio $7 : 2$. How many sides does it have?

They add up to $180°$, so exterior $= \dfrac{2}{9} \times 180° = 40°$.

$n = 360 \div 40 = 9$ sides.
:::

::: example Polygons meeting at a point (grade 8)
A square, a regular hexagon and a regular polygon $P$ fit together exactly around a point. How many sides has $P$?

Square $90°$; hexagon interior $= 180° - 60° = 120°$.

$P$'s interior angle $= 360° - 90° - 120° = 150°$ (angles around a point).

Exterior $= 30°$, so $n = 360 \div 30 = 12$ sides.
:::

::: example Deriving a result (grade 8)
$AB$ and $DE$ are parallel lines, with $B$ and $E$ on the right. $C$ is a point between the lines, to the right of $A$ and $D$, so that $ACD$ forms an arrowhead pointing right. $\angle BAC = a$ and $\angle CDE = b$. Prove that $\angle ACD = a + b$.

Draw a line through $C$ parallel to $AB$ (and so parallel to $DE$).

The part of $\angle ACD$ above this line equals $a$ (alternate angles are equal, with $AB$). The part below equals $b$ (alternate angles are equal, with $DE$).

So $\angle ACD = a + b$. ∎
:::

::: example Diagonals of a regular pentagon (grade 9)
$ABCDE$ is a regular pentagon. Diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ cross at $P$. Work out $\angle APB$, giving reasons.

Interior angle $= 180° - 360° \div 5 = 108°$.

Triangle $ABC$: $AB = BC$ (regular), so $\angle BCA = (180° - 108°) \div 2 = 36°$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle). Likewise in triangle $BCD$, $\angle CBD = 36°$.

In triangle $BPC$: $\angle BPC = 180° - 36° - 36° = 108°$ (angles in a triangle).

$\angle APB = 180° - 108° = 72°$ (angles on a straight line, since $A$, $P$, $C$ are collinear).
:::

::: q
Two straight lines cross. One of the angles formed is $47°$. Find the other three.
---
$133°$ and $133°$ (angles on a straight line add up to $180°$), and $47°$ (vertically opposite angles are equal).
:::

::: q
A transversal crosses two parallel lines. One angle is $72°$. Its co-interior partner is $y$. Find $y$.
---
$y = 180° - 72° = 108°$ (co-interior angles add up to $180°$).
:::

::: q
Find the sum of the interior angles of an octagon, and each interior angle of a regular decagon.
---
Octagon: $6 \times 180° = 1080°$. Regular decagon: exterior $36°$, interior $180° - 36° = 144°$.
:::

::: q
Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $20°$. How many sides does it have?
---
$360 \div 20 = 18$ sides.
:::

::: q
Can a regular polygon have an interior angle of $145°$? Explain.
---
Exterior $= 35°$ and $360 \div 35 = 10.28\ldots$, not a whole number, so **no**.
:::

::: q
The angles of a quadrilateral are $3x$, $4x$, $5x$ and $6x$. Find them.
---
$18x = 360 \Rightarrow x = 20$: angles $60°, 80°, 100°, 120°$.
:::

::: q
Which special quadrilaterals always have diagonals that cross at right angles?
---
Square, rhombus and kite.
:::

::: q
$ABCD$ is a kite with $AB = AD$ and $CB = CD$. $\angle BAD = 80°$ and $\angle BCD = 50°$. Find $\angle ABC$.
---
$\angle ABC = \angle ADC$ (the kite's equal pair of opposite angles). $2\angle ABC = 360° - 80° - 50° = 230°$, so $\angle ABC = 115°$.
:::

::: q
Use the interior angle sum to prove that the exterior angles of any $n$-sided polygon add up to $360°$.
---
At each vertex interior + exterior $= 180°$, so all $n$ pairs total $180n$. Interior angles total $180(n - 2) = 180n - 360$. Exterior total $= 180n - (180n - 360) = 360°$. ∎
:::

::: watch
- **Reasons in non-standard words.** "Z angles", "F angles" and "because of parallel lines" score nothing — write "alternate angles are equal", "corresponding angles are equal", "co-interior angles add up to $180°$".
- **Mixing up interior and exterior.** $360 \div n$ is the **exterior** angle; the interior is $180°$ minus it.
- **Using $(n - 2) \times 180°$ as one angle** — it is the total; divide by $n$ only if the polygon is regular.
- **Assuming lines are parallel** or sides are equal because the diagram looks that way. Use only what is given.
- **Answering $x$** when the question asked for an angle — substitute back.
- **Kite angles:** the equal angles are **between** the unequal sides, not at the top and bottom.
:::

::: test
Angles $x$, $2x$ and $3x$ lie together on a straight line. Find $x$. (2)
---
$6x = 180$ (M1); $x = 30°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$PQ$ is parallel to $RS$. A transversal meets $PQ$ at $T$ and $RS$ at $U$, with $P$ and $R$ on the same side of the transversal. $\angle PTU = 64°$. Find $\angle TUS$ and $\angle TUR$, giving reasons. (4)
---
$\angle TUS = 64°$ (B1) (alternate angles are equal) (B1). $\angle TUR = 116°$ (B1) (co-interior angles add up to $180°$, or angles on a straight line) (B1).
:::

::: test
Work out the sum of the interior angles of an 11-sided polygon. (2)
---
$(11 - 2) \times 180°$ (M1) $= 1620°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Each interior angle of a regular polygon is $162°$. How many sides does it have? (3)
---
Exterior $= 180° - 162° = 18°$ (M1); $360 \div 18$ (M1) $= 20$ sides (A1).
:::

::: test
Five of the interior angles of a hexagon are $100°$, $110°$, $120°$, $130°$ and $140°$. Find the sixth. (3)
---
Sum $= 4 \times 180° = 720°$ (M1); $100 + 110 + 120 + 130 + 140 = 600$ (M1); sixth angle $= 120°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = AC$. A line through $A$ is parallel to $BC$ and makes an angle of $65°$ with $AB$ (on the side away from $C$). Find $\angle BAC$, giving reasons. (4)
---
$\angle ABC = 65°$ (alternate angles are equal) (B1). $\angle ACB = 65°$ (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal) (B1). $\angle BAC = 180° - 130°$ (M1) $= 50°$ (angles in a triangle add up to $180°$) (A1).
:::

::: test
The interior angle of a regular polygon is 4 times its exterior angle. Show that the polygon has 10 sides. (3)
---
Let exterior $= e$: $4e + e = 180$ (M1), so $e = 36°$ (A1). $n = 360 \div 36 = 10$ (A1).
:::

::: test
$ABCDE$ is a regular pentagon and $ABFG$ is a square, drawn on side $AB$ outside the pentagon. Work out $\angle AEG$, giving reasons. (5)
---
Pentagon interior $= 108°$ (B1). $\angle EAG = 360° - 108° - 90° = 162°$ (angles around a point) (M1 A1). $AE = AB = AG$, so triangle $AEG$ is isosceles (B1). $\angle AEG = (180° - 162°) \div 2 = 9°$ (A1).
:::

::: test
Prove that the angles in any triangle add up to $180°$. (3)
---
Draw a line through $A$ parallel to $BC$ (B1). The angles on either side of $A$ equal $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ (alternate angles are equal) (B1). Together with $\angle A$ they make a straight line, so $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180°$ (angles on a straight line) (B1).
:::
