## What it is

A **rate of change** says how fast one quantity changes as another changes: metres per second, pounds per kilogram, degrees per minute. On a graph, the rate of change **is the gradient**. This topic is about reading gradients as rates — on straight lines, where the rate is constant, and on curves, where it keeps changing.

### Gradient of a straight line as a rate

$$\text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$

and its **units are the $y$-units per $x$-unit**. That is how you interpret it:

| Graph | Gradient means | Units |
|---|---|---|
| distance against time | speed | m/s, km/h |
| velocity against time | acceleration | m/s² |
| cost against number of items | price per item | £ per item |
| volume against time | rate of flow | litres per minute |
| temperature against time | rate of heating or cooling | °C per minute |

A plumber's charge $C = 40 + 30h$ (in pounds, for $h$ hours) is a straight line with gradient $30$ — she charges £30 **per hour** — and $C$-intercept $40$, a £40 call-out fee. A negative gradient is a decreasing rate: a tank with $V = 500 - 20t$ litres is emptying at $20$ litres per minute.

### Graphs of direct and inverse proportion

- **Direct proportion** $y = kx$: a straight line **through the origin**. The gradient $k$ is the constant rate — the cost of one item, the speed.
- **Linear but not proportional** $y = mx + c$ with $c \ne 0$: a straight line that misses the origin (the plumber above).
- **Inverse proportion** $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$: a curve that falls steeply and then levels off, never touching the axes. Its gradient is negative and changes all the time — time to finish a job against the number of workers, for example.

### Average rate of change: the gradient of a chord

On a curve the rate is different at every point. The **average rate of change** between two points is the gradient of the straight line (the **chord**) joining them.

The temperature of a cup of tea is $90$ °C at $t = 0$ and $58$ °C at $t = 10$ minutes, so its average rate of change is $\dfrac{58 - 90}{10} = -3.2$ °C per minute — it cools at an average of $3.2$ °C per minute.

With an equation, work out the two $y$-values first. For $y = x^2$ between $x = 1$ and $x = 3$: $\dfrac{9 - 1}{3 - 1} = 4$.

Average speed on a distance–time curve is exactly this: total distance ÷ total time is the gradient of the chord from start to finish.

### Instantaneous rate of change: the gradient of a tangent

The rate of change **at one moment** is the gradient of the **tangent** to the curve at that point — the straight line that just touches the curve there, running in the same direction as the curve.

**From a graph** you estimate it: draw the tangent carefully with a ruler, pick two points on it that are far apart, and find $\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$ using the axis scales. It is only an estimate because the tangent is drawn by eye.

**Numerically**, chords over smaller and smaller intervals home in on the tangent. For $y = x^2$ at $x = 2$:

| Chord from $x = 2$ to | $2.1$ | $2.01$ | $2.001$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gradient | $\dfrac{4.41 - 4}{0.1} = 4.1$ | $\dfrac{4.0401 - 4}{0.01} = 4.01$ | $4.001$ |

The gradients approach $4$, so the instantaneous rate of change at $x = 2$ is $4$. (GCSE does not use calculus; this chord method is all that is needed.)

### Reading the shape

On a distance–time graph, a steeper section means faster, a horizontal section means stationary, and a curve getting steeper means speeding up. Filling a container at a constant rate, the depth rises **fastest where the container is narrowest**.

::: terms
- **Rate of change**: how much one quantity changes for each unit change in another, such as metres per second.
- **Gradient**: change in y divided by change in x; on a graph of a real situation it is a rate, with units of y per x.
- **Chord**: a straight line joining two points on a curve; its gradient is the average rate of change between them.
- **Tangent**: a straight line that touches a curve at one point and has the same direction as the curve there.
- **Average rate of change**: the total change in y divided by the total change in x over an interval.
- **Instantaneous rate of change**: the rate of change at a single point, equal to the gradient of the tangent there.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the rate before you calculate.** Say what the gradient represents from the axes: "$y$-unit per $x$-unit". Most interpretation marks are for this sentence, in context, with units.
2. **Straight line:** pick two points far apart where the line crosses grid-lines exactly; gradient $=$ rise $\div$ run, **using the axis scales**, not counting squares.
3. **Average rate:** find the two points (read from the graph, table or equation), then the gradient of the chord. Show both $y$-values.
4. **Instantaneous rate from a graph:** draw the tangent at the point with a ruler, extend it across the grid, choose two far-apart points on the tangent and find its gradient (M1 for the tangent, M1 for rise $\div$ run). Say it is an **estimate**.
5. **Instantaneous rate from an equation:** use chords over shrinking intervals (e.g. to $x + 0.1$, $x + 0.01$) and state the value they approach.
6. **Signs matter:** a negative gradient means a decrease — write "decreasing at 3.2 °C per minute" rather than a bare $-3.2$.
7. **Both papers:** on the calculator papers these are often worked from an equation; on Paper 1 expect graph reading and friendly numbers.
:::

::: example Interpreting a straight line
The graph of the cost, $C$ pounds, of electricity against the number of units used, $n$, is a straight line through $(0, 12)$ and $(200, 72)$. Interpret the gradient and the intercept.

Gradient $= \dfrac{72 - 12}{200 - 0} = 0.3$: each unit of electricity costs £0.30 (30p).

Intercept $12$: a fixed charge of £12 is paid even if no electricity is used. The cost is **not** directly proportional to $n$, because the line misses the origin.
:::

::: example Average speed from an equation
A ball is thrown upwards. Its height after $t$ seconds is $h = 20t - 5t^2$ metres. Work out its average velocity (a) from $t = 0$ to $t = 1$, (b) from $t = 1$ to $t = 3$. Interpret (b).

$h(0) = 0$, $h(1) = 15$, $h(3) = 60 - 45 = 15$.

(a) $\dfrac{15 - 0}{1} = 15$ m/s. $\quad$ (b) $\dfrac{15 - 15}{2} = 0$ m/s.

In (b) the ball goes up and comes back to the same height, so its overall change in height is zero — an average velocity of 0 does not mean it stood still.
:::

::: example Estimating from a tangent
A tangent is drawn to a distance–time curve at $t = 3$ s. It passes through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 30)$, with distance in metres. Estimate the speed at $t = 3$.

Gradient $= \dfrac{30 - 2}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{28}{4} = 7$, so the speed is about $7$ m/s. It is an estimate because the tangent is drawn by eye.
:::

::: example Instantaneous rate by chords (grade 8)
$y = x^3$. Work out the gradients of the chords from $x = 2$ to $x = 2.1$ and from $x = 2$ to $x = 2.01$, and estimate the rate of change of $y$ at $x = 2$.

$2.1^3 = 9.261$: gradient $= \dfrac{9.261 - 8}{0.1} = 12.61$.

$2.01^3 = 8.120601$: gradient $= \dfrac{0.120601}{0.01} = 12.0601$.

The gradients approach $12$, so the rate of change at $x = 2$ is about $12$.
:::

::: example Chords that equal the tangent (grade 9)
Show that the chord of $y = x^2$ from $x = a - h$ to $x = a + h$ has gradient $2a$, whatever the value of $h$. Hence find the gradient of the curve at $x = 3$, and the point where the tangent is parallel to the chord joining $(1, 1)$ and $(5, 25)$.

Gradient $= \dfrac{(a + h)^2 - (a - h)^2}{(a + h) - (a - h)} = \dfrac{4ah}{2h} = 2a$.

As $h$ shrinks, these chords become the tangent at $x = a$, and their gradient is always $2a$. So the gradient at $x = 3$ is $6$.

The chord from $(1, 1)$ to $(5, 25)$ has gradient $\dfrac{24}{4} = 6$, and it is centred on $x = 3$. The tangent at $(3, 9)$ is parallel to it.
:::

::: q
A distance–time graph is a straight line from $(0, 0)$ to $(2.5, 150)$, with time in hours and distance in km. Work out the speed.
---
Gradient $= 150 \div 2.5 = 60$ km/h.
:::

::: q
A car's velocity rises steadily from 0 to 24 m/s in 8 seconds. Work out its acceleration.
---
Gradient of the velocity–time graph $= 24 \div 8 = 3$ m/s².
:::

::: q
A tank holds $V = 500 - 20t$ litres after $t$ minutes. What does the gradient of the graph of $V$ against $t$ represent?
---
The gradient is $-20$: the tank is emptying at 20 litres per minute.
:::

::: q
Which of these show direct proportion between $C$ and $n$: $C = 12n$, $C = 12n + 5$, $C = \dfrac{60}{n}$?
---
Only $C = 12n$ (straight line through the origin). $C = 12n + 5$ is linear but misses the origin; $C = \dfrac{60}{n}$ is inverse proportion.
:::

::: q
A town's population was 12 000 in 2010 and 15 600 in 2025. Work out the average rate of change of the population.
---
$\dfrac{15\,600 - 12\,000}{15} = 240$ people per year.
:::

::: q
Work out the average rate of change of $y = x^3$ between $x = 1$ and $x = 3$.
---
$\dfrac{27 - 1}{3 - 1} = 13$.
:::

::: q
The tangent to a curve at $t = 4$ passes through $(2, 10)$ and $(8, 34)$. Estimate the rate of change at $t = 4$.
---
$\dfrac{34 - 10}{8 - 2} = 4$ (units of $y$ per unit of $t$).
:::

::: q
$y = 2x^2$. Find the gradients of the chords from $x = 1$ to $x = 1.1$ and from $x = 1$ to $x = 1.01$, and estimate the gradient at $x = 1$.
---
$\dfrac{2.42 - 2}{0.1} = 4.2$ and $\dfrac{2.0402 - 2}{0.01} = 4.02$. Estimate $4$.
:::

::: q
Water is poured at a constant rate into a vase that is wide at the bottom and narrow at the top. Describe the graph of depth against time.
---
The depth rises slowly at first and then faster and faster: a curve that gets steeper, because the narrower the vase, the faster the level rises.
:::

::: watch
- **Use the scales, not the squares.** Counting grid squares for rise and run gives the wrong gradient whenever the axes have different scales.
- **Interpret in context with units.** "The gradient is 0.3" scores nothing; "the cost per unit of electricity is £0.30" scores the mark.
- **Tangent, not chord, for "at" a moment.** "The speed at $t = 3$" needs a tangent at $t = 3$; "the average speed between…" needs a chord.
- **Draw long tangents** and pick points far apart; a short tangent magnifies reading errors. Say the answer is an estimate.
- A straight line must pass through the origin to show direct proportion.
- A negative gradient is a rate of decrease — say "decreasing by…" instead of giving a negative speed.
:::
