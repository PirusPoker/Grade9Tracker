## What it is

Right-angled triangles are everywhere in GCSE geometry. **Pythagoras' theorem** links the three sides; the **trigonometric ratios** link sides and angles. At Higher tier you apply them in 2D and 3D, inside other shapes (isosceles triangles, rectangles, cuboids, pyramids), and you must know the **exact values** of sin, cos and tan for the special angles without a calculator. All of these formulae must be learned — none is printed in the question.

### Pythagoras' theorem

In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $c$ (the longest side, opposite the right angle):
$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2.$$
- **Finding the hypotenuse:** square, **add**, square-root.
- **Finding a shorter side:** square, **subtract**, square-root: $a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$.

*Mini-example.* Sides 9 and 12: hypotenuse $= \sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15$. Hypotenuse 13, one side 5: other side $= \sqrt{169 - 25} = 12$.

The **converse**: if $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ for the three sides, the triangle is right-angled. On Paper 1, leave answers as surds when they are not whole: $\sqrt{4^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13}$.

### Trigonometric ratios

Label the sides relative to the angle $\theta$: **hypotenuse** (opposite the right angle), **opposite** (facing $\theta$), **adjacent** (next to $\theta$, not the hypotenuse).
$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}} \qquad (\text{SOH CAH TOA})$$

- **Finding a side:** pick the ratio using the side you know and the side you want, then rearrange. If the unknown is on top, multiply: $x = 10\sin 35° = 5.74$. If it is on the bottom, divide: $x = \dfrac{8}{\cos 50°} = 12.4$.
- **Finding an angle:** use the inverse function: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{7}{4} \Rightarrow \theta = \tan^{-1}(1.75) = 60.3°$.

An **angle of elevation** is measured up from the horizontal; an **angle of depression** is measured down from the horizontal. They are equal (alternate angles) when looking between two points.

### Non-right-angled triangles, where possible

"Where possible, general triangles" means creating a right angle. The key move: drop a **perpendicular**. An isosceles triangle splits into two congruent right-angled triangles; the height of any triangle makes two right-angled triangles you can solve one after the other.

*Mini-example.* An isosceles triangle has equal sides 10 cm and base 12 cm. The perpendicular from the apex bisects the base: height $= \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8$ cm, and each base angle $= \cos^{-1}\frac{6}{10} = 53.1°$.

### Pythagoras and trigonometry in 3D

Find a right-angled triangle that lies in a **flat plane** inside the solid, sketch it separately, and solve it. Often you need two triangles one after the other.

**Space diagonal of a cuboid** $a \times b \times c$: first the base diagonal $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, then up the height:
$$d = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}.$$
*Mini-example.* A cuboid 3 by 4 by 12: base diagonal $= 5$, space diagonal $= \sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$.

**Angle between a line and a plane**: drop a perpendicular from the top of the line to the plane; the angle is between the line and its "shadow" (projection) on the plane. For the space diagonal above, the angle with the base is $\tan^{-1}\frac{12}{5} = 67.4°$.

For a **square-based pyramid** with its apex above the centre, the key right-angled triangle joins the apex, the centre of the base and a corner (half the base diagonal).

### Exact trigonometric values

These come from two triangles. A **square of side 1** cut along its diagonal gives a $45°$ triangle with sides $1, 1, \sqrt2$. An **equilateral triangle of side 2** cut in half gives a $30°$–$60°$ triangle with sides $1, \sqrt3, 2$.

| $\theta$ | $0°$ | $30°$ | $45°$ | $60°$ | $90°$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin\theta$ | $0$ | $\frac12$ | $\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $\cos\theta$ | $1$ | $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$ | $\frac12$ | $0$ |
| $\tan\theta$ | $0$ | $\frac{\sqrt3}{3}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt3$ | — |

$\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$ is the same as $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$, and $\frac{\sqrt3}{3}$ is the same as $\frac{1}{\sqrt3}$. $\tan 90°$ is undefined, so it is not in the specification. Notice $\sin$ goes up $0, \frac12, \frac{\sqrt2}{2}, \frac{\sqrt3}{2}, 1$ and $\cos$ is the same list backwards.

*Mini-example.* A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 14 cm and an angle of $30°$. The side opposite it is $14\sin 30° = 14 \times \frac12 = 7$ cm, and the adjacent side is $14\cos 30° = 14 \times \frac{\sqrt3}{2} = 7\sqrt3$ cm — exact, no calculator.

::: terms
- **Hypotenuse**: the longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle.
- **Pythagoras' theorem**: in a right-angled triangle a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse.
- **Opposite and adjacent**: the side facing the angle being used, and the non-hypotenuse side next to it.
- **SOH CAH TOA**: sin = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, cos = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, tan = opposite ÷ adjacent.
- **Angle of elevation**: the angle measured upwards from the horizontal to a line of sight.
- **Angle of depression**: the angle measured downwards from the horizontal to a line of sight.
- **Exact value**: a value written with fractions and surds rather than a rounded decimal, such as sin 60° = √3/2.
- **Angle between a line and a plane**: the angle between the line and its projection (shadow) on the plane.
:::

::: method
1. **Sketch** the right-angled triangle on its own, even if it sits inside a bigger shape or a 3D solid. Mark the right angle and the sides you know and want.
2. **Choose the tool:** two sides known, third side wanted, no angle involved → Pythagoras. An angle involved → trigonometry.
3. **Pythagoras:** hypotenuse = square, add, root; shorter side = square, subtract, root. Check the hypotenuse is the longest side.
4. **Trigonometry:** label hyp/opp/adj from the angle, pick the ratio that uses the known side and the wanted side, write it as an equation, e.g. $\cos 40° = \dfrac{x}{12}$, then rearrange. For an angle use $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ or $\tan^{-1}$.
5. **3D:** find a flat right-angled triangle containing the wanted length or angle. If one of its sides is unknown (often a diagonal of the base), get it first from another triangle in the base.
6. **Paper 1 (non-calculator):** expect exact values for $30°$, $45°$ and $60°$, and answers as surds ("give your answer in the form $a\sqrt{b}$"). Simplify surds fully.
7. **Accuracy:** keep full calculator values between steps and round at the end (3 s.f. for lengths, 1 d.p. for angles unless told). Check your calculator is in **degrees**.
8. **"Show that"** answers must show the unrounded value, e.g. "$= 7.0710\ldots$, which rounds to 7.07".
:::

::: example Pythagoras in context
A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall with its foot 2.5 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse: $h = \sqrt{6.5^2 - 2.5^2} = \sqrt{42.25 - 6.25} = \sqrt{36} = 6$ m.
:::

::: example Angle of elevation
From a point 40 m from the foot of a tower, on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top is $32°$. Find the height of the tower.

Opposite (height) and adjacent (40 m) → tan: $\tan 32° = \dfrac{h}{40} \Rightarrow h = 40\tan 32° = 25.0$ m (3 s.f.).
:::

::: example Isosceles triangle split in two
An isosceles triangle has sides 9 cm, 9 cm and 14 cm. Find its angles and area.

The perpendicular from the apex bisects the 14 cm base, giving right-angled triangles with hypotenuse 9 and base 7.

Base angle $= \cos^{-1}\dfrac{7}{9} = 38.9°$ (both base angles). Apex angle $= 180° - 2 \times 38.94° = 102.1°$.

Height $= \sqrt{81 - 49} = \sqrt{32}$, so area $= \dfrac12 \times 14 \times \sqrt{32} = 39.6\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: example Exact values (non-calculator)
Triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $C$. $AC = 6$ cm and angle $BAC = 60°$. Find the exact length of $BC$ and the exact area of the triangle.

$\tan 60° = \dfrac{BC}{6} \Rightarrow BC = 6\sqrt3$ cm.

Area $= \dfrac12 \times 6 \times 6\sqrt3 = 18\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: example Cuboid diagonal and angle
A cuboid $ABCDEFGH$ has base $ABCD$ with $AB = 8$ cm, $BC = 6$ cm, and height $CG = 5$ cm. Find the length $AG$ and the angle $AG$ makes with the base.

Base diagonal $AC = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$ cm. Triangle $ACG$ has a right angle at $C$: $AG = \sqrt{10^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{125} = 5\sqrt5 = 11.2$ cm.

The projection of $AG$ on the base is $AC$, so the angle is $GAC = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{5}{10} = 26.6°$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: square-based pyramid
A pyramid has a square base $ABCD$ of side 10 cm and apex $V$ directly above the centre $M$ of the base. Each sloping edge is 13 cm. Find the height of the pyramid and the angle between $VA$ and the base.

$AC = \sqrt{10^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{200}$, so $AM = \frac12\sqrt{200} = \sqrt{50}$.

Triangle $VMA$ is right-angled at $M$: $VM = \sqrt{13^2 - 50} = \sqrt{119} = 10.9$ cm.

Angle $VAM = \cos^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{50}}{13} = 57.0°$ (1 d.p.).
:::

A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 17 cm and one other side 8 cm. Find the third side.
---
$\sqrt{17^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{289 - 64} = \sqrt{225} = 15$ cm.
:::

Find the exact length of the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 3 cm and 7 cm.
---
$\sqrt{9 + 49} = \sqrt{58}$ cm.
:::

::: q
In a right-angled triangle the hypotenuse is 15 cm and one angle is $42°$. Find the side opposite the $42°$ angle.
---
$x = 15\sin 42° = 10.0$ cm (3 s.f.).
:::

::: q
In a right-angled triangle the side opposite an angle of $28°$ is 9 cm. Find the adjacent side.
---
$\tan 28° = \dfrac{9}{x} \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{9}{\tan 28°} = 16.9$ cm.
:::

::: q
A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 11 cm and a side of 5 cm opposite angle $\theta$. Find $\theta$.
---
$\sin\theta = \dfrac{5}{11} \Rightarrow \theta = 27.0°$ (1 d.p.).
:::

::: q
Without a calculator, find the exact value of (a) $\sin 30° + \cos 60°$ (b) $\tan 45° \times \cos 30°$ (c) $(\sin 60°)^2$.
---
(a) $\frac12 + \frac12 = 1$. (b) $1 \times \frac{\sqrt3}{2} = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}$. (c) $\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac34$.
:::

::: q
From the top of an 80 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is $18°$. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff?
---
The angle of elevation from the boat is also $18°$: $\tan 18° = \dfrac{80}{d} \Rightarrow d = \dfrac{80}{\tan 18°} = 246$ m.
:::

::: q
A cuboid measures 4 cm by 3 cm by 12 cm (height). Find the length of its space diagonal and the angle it makes with the base.
---
Base diagonal $= 5$. Space diagonal $= \sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$ cm. Angle $= \tan^{-1}\frac{12}{5} = 67.4°$.
:::

::: q
Is a triangle with sides 20 cm, 21 cm and 29 cm right-angled? Show how you decide.
---
$20^2 + 21^2 = 400 + 441 = 841 = 29^2$, so yes, by the converse of Pythagoras (the right angle is opposite the 29 cm side).
:::

::: q
A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 12 cm and an angle of $30°$. Find the exact length of the side adjacent to the $30°$ angle.
---
$12\cos 30° = 12 \times \frac{\sqrt3}{2} = 6\sqrt3$ cm.
:::

::: watch
- **Adding instead of subtracting** in Pythagoras when finding a shorter side. Check: the hypotenuse must be the longest side.
- **Mislabelled sides:** opposite and adjacent depend on which angle you are using. Relabel for every new angle.
- **Calculator in radians** or gradians gives nonsense — check $\sin 30 = 0.5$ first.
- **Rounding mid-way** in two-step 3D problems: keep the base diagonal as $\sqrt{200}$ or the full calculator value.
- **3D angle with the wrong line:** the angle between a line and a plane is measured to the line's projection on the plane (usually a diagonal of the base), not to an edge.
- **Exact values:** mixing up $\sin 30° = \frac12$ with $\cos 30° = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}$. Sketch the half-equilateral triangle (sides $1, \sqrt3, 2$) if unsure.
:::

::: test
*Non-calculator.* $D$ lies on $AC$ and $BD$ is perpendicular to $AC$. $AB = 12$ cm, angle $BAD = 30°$ and angle $BCD = 45°$. Show that $AC = 6 + 6\sqrt3$ cm. (5)
---
$BD = 12\sin 30° = 6$ (M1 A1). $AD = 12\cos 30° = 6\sqrt3$ (M1). In triangle $BDC$, $\tan 45° = \frac{BD}{DC} = 1$, so $DC = 6$ (M1). $AC = AD + DC = 6 + 6\sqrt3$ cm (A1).
:::
