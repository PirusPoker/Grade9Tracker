## What it is

This topic is about measuring things in the right units: converting between **standard units** (length, area, volume, mass, time), working with **compound units** that combine two measures (speed, density, pressure, rates of pay, unit prices), and using **scales** on maps and scale drawings.

### Standard units

| Measure | Conversions to know |
|---|---|
| Length | $10\text{ mm} = 1\text{ cm}$, $100\text{ cm} = 1\text{ m}$, $1000\text{ m} = 1\text{ km}$ |
| Mass | $1000\text{ g} = 1\text{ kg}$, $1000\text{ kg} = 1\text{ tonne}$ |
| Capacity | $1000\text{ ml} = 1\text{ litre}$, $1\text{ ml} = 1\text{ cm}^3$, $1000\text{ litres} = 1\text{ m}^3$ |
| Time | $60\text{ s} = 1\text{ min}$, $60\text{ min} = 1\text{ h}$, $24\text{ h} = 1\text{ day}$ |

Converting to a **smaller** unit gives a **bigger** number (multiply); to a larger unit gives a smaller number (divide). $4.2\text{ km} = 4200\text{ m} = 420\,000\text{ cm}$.

**Time is not decimal.** $2.4$ hours is $2$ hours and $0.4 \times 60 = 24$ minutes, not 2 h 40 min. And $3$ h $15$ min is $3.25$ hours.

### Area and volume units

Square the length conversion for area, cube it for volume:

- $1\text{ m} = 100\text{ cm}$, so $1\text{ m}^2 = 100^2 = 10\,000\text{ cm}^2$ and $1\text{ m}^3 = 100^3 = 1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3$.
- $1\text{ cm}^2 = 100\text{ mm}^2$ and $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1000\text{ mm}^3$.

So $3.5\text{ m}^2 = 35\,000\text{ cm}^2$ and $450\text{ mm}^2 = 4.5\text{ cm}^2$. Picture a $1\text{ m}$ square: it is 100 cm by 100 cm, so it holds $10\,000$ one-centimetre squares.

### Compound units

A compound unit is one measure **per** another. Learn these three by heart, with their units:

$$\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} \qquad \text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}} \qquad \text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$$

Speed in km/h or m/s; density in g/cm³ or kg/m³; pressure in newtons per square metre (N/m², also called pascals). The unit tells you the formula: "g/cm³" means grams **divided by** cubic centimetres.

Rearrange by the formula triangle or by algebra: distance $=$ speed $\times$ time, mass $=$ density $\times$ volume, force $=$ pressure $\times$ area.

Other rates work the same way: pay in £ per hour, fuel use in miles per gallon, prices in pence per gram (used to find the **best buy**), flow in litres per minute.

**Converting a compound unit** means converting both parts. $72\text{ km/h}$: $72\,000\text{ m}$ in $3600\text{ s}$, so $72\,000 \div 3600 = 20\text{ m/s}$. (Shortcut: km/h $\div 3.6$ = m/s.)

For density, $1\text{ g/cm}^3 = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$ (a million cm³ in a m³, a thousand g in a kg).

### Algebraic contexts

The same reasoning works with letters. A car travels $d$ km in $m$ minutes. Its speed in km/h is $\dfrac{d}{m/60} = \dfrac{60d}{m}$; in m/s it is $\dfrac{1000d}{60m} = \dfrac{50d}{3m}$.

### Scale factors, scale diagrams and maps

A **scale drawing** is an enlargement of the real object by a **scale factor**. "1 cm represents 5 m" means every real length is $500$ times the drawn one.

A **map scale** written as a ratio $1 : 25\,000$ means 1 cm on the map is $25\,000$ cm $= 250$ m on the ground, so $4$ cm represents 1 km.

- Map to real: **multiply** by the scale, then convert units. $6.4\text{ cm} \times 25\,000 = 160\,000\text{ cm} = 1.6\text{ km}$.
- Real to map: convert to cm, then **divide**. $3\text{ km} = 300\,000\text{ cm}$, $\div 25\,000 = 12\text{ cm}$.

Scale drawings often combine with bearings and constructions (G15): measure a length on the drawing, then scale it up.

**Areas on maps** scale by the square of the length factor. On a $1 : 50\,000$ map, 1 cm represents $0.5$ km, so $1\text{ cm}^2$ represents $0.5^2 = 0.25\text{ km}^2$. A lake covering $12\text{ cm}^2$ on the map is $12 \times 0.25 = 3\text{ km}^2$ in real life.

::: terms
- **Compound unit**: a unit made from two measures, such as km/h, g/cm³ or N/m², meaning one quantity per unit of another.
- **Speed**: distance travelled divided by time taken.
- **Density**: mass divided by volume, often in g/cm³ or kg/m³.
- **Pressure**: force divided by the area it acts on, measured in N/m² (pascals).
- **Scale factor**: the number every length is multiplied by to go from the drawing to real life, or the reverse.
- **Map scale**: a ratio such as 1 : 25 000 comparing a length on the map with the real length in the same units.
- **Unit price**: the cost of one unit (per gram, per litre) used to compare value for money.
:::

::: method
1. **Write the units on every line.** Many conversion marks are lost by mixing cm and m, or minutes and hours, halfway through.
2. **Convert before you calculate.** Get everything into matching units first — a speed in km/h needs time in hours; a density in g/cm³ needs cm³.
3. **Area and volume:** square or cube the length factor. Never multiply m² by 100.
4. **Compound measures:** write the formula in words (M1 for a correct substitution), then the answer with its compound unit. If the question gives two parts to combine (e.g. two metals in an alloy), work out each **mass** separately — densities cannot be added.
5. **Time:** convert minutes to hours by dividing by 60 ($15$ min $= 0.25$ h). Give answers "in hours and minutes" when asked, not as a decimal.
6. **Best buy:** compare the same thing for each option (price per 100 g, or grams per £1) and **state** which is better value — the conclusion is the final mark.
7. **Maps:** multiply by the scale for map → real, divide for real → map; for areas use the scale squared.
8. **Paper:** these questions appear on all three papers. On Paper 1, expect easy numbers (divide by 3.6 using $72 = 3.6 \times 20$); on calculator papers, round only at the end to the accuracy asked.
:::

::: example Area units
A floor has area $3.5\text{ m}^2$. How many $\text{cm}^2$ is this? A tile has area $400\text{ cm}^2$; how many tiles are needed?

$3.5 \times 10\,000 = 35\,000\text{ cm}^2$. Tiles: $35\,000 \div 400 = 87.5$, so 88 tiles (round **up** — you cannot buy half a tile).
:::

::: example Speed, with time in hours and minutes
A train travels 234 km in 3 hours 15 minutes. Find its average speed in km/h, then in m/s.

$3$ h $15$ min $= 3.25$ h. Speed $= 234 \div 3.25 = 72$ km/h.

$72\text{ km/h} = 72\,000\text{ m} \div 3600\text{ s} = 20\text{ m/s}$.
:::

::: example Grade 6: pressure with area in cm²
A box weighs 600 N and rests on a face measuring 50 cm by 30 cm. Find the pressure in N/m².

Area $= 0.5 \times 0.3 = 0.15\text{ m}^2$ (convert the lengths **first**). Pressure $= 600 \div 0.15 = 4000\text{ N/m}^2$.
:::

::: example Grade 7: density of an alloy
300 cm³ of metal A (density $8\text{ g/cm}^3$) is mixed with 200 cm³ of metal B (density $3\text{ g/cm}^3$). Find the density of the alloy.

Mass of A $= 8 \times 300 = 2400$ g. Mass of B $= 3 \times 200 = 600$ g.

Total mass 3000 g, total volume 500 cm³. Density $= 3000 \div 500 = 6\text{ g/cm}^3$. (Averaging 8 and 3 would give the wrong answer, 5.5.)
:::

::: example Map scales both ways
A map has scale $1 : 25\,000$. (a) Two villages are 6.4 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they? (b) A path is 3 km long. How long is it on the map?

(a) $6.4 \times 25\,000 = 160\,000\text{ cm} = 1600\text{ m} = 1.6\text{ km}$.

(b) $3\text{ km} = 300\,000\text{ cm}$; $300\,000 \div 25\,000 = 12\text{ cm}$.
:::

::: example Grade 9: area on a map, and an algebraic rate
(a) On a map of scale $1 : 20\,000$ a forest covers $18\text{ cm}^2$. Find its real area in km².

(b) A tank of $2.4\text{ m}^3$ is filled at $x$ litres per minute. Find an expression for the time taken in hours.

(a) 1 cm represents $20\,000$ cm $= 0.2$ km, so $1\text{ cm}^2$ represents $0.2^2 = 0.04\text{ km}^2$. Area $= 18 \times 0.04 = 0.72\text{ km}^2$.

(b) $2.4\text{ m}^3 = 2400$ litres. Time $= \dfrac{2400}{x}$ minutes $= \dfrac{2400}{60x} = \dfrac{40}{x}$ hours.
:::

::: q
Convert 3500 ml to litres, 0.45 kg to grams, and 2.6 m³ to cm³.
---
$3.5$ litres; $450$ g; $2.6 \times 1\,000\,000 = 2\,600\,000\text{ cm}^3$.
:::

::: q
Convert $450\text{ mm}^2$ to cm² and $0.08\text{ m}^2$ to cm².
---
$450 \div 100 = 4.5\text{ cm}^2$; $0.08 \times 10\,000 = 800\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

::: q
Write 2.4 hours in hours and minutes, and 1 hour 48 minutes as a decimal of an hour.
---
$0.4 \times 60 = 24$: 2 h 24 min. $48 \div 60 = 0.8$: $1.8$ h.
:::

::: q
Convert 90 km/h to m/s, and 15 m/s to km/h.
---
$90\,000 \div 3600 = 25$ m/s. $15 \times 3600 = 54\,000$ m per hour $= 54$ km/h.
:::

::: q
Sam is paid £11.40 per hour and works 37.5 hours. How much is he paid?
---
$11.40 \times 37.5 = £427.50$.
:::

::: q
A stone of volume 250 cm³ has mass 675 g. Find its density.
---
$675 \div 250 = 2.7\text{ g/cm}^3$.
:::

::: q
A force of 2000 N produces a pressure of 500 N/m². Find the area it acts on.
---
Area $= \dfrac{\text{force}}{\text{pressure}} = 2000 \div 500 = 4\text{ m}^2$.
:::

::: q
A scale drawing uses 1 cm to represent 4 m. A room is 5.5 cm by 3.2 cm on the drawing. Find the real floor area.
---
Real: $22$ m by $12.8$ m. Area $= 22 \times 12.8 = 281.6\text{ m}^2$.
:::

::: q
On a map of scale $1 : 50\,000$, how long is a 5 km road?
---
$5\text{ km} = 500\,000\text{ cm}$; $500\,000 \div 50\,000 = 10$ cm.
:::

::: watch
- **Area and volume conversions with the length factor.** $1\text{ m}^2$ is $10\,000\text{ cm}^2$, not $100$; $1\text{ m}^3$ is $1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3$.
- **Decimal time.** $2.4$ h is 2 h 24 min; 1 h 15 min is $1.25$ h, not $1.15$.
- **Averaging densities or speeds.** Work with total mass ÷ total volume, total distance ÷ total time.
- **Pressure with lengths in cm** but an answer wanted in N/m² — convert the lengths to metres **before** finding the area.
- **Best buy with no conclusion.** Say which is the better value and why.
- **Map scales:** forgetting to convert cm to km at the end, or using the length scale for an area.
:::

Pack A holds 750 g of rice for £2.40. Pack B holds 1.2 kg for £3.72. Which is better value? Show your working. (3)
---

::: test
A map has scale $1 : 40\,000$. Two towns are 7.5 cm apart on the map. Work out the real distance in km. (2)
---
$7.5 \times 40\,000 = 300\,000$ cm (M1) $= 3$ km (A1).
:::

::: test
A gold bar is a cuboid 2 cm by 5 cm by 10 cm. Gold has density $19.3\text{ g/cm}^3$. Work out the mass of the bar in kg. (3)
---
Volume $= 100\text{ cm}^3$ (M1). Mass $= 19.3 \times 100 = 1930$ g (M1) $= 1.93$ kg (A1).
:::

::: test
A crate exerts a force of 720 N on the floor. The face in contact measures 40 cm by 30 cm. Work out the pressure in N/m². (4)
---
$0.4 \times 0.3 = 0.12\text{ m}^2$ (M1 for converting, M1 for area). Pressure $= 720 \div 0.12$ (M1) $= 6000\text{ N/m}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
60 cm³ of metal A with density $8.5\text{ g/cm}^3$ is mixed with 40 cm³ of metal B to make an alloy of density $7.3\text{ g/cm}^3$. Find the density of metal B. (4)
---
Alloy mass $= 7.3 \times 100 = 730$ g (M1). Mass of A $= 8.5 \times 60 = 510$ g (M1). Mass of B $= 220$ g (M1). Density of B $= 220 \div 40 = 5.5\text{ g/cm}^3$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A lake covers $5\text{ cm}^2$ on a map of scale $1 : 40\,000$. Work out the real area of the lake in km². (3)
---
1 cm represents $40\,000$ cm $= 0.4$ km (M1). $1\text{ cm}^2$ represents $0.4^2 = 0.16\text{ km}^2$ (M1). Area $= 5 \times 0.16 = 0.8\text{ km}^2$ (A1).
:::

::: test
A cyclist travels $y$ metres in $t$ seconds. Show that her average speed in km/h is $\dfrac{18y}{5t}$. (3)
---
Speed $= \dfrac{y}{t}$ m/s (M1). In one hour she travels $\dfrac{3600y}{t}$ m $= \dfrac{3.6y}{t}$ km (M1). $3.6 = \dfrac{18}{5}$, so speed $= \dfrac{18y}{5t}$ km/h (A1).
:::
