## What it is

**Standard form** is a compact way to write very large and very small numbers. A number is in standard form when it is written as

$$A \times 10^n \qquad\text{where } 1 \le A < 10 \text{ and } n \text{ is an integer}$$

So $A$ has exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point. The distance from Earth to the Sun, $150\,000\,000$ km, is $1.5 \times 10^8$ km; the mass of a hydrogen atom, about $0.000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,001\,67$ g, is $1.67 \times 10^{-24}$ g.

### Converting to and from ordinary numbers

The power of 10 counts how many places the digits move.

- **Large numbers** have a positive power: $47\,300 = 4.73 \times 10^4$ (the point moves 4 places to get from $4.73$ to $47\,300$).
- **Small numbers** (between 0 and 1) have a negative power: $0.000\,592 = 5.92 \times 10^{-4}$ (the first significant digit, 5, is in the fourth decimal place).
- **Numbers from 1 up to 10** have power 0: $6.1 = 6.1 \times 10^0$.

Going back: $3.08 \times 10^5 = 308\,000$ and $7 \times 10^{-3} = 0.007$.

**Not quite in standard form.** $32 \times 10^4$ and $0.6 \times 10^{-2}$ are correct values but $A$ is outside $1 \le A < 10$. Adjust the power to balance the change in $A$:

$$32 \times 10^4 = 3.2 \times 10^1 \times 10^4 = 3.2 \times 10^5 \qquad 0.6 \times 10^{-2} = 6 \times 10^{-1} \times 10^{-2} = 6 \times 10^{-3}$$

Rule of thumb: making $A$ smaller makes the power bigger, and vice versa.

### Ordering

Compare the powers first — the bigger the power, the bigger the number (for positive numbers). Only if the powers are equal compare the $A$ values. So $9.9 \times 10^{-5} < 1.2 \times 10^{-4} < 3 \times 10^{-4} < 2 \times 10^{3}$. Always put numbers into proper standard form before comparing: $45 \times 10^{3} = 4.5 \times 10^4$.

### Multiplying and dividing (no calculator)

Deal with the numbers and the powers of 10 separately, using the index laws, then tidy up.

$$(4 \times 10^5) \times (6 \times 10^{-2}) = 24 \times 10^{3} = 2.4 \times 10^4$$

$$\frac{3.6 \times 10^{4}}{9 \times 10^{-3}} = 0.4 \times 10^{7} = 4 \times 10^6$$

Powers of standard-form numbers: $(3 \times 10^{4})^2 = 9 \times 10^{8}$, and $\sqrt{1.6 \times 10^{9}} = \sqrt{16 \times 10^{8}} = 4 \times 10^4$ (make the power even before taking a square root).

### Adding and subtracting (no calculator)

The index laws do **not** help here. Either convert to ordinary numbers, or rewrite both with the same power of 10:

$$4.2 \times 10^5 + 3.5 \times 10^4 = 42 \times 10^4 + 3.5 \times 10^4 = 45.5 \times 10^4 = 4.55 \times 10^5$$

$$6 \times 10^{-3} - 8 \times 10^{-4} = 0.006 - 0.0008 = 0.0052 = 5.2 \times 10^{-3}$$

### Using a calculator

Enter standard form with the $\times 10^x$ key (often labelled EXP or $\times 10^x$), which keeps the number as one unit. Typing $\div 3 \times 10^{4}$ without that key or brackets divides by 3 and then multiplies by $10^4$ — wrong. Brackets round each standard-form number are the safe habit.

Calculators may display an answer such as `2.35E-7` or `2.35 × 10⁻⁷`. Write it properly as $2.35 \times 10^{-7}$; never copy "2.35E-7" or "2.35⁻⁷" as your answer.

### Interpreting standard form in context

Standard form turns up in real contexts — populations, distances in space, sizes of cells, computer memory. Typical questions:

- **How many times bigger?** Divide: a star $6 \times 10^{12}$ km away is $\dfrac{6 \times 10^{12}}{1.5 \times 10^8} = 4 \times 10^4$ times as far as the Sun.
- **Totals and averages:** a mass of $5.4 \times 10^{3}$ kg shared between $1.8 \times 10^{5}$ people is $3 \times 10^{-2}$ kg each, i.e. 30 g.
- **Units:** convert before comparing. $1$ nanometre $= 10^{-9}$ m; $1$ gigabyte $= 10^9$ bytes (in the decimal convention the exam uses unless told otherwise).

::: terms
- **Standard form**: a number written as A × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer.
- **Ordinary number**: a number written out in full, e.g. 47 300 rather than 4.73 × 10⁴.
- **Positive power of 10**: used for numbers of 10 or more; the power counts the places the digits move left.
- **Negative power of 10**: used for numbers between 0 and 1, e.g. 0.00059 = 5.9 × 10⁻⁴.
- **Index laws**: 10ᵃ × 10ᵇ = 10ᵃ⁺ᵇ and 10ᵃ ÷ 10ᵇ = 10ᵃ⁻ᵇ, used to multiply and divide in standard form.
- **Significant figures**: the digits of a number from the first non-zero digit, used for rounding A.
:::

::: method
1. **Convert:** count the places the digits move. Large number → positive power; small number → negative power. Check that $1 \le A < 10$.
2. **Paper 1 multiply/divide:** multiply or divide the $A$ values, combine the powers with the index laws, then adjust so $A$ is between 1 and 10. Write the intermediate line (e.g. $24 \times 10^3$) — it earns the method mark.
3. **Paper 1 add/subtract:** match the powers of 10 first (or convert to ordinary numbers), then add or subtract, then return to standard form.
4. **Calculator papers:** put each standard-form number in brackets or use the $\times 10^x$ key. Write down the full display before rounding, then round $A$ to the accuracy asked (often 3 s.f.).
5. **"Give your answer in standard form"** — an ordinary number, or $24 \times 10^3$, loses the final mark. If the question does not ask for standard form, either form is fine.
6. **Context:** decide whether to multiply or divide from the units ("per", "how many times", "each"), state the answer with units, and sense-check the size.
:::

::: example Converting
(a) Write $0.000\,0304$ in standard form. (b) Write $7.01 \times 10^6$ as an ordinary number. (c) Write $0.52 \times 10^{-3}$ in standard form.

(a) The first significant digit, 3, is in the 5th decimal place: $3.04 \times 10^{-5}$.

(b) $7\,010\,000$.

(c) $0.52 = 5.2 \times 10^{-1}$, so $5.2 \times 10^{-1} \times 10^{-3} = 5.2 \times 10^{-4}$.
:::

::: example Non-calculator multiply and divide
Work out, giving your answers in standard form: (a) $(5 \times 10^{-3}) \times (7 \times 10^{8})$, (b) $\dfrac{2.4 \times 10^{-2}}{8 \times 10^{5}}$.

(a) $35 \times 10^{5} = 3.5 \times 10^6$.

(b) $\dfrac{2.4}{8} = 0.3$ and $10^{-2-5} = 10^{-7}$, so $0.3 \times 10^{-7} = 3 \times 10^{-8}$.
:::

::: example Non-calculator addition
Work out $3.8 \times 10^{-4} + 5 \times 10^{-5}$, giving your answer in standard form.

Match powers: $3.8 \times 10^{-4} = 38 \times 10^{-5}$. Sum: $43 \times 10^{-5} = 4.3 \times 10^{-4}$.

(Check with ordinary numbers: $0.00038 + 0.00005 = 0.00043$. ✓)
:::

::: example Calculator: a formula
The speed of light is $3.00 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. Light from the Sun takes about $499$ seconds to reach Earth. Work out the distance from the Sun to Earth in km, in standard form to 3 significant figures.

Distance $= 3.00 \times 10^8 \times 499 = 1.497 \times 10^{11}$ m.

In km: $\div 1000$, giving $1.497 \times 10^{8}$ km $\approx 1.50 \times 10^8$ km.
:::

::: example Grade 8–9: algebra with standard form
$x = 4 \times 10^{n}$, where $n$ is an integer. Find, in standard form in terms of $n$, (a) $x^2$, (b) $\dfrac{1}{x}$.

(a) $x^2 = 16 \times 10^{2n} = 1.6 \times 10^{1} \times 10^{2n} = 1.6 \times 10^{2n + 1}$.

(b) $\dfrac{1}{4 \times 10^n} = 0.25 \times 10^{-n} = 2.5 \times 10^{-1} \times 10^{-n} = 2.5 \times 10^{-n-1}$.
:::

::: q
The population of a country is $6.7 \times 10^{7}$. Its area is $2.4 \times 10^{5}$ km². Work out the population density, to the nearest whole number of people per km².
---
$\dfrac{6.7 \times 10^7}{2.4 \times 10^5} = 279.16\ldots \approx 279$ people per km².
:::

::: q
A grain of sand has mass $6.5 \times 10^{-4}$ g. How many grains are in 2 kg of sand? Give your answer in standard form to 2 significant figures.
---
$2$ kg $= 2000$ g. $\dfrac{2000}{6.5 \times 10^{-4}} = 3.0769\ldots \times 10^{6} \approx 3.1 \times 10^6$ grains.
:::

::: watch
- **$A$ not between 1 and 10.** $24 \times 10^3$ is not standard form; it is $2.4 \times 10^4$. Adjusting $A$ down by a factor of 10 means the power goes **up** by 1.
- **Sign of the power.** Small numbers have negative powers: $0.004 = 4 \times 10^{-3}$, not $4 \times 10^{3}$.
- **Adding powers when adding numbers.** $2 \times 10^3 + 3 \times 10^4 \ne 5 \times 10^7$. Match the powers or use ordinary numbers.
- **Calculator entry without brackets.** $\div 4 \times 10^{-6}$ typed without the $\times 10^x$ key or brackets gives a wildly wrong answer.
- **Copying the display.** "3.1E6" is not an acceptable answer; write $3.1 \times 10^6$.
- **Ordering by $A$ alone.** $9 \times 10^{-5}$ is smaller than $1 \times 10^{-4}$ — compare powers first.
:::

$a = 5 \times 10^{m}$ and $b = 2 \times 10^{n}$, where $m$ and $n$ are integers. Find, in standard form in terms of $m$ and $n$, (a) $ab$, (b) $\dfrac{b}{a}$. (4)
---
(a) $10 \times 10^{m + n}$ (M1) $= 1 \times 10^{m + n + 1}$ (A1). (b) $0.4 \times 10^{n - m}$ (M1) $= 4 \times 10^{n - m - 1}$ (A1).

::: test
$\sqrt{\dfrac{x}{2.5 \times 10^{-3}}} = 4 \times 10^{5}$. Find $x$, giving your answer in standard form. (3)
---
Square both sides: $\dfrac{x}{2.5 \times 10^{-3}} = 16 \times 10^{10}$ (M1). $x = 16 \times 10^{10} \times 2.5 \times 10^{-3} = 40 \times 10^{7}$ (M1) $= 4 \times 10^{8}$ (A1).
:::
