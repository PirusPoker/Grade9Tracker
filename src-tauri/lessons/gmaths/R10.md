## What it is

Two quantities are **in proportion** when they are linked by a fixed multiplicative rule. There are two kinds you must recognise, work with, and spot on a graph or in a table.

### Direct proportion

$y$ is **directly proportional** to $x$ if multiplying $x$ by any number multiplies $y$ by the same number: double the number of tickets, double the cost. The ratio $y : x$ is always the same, so

$$y = kx \qquad \text{where } k = \frac{y}{x} \text{ is the constant of proportionality.}$$

**Graph:** a straight line **through the origin**, with gradient $k$. A straight line that does *not* pass through the origin (like a taxi fare with a fixed call-out charge, $C = 2x + 3$) is linear but **not** direct proportion — doubling $x$ does not double $C$.

**Unitary method.** Find the value for one, then multiply up. If $5$ pens cost $£3.40$, one pen costs $£0.68$, so $8$ pens cost $8 \times 0.68 = £5.44$. Or use a **scale factor**: $250$ g of flour makes $12$ biscuits, so $30$ biscuits need $250 \times \dfrac{30}{12} = 625$ g.

**Best buys** are direct proportion too: compare the cost of one unit (price per gram, grams per penny) for each option.

### Inverse proportion

$y$ is **inversely proportional** to $x$ if multiplying $x$ by a number *divides* $y$ by the same number: twice as many workers finish in half the time. The **product** $xy$ is always the same, so

$$xy = k \qquad \text{or} \qquad y = \frac{k}{x}.$$

**Graph:** a reciprocal curve $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$ — in real contexts just the part in the first quadrant, falling steeply then levelling out, never touching either axis.

**Method — find the total "job" first.** $4$ painters take $9$ days, so the job is $4 \times 9 = 36$ painter-days. With $6$ painters it takes $36 \div 6 = 6$ days. This assumes everyone works at the same rate.

### Deciding which it is from a table

| Test | Direct | Inverse |
|---|---|---|
| Constant quantity | $\dfrac{y}{x}$ is the same every time | $xy$ is the same every time |
| As $x$ goes up | $y$ goes up | $y$ goes down |
| Graph | line through $(0, 0)$ | curve $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$ |

$x: 2, 5, 8$ with $y: 7, 17.5, 28$ — every $\dfrac{y}{x} = 3.5$, so direct: $y = 3.5x$.

$x: 2, 4, 5$ with $y: 30, 15, 12$ — every $xy = 60$, so inverse: $y = \dfrac{60}{x}$.

If neither is constant, it is neither — "$y$ goes down as $x$ goes up" is **not** enough on its own to prove inverse proportion.

### Algebraic and graphical problems

When a question says "$y$ is directly proportional to $x$", write $y = kx$, use the given pair of values to find $k$, then use the formula. For inverse, write $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$. On a graph, read off one clear point (ideally where the line crosses grid-lines exactly) and find $k$ from it; for a direct proportion line, $k$ is the gradient.

### Problems with more than two quantities

Combine the ideas one at a time. If $6$ machines make $900$ bottles in $15$ hours, the work is $6 \times 15 = 90$ machine-hours for $900$ bottles, i.e. $0.1$ machine-hours per bottle. Output is directly proportional to machine-hours; for a fixed job, time is inversely proportional to the number of machines.

::: terms
- **Direct proportion**: two quantities whose ratio is constant, so y = kx and doubling one doubles the other.
- **Inverse proportion**: two quantities whose product is constant, so y = k/x and doubling one halves the other.
- **Constant of proportionality**: the fixed number k linking the two quantities in y = kx or y = k/x.
- **Unitary method**: finding the value for one item first, then multiplying to get the value for any number.
- **Best buy**: the option with the lowest cost per unit (or most units per pound).
- **Person-days (worker-hours)**: the total amount of work in a job, found by multiplying the number of workers by the time taken.
:::

::: method
1. **Decide direct or inverse.** Ask: "if one quantity doubles, does the other double (direct) or halve (inverse)?" More workers means *less* time: inverse. More items means *more* cost: direct.
2. **Direct:** find the value for one (unitary method) or a scale factor, then multiply. Algebraically, write $y = kx$ and find $k$ from the given pair.
3. **Inverse:** multiply to find the total (e.g. worker-days), then divide by the new number. Algebraically, $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$ with $k = xy$.
4. **Several quantities:** change one at a time, or work out the amount of work per unit of output (machine-hours per bottle).
5. **Tables:** show $\dfrac{y}{x}$ or $xy$ for **every** pair, then state the conclusion.
6. **Graphs:** direct proportion is a straight line through the origin; say both "straight line" and "through the origin" when explaining.
7. **Non-calculator paper:** choose friendly multipliers — to go from 12 to 30, use $\times \dfrac{5}{2}$. **Calculator paper:** keep $k$ unrounded in the memory.
8. **Round sensibly in context** — you cannot hire 7.5 pumps, so round up to 8 — and state any assumption (all workers work at the same rate) if asked.
:::

::: example Direct proportion by scale factor
A recipe for 12 biscuits uses 250 g of flour and 90 g of sugar. How much of each is needed for 30 biscuits?

Scale factor $\dfrac{30}{12} = 2.5$. Flour $250 \times 2.5 = 625$ g; sugar $90 \times 2.5 = 225$ g.
:::

::: example Inverse proportion
It takes 5 pumps 12 hours to empty a tank. The tank must be emptied in 8 hours. How many pumps are needed?

Total work $= 5 \times 12 = 60$ pump-hours. $60 \div 8 = 7.5$, so **8 pumps** are needed (7 would not be enough).
:::

::: example Identifying from a table
Decide whether each table shows direct proportion, inverse proportion or neither.

(a) $x: 3, 6, 9$; $y: 10, 5, \frac{10}{3}$. $\quad$ (b) $x: 1, 2, 3$; $y: 4, 7, 10$.

(a) $xy = 30, 30, 30$: inverse, $y = \dfrac{30}{x}$.

(b) $\dfrac{y}{x} = 4, 3.5, 3.33\ldots$ and $xy = 4, 14, 30$: neither. (It is linear, $y = 3x + 1$, but the line misses the origin.)
:::

::: example Machines (grade 7)
6 identical machines make 900 bottles in 15 hours. How long would 5 of the machines take to make 1200 bottles?

$6 \times 15 = 90$ machine-hours for 900 bottles, so $0.1$ machine-hours per bottle.

1200 bottles need $1200 \times 0.1 = 120$ machine-hours. With 5 machines: $120 \div 5 = 24$ hours.
:::

::: example Feed that runs out (grade 8)
A farmer has enough feed to last 30 cows for 24 days. After 4 days, he buys 10 more cows. For how many more days will the feed last?

Total feed $= 30 \times 24 = 720$ cow-days. After 4 days, $720 - 30 \times 4 = 600$ cow-days are left.

With 40 cows: $600 \div 40 = 15$ more days.
:::

::: example Percentage change in an inverse proportion (grade 9)
$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$. $x$ is increased by $25\%$. Find the percentage change in $y$.

$y = \dfrac{k}{x}$. New $y = \dfrac{k}{1.25x} = \dfrac{1}{1.25} \times \dfrac{k}{x} = 0.8y$.

$y$ decreases by $20\%$ — not $25\%$.
:::

::: q
5 pens cost £3.40. How much do 8 pens cost?
---
One pen: $3.40 \div 5 = £0.68$. Eight: $8 \times 0.68 = £5.44$.
:::

::: q
3 builders take 20 days to build an extension. How long would 5 builders take, working at the same rate?
---
$3 \times 20 = 60$ builder-days; $60 \div 5 = 12$ days.
:::

::: q
Rice is sold in a 400 g bag for £2.60 or a 750 g bag for £4.70. Which is better value?
---
$260 \div 400 = 0.65$p per gram; $470 \div 750 = 0.627$p per gram. The 750 g bag is better value.
:::

::: q
$y$ is directly proportional to $x$, and $y = 12$ when $x = 8$. Find $y$ when $x = 14$.
---
$k = \dfrac{12}{8} = 1.5$, so $y = 1.5x$. $y = 1.5 \times 14 = 21$.
:::

::: q
$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$, and $y = 6$ when $x = 10$. Find $y$ when $x = 4$, and $x$ when $y = 2.5$.
---
$k = 6 \times 10 = 60$, so $y = \dfrac{60}{x}$. $y = \dfrac{60}{4} = 15$. $x = \dfrac{60}{2.5} = 24$.
:::

::: q
A car uses 6.4 litres of fuel to travel 80 km. At the same rate, how far can it travel on 20 litres?
---
$80 \div 6.4 = 12.5$ km per litre; $12.5 \times 20 = 250$ km.
:::

::: q
A straight-line graph converting pounds to euros passes through $(0, 0)$ and $(50, 58)$. Convert £175 to euros.
---
Gradient $k = \dfrac{58}{50} = 1.16$ euros per pound. $175 \times 1.16 = €203$.
:::

::: q
A taxi firm charges £3 plus £2 per mile. Is the cost directly proportional to the distance? Explain.
---
No. $C = 2d + 3$; the graph is a straight line but it does not pass through the origin (0 miles still costs £3), so doubling the distance does not double the cost.
:::

::: q
Complete the table so that $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$: $x: 4, 8, \_\_, 32$; $y: 24, \_\_, 6, \_\_$.
---
$k = 4 \times 24 = 96$. $y = 96 \div 8 = 12$; $x = 96 \div 6 = 16$; $y = 96 \div 32 = 3$.
:::

::: watch
- **Check which kind it is before calculating.** Treating "more workers" as direct proportion gives more days, which is obviously wrong — sense-check every answer.
- **Linear is not the same as proportional.** A straight line has to go through the origin to show direct proportion; say both facts when explaining.
- **Show the test for every pair** in a table. One pair proves nothing.
- **Round in context.** 7.5 pumps means 8 pumps; 13.3 boxes needed means 14 boxes; 13.3 boxes affordable means 13.
- A percentage increase in $x$ does not give the same percentage decrease in $y$ for inverse proportion: $\times 1.25$ becomes $\div 1.25 = \times 0.8$.
- Best buys: compare like with like (both in pence per gram, or both grams per penny), then **state which is better**.
:::

6 cinema tickets cost £45. Work out the cost of 10 tickets. (2)
---
One ticket $= 45 \div 6 = £7.50$ (M1); $10 \times 7.50 = £75$ (A1).

::: test
8 workers take 15 days to build a wall. (a) How long would 12 workers take? (2) (b) State one assumption you have made. (1)
---
(a) $8 \times 15 = 120$ worker-days (M1); $120 \div 12 = 10$ days (A1). (b) All the workers work at the same rate (B1).
:::

::: test
Eggs are sold in boxes of 12 for £2.28 and in trays of 30 for £5.40. Which is the better value? Show your working. (3)
---
$228 \div 12 = 19$p per egg (M1); $540 \div 30 = 18$p per egg (A1). Trays of 30 are better value (A1).
:::

::: test
$y$ is directly proportional to $x$. When $x = 5$, $y = 32$. (a) Find a formula for $y$ in terms of $x$. (2) (b) Find $y$ when $x = 12.5$. (1) (c) Find $x$ when $y = 48$. (1)
---
(a) $y = kx$, $32 = 5k$ (M1), $y = 6.4x$ (A1). (b) $y = 6.4 \times 12.5 = 80$ (B1). (c) $x = 48 \div 6.4 = 7.5$ (B1).
:::

::: test
$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$. Complete the table. (3)

$x: 2, 5, \_\_, 20$; $\quad y: 45, \_\_, 9, \_\_$
---
$k = 2 \times 45 = 90$ (M1). $y = 90 \div 5 = 18$; $x = 90 \div 9 = 10$ (A1); $y = 90 \div 20 = 4.5$ (A1).
:::

::: test
The graph of the cost, $C$ pounds, of $w$ kg of cheese is a straight line through the origin and the point $(0.4, 3)$. (a) Explain how the graph shows that $C$ is directly proportional to $w$. (1) (b) Work out the cost of 1.5 kg. (2)
---
(a) It is a straight line through the origin (B1). (b) $k = 3 \div 0.4 = 7.5$ pounds per kg (M1); $1.5 \times 7.5 = £11.25$ (A1).
:::

::: test
6 identical machines make 900 bottles in 15 hours. How long would 5 of these machines take to make 1600 bottles? (3)
---
$6 \times 15 = 90$ machine-hours for 900 bottles (M1). 1600 bottles need $90 \times \dfrac{1600}{900} = 160$ machine-hours (M1). $160 \div 5 = 32$ hours (A1).
:::

::: test
A school has enough food to feed 120 students on a trip for 10 days. After 2 days, 40 more students join the trip. For how many more days will the food last? (3)
---
Total $= 120 \times 10 = 1200$ student-days (M1). Left after 2 days: $1200 - 240 = 960$ (M1). $960 \div 160 = 6$ more days (A1).
:::

::: test
$P$ is inversely proportional to $V$. $V$ is decreased by $20\%$. Find the percentage change in $P$. (3)
---
$P = \dfrac{k}{V}$ (M1). New $P = \dfrac{k}{0.8V} = 1.25 \times \dfrac{k}{V}$ (M1). $P$ increases by $25\%$ (A1).
:::
