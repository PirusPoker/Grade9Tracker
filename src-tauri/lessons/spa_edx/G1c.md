## What it is

This **Higher-only** lesson finishes Edexcel's list of pronouns and determiners. It adds:
- **aquel, aquella, aquellos, aquellas** (that, those: far away in space or time) and the neuter **aquello**;
- **nos** and **os** as object pronouns, and the plural reflexives **nos, os, se**, with one and two verbs;
- the relatives **lo que**, **el que** and **el cual** (with their feminine and plural forms) in subject clauses, and relative clauses with **cuando, donde, que**;
- the **possessive pronouns** el mío, el tuyo, el suyo, el nuestro, el vuestro, and mío or tuyo after ser;
- **pronouns after prepositions** (para mí, sin ti, con ella), **conmigo** and **contigo**, and the emphatic **a mí, a ti**.

Where it turns up:
- **Paper 4.** These are exactly the structures Pearson's writing grids reward as complex language: relative clauses, varied pronouns, longer sentences. "Lo que más me gusta es…" and "mi amiga, la que vive en Madrid, …" lift an AO3 mark straight away.
- **Paper 3 and Section B translation.** Lo que, el cual and aquel appear in longer texts. Lo que más me preocupa (what worries me most) is a phrase you must translate as a whole.
- **Paper 1 conversation.** A mí me encanta…, pero a mi hermano no le gusta… is natural, quick and shows control of pronouns.

### Aquel and aquello

| | masc. | fem. |
|---|---|---|
| that | aquel | aquella |
| those | aquellos | aquellas |

Spanish has three distances: **este** (this, near me), **ese** (that, near you), **aquel** (that over there, far from both of us). Aquel is also used for the **distant past**: **aquel verano** (that summer, long ago), **en aquellos días** (in those days). Mind the spelling: aquel has no -o in the masculine singular.

**Aquello** (neuter) means "that" for an idea, event or unknown thing far away, never with a noun: **¿Qué es aquello?** (What is that over there?) **Aquello fue increíble** (That was amazing, about an event in the past).

### Nos and os, and plural reflexives

| | us | you (vosotros) |
|---|---|---|
| direct and indirect object | nos | os |
| reflexive | nos | os |

The third person plural reflexive is **se**. The positions are the same as for the singular pronouns (G1b):
- **One verb: before it.** Os veo mañana. Nos dan un descuento. Nos levantamos a las siete. Se divierten mucho.
- **Two verbs: before the first, or on the end of the infinitive.** **Os puedo ver** = **Puedo veros**. Nos van a llamar = Van a llamarnos. Nos tenemos que levantar = Tenemos que levantarnos. ¿Os vais a quedar? = ¿Vais a quedaros?
- **Negative: no first.** No os entiendo. No nos podemos quedar.

Plural reflexives can be **reciprocal** (each other): **Nos vemos los viernes** (we see each other on Fridays). **Os entendéis bien** (you understand each other well). **Se escriben cada día** (they write to each other every day).

### Lo que

**Lo que** never changes. It means **what** (the thing that) or **which** (referring back to a whole idea):
- **Lo que más me gusta es el deporte.** = What I like most is sport.
- **No entiendo lo que pasa.** = I don't understand what is happening.
- **Lo que me preocupa es el cambio climático.** = What worries me is climate change.
- **Llegó tarde, lo que me molestó mucho.** = He arrived late, which annoyed me a lot.

Never use qué (with an accent) in these sentences: qué is only for questions and exclamations. "No sé qué hacer" (I don't know what to do) is an indirect question and is fine.

### El que and el cual

Both mean **who, which, the one that** and agree with the noun they refer to.

| | masc. sing. | fem. sing. | masc. pl. | fem. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| el que | el que | la que | los que | las que |
| el cual | el cual | la cual | los cuales | las cuales |

In Edexcel's list they appear in **subject relative clauses** (the relative is the subject of the next verb):
- **Mi tía, la que vive en Madrid, es médica.** = My aunt, the one who lives in Madrid, is a doctor.
- **Los que estudian mucho sacan buenas notas.** = Those who study a lot get good marks.
- **Tengo dos primos, los cuales viven en Ecuador.** = I have two cousins, who live in Ecuador.
- **Hay un parque nuevo, el cual tiene una piscina enorme.** = There is a new park, which has a huge swimming pool.

**El cual** is more formal and is mostly used after a comma (adding extra information). **El que** also works without a noun: **el que llega primero gana** (whoever arrives first wins). After a preposition (la casa en la que vivo, el amigo con el cual salgo) the same words are used in reading texts; in your own writing that use is not required, credited if correct.

**Quien** (who) is on the Higher vocabulary list. It refers only to people: **Ana, quien vive en mi calle, es mi mejor amiga**; **¿Con quién vas?** is the question form, with an accent.

### Relative clauses with donde, cuando and que

- **donde** (where): **el pueblo donde vivo** (the village where I live), el instituto donde estudio.
- **cuando** (when), usually after a comma: **en verano, cuando hace calor, vamos a la playa.**
- **que** (who, which, that) remains the everyday relative: la chica que canta, el tren que sale a las diez.

No accent on donde, cuando and que in these sentences; they only take one in questions (¿dónde?, ¿cuándo?, ¿qué?).

### Possessive pronouns

| owner | m. sing. | f. sing. | m. pl. | f. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mine | el mío | la mía | los míos | las mías |
| yours (tú) | el tuyo | la tuya | los tuyos | las tuyas |
| his, hers, theirs, yours (usted) | el suyo | la suya | los suyos | las suyas |
| ours | el nuestro | la nuestra | los nuestros | las nuestras |
| yours (vosotros) | el vuestro | la vuestra | los vuestros | las vuestras |

They replace a possessive + noun and agree with the **thing owned**: mi móvil → **el mío**; tu casa → **la tuya**; nuestros padres → **los nuestros**. **Mi móvil es más nuevo que el tuyo** (My mobile is newer than yours).

**After ser** you drop the article: **Esta casa es mía.** **¿Es tuyo este bolígrafo?** **Las llaves son nuestras.** (The possessive still agrees with the noun.)

### Pronouns after prepositions

After **para, sin, de, a, por, en, cerca de, delante de** and other prepositions, use **mí, ti, él, ella, usted, nosotros/as, vosotros/as, ellos/as, ustedes**:
- **Este regalo es para ti.** **No puedo vivir sin ella.** **Hablamos de vosotros.**
- **mí** has an accent (mi without one means "my"); **ti** never has one.
- With **con**: **conmigo** (with me) and **contigo** (with you); the rest are regular: con él, con nosotros, con usted. **Con mí** and **con ti** are always wrong.

**Emphatic a mí.** With gustar-type verbs and any indirect object, a + pronoun adds emphasis or contrast. The ordinary pronoun stays: **A mí me encanta el cine, pero a él no le gusta nada.** **¿Y a ti?** (And you?) is a natural follow-up in conversation. **A mí gusta** without me is wrong.

::: terms
- **aquel / aquella**: that, those (aquellos, aquellas) for things far away in space or time: aquel verano.
- **aquello**: neuter "that" for an idea or distant event, never with a noun: aquello fue increíble.
- **nos / os**: us and you (vosotros), as direct, indirect or reflexive pronouns: os veo, nos levantamos.
- **reciprocal**: a plural reflexive meaning "each other": nos vemos, se escriben.
- **lo que**: invariable "what" (the thing that) or "which" (referring to a whole idea).
- **el que / el cual**: who, which, the one that; agree with their noun (la que, los cuales).
- **possessive pronoun**: el mío, la tuya, los nuestros...; replaces a possessive + noun and agrees with the thing owned.
- **prepositional pronoun**: mí, ti, él, ella, nosotros...; the form used after para, sin, de, con.
- **conmigo / contigo**: with me, with you (tú); the only irregular forms after con.
- **emphatic a mí**: a + pronoun added for contrast; the object pronoun stays: a mí me gusta.
:::

::: method
1. **For "that" or "those"**, decide the distance: near you → ese; far away or long ago → aquel. For an idea with no noun, use eso or aquello.
2. **For us or you all**, use nos or os and place them like any pronoun: before the conjugated verb, or on the end of the infinitive.
3. **For "what" in a statement**, use lo que, never qué. For "which" referring to a whole idea, use lo que after a comma.
4. **For "who / which / the one that"** after a noun, use que in simple sentences; use el que or el cual (agreeing) after a comma or for "the one who".
5. **To replace "my X", "your X"**, use el mío, la tuya... with the noun's gender and number; after ser, drop the article.
6. **After a preposition**, switch to mí, ti, él...; with con use conmigo and contigo.
7. **For emphasis**, add a mí / a ti / a él but keep me / te / le before the verb.
:::

::: example Choosing the relative
Complete with lo que, la que or los cuales: (a) ___ más me gusta del verano es el buen tiempo. (b) Mi amiga, ___ vive en Medellín, viene a verme. (c) Tengo tres hermanos, ___ son mayores que yo. (3)

**Answer.**
- (a) **Lo que** (1): "what", no noun to agree with.
- (b) **la que** (1): refers to mi amiga, feminine singular; la cual also scores.
- (c) **los cuales** (1): refers to hermanos, masculine plural; los que also scores.
:::

::: example Possessives and prepositions
Translate into Spanish: "Your house is bigger than mine. Do you want to come with me?" (4)

**Answer.** Tu casa es más grande (1) que la mía (1): la because casa is feminine. ¿Quieres venir (1) conmigo? (1) Reject con mí.
:::

::: example Nos and os with two verbs
Translate into Spanish, giving both word orders for (b): (a) We get up at seven. (b) I can see you (vosotros) tomorrow. (3)

**Answer.** (a) Nos levantamos a las siete (1). (b) Os puedo ver mañana (1) / Puedo veros mañana (1).
:::

::: example Emphatic pronouns in conversation
Answer the conversation question in Spanish, contrasting yourself with a family member: ¿Te gusta la música clásica? (2)

**Answer.** A mí no me gusta nada la música clásica (1), pero a mi abuelo le encanta (1). A mí no me…: the emphatic pronoun, then the negative, then the ordinary pronoun. A mi abuelo le…: a + noun works the same way.
:::

::: example Reading: lo que decides the answer
Read the sentence and answer in English.

> Lo que más me gusta de mi instituto son los profesores, pero lo que no me gusta nada es el uniforme.

(a) What does the writer like most? (b) What does the writer not like at all? (2)

**Answer.** (a) The teachers (1). (b) The uniform (1). Each lo que introduces a different answer, so read past the comma before writing.

::: watch
- **Con mí and con ti do not exist**: conmigo, contigo. Para mí has an accent; para ti does not.
- **Keep the ordinary pronoun with a mí**: a mí me gusta, a ella le encanta. Leaving out me or le is a verb-formation error.
- **Lo que, not qué, for "what" in a statement**: lo que quiero es dormir. Qué belongs to questions.
- **El que and el cual agree with their noun**: la que, los cuales. A masculine form after a feminine noun is an agreement error.
- **Possessive pronouns agree with the thing owned**, not the owner: Marta's brother is su hermano, and "hers" (referring to the brother) is el suyo.
- **Aquel, not aquello, before a noun**: aquel día. Aquello stands alone.
- **No accent on relative donde, cuando, que**; only the question words have one.
:::

::: test
Translate this passage into English.

> Aquel verano fui a Ecuador con mi tía, la que trabaja en una escuela allí. Lo que más me gustó fue la naturaleza. Nos levantábamos muy temprano para ver los animales. Un día un chico del pueblo, el cual hablaba inglés, nos acompañó a la montaña. A mí me encantó, pero mi tía estaba muy cansada. (10)
---
Marked as a whole. **9–10**: the full meaning is transferred in natural English. **6–8**: most of the meaning comes across; occasional errors blur a detail. **3–5**: parts are transferred, but some errors lose the message. **1–2**: only isolated parts are clear.

**Model.** That summer I went to Ecuador with my aunt, the one who works at a school there. What I liked most was the nature. We used to get up very early to see the animals. One day a boy from the village, who spoke English, went with us to the mountain. I loved it, but my aunt was very tired.

**Key points:** aquel verano (that summer, long ago); la que (the one who); lo que más me gustó (what I liked most); nos levantábamos (we used to get up); el cual (who); nos acompañó (went with us); a mí me encantó (I loved it, with emphasis).
:::
