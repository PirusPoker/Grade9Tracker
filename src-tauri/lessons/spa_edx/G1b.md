## What it is

This lesson covers the **Foundation and Higher** determiners and pronouns on Edexcel's grammar list: the small words that point to a noun (**este, ese, cada, mismo, otro, todo, algún, ningún**), the possessives (**mi, tu, su, nuestro, vuestro**), the subject pronouns and when to leave them out, **one object or reflexive pronoun** with one verb, two verbs or a command, the question words **cuál, cuánto, quién**, relative **que**, neuter **esto** and **eso**, and the pronouns **alguno** and **ninguno**. The Higher-only pronouns (aquel, nos and os, lo que, el mío, conmigo) are in G1c.

Where it turns up:
- **Papers 2 and 3.** Pronouns decide who does what to whom. "Mi madre me llama" (my mum calls me) and "Mi madre la llama" (my mum calls her) differ by one small word, and in a short-answer question about who calls whom, that word is the answer.
- **Paper 4.** Pronouns are how you avoid repeating nouns, and Pearson's description of complex language includes **varied pronouns**. A **mismatched possessive** (mi amigos) is one of the errors its mark schemes name.
- **Paper 1 role play.** The teacher speaks as usted (¿Cómo puedo ayudarle?), and you must ask questions with ¿Cuánto…? ¿Cuál…? ¿Quién…?

### Demonstratives: this and that

| | masc. sing. | fem. sing. | masc. pl. | fem. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| this / these | este | esta | estos | estas |
| that / those | ese | esa | esos | esas |

**Este** is for something near the speaker (este libro, esta semana, estos días); **ese** for something near the listener or a little further away (esa chaqueta que llevas). Time words: esta mañana (this morning), este fin de semana, estas vacaciones. Note **este** (this) has no accent; **esté** is a different word, a form of estar.

### Indefinite determiners

- **cada** (each, every) never changes: cada día, cada semana, cada estudiante.
- **mismo** (same) agrees and comes after the article: el mismo día, la misma clase, los mismos amigos.
- **otro** (another, other) agrees and **never has un or una in front**: otro día, otra vez (again), otros países. Un otro is an English pattern.
- **todo** (all, every) agrees and is followed by the article: todo el día (all day), toda la semana, **todos los días** (every day), todas las noches. Todo el mundo = everyone.
- **algún, alguna, algunos, algunas** (some, any): ¿Tienes algún problema? Algunas tiendas abren el domingo.
- **ningún, ninguna** (no, not any) come with no before the verb: **No tengo ningún problema. No hay ninguna tienda.** The plurals ningunos and ningunas are rare.

Algún and ningún lose the final -o before a masculine singular noun. Pearson's list notes that the base forms alguno and ninguno will not be tested as determiners (they are pronouns, see below); the inflected forms algún, alguna, algunos, algunas, ningún, ninguna will be.

### Possessive adjectives

| owner | one thing | more than one thing |
|---|---|---|
| my | mi | mis |
| your (tú) | tu | tus |
| his, her, its, their, your (usted, ustedes) | su | sus |
| our | nuestro / nuestra | nuestros / nuestras |
| your (vosotros) | vuestro / vuestra | vuestros / vuestras |

The possessive agrees with the **thing owned**, not the owner: **mis amigos** (my friends), **nuestra casa** (our house), **sus hermanas** (his or her sisters). Mi amigos and nuestro casa are agreement errors. **Tu** (your) has no accent; **tú** (you) has one.

### Subject pronouns

yo, tú, él, ella, usted (I, you, he, she, you formal); nosotros/nosotras, vosotros/vosotras, ellos/ellas, ustedes (we, you, they, you formal plural). **Usted** and **ustedes** take the él and ellos verb forms: ¿Usted habla inglés? ¿Ustedes tienen habitaciones libres?

Spanish **leaves the subject pronoun out** by default, because the verb ending shows the person: juego al tenis, not yo juego al tenis. Use the pronoun only for:
- **a change of subject or contrast**: Yo prefiero la música pop, pero él prefiere el rock.
- **emphasis**: ¿Quién lo hizo? Yo.

**Algo** (something) and **alguien** (someone) never change: ¿Quieres algo? Alguien llama a la puerta.

### Object pronouns: one pronoun at a time

| | me | you (tú) | him, it (m) | her, it (f) | them (m) | them (f) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| direct | me | te | lo | la | los | las |
| indirect (to / for) | me | te | le | le | les | les |
| reflexive (myself…) | me | te | se | se | | |

**Direct or indirect?** A direct object receives the action: Veo **a Marta** → **La** veo. An indirect object is the person something is given, sent, said or shown **to**: Escribo un mensaje **a Marta** → **Le** escribo un mensaje. Verbs like dar, decir, escribir, mandar, enviar, explicar, mostrar, pedir and prestar usually take an indirect pronoun for the person. In Spain you will also hear le for a male person as a direct object (le veo); it is standard there and accepted.

**Where does it go?** Edexcel tests three positions, always with a **single** pronoun:
1. **One verb: before it.** Lo leo. Te doy el dinero. Se prepara. With a negative, no comes first: **No lo leo.**
2. **Two verbs (conjugated verb + infinitive): before the first verb, or joined to the end of the infinitive.** Lo puedo leer = Puedo leerlo. Te quiero dar = Quiero darte. Se debe preparar = Debe prepararse. Lo voy a comprar = Voy a comprarlo.
3. **Affirmative command: joined to the end.** ¡Léelo! ¡Dame! ¡Prepárate! ¡Cómpralo! ¡Llámame!

**The accent on commands.** Adding a syllable can move the natural stress, so write an accent to keep it where it was: lee + lo → **léelo**; compra + la → **cómprala**; prepara + te → **prepárate**; escucha + me → **escúchame**. One-syllable commands need no accent with one pronoun: **dame, dime, hazlo, ponlo**.

With the perfect tense the pronoun always goes before haber: **lo he leído**, never he leídolo. Spanish often repeats an indirect object for clarity, which is normal and correct: **Le compré un regalo a mi madre.**

Two pronouns together (se lo, me lo) are not on the list: not required, credited if correct.

### Question words

**¿Cuál? / ¿Cuáles?** (which, what) for a choice: ¿Cuál es tu asignatura favorita? ¿Cuáles prefieres? Before a noun, use **¿qué?**: ¿Qué película quieres ver?
**¿Cuánto? ¿Cuánta? ¿Cuántos? ¿Cuántas?** (how much, how many) agree with the noun: ¿Cuánto cuesta? ¿Cuánta agua bebes? ¿Cuántos hermanos tienes? ¿Cuántas personas hay?
**¿Quién? / ¿Quiénes?** (who): ¿Quién es? ¿Quiénes son tus mejores amigos?
Every question word carries an accent, and every question opens with **¿**.

### Que, esto and eso, alguno and ninguno

**Que** (who, which, that) joins a clause to a noun when the noun is the **subject** of the new verb: **el chico que vive en mi calle** (the boy who lives in my street), **la tienda que vende ropa de segunda mano**. English can sometimes drop "that"; Spanish never drops que. (Que also means "that" after verbs: dice que…)

**Esto** (this) and **eso** (that) refer to an idea or an unknown thing, never to a noun: **¿Qué es esto?** **Eso es verdad.** **Por eso** = that is why.

**Alguno / alguna** (one, some) and **ninguno / ninguna** (none, not one) stand alone as pronouns, agreeing with the noun they replace: ¿Hay alguna farmacia por aquí? — No, no hay ninguna. ¿Tienes algún libro en español? — Sí, tengo alguno. / No, no tengo ninguno.

::: terms
- **demonstrative**: este (this) and ese (that), agreeing with the noun: esta semana, esos chicos.
- **determiner**: a word before a noun that limits it: cada, mismo, otro, todo, algún, ningún, mi.
- **possessive adjective**: mi, tu, su, nuestro, vuestro; it agrees with the thing owned (mis amigos).
- **subject pronoun**: yo, tú, él, ella, usted...; normally omitted except for contrast or emphasis.
- **direct object pronoun**: me, te, lo, la, los, las; the person or thing receiving the action (la veo).
- **indirect object pronoun**: me, te, le, les; the person something goes to (le doy el libro).
- **reflexive pronoun**: me, te, se; the subject does the action to itself (me levanto).
- **two-verb construction**: verb + infinitive; the pronoun goes before the first verb or on the infinitive.
- **relative que**: who / which / that joining a clause to a noun: la chica que canta.
- **neuter pronoun**: esto and eso, for an idea or unknown thing: eso es verdad.
:::

::: method
1. **Match every determiner to its noun**: gender and number for este, ese, mismo, otro, todo, nuestro; mi, tu and su only change for number.
2. **Leave out yo, tú, él...** unless two people are being contrasted or one is stressed.
3. **To replace a noun with a pronoun**, ask: does the person or thing receive the action (lo, la, los, las), or is something given, said or sent to them (le, les)?
4. **Count the verbs.** One verb: pronoun before it. Verb + infinitive: before the first verb or on the end of the infinitive. Affirmative command: on the end.
5. **Check the stress** on a command with a pronoun and add an accent if it now falls three syllables from the end: ¡cómpralo!
6. **Put no in front of the whole group**: no lo sé, no te puedo ayudar.
7. **In reading**, track each pronoun back to its noun before you answer a "who" question.
:::

::: example One pronoun, three positions
Rewrite each sentence replacing the noun in bold with a pronoun: (a) Leo **la revista**. (b) Quiero comprar **el regalo**. (c) ¡Llama **a tus abuelos**! (3)

**Answer.**
- (a) **La leo.** La is feminine singular, before the one verb. (1)
- (b) **Lo quiero comprar** or **Quiero comprarlo.** Both positions score. (1)
- (c) **¡Llámalos!** Joined to the command; an accent keeps the stress on lla-. (1)
:::

::: example Direct or indirect?
Translate into Spanish: "I see my grandmother every week and I always give her a present." (3)

**Answer.** Veo a mi abuela (1) todas las semanas / cada semana (1) y siempre le doy un regalo (1). La abuela receives the seeing, but the present is given to her, so le, not la.

Complete the sentence with the correct forms: "___ (all) los días voy al instituto con ___ (the same) amigos, pero ___ (this) semana voy con ___ (another) chica." (4)

::: example Reading: who does what?
Read the sentence and answer in English.

> Mi hermana siempre me pide dinero, pero yo nunca le doy nada.

(a) Who asks for money? (b) What does the writer give? (2)

**Answer.** (a) The writer's sister (1): me pide = asks me. (b) Nothing (1): le doy = I give her, and nada makes it nothing. Yo is used here for the contrast with mi hermana.

::: q
Why is "Yo tengo quince años. Yo vivo en Londres. Yo juego al fútbol." poor style? (1)
---
The subject pronoun is repeated for no reason; the verb endings already show "I" (1). Use yo only for contrast or emphasis.

::: watch
- **No goes before the pronoun**: no lo sé, no te creo. "Lo no sé" is always wrong.
- **Possessives agree with the thing owned**: mis padres, nuestra clase, sus amigas. Mi amigos is the error Pearson's schemes name.
- **Otro never takes un or una**: otro día, otra vez.
- **Le for "to him / to her"**: dar, decir, escribir and mandar take le or les for the person. "La doy un regalo" is wrong.
- **Accents on commands with a pronoun**: ¡léelo!, ¡cómpralo!, ¡prepárate!; but dame, dime and hazlo have none.
- **Que is never dropped**: the film that won is la película que ganó.
- **¿Cuál? before a noun** is wrong in standard Spanish: ¿Qué libro? but ¿Cuál es tu libro favorito?
- **Overusing yo** makes writing sound like English; use it only for contrast or emphasis.
:::

::: test
Translate this passage into English.

> Cada verano mis padres alquilan la misma casa en la costa. Mi hermano siempre se levanta temprano para ir a la playa, pero yo prefiero dormir. ---
Marked as a whole. **9–10**: the full meaning is transferred in natural English. **6–8**: most of the meaning comes across; occasional errors blur a detail. **3–5**: parts are transferred, but some errors lose the message. **1–2**: only isolated parts are clear.

This year my aunt is going to come with us and wants to bring her dog. Every day my mum asks me for help in the kitchen, but I don't mind. Who wants to do anything else on holiday?

**Key points:** cada verano (every summer); la misma casa (the same house); yo for contrast; su perro (her dog, as tía is the owner); me pide ayuda (asks me for help); otra cosa (anything else / something else).
