## What it is

**Paper 1 Speaking (1SP1/1H)** is worth **50 marks, 25%** of the GCSE. Your teacher conducts it in April or May and records it, and Pearson marks the recording. It lasts **10–12 minutes**, after **15 minutes' preparation**, and the tasks always come in this order:

| task | what you do | time | marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Read aloud | read a 50–55-word text, then answer two unprepared questions | 2–2½ min | 8 + 4 = **12** |
| 2 Role play | five prompts in a formal setting: two questions to ask, one prompt in the future | 1–1½ min | **10** |
| 3 Picture task | describe one of two pictures, answer two questions, then hold a conversation | about 8 min | 8 + 4 + 16 = **28** |

### The preparation time

- **14 minutes in a supervised room**, with all three cards and **one sheet of A4** for notes. You may **annotate the read-aloud card**, but you may not write on the role-play or picture cards. There is no dictionary and no phone, and you may not practise the reading out loud yet.
- **1 minute in the exam room**, with the recording already running. You may now practise the text out loud. The teacher gives no help, then says the test is starting.
- **Hand each card back** after its task. Your notes go back at the end.

**Using the 14 minutes:** about 4 on the read aloud (mark the traps, below), 4 on the role play (write the Spanish for every prompt), and 6 on the picture (people, place, activity, opinion, and likely questions).

### Task 1: read aloud (8) and two questions (4)

An English sentence sets the scene, then you read **four sentences** on one thematic context. It is marked **as a whole** on how **clear and comprehensible** your pronunciation is. That depends on the **sound-symbol correspondences** (G8) and, at Higher, on **key stress**. The examiner first listens without the text and asks how much they understand.
- **7–8:** consistently clear; any slips don't affect the message.
- **5–6:** clear; slips have minimal impact.
- **3–4:** generally clear; slips have a limited impact.
- **1–2:** only sometimes clear; slips sometimes block the message.

An English accent costs nothing. What costs marks:
- **a sound so wrong the word can't be recognised**: ll said as English "l", j as English "j", qu as "kw";
- **a sound that makes a different Spanish word**: **está** read as esta; **cuarto** read as cuatro; **pero** for perro;
- **wrong stress on a verb ending**: **encontró** (he found) read as encontro;
- **English cognates**: hotel, internet, aeropuerto, habitación, wifi said as in English.

**Annotate the card like this.** Here is an original practice text, with the traps listed under it:

> Mi hermana pasa muchas horas en las redes sociales. Dice que es la mejor manera de hablar con sus amigos, pero a veces se acuesta muy tarde. Ayer encontró una aplicación nueva para hacer vídeos. Mis padres piensan que la tecnología es útil, aunque les preocupa la seguridad en internet.

- **silent h**: hermana, horas, hablar;
- **rolled r** at the start of redes; **single tapped r** in horas, manera, pero;
- **c before e/i = "th"**: sociales, dice, veces, aplicación;
- **j = "kh"**: mejor;
- **qu = "k"**: que, aunque;
- **stress marks**: encontró (en-kohn-TROH), aplicación, vídeos (BEE-deh-ohs), tecnología (tehk-noh-loh-KHEE-ah), útil (OO-teel), seguridad (ends in d: last syllable);
- **cognates said the Spanish way**: aplicación, tecnología, internet (een-tehr-NEHT).

**The two follow-up questions** are in the **present tense** and ask for your likes, dislikes, preferences or opinions on the topic of the text. The teacher reads them exactly as printed, and may repeat each twice but never rephrase it. Each is marked **2** (fully communicated), **1** (partly, with some ambiguity) or **0**. **A one-word answer can never score more than 1.**

| ¿Qué piensas de las redes sociales? | mark |
|---|---|
| Me encantan porque puedo hablar con mis amigos todos los días. | 2 |
| Redes sociales… muy divertido. | 1: an opinion, but unclear and without a verb |
| Tengo un móvil. | 0: does not answer the question |

One clear sentence with a reason is enough. Don't launch into a speech: the time belongs to Task 3.

### Task 2: role play (10)

The setting comes from a fixed list: a café or restaurant; a shop, market or shopping centre; a hotel; a train station; a tourist information office; a cinema, theatre or concert hall; a campsite; a leisure centre; a doctor's surgery or hospital; in town. The card gives the scene and **five prompts in English**. The teacher plays the Spanish speaker, usually with **usted**.

At Higher, **two prompts make you ask a question**, and **one asks for a future time frame**. The rest need the present, or **me gustaría** / **quisiera**. Each prompt scores **2 / 1 / 0** for **communication only**. Grammar errors don't matter if the message is clear, but a one-word answer is capped at 1. You do not need to use usted yourself.

**Question toolkit**, which fits almost any card: **¿Cuánto cuesta…?** (How much is…?), **¿A qué hora abre / sale / empieza…?** (What time does … open / leave / start?), **¿Hay… cerca de aquí?** (Is there … near here?), **¿Se puede…?** (Is it possible to…?), **¿Dónde está…?**, **¿Tiene…?** (Do you have…?), **¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?**

Make your voice **rise at the end** so it sounds like a question. A statement such as "Me gusta el museo" does not answer an "Ask…" prompt.

**Future toolkit:** **voy a + infinitive** (voy a visitar…), or the future tense (visitaré…). Add a time phrase: **mañana, este fin de semana, el año que viene**.

### Task 3: the picture task (28)

**Two weeks before** the exam, you choose one of two thematic contexts that Pearson picks at random. On the day, the card shows **two colour pictures** from that context. The teacher asks which one you have chosen, then tells you to describe it.

**Description (8 = AO2 4 + AO3 4).** Cover the three bullets: **people, location, activity**. If you stop, the teacher can prompt with **¿Y la gente? ¿Y dónde están? ¿Y qué están haciendo? ¿Algo más?**
- **AO2 (4)**: the top mark needs all three bullets, with consistent, detailed development of different aspects, easily understood. Lower marks have less development and less clarity, down to two bullets covered with some development.
- **AO3 (4)**: the top mark needs a wide range of vocabulary and structures, with consistently accurate language. Lower marks have less range and more errors.

Use these building blocks:
- **en la foto hay…**; **a la izquierda / a la derecha / al fondo** (in the background);
- **lleva / llevan…** (is / are wearing);
- **están en…**, **parece que…**, **creo que…**, **seguramente** (probably);
- the present continuous: **están comiendo, está riéndose**;
- an opinion to finish: **me parece que…, en mi opinión…**

**Model.** The picture shows a family having a picnic under a tree in a sunny park; people are cycling in the background.

> En la foto hay una familia: una madre, un padre y dos niños. Están en un parque y hace sol, así que creo que es verano. Están sentados debajo de un árbol y están comiendo bocadillos y fruta. El padre lleva una camiseta azul y la niña lleva gafas de sol. Al fondo hay gente que está montando en bicicleta. Parece que todos lo están pasando muy bien. En mi opinión, es una buena manera de pasar el fin de semana, porque es sano y no cuesta mucho.

**Two compulsory questions (4)** follow, about your own experience of the picture's topic. **The first is in the present and the second is in the past**, and each scores **2 / 1 / 0**. Answer the past one in the past, with a detail:
- **¿Qué haces normalmente con tu familia los fines de semana?** Normalmente vamos a casa de mis abuelos y comemos todos juntos.
- **¿Qué hiciste el verano pasado?** El verano pasado fuimos a la costa y pasamos mucho tiempo en la playa.

**Conversation (16 = AO1 12 + AO3 4).** This lasts **5–5½ minutes**. The teacher must start from a **set subject** on the card, then moves around the whole thematic context with present, past and future questions.
- **AO1 (12)** rewards answers that are relevant, extended, developed and easy to understand. **10–12** needs this consistently, **7–9** frequently, **4–6** sometimes and **1–3** only occasionally.
- **AO3 (4)** rewards range and complex language, **three time frames** used successfully, and accuracy. **4** needs all three time frames consistently, wide range, frequent complex language, and only minor errors. **3** needs at least two time frames used frequently and some complex language.

**Develop every answer** with two or three of these: a **reason** (porque, ya que), an **example** (por ejemplo), a **contrast** (sin embargo, aunque), **another person's view** (mis padres piensan que…), a **time shift** (antes…, ahora…, en el futuro…), or a **Higher structure** (cuando sea mayor, me gustaría, lo bueno es que, desde hace).

**Model exchange** (lifestyle and wellbeing):
- **¿Qué haces para llevar una vida sana?** Intento comer mucha fruta y verdura, y juego al baloncesto dos veces por semana con el equipo del instituto. Sin embargo, me encanta el chocolate, así que no siempre como bien. Mis padres dicen que debo dormir más, porque me quedo hasta tarde con el móvil.
- **¿Qué comiste ayer?** A mediodía comí un bocadillo de queso en el instituto y por la noche mi padre preparó pescado con ensalada, que estaba riquísimo. Después salí a correr con mi hermana, aunque estaba bastante cansado.
- **¿Qué vas a hacer en el futuro para mejorar tu salud?** El año que viene voy a hacer más deporte y voy a intentar acostarme antes de las once. Cuando sea mayor, me gustaría correr una maratón, pero primero tendré que entrenar mucho.

If you do not understand, say **¿Puede repetir la pregunta, por favor?** Silence scores nothing.

**Inclusive language.** Pearson credits non-binary pronouns and agreements (**elle**, **-e** endings) when they are used correctly and consistently. The list includes **no binario** and **transgénero**. The standard forms always earn full marks.

::: terms
- **read aloud**: Task 1, a 50–55-word text marked out of 8 for clear pronunciation, judged on SSCs and key stress.
- **follow-up questions**: two unprepared present-tense opinion questions after the read aloud, worth 2 marks each.
- **one-word cap**: the rule that a one-word answer scores at most 1 of the 2 marks.
- **role play**: Task 2, five prompts in a formal transactional setting, 2 marks each for communication only.
- **"Ask" prompt**: a Higher role-play prompt that must be answered with a real question in Spanish.
- **future prompt**: the role-play prompt that needs a future time frame: voy a… or the future tense.
- **picture description**: Task 3 part 1, covering people, location and activity (AO2 4 + AO3 4).
- **compulsory questions**: two questions after the description, the first present, the second past.
- **conversation**: 5–5½ minutes from a set subject in the chosen context (AO1 12 + AO3 4).
- **development**: an extra clause that adds a reason, example, contrast, opinion or time shift to an answer.
- **time frames**: past, present and future; using all three successfully is needed for the top AO3 mark.
:::

::: method
1. **In the 14 minutes:** annotate the read-aloud card (silent h, ll, j, ge/gi, ce/ci, z, ñ, rr, qu/gu, stressed syllables); write a Spanish answer for each role-play prompt; plan the picture as people, place, activity, opinion.
2. **In the 1 minute:** say the hardest words of the text out loud.
3. **Read aloud** steadily, keeping vowels pure and stress where your marks show it. Correct a slip calmly and carry on.
4. **Follow-up questions:** give one full sentence with an opinion and a reason, in the present.
5. **Role play:** answer each prompt with a short full sentence; make "Ask" prompts real questions; use voy a + infinitive for the future prompt.
6. **Picture:** cover people, location and activity, then add details and an opinion; answer the second compulsory question in the past.
7. **Conversation:** develop every answer with a reason plus one more idea, and get all three time frames in more than once.
:::

::: example A 2-mark follow-up answer
The teacher asks: ¿Qué te gusta hacer en internet? Give a 2-mark answer, and say why "Vídeos" scores only 1. (2)

**Answer.** **Me gusta ver vídeos de música y jugar en línea con mi primo.** (1) It is a full, clear answer in the present with two details. "Vídeos" is a single word, so it is capped at 1 (1).
:::

::: example Role play: at the train station
You are at the ticket office of a train station in Spain. The teacher is the employee.
1. Say where you want to travel to. (Buenos días. ¿En qué puedo ayudarle?)
2. Say how many people are travelling. (Muy bien. ¿Para cuántas personas?)
3. Ask a question about the time of the train. (Vale. ¿Tiene alguna pregunta?)
4. Say what you will do at your destination. (El tren sale a las diez. ¿Qué va a hacer en Madrid?)
5. Ask a question about food. (Aquí tiene los billetes. ¿Tiene otra pregunta?) (10)

**Answer.** 2 marks each.
1. **Quiero un billete para Madrid, por favor.**
2. **Somos dos personas, mi hermano y yo.**
3. **¿A qué hora llega el tren a Madrid?**
4. **Voy a visitar a mis abuelos y vamos a ir al museo.** Future time frame.
5. **¿Se puede comprar comida en el tren?**

"Madrid." alone for prompt 1 would score 1.
:::

::: example Developing a conversation answer
Develop this answer so it shows two time frames and a reason: "¿Te gusta tu instituto?" "Sí, me gusta." (4)

**Answer.** **Sí, me gusta mucho mi instituto porque los profesores son simpáticos y tengo muchos amigos** (1: opinion + reason). **El año pasado fuimos de excursión a Madrid y lo pasamos genial** (1: past). **Sin embargo, creo que hay demasiados deberes** (1: contrast). **Cuando sea mayor, me gustaría ser profesora** (1: future, complex language).
:::

::: watch
- **One-word answers are capped at 1 mark** in the follow-up questions, the role play and the compulsory questions. Always give a short full sentence.
- **"Ask a question" means a real question**: ¿Cuánto cuesta…? with a rising voice, not a statement.
- **The future prompt needs a clear future time frame**: voy a… or the future tense. A past-tense answer only partly communicates.
- **Cognates said the English way can make a word unrecognisable**: hotel, internet, aeropuerto, habitación.
- **Wrong stress on a verb ending changes the tense**: encontró, desayuno / desayunó.
- **Confusable words** such as cuatro / cuarto and pero / perro can make the message ambiguous.
- **Describe all three picture bullets**: people, location, activity. Then develop them.
- **Answer the second compulsory question in the past.**
- **In the conversation, short answers starve both grids**: develop every answer and use all three time frames more than once.
:::
