## What it is

**Paper 2 Listening (1SP1/2H)** lasts **60 minutes**, including **5 minutes' reading time** at the start. It is worth **50 marks, 25%** of the GCSE, and has two sections:

| section | what you do | marks |
|---|---|---|
| A Listening comprehension | about nine questions in English on recorded extracts; answers in English | **40** |
| B Dictation | write six short Spanish sentences you hear | **10** |

**How Section A works:**
- Each extract is played **three times**, with a pause after each question. You may write at any time.
- The extracts total about 700–850 words, spoken in standard Spanish at a moderate pace. They are interviews, conversations, announcements, adverts and reports across all six thematic contexts.
- Tariffs are small and varied. In Pearson's sample Higher paper the questions are worth 3, 5, 3, 4, 3, 3, 6, 6 and 7 marks, and they get harder through the paper.
- **There are no marks for English spelling**, but an answer must be clear and must answer the question asked.

### The question types

| type | instruction | the trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiple choice** | complete the sentence: "put a cross in the correct box" (A, B or C) | all three options are often mentioned; only one fits the speaker, the time and the question |
| **Multiple response** | "put a cross in each one of the three correct boxes" from six | wrong options are mentioned but negated, belong to someone else, or are only nearly right |
| **Gap-fill from a box** | complete each English sentence with a word or phrase from a box; there are more words than gaps | the box word is a **synonym** of what you hear, not a cognate; the same topic words appear in several options |
| **Short answers / tables** | "complete the table in English; you do not need to write in full sentences" | you need the exact detail asked for, such as a reason, a frequency, an advantage or a disadvantage |

### Using the five minutes' reading time

1. **Read every question** and underline the key words: **who** (which speaker), **when** (now, last year, in the future), and **what** (a reason, a problem, how often).
2. **Predict the Spanish.** For "how often", expect **una vez / dos veces por semana, todos los días, a veces, nunca**. For a time, expect **a las ocho y cuarto, a mediodía**. For a disadvantage, expect **lo malo / lo peor es que…, el problema es que…**. For the future, expect **voy a…, el año que viene, cuando sea mayor**.
3. **Translate the box words** into Spanish in your head. "Expensive" may be **caro** or **cuesta mucho**; "quiet" may be **tranquilo** or **no hay mucho ruido**.
4. **Note the tariff**: 2 marks for a table cell means two details, or one detail in each of two cells.

### Using the three plays

- **First play:** get the gist and pencil in a first answer.
- **Second play:** confirm or change it, and fill the gaps.
- **Third play:** check the details that decide the mark: negatives, numbers, times and who said it.

### The distractors

Pearson's extracts are written so that the obvious word is often the wrong answer. Listen for the words that flip or limit meaning:
- **Contrast**: **pero, sin embargo, aunque, en cambio**. The speaker says one thing, then the opposite.
- **Negation**: **no, nunca, ya no** (no longer), **todavía no** (not yet), **tampoco** (not either), **ni… ni…**, **nadie, nada**.
- **A change of plan**: **pensábamos ir… pero al final…**, **iba a…, pero…**
- **Time**: **antes** (before), **ahora** (now), **cuando era pequeño** (as a child), **el año que viene** (next year). An option that was true in the past is often the trap for a question about now.
- **Other people**: **mi hermano prefiere…, mis padres dicen que…** The opinion is not the speaker's own.
- **Numbers and times**: **cuatro / cuarto** (four / quarter), **dos / doce**, **seis / siete**, **sesenta / setenta**, **y cuarto / menos cuarto** (quarter past / quarter to), **y media** (half past).

### Answering in English

- **Give the detail asked for, in as few words as make it clear.** You do not need full sentences.
- **An isolated word that does not answer the question scores 0.** If the answer is "it has a wide range of shops", then "shops" alone does not show what the speaker said about them.
- **Don't add a second, contradictory answer** to be safe; a wrong extra detail can cost the mark.
- **Keep the time frame and the person right**: "he used to swim" is not "he swims".
- **Never leave a box blank.** A sensible guess can score; a blank cannot.

### Section B: the dictation

You hear **six sentences**, each of **6–7 words**, and each is read **three times**. Then you have **2 minutes** to check.
- **Sentences 1 and 2** are printed with gaps. You write only the missing words.
- **Sentences 3 to 6** you write in full.

**Scoring.** There are **30 rewardable words**, converted on a sliding scale at three words per mark: 28–30 = 10, 25–27 = 9, 22–24 = 8, and so on down to 1–3 = 1.

**A word is rewardable** if it is spelt exactly, or with a slip that keeps the sound: **b / v** (vastante), **y / ll** (yevar), **ge / je** (jente), **gi / ji** (colejio).

**A word is not rewardable**:
- if it breaks a sound-symbol rule: **gitarra, dansa, pequeno, passar**, or **pero** for perro;
- if it is so phonetic that it cannot be recognised on its own;
- **at Higher**, if a stress accent is missing or added where it should not be: **movil, telefono, sociáles**.

Pearson builds **two stress-test words** into every Higher dictation, and **words not on the vocabulary list** can appear. So rely on the SSC rules (G8), not on memory.

**Dictation routine:**
1. **First hearing:** write every word you catch, leaving gaps.
2. **Second hearing:** fill the gaps. Use grammar to help: a plural noun needs a plural adjective (**las casas blancas**), and the verb ending must fit the subject (**mis padres trabajan**).
3. **Third hearing:** listen for the stressed syllable in each long word and check the accent rules.
4. **In the 2 minutes:** check for ñ, rr, qu/gu, a silent h, and accents on verb endings (**habló**, **está**).

::: terms
- **reading time**: the 5 minutes before the first extract, used to underline key words and predict the Spanish.
- **extract**: one recording, played three times, followed by a pause.
- **distractor**: an option that is mentioned in the extract but is wrong because it is negated, past, or someone else's view.
- **multiple response**: a question asking for the three correct statements out of six.
- **gap-fill from a box**: English sentences to complete with box words; there are more words than gaps.
- **isolated word**: a single word that does not answer the question; it scores 0.
- **time frame**: whether a detail is past, present or future; questions often turn on it.
- **dictation**: Section B; six sentences, 30 rewardable words, three words per mark.
- **rewardable word**: a dictation word spelt exactly or with a tolerated slip (b/v, y/ll, ge/je, gi/ji).
- **stress-test word**: one of the two Higher dictation words whose accent (or lack of one) decides whether it scores.
:::

::: method
1. **Reading time:** underline who, when and what; predict Spanish numbers, times, frequency words and synonyms for the box words.
2. **First play:** get the gist and pencil in an answer.
3. **Second play:** listen for pero, sin embargo, ya no, antes and ahora, and change the answer if the meaning flips.
4. **Third play:** check numbers, times, negatives and which speaker said it.
5. **Write the precise detail in English**, in the right time frame; never just an isolated word.
6. **Dictation:** write on the first hearing, fill gaps on the second, check stress and accents on the third.
7. **Final 2 minutes:** check ñ, rr, qu/gu, silent h, agreements and verb-ending accents.
:::

::: example Predicting in the reading time
A question reads: "(a) How often does Gaby train? (b) What is the disadvantage of the gym? (c) What will she do next summer?" Note the Spanish you expect to hear for each. (3)

**Answer.**
- (a) Frequency words: **dos veces por semana, todos los días, los fines de semana, a veces** (1).
- (b) **lo malo / lo peor es que…, el problema es que…, sin embargo**, followed by a reason such as **es caro, hay mucha gente** (1).
- (c) A future marker: **el verano que viene / el próximo verano** with **voy a…** or a future tense (1).
:::

::: example Beating the distractor
Listening practice (read the transcript once, then cover it).

> Antes jugaba al fútbol todos los sábados, pero ya no tengo tiempo. Ahora voy al gimnasio dos veces por semana con mi hermano. Él prefiere correr en el parque, aunque yo creo que el gimnasio es mejor.

Now the speaker… A plays football every Saturday; B goes to the gym twice a week; C runs in the park. (1)

**Answer.** **B** (1). A was true before (antes… ya no). C is the brother's preference, not the speaker's.
:::

::: example A table answer that scores
Listening practice (read the transcript once, then cover it).

> Mi barrio tiene muchas ventajas. Lo mejor es que hay un mercado enorme con todo tipo de fruta y verdura. Lo peor es que no hay nada para los jóvenes por la noche.

Complete the table. Advantage (1); Disadvantage (1). (2)

**Answer.** Advantage: **a huge market with all kinds of fruit and veg** (1). "Fruit" alone is an isolated word and scores 0. Disadvantage: **nothing for young people at night** (1). "Young people" alone scores 0.
:::

::: example A dictation sentence
Write the sentence in Spanish. It sounds like "mee ah-BWEH-lah BEE-beh THEHR-kah deh lah ehs-tah-THYOHN". (7)

**Answer.** **Mi abuela vive cerca de la estación.** (1 per word)
- Accept **avuela** and **bibe** (b / v).
- **Cerca** must have c, because s would sound "s" rather than "th".
- **Estación** is a stress-test word: last-syllable stress on a word ending in n, so it needs the accent.
:::

::: example Converting a dictation score
A student's six sentences contain 26 rewardable words. What mark do they get, and how many more words would they need for full marks? (2)

**Answer.** 25–27 words = **9 marks** (1). Two more words (28) would reach 10 (1).
:::

::: watch
- **The word you recognise is often the trap.** Check whether it is negated (ya no, nunca), past (antes), or someone else's view.
- **A change of mind decides the answer**: pensábamos… pero al final…
- **Isolated words score 0**: give the detail that answers the question.
- **Keep the time frame**: a question about now is not answered by what someone used to do.
- **Numbers and times trip people up**: cuatro / cuarto, y cuarto / menos cuarto, sesenta / setenta.
- **Use the box words as synonyms**: you will rarely hear the English word's cognate.
- **In the dictation, a broken SSC loses the word**: gitarra, dansa, pequeno, passar, pero for perro.
- **At Higher, a missing or extra stress accent loses the word**: móvil, teléfono, estación; never sociáles.
:::

::: test
Listening practice (read the transcript once, then cover it). Sofía talks about her Saturday job.

> Trabajo en un café del centro los sábados desde hace dos meses. Antes trabajaba en una tienda de ropa, pero no me gustaba nada. Lo que más me gusta ahora es hablar con los clientes, aunque a veces hay demasiado trabajo. El año que viene voy a dejar el trabajo para estudiar más, porque tengo exámenes importantes.

Write the letter of the correct ending. (a) Sofía has worked at the café for… A two months; B two years; C a year. (b) What she likes most is… A the pay; B talking to the customers; C the hours. (c) Next year she will… A work more hours; B leave the job; C work in a clothes shop. (3)
---
(a) A (1). (b) B (1). (c) B (1); C is where she used to work.
:::
