## What it is

This lesson covers two verb forms used to tell, ask or want someone else to do something.

**The imperative** gives instructions and commands: **¡Ven aquí!** (Come here!), **¡Escuchad!** (Listen!). Edexcel's list has **affirmative forms only**:
- the **tú** form of regular verbs and of **ser, ir, tener, venir, hacer, decir, poner** and **salir**;
- **Higher only:** the **vosotros** form of the same verbs;
- one object or reflexive pronoun joined to the end: **¡léelo!, ¡dame!, ¡prepárate!**, and the listed **¡vete!** (go away / leave).

**Negative commands** (no hagas eso, no vayas) are **not on the list**. They use subjunctive forms, and you will not be asked to produce them. **Usted commands** (siga todo recto) are not required either; you may recognise them in a role play. Both are credited if correct.

**The present subjunctive** is **Higher only**, and the list keeps it small. You need:
- **five verbs**, **hacer, ser, ir, venir** and **tener**, in the **singular persons only** (yo, tú, él / ella / usted);
- **four contexts**:
  - **cuando** with a future meaning: **cuando vengas a mi casa**;
  - after verbs of **wishing, command, request and emotion** + que: **él quiere que seas responsable**;
  - after **que** in phrases such as **me gusta que hagas los deberes**;
  - purpose with **para que**: **te ayudo para que tengas tiempo para ir al cine**.

Other verbs and the plural persons (hagamos, sean, vayan) are not required, but they are credited if correct. In Paper 4, one well-placed **cuando sea mayor** or **quieren que vaya** is exactly the "complex language" the AO3 grid rewards. In Papers 2 and 3, you must recognise **sea, vaya, tenga, venga, haga** and their -s forms.

### The tú imperative

**Regular verbs:** use the **él / ella form of the present**, stem changes included:

| infinitive | command | example |
|---|---|---|
| hablar | ¡habla! | ¡Habla más despacio! (Speak more slowly!) |
| comer | ¡come! | ¡Come fruta! |
| escribir | ¡escribe! | ¡Escribe tu nombre! |
| pensar | ¡piensa! | ¡Piensa antes de hablar! |
| volver | ¡vuelve! | ¡Vuelve pronto! |
| pedir | ¡pide! | ¡Pide la cuenta! |
| jugar | ¡juega! | ¡Juega con tu hermano! |

**The eight irregular tú commands.** These are listed by name, so learn them as a set:

| infinitive | command | example |
|---|---|---|
| ser | ¡sé! | ¡Sé bueno! (Be good!) |
| ir | ¡ve! | ¡Ve a tu habitación! (Go to your room!) |
| tener | ¡ten! | ¡Ten cuidado! (Be careful!) |
| venir | ¡ven! | ¡Ven conmigo! (Come with me!) |
| hacer | ¡haz! | ¡Haz los deberes! (Do your homework!) |
| decir | ¡di! | ¡Di la verdad! (Tell the truth!) |
| poner | ¡pon! | ¡Pon la mesa! (Lay the table!) |
| salir | ¡sal! | ¡Sal de aquí! (Get out of here!) |

**Sé** (be!) has an accent; **se** (the pronoun) does not. **Ve** (go!) and **ve** (he sees) look the same; the context tells you which.

### Higher: the vosotros imperative

Replace the final **-r** of the infinitive with **-d**. There are **no irregular forms**:

| infinitive | tú | vosotros |
|---|---|---|
| hablar | habla | **hablad** |
| comer | come | **comed** |
| escribir | escribe | **escribid** |
| ser | sé | **sed** |
| ir | ve | **id** |
| tener | ten | **tened** |
| venir | ven | **venid** |
| hacer | haz | **haced** |
| decir | di | **decid** |
| poner | pon | **poned** |
| salir | sal | **salid** |

Teachers and guides use these to talk to a group: **¡Escuchad!**, **¡Abrid los libros!**, **¡Venid aquí!** When a reflexive os is joined on, the d drops (¡sentaos!, sit down). You may recognise this, but it is not required.

### Pronouns with commands

One object or reflexive pronoun is **joined to the end** of an affirmative command. If that moves the stress, write an accent on the stressed syllable to keep it in place:
- **llama + me → ¡llámame!** (call me!); **escucha + me → ¡escúchame!**; **lee + lo → ¡léelo!**;
- **levanta + te → ¡levántate!** (get up!); **prepara + te → ¡prepárate!**; **sienta + te → ¡siéntate!**;
- one-syllable commands need no accent: **¡dame!** (give me!), **¡dime!** (tell me!), **¡hazlo!** (do it!), **¡ponlo aquí!**, **¡ponte el abrigo!**, **¡vete!**
- vosotros forms take a pronoun without an accent: **¡ayudadme!** (help me!), **¡hacedlo!**

Double pronouns (dámelo) are not on the list.

### Higher: the present subjunctive of five verbs

| | hacer | ser | ir | venir | tener |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yo | haga | sea | vaya | venga | tenga |
| tú | hagas | seas | vayas | vengas | tengas |
| él / ella / usted | haga | sea | vaya | venga | tenga |

Hacer, venir and tener build on the yo form of the present (hago, vengo, tengo): drop the -o and add **-a**. Ser and ir are irregular. The reflexive **irse** gives **me vaya, te vayas, se vaya**.

### Context 1: cuando with a future meaning

When **cuando** looks ahead to something that has not happened yet, the verb after it is subjunctive. The main verb is usually future:
- **Cuando sea mayor, viviré en el extranjero.** (When I am older, I will live abroad.)
- **Cuando tenga dieciocho años, voy a aprender a conducir.**
- **Cuando vaya a España, probaré la paella.**
- **Cuando vengas a mi casa, te enseñaré mis fotos.**

English uses the present ("when I am older"), so do not use the future after cuando: cuando seré mayor is a major error. When cuando describes a habit or the past, use the indicative: **Cuando hace sol, vamos a la playa** (whenever it is sunny); **Cuando era pequeño, vivía en Madrid**.

### Context 2: wishing, command, request and emotion + que

When one person wants, asks, tells or feels something about **another person's action**, use **que + subjunctive**:
- **wishing:** **Mis padres quieren que sea médico.** (My parents want me to be a doctor.) **Espero que haga buen tiempo.** (I hope the weather is good.) **Prefiero que vengas mañana.**
- **command and request:** **Mi madre me dice que haga la cama.** (My mum tells me to make my bed.) **Te pido que tengas cuidado.**
- **emotion:** **Me preocupa que mi hermano no tenga amigos.** **Me encanta que vengas a la fiesta.**

**The key test is a change of subject.** If the same person does both things, use the **infinitive**:
- **Quiero ser médico.** (I want to be a doctor.) Same person, so the infinitive.
- **Mis padres quieren que sea médico.** (My parents want me to be a doctor.) Different people, so the subjunctive.

English "want someone to do" never becomes querer + infinitive in Spanish: quieren me ser is meaningless.

### Context 3: after que, as in me gusta que…

Phrases of liking and disliking work the same way when someone else is the subject: **Me gusta que hagas los deberes** (I like it that you do your homework); **No me gusta que mi hermano vaya solo al centro**. You will also meet impersonal phrases built the same way, such as **es importante que tengas un plan**.

### Context 4: para que, "so that"

**Para que + subjunctive** gives a purpose when the subject changes. Compare **para + infinitive** when the subject stays the same:
- **Te ayudo para que tengas tiempo para ir al cine.** (I'm helping you so that you have time to go to the cinema.)
- **Mis padres trabajan mucho para que yo tenga un buen futuro.**
- but **Trabajo para ganar dinero.** (I work to earn money.) Same subject, so the infinitive.

::: terms
- **imperative**: the command form; Edexcel tests affirmative commands only: ¡escucha!, ¡escuchad!
- **tú command**: the él form of the present for regular verbs: ¡come!, ¡piensa!, ¡vuelve!
- **irregular tú commands**: sé, ve, ten, ven, haz, di, pon, sal, the eight listed forms.
- **vosotros command**: Higher form made by changing the infinitive's -r to -d: ¡id!, ¡haced!, ¡venid!
- **pronoun attachment**: one pronoun joined to an affirmative command, with an accent if needed: ¡llámame!
- **present subjunctive**: Higher mood after wishes, emotions, future cuando and para que: sea, vaya, tenga.
- **change of subject**: the trigger for que + subjunctive; with no change, use the infinitive: quiero ir.
- **cuando + subjunctive**: "when" about the future: cuando sea mayor, cuando vaya a España.
- **para que**: "so that", followed by the subjunctive when the subject changes: para que tengas tiempo.
- **¡vete!**: the listed command of irse, meaning go away or leave.
:::

::: method
1. **Is it a command?** For tú, use the él form of the present unless the verb is one of the eight irregulars (sé, ve, ten, ven, haz, di, pon, sal). For vosotros (Higher), change -r to -d.
2. **Attach any single pronoun to the end of the command**, and add an accent if the stress would otherwise move (¡llámame!, ¡levántate!).
3. **Is it a wish, request, command or feeling about someone else's action?** Use que + subjunctive. If it is about the same person, use the infinitive.
4. **Is cuando pointing to the future?** Use the subjunctive after it and the future in the main clause.
5. **Is it "so that" with a new subject?** Use para que + subjunctive; with the same subject, use para + infinitive.
6. **Pick the form**: haga, sea, vaya, venga or tenga, adding -s for tú.
7. **When translating into English**, the subjunctive usually becomes an English present or an infinitive: quiere que vaya = she wants me to go.
:::

::: example Tú commands
Give the tú command: (a) escuchar (b) volver (c) hacer (d) salir (e) ser. (5)

**Answer.** (a) **¡Escucha!** (1) (b) **¡Vuelve!** (1): it keeps the stem change. (c) **¡Haz!** (1) (d) **¡Sal!** (1) (e) **¡Sé!** (1): with the accent.
:::

::: example Higher: vosotros commands
A teacher speaks to the class. Translate into Spanish: (a) Listen! (b) Open your books! (c) Come here! (3)

**Answer.** (a) **¡Escuchad!** (1) (b) **¡Abrid los libros!** (1) (c) **¡Venid aquí!** (1) Spanish says los libros, not vuestros libros, when the owner is obvious.
:::

::: example Commands with a pronoun
Rewrite each command with a pronoun in place of the words after the verb: (a) Llama a tu madre. (b) Haz los deberes. Then (c) explain why ¡levántate! has an accent. (3)

**Answer.** (a) **¡Llámala!** (1): the accent keeps the stress on lla-. (b) **¡Hazlos!** (1): los replaces los deberes; no accent on a one-syllable command. (c) The accent on **levántate** keeps the stress on -van- after te is added (1).
:::

::: example Cuando: future or habit?
Translate into Spanish: (a) When I go to Spain, I will speak Spanish every day. (b) When I go to my grandparents' house, I always eat a lot. (2)

**Answer.**
- (a) **Cuando vaya a España, hablaré español todos los días.** (1) This is in the future, so cuando takes the subjunctive.
- (b) **Cuando voy a casa de mis abuelos, siempre como mucho.** (1) This is a habit, so cuando takes the indicative.
:::

::: example Infinitive or subjunctive?
Translate into Spanish: (a) I want to go to the concert. (b) My friend wants me to go to the concert. (2)

**Answer.**
- (a) **Quiero ir al concierto.** (1) The same person wants and goes, so use the infinitive.
- (b) **Mi amigo/a quiere que vaya al concierto.** (1) A different person goes, so use que + subjunctive.
:::

::: watch
- **Only affirmative commands are tested.** Do not try to write negative commands; rephrase instead (deberías…, es mejor…).
- **Learn the eight irregular tú commands as a set**: sé, ve, ten, ven, haz, di, pon, sal. Hace, pone and sale are statements, not commands.
- **Regular tú commands keep the stem change**: ¡piensa!, ¡vuelve!, ¡pide!
- **Vosotros commands end in -d**: ¡id!, ¡sed!, ¡haced!, never ¡ir! or ¡hacéis!
- **Add an accent when a pronoun is joined to a command of two or more syllables**: ¡llámame!, ¡levántate!, ¡escúchame!
- **No future after cuando about the future**: cuando sea mayor, not cuando seré mayor.
- **Subjunctive only with a change of subject**: quiero ser médico, but mis padres quieren que sea médico.
- **The subjunctive forms to know are singular**: haga, sea, vaya, venga, tenga (+ -s for tú).
:::
