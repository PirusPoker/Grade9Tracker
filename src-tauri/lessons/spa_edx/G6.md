## What it is

Prepositions are the small linking words: **a, de, en, con, para, por, sin**. Spanish and English often use different ones, or one language uses a preposition where the other has none. That makes word-for-word translation go wrong in both directions. Edexcel's grammar list (Appendix 2) names these points:

- **Foundation + Higher:**
  - the **personal a**: **conozco a María**;
  - **de** for possession: **la casa de Marta**;
  - **para, sin, antes de** and **después de** + infinitive: **para ver, sin parar, antes de ir, después de venir**;
  - the prepositions some verbs need before a noun or an infinitive (**disfruto de las vacaciones**), including verbs whose meaning changes with a preposition: **llegar** (to arrive), **llegar a** (to manage to).
- **Higher only:** a preposition placed **before a question word**: **¿Con quién hablas?**
- The vocabulary list adds seven **Higher-only prepositions**: **según** (according to), **por** in the senses "around" and "because of", **a pesar de** (despite), **debido a** (due to), **salvo** (except for), **a través de** (through, across) and **hacia** (towards).

The everyday prepositions (a, de, en, con, sin, por, para, entre, hasta, desde, durante, sobre, contra) are Foundation vocabulary you already use. The contractions **al** (a + el) and **del** (de + el) are in G1a, and pronouns after prepositions (**para mí, conmigo**) are in G1c.

### The golden rule: preposition + infinitive

After any preposition, Spanish uses the **infinitive**. English often uses an -ing form here, and that is where students go wrong.

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| before going out | **antes de salir** |
| after eating | **después de comer** |
| without saying anything | **sin decir nada** |
| (in order) to pass | **para aprobar** |
| despite being tired | **a pesar de estar cansado** |

**Antes de saliendo** and **sin diciendo** are always wrong. A reflexive pronoun joins the end of the infinitive: **antes de acostarme** (before going to bed), **después de levantarme** (after getting up).

Without a verb, the same prepositions simply take a noun: **antes del partido, después de las clases, sin dinero, para mi madre**.

### The personal a

When the **direct object** of a verb is a **specific person**, put **a** in front of it. English has nothing similar, so it is easy to forget:
- **Conozco a María.** (I know María.)
- **Ayer vi a Diego en el parque.** (Yesterday I saw Diego in the park.)
- **Busco a mi hermano.** (I'm looking for my brother.) But **Busco mi móvil**: a thing, so no a.
- **Visito a mis abuelos los domingos.** But **Visito el museo**: a place, so no a.
- **No conozco a nadie. ¿Ves a alguien?** Nadie and alguien take the personal a.
- **a + el = al**: **Llamé al médico.**
- A much-loved pet often takes it too: **Quiero mucho a mi perro.**

There is **no personal a after tener or hay**: **Tengo dos hermanas. Hay muchos turistas.**

### De for possession

Spanish has no 's. Turn the phrase round: **the thing + de + the owner**.
- Marta's house → **la casa de Marta**
- my parents' car → **el coche de mis padres**
- the teacher's bag → **la bolsa del profesor** (de + el = del)
- Diego's sister's friend → **la amiga de la hermana de Diego**

**De** also joins two nouns where English puts one noun in front of the other: **la clase de inglés** (the English class), **la estación de tren** (the train station), **un zumo de naranja** (an orange juice).

### Para, sin, antes de, después de + infinitive

- **para + infinitive** = in order to. Spanish needs para where English often just says "to": **Trabajo los sábados para ganar dinero.** (I work on Saturdays to earn money.)
- **sin + infinitive** = without -ing: **Se fue sin decir adiós.** (He left without saying goodbye.)
- **antes de + infinitive** = before -ing: **Antes de salir, hago la cama.**
- **después de + infinitive** = after -ing: **Después de comer, vamos al parque.**

### Verbs that need a preposition

Some verbs need a preposition before a noun or an infinitive. Learn the verb and its preposition as one unit:

| verb + preposition | example | English |
|---|---|---|
| disfrutar de | Disfruto de las vacaciones. | I enjoy the holidays. |
| jugar a | Juego al baloncesto. | I play basketball. |
| ayudar a + inf. | Ayudo a mi padre a cocinar. | I help my dad to cook. |
| empezar a + inf. | Empecé a estudiar a las seis. | I started studying at six. |
| aprender a + inf. | Aprendí a tocar el piano. | I learnt to play the piano. |
| depender de | Depende del tiempo. | It depends on the weather. |
| consistir en | Consiste en ayudar a los clientes. | It consists of helping customers. |
| pensar en | Pienso mucho en mis amigos. | I think about my friends a lot. |
| casarse con | Mi tía se casó con un chico de Bolivia. | My aunt married a man from Bolivia. |
| llevarse bien con | Me llevo bien con mi hermano. | I get on well with my brother. |
| acordarse de | No me acuerdo de su nombre. | I don't remember his name. |
| asistir a | Asisto a clases de baile. | I attend dance classes. |

English often has **no preposition** here (enjoy the holidays, married a man, attend classes) or **a different one** (depends on, consists of, think about).

### Verbs whose meaning changes with a preposition

| verb on its own | verb + preposition |
|---|---|
| **llegar** to arrive: Llegué tarde. | **llegar a** + inf. to manage to, end up: Al final llegué a entender el problema. |
| **volver** to return: Volví a casa. | **volver a** + inf. to do again: Volví a ver la película. (I watched the film again.) |
| **dejar** to leave, to let: Dejé el móvil en casa. | **dejar de** + inf. to stop: He dejado de comer carne. |
| **tratar** to treat: Mi jefe me trata bien. | **tratar de** + inf. to try to: Trato de dormir ocho horas. **Tratarse de** to be about: Se trata de un problema serio. |
| **acabar** to finish: Acabé los deberes. | **acabar de** + inf. to have just (see G2c): Acabo de llegar. |

### Higher: prepositions before question words

English often leaves the preposition at the end of a question. Spanish must put it **first**:
- Who do you live with? → **¿Con quién vives?**
- Where are you from? → **¿De dónde eres?**
- Where are you going (to)? → **¿Adónde vas?** (also written **¿A dónde vas?**)
- What is the film about? → **¿De qué trata la película?**
- Who is the present for? → **¿Para quién es el regalo?**
- Whose is this bag? → **¿De quién es esta bolsa?**
- What are you thinking about? → **¿En qué piensas?**

In the role play you must ask two questions, and these patterns make them precise.

### Higher: the seven extra prepositions

| preposition | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| **según** | according to | Según mi madre, el barrio es peligroso por la noche. With a pronoun: **según tú, según él**. |
| **por** | around, through, along | Dimos una vuelta por el centro. |
| **por** | because of | No jugamos por la lluvia. |
| **a pesar de** | despite | A pesar del frío, fuimos a la playa. A pesar de estar cansada, salió. |
| **debido a** | due to | El tren llegó tarde debido a la nieve. |
| **salvo** | except (for) | El museo abre todos los días salvo los lunes. |
| **a través de** | through, across, via | Conocí a mi novia a través de las redes sociales. |
| **hacia** | towards | Caminamos hacia la playa. |

These are high-value in Paper 3 (they often carry the answer: "despite", "because of", "except") and they add variety in Paper 4.

### Por or para?

Both can mean "for", so choose by **meaning**. **Para** points forward to a goal. **Por** looks back to a cause, or describes a route or an exchange.

| para | por |
|---|---|
| purpose: **para + inf.**: Estudio para aprobar. | route, through, around: Paseamos por el parque. |
| recipient: Este regalo es para ti. | cause: Gracias por el regalo. Por eso no fui. |
| destination: el tren para Madrid | exchange, price: Lo compré por diez euros. |
| deadline: Es para el lunes. | means: por teléfono, por internet |
| opinion: Para mí, es importante. | rate, per: dos veces por semana |
| | parts of the day: por la mañana, por la noche |

Fixed phrases with por: **por favor, por ejemplo, por supuesto, por fin, por lo tanto, por un lado… por otro lado**. For a length of time, Spain normally uses **durante** (durante dos horas) or no preposition at all (estuve dos horas allí).

::: terms
- **preposition**: a linking word such as a, de, en, con, para, por or sin, placed before a noun, pronoun or infinitive.
- **preposition + infinitive**: the Spanish rule that replaces the English -ing form: antes de salir, sin decir nada.
- **personal a**: the a placed before a specific person who is the object of a verb: conozco a María, vi a Diego.
- **de for possession**: the Spanish replacement for 's: la casa de Marta, el coche de mis padres.
- **verb + preposition**: a verb that needs a set preposition: disfrutar de, jugar a, ayudar a, pensar en.
- **meaning change**: a verb that means something new with a preposition: llegar a (manage to), volver a (do again), dejar de (stop).
- **preposition before a question word**: the Higher rule that the preposition comes first: ¿Con quién vives?
- **según**: Higher, according to; takes subject pronouns: según tú, según él.
- **a pesar de / debido a**: Higher, despite / due to; both take a noun or (a pesar de) an infinitive.
- **salvo / a través de / hacia**: Higher, except for / through or via / towards.
- **para**: for a goal: purpose, recipient, destination, deadline, opinion.
- **por**: for a route, a cause, an exchange, a means or a rate: por el parque, por la lluvia, por teléfono.
:::

::: method
1. **Is a verb following a preposition?** Use the infinitive, never an -ing form: antes de comer, sin hablar, para ganar.
2. **Is a specific person the object of the verb?** Add the personal a (or al). Do not add it to things, places, tener or hay.
3. **Is there an 's?** Rebuild it as thing + de + owner, and contract de + el to del.
4. **Does the verb need a preposition?** Check your verb-plus-preposition list: disfrutar de, jugar a, ayudar a, empezar a, depender de, pensar en.
5. **Higher: is it a question ending in a preposition in English?** Move the preposition to the front: ¿Con quién…? ¿De qué…? ¿Para quién…?
6. **Is it "for"?** Ask: a goal, recipient, deadline or opinion → para; a route, cause, exchange, means or rate → por.
7. **In reading, translate the Higher prepositions exactly**: a pesar de (despite), debido a (due to), salvo (except), según (according to). They often decide the answer.
:::

::: example The personal a
Decide whether each sentence needs a personal a. Write a, al or nothing. (a) Ayer vi ___ Sara en la playa. (b) Este verano voy a visitar ___ Barcelona. (c) Llamé ___ médico. (d) Tengo ___ dos primos. (4)

**Answer.** (a) **a** (1): Sara is a person and the object of vi. (b) **nothing** (1): Barcelona is a place being visited. (c) **al** (1): a + el médico. (d) **nothing** (1): no personal a after tener.
:::

::: example From -ing to the infinitive
Translate into Spanish: (a) Before going out, I make my bed. (b) My brother left without saying goodbye. (2)

**Answer.**
- (a) **Antes de salir, hago la cama.** (1) Antes de + infinitive; "going" is not translated as a gerund.
- (b) **Mi hermano se fue sin decir adiós.** (1) Sin + infinitive. Accept salió.
:::

::: example Por or para?
Complete with por or para: (a) Estudio mucho ___ aprobar. (b) Gracias ___ tu ayuda. (c) Hago deporte tres veces ___ semana. (d) Este regalo es ___ mi madre. (4)

**Answer.** (a) **para** (1): purpose + infinitive. (b) **por** (1): thanking someone for a cause. (c) **por** (1): a rate, per week. (d) **para** (1): the recipient.
:::

::: example Higher: questions that start with a preposition
Translate into Spanish: (a) Who do you live with? (b) What is the film about? (c) Where is your teacher from? (3)

**Answer.** (a) **¿Con quién vives?** (1) (b) **¿De qué trata la película?** (1) Accept ¿De qué se trata la película? (c) **¿De dónde es tu profesor / profesora?** (1) The preposition moves to the front each time.
:::

::: example Higher: reading the extra prepositions
Translate into English: (a) A pesar de la lluvia, salimos. (b) Según Hugo, la película es aburrida. (c) Todos vinieron a la fiesta salvo Jorge. (3)

**Answer.** (a) **Despite the rain, we went out.** (1) (b) **According to Hugo, the film is boring.** (1) (c) **Everyone came to the party except (for) Jorge.** (1) Missing "despite" or "except" reverses the meaning.
:::

::: watch
- **Never put an -ing form after a preposition**: antes de salir, sin decir, después de comer; antes de saliendo is always wrong.
- **Remember the personal a before people**, including nadie and alguien, but not before things, places, or after tener and hay.
- **Rebuild every 's**: the thing + de + the owner, with del for de + el.
- **Learn verbs with their prepositions**: disfrutar de, jugar a, ayudar a, empezar a, pensar en, depender de.
- **Read volver a, dejar de and llegar a as new meanings**: again, stop, manage to.
- **Put the preposition first in a Higher question**: ¿Con quién…? not ¿Quién… con?
- **Do not mix up por and para**: para for goals, recipients and deadlines; por for routes, causes, exchanges and rates.
- **In reading, a pesar de, salvo and debido a reverse or explain the meaning**: translate them, do not skip them.
:::
