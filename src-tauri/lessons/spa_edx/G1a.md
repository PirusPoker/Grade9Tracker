## What it is

Every Spanish noun is **masculine or feminine**, and everything that goes with it (the article, the adjective, este, mi) has to **agree** in gender and number. This lesson covers the first block of Edexcel's grammar list: **forming feminine nouns**, **forming plurals**, **infinitives used as nouns**, **languages and nationalities**, and the **articles** (el, la, los, las; un, una, unos, unas), including **al** and **del** and the places where Spanish uses or drops an article differently from English.

Where it turns up:
- **Paper 4 Question 3** (translation into Spanish, 10 marks). Sentences such as "My aunt is a nurse" or "Education is important" are set precisely because English and Spanish treat the article differently. Pearson lists de el among the minor errors in its writing mark schemes: one slip will not stop the meaning, but a string of them pulls the AO3 accuracy mark down.
- **Paper 3 Section B** (translation into English). You must turn Spanish articles back into natural English: la educación es importante is "education is important", not "the education".
- **Papers 2 and 3.** Gender and number carry meaning: mi profesora is a woman, los sábados means every Saturday, mis padres means my parents, not my fathers.
- **Paper 2 dictation.** Plurals such as canciones and jardines test whether you know when the written accent disappears.

### Forming feminine nouns

| Rule | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| -o → -a | el vecino, el amigo, el hermano, el médico | la vecina, la amiga, la hermana, la médica |
| add -a after -or | el profesor, el jugador, el director, el escritor | la profesora, la jugadora, la directora, la escritora |
| no change: -ante, -ente, -ista | el estudiante, el cantante, el artista, el turista | la estudiante, la cantante, la artista, la turista |
| jobs in flux | el jefe | la jefa or la jefe |

With -ante, -ente and -ista nouns only the **article** shows the gender: el artista is a man, la artista a woman. Pearson's list says the feminine forms of some job nouns are **in flux**, so the board tolerates both widespread forms: la jefa and la jefe, la médica and la médico. Choose one and use it consistently. A few people nouns are simply different words: el padre / la madre, el hombre / la mujer, el actor / la actriz.

A **mixed group** takes the masculine plural: mis padres (my parents), mis hermanos (my brothers and sisters, or my brothers), mis tíos (my aunt and uncle, or my uncles). Read the context before you translate.

**Gender of other nouns.** Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and most ending in -a are feminine. Nouns ending in **-ción, -sión, -dad** are feminine (la canción, la televisión, la ciudad). Learn the common exceptions with their article: **el día, el mapa, el problema, el programa, el tema, el clima** are masculine; **la mano, la foto, la radio** are feminine.

### Forming plurals

| Rule | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| ends in a vowel: add -s | el sitio, la casa, el café | los sitios, las casas, los cafés |
| ends in a consonant: add -es | el plan, la ciudad, el móvil | los planes, las ciudades, los móviles |
| consonant + accent on the last syllable: add -es, accent goes | el jardín, el autobús | los jardines, los autobuses |
| -z → -ces | la nariz, la vez, la luz, el pez | las narices, las veces, las luces, los peces |
| -ión → -iones, accent goes | la canción, la estación, la competición | las canciones, las estaciones, las competiciones |

Why does the accent go? The stress stays on the same syllable (can-CIÓN, can-CIO-nes). In the plural the word ends in -es, and a word ending in a vowel, -n or -s is stressed on the second-to-last syllable anyway, so the accent is no longer needed. The same logic sometimes **adds** an accent: el examen → los exámenes, el joven → los jóvenes (the stress stays on the same syllable, which is now third from the end). Words ending in an unstressed -s do not change: el lunes → los lunes, el cumpleaños → los cumpleaños.

### Infinitives used as nouns

Where English uses an **-ing word as a noun**, Spanish uses the **infinitive**, never the gerund:
- **Hacer ejercicio es bueno para la salud.** = Doing exercise is good for your health.
- **Leer es mi pasatiempo favorito.** = Reading is my favourite hobby.
- **Me encanta cantar.** = I love singing.
- **Fumar es peligroso.** = Smoking is dangerous.

Haciendo ejercicio es bueno is a classic error: the gerund (-ando, -iendo) is for "I am doing", not for "doing" as a subject.

### Languages and nationalities

Languages are the **masculine adjective** used as a noun, with **el** when it is the subject, and **no capital letter**: el inglés, el español, el francés. **El español es fácil** (Spanish is easy). After hablar, estudiar and aprender the article is usually dropped: hablo inglés, estudio francés.

Nationality nouns put the article in front of the nationality adjective in all four forms: **el español, la española, los españoles, las españolas** (a Spaniard, Spaniards); likewise los ingleses, las mexicanas, los argentinos, los británicos. Again **no capital**. Watch the accent: inglés and francés lose it in every other form (inglesa, ingleses, francesa, franceses).

### Articles: agreement

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| the (singular / plural) | el / los | la / las |
| a, some | un / unos | una / unas |

**Unos / unas** mean "some" or "a few": unos amigos (some friends). One quirk you will meet in reading: a feminine noun that starts with a stressed a- takes el in the singular, el agua, but it stays feminine (el agua fría, las aguas).

### Where Spanish uses the article and English does not

1. **General and abstract nouns**, especially as the subject: **La educación es importante.** **El dinero no da la felicidad.** **Los perros son muy inteligentes** (dogs in general).
2. **After gustar-type verbs**, because the thing liked is the subject: **Me gusta el deporte. Me encantan las películas.**
3. **Days**: **el sábado** = on Saturday (one occasion); **los sábados** = on Saturdays (every week). Never en el sábado.
4. **Times and frequency**: a las ocho; dos veces a la semana (twice a week); dos euros el kilo (two euros a kilo).
5. **Parts of the body and clothes** with reflexive verbs: **me lavo los dientes** (I brush my teeth), me pongo la chaqueta.

### Where Spanish drops the article and English keeps it

1. **After ser with a job, nationality or religion** when there is no adjective: **Mi madre es profesora.** **Quiero ser médico.** Add an adjective and the article comes back: Es una profesora excelente.
2. **Before otro, cien, mil, medio**, and after ¡qué! in exclamations: **otro día** (another day, never un otro), cien euros, mil personas, media hora, **¡Qué pena!** (What a shame!).
3. **After tener and similar verbs in questions and negatives**, when the number does not matter: **No tengo coche. ¿Tienes perro?**

### Al and del

**a + el = al** and **de + el = del**: voy al colegio, después del viaje, la casa del vecino. Only **el** contracts; a la, de la, a los, de los never change. The pronoun él (with an accent, "him") does not contract either: el libro es de él.

::: terms
- **gender**: every noun is masculine or feminine, and articles and adjectives agree with it.
- **-ante / -ente / -ista nouns**: the same form for both genders; only the article changes (el / la estudiante).
- **mixed plural**: a group of males and females takes the masculine plural (mis padres = my parents).
- **-ión plural**: add -es and drop the written accent (la canción, las canciones).
- **-z plural**: change z to c and add -es (la vez, las veces).
- **infinitive as a noun**: the Spanish for an English -ing subject (Leer es divertido = reading is fun).
- **definite article**: el, la, los, las; also used for general and abstract nouns in Spanish.
- **indefinite article**: un, una, unos, unas; dropped after ser + job (es médico).
- **al / del**: the compulsory contractions of a + el and de + el.
- **nationality noun**: el / la + nationality adjective, no capital (los españoles = Spaniards).
:::

::: method
1. **Find the noun's gender and number first.** Check the ending and any exceptions you know (el día, la mano, el problema).
2. **For a feminine form**, apply the rule: -o → -a, -or → -ora, no change for -ante, -ente, -ista.
3. **For a plural**, look at the last letter: vowel → -s; consonant → -es; -z → -ces; then check the stress and add or remove an accent.
4. **Decide on the article** by asking whether Spanish needs one: general or abstract noun or a gustar-type verb → definite article; ser + job → none; otro, cien, mil, medio → none.
5. **Check every a el and de el** and contract them to al and del.
6. **When translating into English**, drop the article where English would (la salud = health) and translate mixed plurals as a mixed group.
:::

::: example Feminine and plural forms
Give the feminine singular and the feminine plural of: el vecino, el jugador, el cantante. (3)

**Answer.**
- la vecina → las vecinas (-o → -a) (1)
- la jugadora → las jugadoras (add -a after -or) (1)
- la cantante → las cantantes (-ante: no change, only the article) (1)
:::

::: example Plurals that gain or lose an accent
Make these plural: la estación, el jardín, el examen, la vez. (4)

**Answer.**
- las estaciones: -ión → -iones, accent goes (1)
- los jardines: add -es, accent goes because the stress now falls naturally on -di- (1)
- los exámenes: add -es, accent added to keep the stress on -xa- (1)
- las veces: -z → -ces (1)
:::

::: example Translation into Spanish: articles that differ from English
Translate into Spanish: "My aunt is a doctor. She says that health is more important than money." (4)

**Answer.** Mi tía es médica (or médico) (1): no un or una after ser + job. Dice que (1) la salud es más importante (1) que el dinero (1): Spanish needs la and el with general nouns.
:::

::: example Gerund or infinitive?
Translate into Spanish: "Learning languages is useful, and I love speaking Spanish with Spaniards." (4)

**Answer.** Aprender idiomas es útil (1): infinitive as the subject, never aprendiendo. Y me encanta hablar español (1) con españoles (1): infinitive after encantar; no article after hablar; nationality noun without a capital. Accept con los españoles (1) for general reference. Overall accuracy (1).
:::

::: example Reading: what the articles tell you
Read the sentence and answer in English.

> Los domingos como con mis abuelos, pero el domingo voy a la fiesta de una amiga.

(a) What does the writer usually do on Sundays? (b) What is different this Sunday? (2)

**Answer.** (a) Has lunch / eats with grandparents (1): los domingos = every Sunday. (b) Is going to a friend's party (1): el domingo = this one Sunday. "Grandfathers" for abuelos is wrong unless both are men: it means grandparents.

::: watch
- **de el and a el** must be del and al. Pearson counts de el as a minor error, but it is an avoidable one in every piece of writing.
- **No article after ser + job**: es enfermera, quiero ser jugador. "Es una profesora" without an adjective is an English pattern.
- **General nouns need the article**: me gusta el fútbol, la música es importante. "Me gusta fútbol" is wrong.
- **Accents in plurals**: canciones, estaciones and jardines have none; exámenes and jóvenes gain one. At Higher, a missing or extra accent on a dictation word loses that word.
- **Gerunds are not nouns**: hacer deporte es sano, not haciendo deporte es sano.
- **Mixed plurals in reading**: mis padres is my parents, mis hermanos can be brothers and sisters. A literal "my fathers" loses the mark.
- **No capitals for languages or nationalities**: el inglés, los españoles.
:::
