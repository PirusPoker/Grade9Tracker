## What it is

This **Higher-only** lesson covers the verb structures on Edexcel's list that have no word-for-word match in English:
- the **passive voice**, in two forms: **ser + past participle + por** (la casa fue vendida por mi amigo) and **se + third person** (se vende pan; se venden huevos);
- **acabar de + infinitive** for "have just done" (acabo de hacer los deberes);
- two ways to show an action that is **still going on**: **seguir + gerund** (seguimos haciendo los deberes) and **llevar + time + gerund** (llevas cinco minutos buscando tus gafas);
- the **present tense with desde hace** for "have been …ing for" (vivimos en esta casa desde hace un año).

Pearson's Issue 2 specification clarified this group as **periphrastic time expressions**. The pattern hace + time + que (hace un año que vivo aquí) is not on the list: you will understand it, but it is not required, and anything you write with it is credited if correct.

Where it turns up:
- **Paper 3 Section B and Paper 4 Question 3.** "I have been learning Spanish for three years" is a classic translation sentence. The commonest error goes both ways: writing he aprendido español por tres años in Spanish, or "I learn Spanish since three years" in English.
- **Papers 2 and 3.** Acaba de llegar (she has just arrived) is not "she arrives at the end"; sigue lloviendo (it is still raining) is not "it follows the rain". Reading and listening questions test exactly these.
- **Paper 4.** A passive, a llevar + gerund or an acabar de is complex language that the AO3 grid rewards, and it is easy to drop into a blog or an article.

### The passive with ser + past participle + por

The passive puts the thing that receives the action first. The doer, if mentioned, comes after **por** (by):
- Active: **Mi amigo vendió la casa.** (My friend sold the house.)
- Passive: **La casa fue vendida por mi amigo.** (The house was sold by my friend.)

**Ser** carries the tense, and the **past participle agrees** with the subject like an adjective:

| tense of ser | example | English |
|---|---|---|
| present | El programa es visto por millones de personas. | The programme is watched by millions of people. |
| preterite | La biblioteca fue construida en el siglo pasado. | The library was built in the last century. |
| perfect | Los premios han sido entregados por la directora. | The prizes have been given out by the headteacher. |
| future | La película será grabada en Madrid. | The film will be recorded in Madrid. |

Participle endings: **-ado** for -ar verbs, **-ido** for -er and -ir verbs, then -a, -os, -as to agree: construido, construida, construidos, construidas. Learn the irregular ones: **hecho** (hacer), **visto** (ver), **escrito** (escribir), **puesto** (poner), **abierto** (abrir), **roto** (romper), **dicho** (decir), **descubierto** (descubrir).

- **La novela fue escrita por una autora joven.** (escrita: feminine, like novela)
- **Las ventanas fueron rotas por el viento.** (rotas: feminine plural)

The ser passive sounds formal. You will meet it in news reports, articles and history texts; in everyday Spanish the **se** passive is more common.

### The passive with se

**Se + third person verb** gives a passive meaning when no doer is named. The verb agrees with the thing:
- **se + singular** for one thing or an uncountable noun: **Se vende pan.** (Bread is sold / Bread for sale.) **Aquí se habla inglés.** (English is spoken here.)
- **se + plural** for several things: **Se venden huevos.** (Eggs are sold.) **Se organizan excursiones todos los días.** (Trips are organised every day.)

It works in all tenses: **se construyó un hospital nuevo** (a new hospital was built), **se han abierto dos tiendas** (two shops have been opened), **se celebrará una fiesta** (a party will be held).

Translate it naturally: as an English passive, or with "they", "you" or "people": **Se dice que…** = They say that… / People say that… **En España se cena tarde.** = In Spain people have dinner late. This is close to the impersonal se puede and se necesita in G2b: in both, nobody in particular is the subject.

When English uses "they" for unnamed people, Spanish uses the third person plural or the se passive, not the ser passive: **They opened a new shop** is **Abrieron una tienda nueva** or **Se abrió una tienda nueva**.

### Acabar de + infinitive: "have just"

**Acabar de + infinitive** in the **present** means **have just (done)**:
- **Acabo de hacer los deberes.** = I have just done my homework.
- **Mi hermana acaba de llegar.** = My sister has just arrived.
- **Acabamos de comer.** = We have just eaten.

Notice the tense change: Spanish uses the **present** (acabo) where English uses a perfect (have just). Acabar on its own means to finish: **acabé los deberes a las nueve** (I finished my homework at nine). In a story, the imperfect **acababa de** means "had just": not required, credited if correct.

### Seguir + gerund: "still, keep on"

**Seguir** (e→i, like pedir) + gerund = to be still doing, to keep on doing, to carry on doing.

| seguir | example |
|---|---|
| sigo | Sigo estudiando en el mismo instituto. (I am still studying at the same school.) |
| sigues | ¿Sigues jugando al fútbol? (Do you still play football?) |
| sigue | Sigue lloviendo. (It is still raining.) |
| seguimos | Seguimos haciendo los deberes. (We are still doing our homework.) |
| seguís | ¿Seguís viviendo en Madrid? (Do you still live in Madrid?) |
| siguen | Mis abuelos siguen trabajando. (My grandparents are still working.) |

### Llevar + time + gerund: "have been …ing for"

**Llevar + time period + gerund** says how long an action has been going on up to now, and implies it is still continuing:
- **Llevas cinco minutos buscando tus gafas.** = You have been looking for your glasses for five minutes.
- **Llevo tres años aprendiendo español.** = I have been learning Spanish for three years.
- **Llevamos una hora esperando el autobús.** = We have been waiting for the bus for an hour.

Llevar is conjugated (llevo, llevas, lleva, llevamos, lleváis, llevan); the gerund never changes.

**Gerund reminder.** -ar → **-ando** (esperando); -er and -ir → **-iendo** (aprendiendo, viviendo). Stems ending in a vowel take **-yendo** (leer → leyendo); verbs like pedir change e → i (pidiendo, siguiendo); dormir gives durmiendo.

### Desde hace + present tense: "have been …ing for"

To say how long something has been happening, Spanish uses the **present tense** with **desde hace + time period**:
- **Vivimos en esta casa desde hace un año.** = We have been living in this house for a year.
- **Juego al tenis desde hace seis meses.** = I have been playing tennis for six months.
- **No veo a mi primo desde hace mucho tiempo.** = I haven't seen my cousin for a long time.

**Why the present?** Because the action is still going on now. Using the perfect (he vivido aquí por un año) suggests it is over, and **por** cannot replace desde hace here.

**Desde or desde hace?** **Desde** alone means **since** a point in time: **vivo aquí desde 2020**, **desde el verano**, **desde que era pequeño**. **Desde hace** means **for** a length of time: **desde hace dos años**. The question is **¿Desde hace cuánto tiempo…?** (How long have you been…?): ¿Desde hace cuánto tiempo estudias español?

Llevar + gerund and desde hace say the same thing in two ways, which is useful in writing to avoid repetition:
- **Llevo dos años viviendo aquí.** = **Vivo aquí desde hace dos años.** = I have been living here for two years.

::: terms
- **passive voice**: the receiver of the action becomes the subject: la casa fue vendida.
- **ser + past participle + por**: the full passive; the participle agrees and por introduces the doer.
- **se passive**: se + third person verb, agreeing with the thing: se vende pan, se venden huevos.
- **past participle**: the -ado / -ido form; irregulars include hecho, visto, escrito, puesto, roto.
- **acabar de + infinitive**: have just done, with acabar in the present tense: acabo de llegar.
- **gerund (present participle)**: the -ando / -iendo form used after estar, seguir and llevar.
- **seguir + gerund**: to be still doing, to keep on doing: sigue lloviendo.
- **llevar + time + gerund**: to have been doing something for a period: llevo un año estudiando.
- **desde hace + present**: have been …ing for a period: vivo aquí desde hace dos años.
- **desde**: since a point in time (desde enero), as opposed to desde hace for a period.
:::

::: method
1. **Spot the English pattern first.** "was built by" → ser passive; "is sold / are spoken" with no doer → se passive; "have just" → acabar de; "still" or "keep on" + -ing → seguir; "have been …ing for" → desde hace or llevar.
2. **For the ser passive**, put ser in the right tense, make the participle agree with the subject, then add por + the doer.
3. **For the se passive**, make the verb singular or plural to match the thing.
4. **For "have just", "still" and "have been …ing for"**, use the present tense of acabar, seguir or llevar, or the present of the main verb with desde hace. Do not use the perfect tense.
5. **Choose desde (since a date or moment) or desde hace (for a length of time).**
6. **When translating into English**, turn these back into natural English: acaba de salir = she has just left; llevo una hora esperando = I have been waiting for an hour.
:::

::: example Active to passive
Rewrite in the passive with ser: (a) El director abrió la exposición. (b) Los estudiantes limpiaron las playas. (2)

**Answer.**
- (a) **La exposición fue abierta por el director.** (1) Abierta is irregular and feminine.
- (b) **Las playas fueron limpiadas por los estudiantes.** (1) Fueron is plural; limpiadas agrees with playas.
:::

::: example Se passive: singular or plural?
Complete with the correct form of the verb: (a) Aquí se ___ (hablar) español e inglés. (b) En el mercado se ___ (vender) frutas y verduras. (c) El año pasado se ___ (construir) un estadio nuevo. (3)

**Answer.** (a) habla or hablan (1): with two languages both are heard; habla is the usual form on signs. (b) venden (1): frutas y verduras is plural. (c) construyó (1): one stadium, preterite, with y in the third person.
:::

::: example "Have been …ing for": two correct versions
Translate into Spanish in two ways: "I have been learning Spanish for four years." (2)

**Answer.** Aprendo español desde hace cuatro años (1). Llevo cuatro años aprendiendo español (1). Reject he aprendido español por cuatro años: the perfect suggests it has stopped.
:::

::: example Translation into English: the classic trap
Translate into English: "Mi madre trabaja en el hospital desde hace diez años y acaba de recibir un premio." (3)

**Answer.** My mum has been working at the hospital for ten years (1) and she has just (1) received / won a prize (1). "Works in the hospital since ten years" loses the first mark; "she finishes receiving" loses the second.
:::

::: example Reading: still or no longer?
Read the sentence and answer in English.

> Mi abuelo tiene ochenta años, pero sigue trabajando en su tienda. Acaba de abrir una página web para vender sus productos.

(a) What does the grandfather still do? (b) What has he just done? (2)

**Answer.** (a) He still works in his shop (1). (b) He has just opened / set up a website (to sell his products) (1). "He follows his work" or "he finishes opening" score 0.

::: watch
- **"Have been …ing for" is present tense in Spanish**: vivo aquí desde hace dos años, llevo dos años viviendo aquí. He vivido aquí por dos años is the error examiners expect.
- **In English, never write "since two years"**: desde hace dos años is "for two years".
- **Acabar de is present for "have just"**: acabo de comer. Writing he acabado de comer means something else (I have finished eating).
- **Make the participle agree in the ser passive**: la casa fue construida; los libros fueron escritos.
- **The se passive agrees with the thing**: se vende ropa; se venden zapatos.
- **Seguir is a stem-changing verb**: sigo, sigues, sigue… and its gerund is siguiendo.
- **Desde vs desde hace**: since (a point) vs for (a period).
:::
