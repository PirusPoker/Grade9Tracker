## What it is

**Paper 4 Writing (1SP1/4H)** lasts **1 hour 20 minutes**. It is worth **50 marks, 25%** of the GCSE. There is no dictionary, and you write in full sentences.

| question | task | length | marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | choose (a) or (b): an informal letter or email to a friend, **four bullet points** | **80–90 words** | **18** (AO2 13 + AO3 5) |
| Q2 | choose (a) or (b): a blog or article, **four bullet points including pros and cons** | **130–150 words** | **22** (AO2 17 + AO3 5) |
| Q3 | translate a short English paragraph of five sentences into Spanish, getting harder sentence by sentence | | **10** (AO2 6 + AO3 4) |

**A timing plan:**
- Q1: about 20 minutes;
- Q2: about 35 minutes;
- Q3: about 10–12 minutes (Pearson suggests about 10);
- checking: the last 10 minutes.

The word counts are **guidance**: nothing is deducted for writing more or less, and everything you write is marked. Going far over wastes time and adds errors.

### How the two grids work (Q1 and Q2)

Each answer gets two marks. **AO2 (response)** asks whether you did the task: bullets covered, ideas developed, easy to understand. **AO3 (language)** asks how good your Spanish is: variety, complex language, time frames, accuracy. Language errors are judged only in AO3. An answer that is wholly irrelevant scores 0 for both.

**Q1 AO2 (13)**, in my own words:
- **11–13:** all four bullets addressed, with frequent development; comprehensible, with only an occasional unclear message.
- **8–10:** at least three bullets, some development, generally comprehensible.
- **5–7:** at least two bullets, occasional brief development; the message sometimes breaks down.
- **1–4:** at least one bullet, little extra detail.

**Q1 AO3 (5):**
- **5:** some variety and some longer sentences, occasional complex language; three time frames generally successful; generally accurate (minor errors, perhaps an occasional major one).
- **3–4:** occasional variety; some success with at least two time frames; mostly minor errors with some major ones.
- **1–2:** short, simple sentences; limited success with time frames; frequent errors.

**Q2 AO2 (17):**
- **14–17:** all four bullets, consistent development, easily comprehensible.
- **11–13:** all four bullets, frequent development, comprehensible.
- **8–10:** at least three bullets, some development.
- **5–7:** at least two bullets, occasional brief development.
- **1–4:** at least one bullet, very limited development.

**Q2 AO3 (5):**
- **4–5:** a wide variety of vocabulary and structures, frequent extended sentences and complex language; three time frames consistently successful; consistently accurate, with only minor errors.
- **2–3:** some variety and complex language; at least two time frames generally successful; mostly minor errors, an occasional major one.
- **1:** occasional variety and complex language; occasional success with time frames; frequent errors, some major.

**Three rules that decide the AO2 band:**
1. **A bullet is "addressed" if there is evidence of a response to it.** The bullets can be covered in any order.
2. **Missing a bullet caps you below the top two bands.** A brilliant Q1 answer that skips one bullet cannot go above 10 of 13; in Q2, it cannot go above 10 of 17.
3. **Development** means an extra clause or sentence that adds detail to a bullet: a reason, an example, an opinion, a consequence. Development does not have to be equal across the bullets.

### What counts as complex language

- longer sentences joined with **y, o, pero, porque, cuando, aunque, así que, mientras**;
- **relative clauses** (**que, donde, lo que**) and **infinitive clauses** (**para ganar dinero, antes de salir, sin pensar**);
- **several conjugated verbs** in one sentence, and **varied pronouns** (**lo, la, le, me, se**);
- structures **unlike English**: **me gusta, me duele, se me da bien, hace falta, desde hace, acabar de**.

**A safe Higher bank, all from Pearson's lists:**
- **desde hace** (+ present): juego al tenis desde hace tres años;
- **acabar de**: acabo de terminar mis exámenes;
- **ya no**: ya no como carne;
- **lo que más me gusta es…**; **lo bueno / lo malo es que…**;
- **vale la pena** (+ inf.): vale la pena visitar Madrid;
- **hace falta** (+ inf.): hace falta proteger el planeta;
- **me apetece** (+ inf.): me apetece ir a la playa;
- **se me da bien / mal**: se me dan bien los idiomas;
- **por un lado… por otro lado…**; **sin embargo**; **por lo tanto**;
- **cuando sea mayor…**; **mis padres quieren que…**; **para que tenga…**

Words that are not on the vocabulary list get equal credit, not extra credit, so write what you know.

### Minor and major errors

| minor: the meaning still comes through | major: the meaning is lost |
|---|---|
| a slight misspelling: biblioteka | a wrong verb form: yo sabo, mi hermano juego |
| a wrong gender or agreement: la cine, una casa bonito | the wrong tense for the time frame: ayer voy al cine |
| a missing accent: ingles | an infinitive instead of a conjugated verb: en el futuro yo trabajar |
| de el instead of del | a possessive that doesn't match the subject: Ana hace tus deberes |
| a wrong preposition: voy en el cine | an English word: hay un shopping centre |
| soy dieciséis años | a misleading spelling that becomes another word |

**The lesson:** make the **verbs** perfect. They carry the time frame, and verb errors are the ones that stop meaning.

### Q3: translation into Spanish

Five English sentences get harder as they go: the first is simple present, the last usually needs a Higher structure.
- **AO2 meaning (6):** 5–6 the meaning is consistently transferred; 3–4 most of it is transferred, with the occasional unclear or missing part; 1–2 only some parts are transferred.
- **AO3 accuracy (4):** 4 consistently accurate, with only minor errors; 3 generally accurate, with rare major errors; 2 sometimes accurate, with some major errors; 1 limited accuracy.

**Method:** translate **element by element**, then check each verb (tense, person, ending) and each adjective (agreement, position).

**The usual traps:**
- **for + time**: "I have been living here for two years" → **Vivo aquí desde hace dos años** (present tense);
- **I like going** → **me gusta ir** (infinitive, not -ando);
- **there was / there were** → **había** (description) or **hubo** (one event);
- **was** → **era / estaba** (description) or **fue / estuvo** (a completed event);
- **when** about the future → **cuando + subjunctive**: cuando sea mayor, cuando vaya a España;
- **so that** with a new subject → **para que + subjunctive**; with the same subject → **para + infinitive**;
- **adjectives after the noun, agreeing**: una ciudad grande y moderna;
- **the personal a**: visito a mis abuelos;
- **"I usually"** → **suelo** + infinitive, or **normalmente**.

### The checking routine

In the last 10 minutes, check in three passes:
1. **Verbs**: right tense for each time phrase, right person, right ending, accents on preterites (**comí, visitó**).
2. **Agreements**: articles, adjectives and possessives with their nouns.
3. **Accents and spelling**: **está, también, después, más, qué** in questions; ñ; questions opened with **¿**.

Then confirm all **four bullets** are covered in both Q1 and Q2.

**Inclusive language.** Pearson equally credits non-binary pronouns and agreements (**elle**, **-e** endings) when they are used correctly and consistently. **No binario** and **transgénero** are on the list. The standard forms always earn full marks.

::: terms
- **bullet point**: one of the four required content points in Q1 or Q2; each must show some response.
- **addressed**: Pearson's word for a bullet with evidence of a response; missing one caps the AO2 band.
- **development**: an extra clause or sentence adding a reason, example, opinion or consequence to a bullet.
- **AO2 (response)**: the mark for doing the task: bullets covered, ideas developed, message clear.
- **AO3 (language)**: the mark for variety, complex language, time frames and accuracy.
- **complex language**: subordinate clauses, relative and infinitive clauses, varied pronouns, Spanish-only structures.
- **time frames**: past, present and future; Q2's top AO3 band needs all three used consistently.
- **minor error**: a slip that doesn't stop meaning, such as a gender, a missing accent or de el.
- **major error**: a slip that stops meaning, such as a wrong verb form, wrong tense or an infinitive instead of a verb.
- **element-by-element translation**: translating each part of an English sentence, then rebuilding it in Spanish order.
:::

::: method
1. **Choose the option** whose four bullets you can all answer well, with all three time frames.
2. **Plan one line per bullet**: the verb in the right time frame, plus one development (Q1) or two (Q2).
3. **For Q2, give the pros and cons a paragraph of their own**, using por un lado… por otro lado… or lo bueno… lo malo…
4. **Write**, aiming for 80–90 or 130–150 words, with one or two Higher structures from the safe bank in each answer.
5. **Q3:** translate sentence by sentence, element by element; check the traps (for + time, cuando, para que, había / hubo).
6. **Check**: verbs, then agreements, then accents; make sure all four bullets are in.
:::

::: example Planning a Q1 answer
Plan this Q1 task (one line per bullet, Spanish verb + development): Write an email to your friend about your free time. Include: what you do after school; your opinion of a TV programme, with reasons; what you did last Saturday; your plans for the summer. (4)

**Answer.** 1 mark per bullet with a suitable verb and development:
- **Present:** Después de las clases juego al fútbol / porque me ayuda a desconectar.
- **Opinion:** Mi programa favorito es un concurso de cocina / porque los participantes son muy divertidos.
- **Past:** El sábado pasado fui al centro con mis primos / y compramos ropa.
- **Future:** Este verano voy a trabajar en un café / para ganar dinero.
:::

::: example Developing a bullet
Develop this bullet answer so that it would count as frequent development: "El fin de semana pasado fui a la playa." (3)

**Answer.** **El fin de semana pasado fui a la playa con mi familia** (who) **porque hacía muchísimo calor** (1: a reason with the imperfect). **Nadamos en el mar y comimos en un restaurante que estaba al lado del puerto** (1: more detail, a relative clause). **Lo pasamos genial, aunque mi hermano se quemó con el sol** (1: an opinion and a contrast).
:::

::: example A Q2 pros-and-cons paragraph
Write a short paragraph on the pros and cons of living in a big city. (4)

**Answer.** **Por un lado, vivir en una ciudad grande tiene muchas ventajas** (1): **hay muchos cines y tiendas, y el transporte público es muy bueno, así que no hace falta tener coche** (1). **Por otro lado, hay mucho tráfico y ruido** (1), **lo que puede ser malo para la salud** (1: a consequence with lo que).
:::

::: example Translating with traps
Translate into Spanish: (a) I have been playing the guitar for three years. (b) I like going out with my friends. (4)

**Answer.**
- (a) **Toco la guitarra** (1) **desde hace tres años** (1). Also accepted: **Llevo tres años tocando la guitarra.** Reject he tocado … por tres años.
- (b) **Me gusta** (1) **salir con mis amigos** (1). Reject me gusta saliendo.
:::

::: example Minor or major?
Say whether each error is minor or major, and correct it: (a) Mi casa es muy bonito. (b) Ayer como en un restaurante. (2)

**Answer.** (a) **Minor** (agreement): Mi casa es muy bonita (1). (b) **Major** (wrong tense for "yesterday"): Ayer comí en un restaurante (1).
:::

::: q
How many words are expected for Q1 and for Q2, and is anything deducted for going over? (3)
---
Q1 80–90 (1); Q2 130–150 (1); no, the counts are guidance and nothing is deducted (1).
:::

::: q
A Q1 answer is excellent but leaves out the past-tense bullet. What is the highest AO2 mark it can get? (1)
---
10 out of 13 (1): with only three bullets, it cannot enter the 11–13 band.
:::

::: q
Give four Higher short phrases from Pearson's list that add complex language. (4)
---
Any four (1 each): desde hace; hace falta; me apetece; por un lado / por otro lado; se me da bien / mal; vale la pena; ya no.
:::

::: q
Translate into Spanish: "When I am older, I will live in Barcelona." (2)
---
Cuando sea mayor (1), viviré / voy a vivir en Barcelona (1). Reject cuando seré / cuando soy.
:::

::: q
Translate into Spanish: (a) I work to earn money. (b) My parents work so that I have a good future. (2)
---
(a) Trabajo para ganar dinero (1): the same subject, so para + infinitive. (b) Mis padres trabajan para que yo tenga un buen futuro (1): a new subject, so para que + subjunctive.
:::

::: q
Name the three passes of the checking routine. (3)
---
Verbs (tense, person, ending) (1); agreements (articles, adjectives, possessives) (1); accents and spelling (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Missing a bullet caps AO2 below the top two bands**, however good the rest is. Tick each bullet off as you write it.
- **The time frame must match the time phrase**: el año pasado + preterite, el año que viene + future. A clash is a major error.
- **Don't use a bare infinitive as a verb**: en el futuro trabajaré, not en el futuro yo trabajar.
- **Lo haré + infinitive is not a future**: use voy a + infinitive or the future tense.
- **Q2 needs a real pros-and-cons section**, not one adjective each way.
- **Develop every bullet with a reason or example**: a list of short sentences sits in the lower bands.
- **Don't write far beyond the word count**: extra words add errors, not marks.
- **Translation traps**: for + time is desde hace + present; I like going is me gusta ir; when + future is cuando + subjunctive.
:::
