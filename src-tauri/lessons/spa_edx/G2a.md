## What it is

This lesson covers two short sections of Edexcel's verb list: **negation** and **interrogatives**. At Foundation and Higher you need **no** and the negatives **nada, nunca, nadie, ninguno**, placed before or after the verb. Higher adds **ya no** (no longer), **tampoco** (not either, neither), **no… ni…** (not… or…) and **ni… ni…** (neither… nor…). For questions you need **intonation** (a statement said as a question), **question word + verb + subject** word order, and, at Higher, **a preposition in front of the question word** (¿Con quién hablas?), which Pearson lists in its prepositions section.

Where it turns up:
- **Papers 2 and 3.** Negatives are Pearson's favourite distractors. "Ya no coge el autobús" means she does **not** take the bus any more, so an option saying "she takes the bus" is a trap. Ni… ni… removes two options at once.
- **Paper 1 role play.** At Higher you must **ask two questions**. A prompt such as "Ask a question about the price" needs a real question (¿Cuánto cuesta?), not a single word.
- **Paper 1 conversation and Paper 4.** "No me gusta nada", "ya no juego al fútbol" and "ni mi hermano ni yo…" are quick ways to show range.
- **Paper 4 Question 3.** "I never…", "nobody…", "I no longer…" and "neither… nor…" are standard translation targets.

### No

**No** goes **directly before the verb**, and before any object pronoun attached to it: **No juego al tenis. No lo sé. No me gusta.** With two verbs it goes before the first: **No puedo salir. No voy a ir.** With the perfect tense it goes before haber: **No he terminado.** It never goes between the pronoun and the verb (lo no sé is wrong).

### Nada, nunca, nadie, ninguno

| Spanish | English | after the verb (with no) | before the verb (without no) |
|---|---|---|---|
| nada | nothing, not anything | No hago nada. | Nada me interesa. |
| nunca | never | No como carne nunca. | Nunca como carne. |
| nadie | nobody, not anyone | No viene nadie. | Nadie viene. |
| ninguno / ninguna (ningún) | none, no, not any | No tengo ningún libro. No queda ninguna. | Ninguno de mis amigos fuma. |

The rule is simple: **if the negative word comes after the verb, put no before the verb**. If it comes first, no is not needed. Spanish is happy with several negatives together: **No hago nunca nada** (I never do anything). **Nadie** as an object needs the personal a: **No conozco a nadie.**

Ninguno agrees with its noun, and becomes **ningún** before a masculine singular noun: no hay ningún museo; no hay ninguna tienda; ¿Libros? No tengo ninguno.

### Higher negatives

**Ya no** = no longer, not any more. It goes before the verb, and it is the opposite of **todavía** (still):
- **Ya no juego al fútbol.** = I don't play football any more.
- **Antes iba al instituto en autobús, pero ya no.** = I used to go to school by bus, but not any more.
- **Todavía vivo con mis padres.** = I still live with my parents.

**Tampoco** = not either, neither. After the verb it needs no; before the verb it stands alone:
- **No me gusta el té. —A mí tampoco.** = I don't like tea. —Me neither.
- **No como carne y tampoco como pescado.** = I don't eat meat and I don't eat fish either. Also: no como carne ni pescado.

It is the negative partner of **también**: Me gusta el café. —A mí también.

**No… ni…** = not… or…, not… nor…: **No tengo tiempo ni dinero.** (I have neither time nor money.) **No bebo café ni té.**

**Ni… ni…** = neither… nor…, for emphasis. Before the verb there is no no, and two singular subjects usually take a plural verb:
- **Ni mi padre ni mi madre hablan inglés.** = Neither my dad nor my mum speaks English.
- **No me gusta ni el fútbol ni el baloncesto.** = I like neither football nor basketball. (After the verb, no comes first.)

### Asking questions

**1. Intonation.** Keep the statement word order and make it a question with your voice, and with ¿ … ? in writing: **¿Tienes hermanos? ¿Vas a la fiesta? ¿Hay un supermercado cerca?** Every question opens with **¿** and every exclamation with **¡**. A question with a question word usually falls in pitch at the end; a yes/no question rises.

**2. Question word + verb + subject.** The subject, if you mention it, comes **after** the verb, and it can often be left out:
- **¿Dónde está (Diego)?** = Where is Diego?
- **¿Qué hace (la chica)?** = What is the girl doing?
- **¿Cuándo empieza la película?** = When does the film start?
- **¿Cómo se llama tu hermana?** = What is your sister called?

| question word | English | example |
|---|---|---|
| ¿qué? | what? which? (+ noun) | ¿Qué haces? ¿Qué película quieres ver? |
| ¿quién? ¿quiénes? | who? | ¿Quién es? ¿Quiénes son? |
| ¿cuándo? | when? | ¿Cuándo vuelves? |
| ¿por qué? | why? | ¿Por qué no vienes? |
| ¿cómo? | how? what… like? | ¿Cómo es tu casa? |
| ¿cuál? ¿cuáles? | which (one)? what? | ¿Cuál es tu número? |
| ¿cuánto/a/os/as? | how much? how many? | ¿Cuánto cuesta? ¿Cuántas personas? |
| ¿dónde? | where? | ¿Dónde vives? |

All question words carry an accent, and they keep it in indirect questions: **No sé dónde está.** Without the accent they are different words: **porque** (because) answers **¿por qué?** (why?); **que** (that), **cuando** (when, as a conjunction), **donde** (where, as a relative), **como** (like, as).

**3. Prepositions before question words (Higher).** English often ends a question with a preposition ("Who are you going with?"). Spanish **always** puts the preposition first:
- **¿Con quién vas al cine?** = Who are you going to the cinema with?
- **¿De dónde eres?** = Where are you from?
- **¿A qué hora sale el tren?** = What time does the train leave?
- **¿Para qué sirve esto?** = What is this for?
- **¿De qué hablas?** = What are you talking about?
- **¿En qué trabaja tu madre?** = What does your mum do (for work)?
- **¿Adónde vas?** = Where are you going (to)? (a + dónde, written as one word.)

### Questions in the role play

The teacher uses **usted** (¿Qué desea? ¿Puedo ayudarle?), but you may ask with tú or usted; only communication is marked. Each question prompt is worth 2 marks if the question is clear. Useful frames:
- **¿Cuánto cuesta…?** ¿Cuánto es?
- **¿A qué hora abre / cierra / empieza / sale…?**
- **¿Dónde está…? ¿Hay… por aquí?**
- **¿Se puede…?** (Can you / one…?) ¿Se puede pagar con tarjeta?
- **¿Tiene…?** ¿Tiene una habitación libre?

::: terms
- **no**: placed directly before the verb and any object pronoun: no lo sé, no puedo ir.
- **double negative**: no before the verb with nada, nunca, nadie, ninguno after it: no veo a nadie.
- **nadie / nada**: nobody / nothing; before the verb they need no extra no (nadie viene).
- **ningún / ninguna**: no, not any; agrees with the noun and loses -o before a masculine singular noun.
- **ya no**: no longer, not any more; the opposite of todavía (still).
- **tampoco**: not either, neither; the negative partner of también (a mí tampoco).
- **ni… ni…**: neither… nor…; no extra no when it comes before the verb.
- **intonation question**: statement word order turned into a question by the voice and ¿ … ?
- **inversion**: question word + verb + subject: ¿Dónde vive tu tía?
- **preposition + question word**: Spanish puts the preposition first: ¿Con quién? ¿De dónde?
:::

::: method
1. **For a negative, put no directly before the verb** (and before any pronoun or haber).
2. **If a negative word follows the verb**, keep no in front: no viene nadie. If it comes first, drop no: nadie viene.
3. **Choose the Higher negative by meaning**: not any more → ya no; not either → tampoco; not X or Y → no… ni…; neither X nor Y → ni… ni….
4. **For a question, pick the question word first** and check its accent; then the verb; then the subject if needed.
5. **If the English question ends in a preposition**, move it to the front in Spanish: ¿Con quién…? ¿De dónde…? ¿A qué hora…?
6. **In reading and listening**, circle every no, nunca, ya no and ni before choosing an answer: the negative usually decides it.
:::

::: watch
- **No before the verb when the negative word follows it**: no hay nadie, no hago nada. Leaving out no is the most common negative error.
- **No before pronouns and haber**: no lo tengo, no he ido. Never "lo no tengo" or "he no ido".
- **Ya no is not "already no"**: it means no longer. Todavía means still. Both flip answers in reading.
- **Isolated words score 0** in reading and listening answers; "bus" does not answer "How does she go to school now?".
- **Accents on question words**: ¿qué?, ¿dónde?, ¿cuándo? need them; porque (because) does not.
- **Prepositions go first** in Spanish questions: ¿De dónde eres?, never ¿Dónde eres de?
- **Role play questions must be questions**: a single word with a rising voice scores at most 1.
:::

::: test
Translate this passage into English.

> Mi abuelo vive solo en un pueblo pequeño. Ya no usa el coche porque no ve bien, y no hay autobuses ni trenes. Nadie lo visita durante la semana, pero yo voy a verlo los domingos. ---
Marked as a whole. **9–10**: the full meaning is transferred in natural English. **6–8**: most of the meaning comes across; occasional errors blur a detail. **3–5**: parts are transferred, but some errors lose the message. **1–2**: only isolated parts are clear.

**Model.** My grandad lives alone in a small village. He no longer uses the car because he can't see well, and there are no buses or trains. Nobody visits him during the week, but I go to see him on Sundays. He never complains, although he doesn't have internet either. Why doesn't he come to live with us? He says he doesn't want to leave his house.

**Key points:** ya no usa el coche (he no longer uses the car); no hay… ni… (no buses or trains); nadie lo visita (nobody visits him); nunca se queja (he never complains); tampoco tiene (he doesn't have… either); ¿por qué no…? (why doesn't…?).
