## What it is

**Secondary storage** is **non-volatile** storage used to keep programs, data and files **permanently** — even when the computer is switched off. It is needed because **RAM is volatile** and loses everything when the power goes, and **ROM is read-only and tiny**. Secondary storage is where the operating system, applications and all of a user's files live when they are not in use. It has **much larger capacity** than primary storage but is **slower** to access.

There are three types on the specification: **magnetic**, **optical** and **solid state**.

**Magnetic storage** (hard disk drive / HDD, magnetic tape)
- **How it works:** data is stored as tiny **magnetised spots** on spinning **platters** (or on tape). The **direction of magnetisation** represents a 1 or a 0, and a **read/write head** moves across the surface to read or change them.
- **HDD:** high capacity and low **cost per gigabyte**, but it has **moving parts**, so it is slower, noisier, uses more power and is easily damaged by knocks.
- **Magnetic tape:** very high capacity and very cheap, but **serial (sequential) access** makes it slow to find data — used for **backups and archiving**.

**Optical storage** (CD, DVD, Blu-ray)
- **How it works:** a **laser** burns and reads a pattern of **pits and lands** on the disc surface. Pits and lands reflect the laser differently, representing 1s and 0s.
- Cheap and portable, but **low capacity**, slow, easily scratched and often **read-only or write-once**. Used mainly for distributing films, music and software (now declining).

**Solid-state storage** (SSD, USB flash drive, SD card)
- **How it works:** data is stored using **flash memory**, which traps **electrical charge** in cells with **no moving parts**.
- **Fast**, silent, **durable** (shock-resistant), low power and **physically small**, but a **higher cost per gigabyte** than magnetic, and each cell wears out after a very large number of writes.

**Comparing storage** — weigh devices on **capacity, speed, portability, durability, reliability and cost (per gigabyte)**:
- **Capacity per pound:** magnetic (HDD, tape) wins — cheapest per gigabyte.
- **Speed and durability:** solid state wins — no moving parts.
- **Portability / cheap distribution:** optical and USB flash are cheap and easy to hand over.

**Choosing a device** means matching these to the **scenario**, for example:
- A **smartphone or thin laptop** → **SSD** (small, durable, fast, low power).
- A **cheap backup of huge amounts of data**, rarely accessed → **magnetic tape** or **HDD** (capacity per pound).
- **Distributing a film**, or giving someone a file physically → **optical disc** or **USB flash drive**.
- A **digital camera** → **SD card** (solid state).

::: method
Choosing and justifying secondary storage:
1. **Say why secondary storage is needed at all:** non-volatile, permanent, high-capacity storage for when the power is off.
2. **Read the scenario's priorities:** does it most need portability, durability, speed, huge capacity or lowest cost?
3. **Pick the type:** solid state for **speed/durability/portability/low power**; magnetic for **capacity per pound / backups**; optical for **cheap read-only distribution**.
4. **Name the actual device** — SSD, HDD, magnetic tape, DVD, SD card, USB flash drive.
5. **Justify against THIS scenario** using the comparison factors, and for top marks **name a drawback and say why it is acceptable here**.
:::

::: example Why secondary storage is needed
Explain why a computer needs secondary storage as well as RAM. [2]

RAM is volatile, so it loses all its contents when the computer is switched off. Secondary storage is non-volatile, so it keeps programs, the operating system and files permanently, and it also provides far more capacity than RAM.
:::

::: example How a hard disk stores data
Describe how a magnetic hard disk drive stores data. [2]

Data is stored as magnetised spots on spinning platters, with the direction of magnetisation representing 1s and 0s. A read/write head moves across the surface of the platters to read the data or to change it.
:::

::: example Storage for a laptop
A student wants storage for a laptop they carry everywhere and want to boot quickly. Recommend a device and justify your choice. [4]

A solid-state drive (SSD). It has no moving parts, so it is durable and will survive being knocked about in a bag, which suits a device carried everywhere. It is also very fast, so the laptop starts up and loads programs quickly, and it is small and low-power, which helps battery life. It costs more per gigabyte than a hard disk, but the capacity of an SSD is enough for a laptop, so the trade-off is worthwhile.
:::

::: example Cheap archive of huge data
A company must back up 50 TB of data as cheaply as possible and will rarely need to read it back. Recommend a suitable storage medium and justify it. [3]

Magnetic tape. It offers very high capacity at the lowest cost per gigabyte, which matters most when backing up such a large amount of data cheaply. Its main drawback is slow, sequential access, but as the backup is rarely read back that does not matter here, so tape is the best fit.
:::

::: example Solid state explained
Describe how solid-state storage stores data, and give one advantage over a hard disk drive. [3]

Solid-state storage uses flash memory, storing data as electrical charge trapped in cells with no moving parts. Because it has no moving parts it is faster to access and more durable than a hard disk drive, which relies on spinning platters and a moving read/write head.
:::

::: q
Why is it important that secondary storage is non-volatile?
---
So that data and programs are kept permanently, even when the computer is switched off.
:::

::: q
How does optical storage represent data?
---
As a pattern of pits and lands on the disc, read by a laser that reflects off them differently.
:::

::: q
How does solid-state storage store data?
---
Using flash memory that traps electrical charge in cells, with no moving parts.
:::

::: q
Give one advantage and one disadvantage of an SSD compared with an HDD.
---
Advantage: faster and more durable (no moving parts). Disadvantage: higher cost per gigabyte, and cells wear out after many writes.
:::

::: q
Which storage medium is best for archiving large amounts of data cheaply?
---
Magnetic tape — very high capacity at the lowest cost per gigabyte.
:::

::: q
Why is an HDD less suitable than an SSD for a device that is frequently moved and knocked?
---
An HDD has moving parts (spinning platters and a head) that are easily damaged by shocks.
:::

::: q
Name a solid-state device used to store photos in a digital camera.
---
An SD (memory) card.
:::

::: q
Give two factors you would compare when choosing a storage device.
---
Any two of: capacity, speed, portability, durability, reliability, cost per gigabyte.
:::

::: watch
- **Justify against the scenario, not in general** — name the device **and** why it fits **this** use.
- Keep the three mechanisms straight: **solid state = flash, no moving parts**; **magnetic = spinning platters + head**; **optical = laser + pits and lands**.
- An **SSD is solid state** — never call it magnetic.
- Cost is usually **cost per gigabyte**: HDD and tape win on capacity per pound, SSD on speed and durability.
- **Optical is low capacity**, not high — don't recommend a DVD for backing up terabytes.
- For the top mark, **admit a drawback** of your chosen device and say **why it is acceptable** in that scenario.
:::
