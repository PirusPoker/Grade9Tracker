## What it is

**Excretion** is the removal of the **waste products of metabolism** (chemical reactions in cells) and of substances in excess. Three organs excrete:

- **Lungs** — **carbon dioxide** (from respiration), and water vapour.
- **Kidneys** — **urea** (made in the liver from excess amino acids), excess **water** and excess **salts** (mineral ions), as **urine**.
- **Skin** — **sweat**: water, salts and a little urea (mainly for cooling).

**The urinary system**: two **kidneys** filter the blood; each is supplied by a **renal artery** and drained by a **renal vein**; urine passes down the **ureters** to the **bladder**, where it is stored, and leaves through the **urethra**.

**The nephron** — each kidney contains about a million of these microscopic tubules, where urine is made:

1. **Ultrafiltration** in the **Bowman's capsule**: blood enters the **glomerulus** (a knot of capillaries) at **high pressure** because the vessel leaving is narrower than the one entering. The pressure forces **small molecules** — water, glucose, salts, urea — out of the blood through the capillary walls and the capsule wall into the nephron, forming the **filtrate**. **Blood cells and large proteins** are too big to pass and stay in the blood. Filtration is **not selective** — everything small goes through, useful or not.
2. **Selective reabsorption** along the tubule: **all the glucose** is reabsorbed into the blood in the **proximal convoluted tubule** by **active transport** (so there is normally no glucose in urine); **most of the water** is reabsorbed by osmosis — in the proximal tubule, the **loop of Henle** and, under hormonal control, the **collecting duct**; salts are reabsorbed as needed.
3. What remains — **urea, excess water and excess salts** — is **urine**, which passes into the collecting duct, the ureter and the bladder.

**Osmoregulation** — controlling the water content of the blood — uses the hormone **ADH** (anti-diuretic hormone), released by the **pituitary gland**:

- Blood too **concentrated** (after sweating, little drinking) → detected by the brain (hypothalamus) → **more ADH** released → the **collecting ducts become more permeable** to water → **more water reabsorbed** into the blood → a **small volume of concentrated urine**.
- Blood too **dilute** (after drinking a lot) → **less ADH** → collecting ducts less permeable → less water reabsorbed → a **large volume of dilute urine**.

This is **negative feedback**: the response reverses the change.

::: method
1. **Excretion table**: organ → product → where the product came from (CO₂ from respiration; urea from the breakdown of amino acids in the liver).
2. **Nephron in two stages**: ultrafiltration (pressure, small molecules through, cells and proteins stay) then selective reabsorption (glucose by active transport, water by osmosis). Say which is selective and which is not.
3. **Glucose in urine** = a sign the reabsorption capacity is exceeded (diabetes) — the tubule normally reabsorbs it all.
4. **ADH chain**: blood concentration → detected → ADH more/less → collecting duct permeability → water reabsorbed → urine volume and concentration. Always give both the volume *and* the concentration of the urine.
5. **Negative feedback**: name it when the change is reversed.
:::

::: example Excretion
Name the main excretory product of the lungs, the kidneys and the skin, and state where urea comes from. (4)

Lungs — carbon dioxide (1); kidneys — urea (1); skin — sweat containing water and salts (1). Urea is made in the liver from the breakdown of excess amino acids (1).
:::

::: example Ultrafiltration
Explain how ultrafiltration takes place in the kidney. (4)

Blood enters the glomerulus at high pressure because the vessel leaving is narrower than the one entering (1). The pressure forces water and small molecules — glucose, salts, urea — out of the capillaries and through the wall of the Bowman's capsule into the nephron (1). Blood cells and large plasma proteins are too big to pass through and remain in the blood (1). The process is not selective: useful substances such as glucose are filtered out along with the waste (1).
:::

::: example Reabsorption
Explain why glucose is normally absent from urine even though it is present in the filtrate. (2)

All the glucose is reabsorbed from the proximal convoluted tubule back into the blood (1) by active transport, using energy from respiration (1).
:::

::: example ADH
A person drinks a large volume of water. Explain how the kidneys respond. (4)

The blood becomes more dilute, which is detected by the hypothalamus in the brain (1). The pituitary gland releases less ADH (1), so the walls of the collecting ducts become less permeable to water (1) and less water is reabsorbed into the blood; a large volume of dilute urine is produced, restoring the blood's water content — negative feedback (1).
:::

::: q
Define excretion. (1)
---
The removal of the waste products of metabolism and of substances in excess from the body.
:::

::: q
Name the vessels that carry blood to and from a kidney. (2)
---
Renal artery (to); renal vein (from).
:::

::: q
Put in order the structures urine passes through after leaving the kidney. (1)
---
Ureter → bladder → urethra.
:::

::: q
Why are proteins not found in the filtrate? (1)
---
They are too large to pass through the capillary and capsule walls.
:::

::: q
Name three substances found in urine. (3)
---
Urea; water; salts (mineral ions).
:::

::: q
Explain why the kidney's reabsorption is described as selective. (2)
---
Only useful substances — all glucose, most water, some salts — are taken back into the blood (1); waste such as urea is left in the tubule to be excreted (1).
:::

::: q
Where is ADH produced, and what is its target organ? (2)
---
The pituitary gland (1); the kidney — specifically the collecting ducts (1).
:::

::: q
On a hot day a person sweats heavily. Describe the effect on ADH release and on their urine. (3)
---
The blood becomes more concentrated, so more ADH is released (1); the collecting ducts reabsorb more water (1); a small volume of concentrated urine is produced (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why kidney failure is life-threatening. (2)
---
Urea and excess salts and water build up in the blood (1), poisoning cells and upsetting the water balance, so dialysis or a transplant is needed (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Filtration is not selective; reabsorption is.** Reversing them is the classic error.
- Glucose is reabsorbed by **active transport** — all of it; water by **osmosis**.
- Urea is made in the **liver**, excreted by the **kidney**.
- ADH: **more ADH → more water reabsorbed → less, more concentrated urine.** Give volume and concentration.
- The **pituitary** releases ADH; the **collecting duct** responds. Name both.
:::

::: test
Describe the structure of a nephron, naming its main parts in order. (3)
---
Bowman's capsule surrounding the glomerulus (1); proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle (1); distal convoluted tubule leading to the collecting duct (1).
:::

::: test
Describe what happens in the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule. (3)
---
Blood enters the glomerulus at high pressure (1); small molecules — water, glucose, salts, urea — are forced out through the capillary walls into the capsule: ultrafiltration (1); blood cells and proteins are too large to pass (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how glucose is returned to the blood from the filtrate, and why. (2)
---
Selectively reabsorbed in the proximal tubule by active transport (1); the body needs glucose for respiration, so none is wasted in urine (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how ADH regulates the water content of the blood on a day when a person drinks very little. (4)
---
The blood becomes too concentrated, detected by the hypothalamus (1); the pituitary releases more ADH (1); ADH makes the collecting ducts more permeable, so more water is reabsorbed into the blood (1); a small volume of concentrated urine is produced (1).
:::

::: test
Name the organ that produces urea and explain where it comes from. (2)
---
The liver (1); from the breakdown (deamination) of excess amino acids (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why the presence of glucose in urine may indicate diabetes. (2)
---
In diabetes the blood glucose can be so high that the kidney cannot reabsorb it all (1); the excess is left in the filtrate and passes into the urine (1).
:::

::: test
Name three substances excreted by the body and the organ responsible for each. (3)
---
Carbon dioxide — lungs (1); urea — kidneys (1); excess water, salts (sweat) — skin, or kidneys (1).
:::

::: test
Describe the path of urine from the kidney to the outside of the body. (1)
---
Kidney → ureter → bladder → urethra.
:::
