## What it is

**A balanced diet** provides the right amounts of every nutrient group for a person's age, sex and activity:

| Nutrient | Function | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | main source of energy | bread, rice, pasta, potatoes |
| **Proteins** | growth and repair of cells; making enzymes and antibodies | meat, fish, eggs, beans |
| **Lipids** | energy store, insulation, cell membranes | butter, oils, nuts |
| **Vitamin A** | good vision (night vision), healthy skin | liver, carrots, fish oil |
| **Vitamin C** | healthy skin and gums, healing; deficiency → **scurvy** | citrus fruit, peppers |
| **Vitamin D** | absorption of calcium for bones and teeth; deficiency → **rickets** | oily fish, eggs, made in skin in sunlight |
| **Calcium** (mineral) | strong bones and teeth | milk, cheese |
| **Iron** (mineral) | making **haemoglobin** in red blood cells; deficiency → **anaemia** | red meat, spinach |
| **Water** | solvent for reactions; transport; temperature control | drinks, food |
| **Dietary fibre** (cellulose) | keeps food moving through the gut; prevents constipation | vegetables, wholegrains |

Energy needs vary with **age** (growing teenagers need more), **activity** (athletes and manual workers need more) and **pregnancy** (more energy, protein, iron, calcium). Too much energy is stored as fat → obesity; too little → underweight and deficiency diseases.

**The alimentary canal** — the tube food passes through — and the process:

1. **Ingestion** — taking food into the mouth.
2. **Digestion** — breaking large insoluble molecules into small soluble ones. **Mechanical** (teeth chewing, stomach churning, bile emulsifying) increases surface area; **chemical** (enzymes) breaks bonds.
3. **Absorption** — soluble products pass through the gut wall into the blood (small intestine) or water into the blood (large intestine).
4. **Assimilation** — absorbed molecules are used by cells (glucose for respiration, amino acids to build proteins).
5. **Egestion** — undigested food leaves as faeces through the anus (not excretion — it was never inside cells).

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Mouth** | teeth chew (mechanical); **saliva** from salivary glands contains **amylase** (starch → maltose) and lubricates |
| **Oesophagus** | muscular tube; **peristalsis** — waves of muscle contraction behind the food push it along |
| **Stomach** | muscular churning; **gastric juice** with **hydrochloric acid** (kills bacteria, pH 2 for pepsin) and **protease (pepsin)** — protein → smaller peptides |
| **Small intestine** — **duodenum** | receives **bile** from the gall bladder and **pancreatic juice** (amylase, protease, lipase) from the pancreas; most chemical digestion happens here; the wall also secretes enzymes (maltase, etc.) |
| **Small intestine** — **ileum** | **absorption** of digested food into the blood; long, folded, covered in **villi** |
| **Large intestine** — **colon** | **water absorbed** into the blood, leaving semi-solid faeces |
| **Rectum and anus** | faeces stored, then egested |
| **Pancreas** | makes pancreatic juice (amylase, protease/trypsin, lipase); alkaline |
| **Liver** | makes **bile**; stored in the **gall bladder**; released into the duodenum |

**Digestive enzymes** — large insoluble → small soluble:

- **Amylase** (saliva, pancreas): starch → **maltose**; **maltase** (small intestine wall): maltose → **glucose**.
- **Proteases** (pepsin in the stomach, trypsin from the pancreas): proteins → **amino acids** (via peptides).
- **Lipase** (pancreas): lipids → **fatty acids and glycerol**.

**Bile** — made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder, released into the duodenum. It is **not an enzyme**. Two jobs: (1) **neutralises stomach acid** — it is alkaline, giving the pH the pancreatic enzymes need; (2) **emulsifies fats** — breaks large fat globules into tiny droplets, increasing the **surface area** for lipase to work on.

**The small intestine is adapted for absorption**: very **long** (about 6 m) and its inner wall is **folded** and covered with millions of finger-like **villi**, each with **microvilli** — an enormous **surface area**; the villus wall is **one cell thick** — short diffusion distance; each villus has a **rich blood supply** (capillary network) to carry away glucose and amino acids, maintaining the concentration gradient, and a **lacteal** (part of the lymph system) to carry away fatty acids and glycerol; **mitochondria** in the cells provide energy for **active transport** of glucose when its concentration in the gut is low.

::: method
1. **Diet questions**: nutrient → function → source → deficiency disease (scurvy, rickets, anaemia). Energy needs: age, activity, pregnancy, with a reason each.
2. **Alimentary canal in order**: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, ileum) → large intestine (colon) → rectum → anus; pancreas, liver and gall bladder are attached organs, not part of the tube.
3. **Enzyme = substrate → product, and where.** Amylase: starch → maltose, mouth and duodenum. Never say "bile digests fat".
4. **Bile: two functions, both about helping lipase** — neutralising acid (pH) and emulsifying (surface area).
5. **Absorption adaptations**: surface area, thin wall, blood supply, lacteal, active transport — each with the reason.
6. **Digestion vs egestion vs excretion**: egestion removes undigested food (never absorbed); excretion removes metabolic waste made in cells.
:::

::: example Diet
Explain why a pregnant woman needs more iron and calcium in her diet than other adults. (2)

Iron is needed to make haemoglobin for the extra red blood cells of the mother and the fetus (1); calcium is needed to build the bones and teeth of the developing fetus (1).
:::

::: example Enzymes
Name the enzyme that digests protein in the stomach, state its product, and explain why the stomach also secretes hydrochloric acid. (3)

Pepsin (a protease); it breaks proteins into peptides/amino acids (1). The acid gives the low pH (about 2) at which pepsin works best (1) and kills bacteria in the food (1).
:::

::: example Bile
Explain the role of bile in digestion. (3)

Bile is alkaline, so it neutralises the acid arriving from the stomach, giving the pH the pancreatic enzymes need (1). It emulsifies fats — breaks large globules into tiny droplets (1) — increasing the surface area for lipase to act on, so fat is digested faster (1). Bile contains no enzymes.
:::

::: example Absorption
Explain how the ileum is adapted for the absorption of digested food. (4)

Its wall is folded and covered with villi and microvilli, giving a huge surface area for absorption (1). The wall of each villus is one cell thick, so the diffusion distance is short (1). A dense capillary network carries away glucose and amino acids, keeping a steep concentration gradient, while a lacteal carries away fats (1). Epithelial cells have many mitochondria to provide energy for the active transport of glucose against its gradient (1).
:::

::: q
State the function of protein and one food that supplies it. (2)
---
Growth and repair (making new cells, enzymes) (1); e.g. meat, fish, eggs, beans (1).
:::

::: q
Name the deficiency disease caused by a lack of vitamin C, vitamin D and iron. (3)
---
Scurvy; rickets; anaemia.
:::

::: q
Explain why fibre is needed in the diet even though it is not digested. (2)
---
It adds bulk that the gut muscles can push against (1), keeping food moving by peristalsis and preventing constipation (1).
:::

::: q
Describe peristalsis. (2)
---
Waves of contraction of muscles in the gut wall behind the food (1) that push it along the alimentary canal (1).
:::

::: q
Name the enzyme, substrate and product for the digestion of starch. (3)
---
Amylase; starch; maltose (then maltase converts maltose to glucose).
:::

::: q
Where is lipase made, and what does it produce? (2)
---
The pancreas (1); fatty acids and glycerol (1).
:::

::: q
Explain the difference between egestion and excretion. (2)
---
Egestion removes undigested food that was never absorbed into the body, as faeces (1); excretion removes waste products made by metabolism in cells, such as urea and carbon dioxide (1).
:::

::: q
What is absorbed in the large intestine? (1)
---
Water.
:::

::: q
Explain why digestion is necessary. (2)
---
Food molecules are large and insoluble (1); they must be broken into small soluble molecules that can be absorbed through the gut wall into the blood (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a person whose gall bladder has been removed may have difficulty digesting fatty meals. (2)
---
Less bile reaches the duodenum, so fats are not emulsified into small droplets (1); the smaller surface area means lipase digests fat more slowly (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Bile is not an enzyme** — it emulsifies fat and neutralises acid. "Bile digests fat" scores nothing.
- Learn each enzyme as **name → substrate → product → where made / where it works**.
- Absorption is in the **small intestine (ileum)**; the large intestine absorbs **water**.
- **Egestion ≠ excretion.**
- Adaptations for absorption must each have a **reason**: surface area, thin wall, blood supply, lacteal, mitochondria for active transport.
:::
