## What it is

Biology begins by asking what "living" means and sorting living things into groups by their features. The exam expects the **eight characteristics** as a list you can reel off, and one or two named examples for each group.

**The eight characteristics of living organisms** — all living things:

1. **Require nutrition** — take in and use materials for energy, growth and repair (plants make food by photosynthesis; animals eat).
2. **Respire** — release energy from food in every cell (not the same as breathing).
3. **Excrete** — remove the waste products of metabolism (carbon dioxide, urea).
4. **Respond to their surroundings** (sensitivity) — detect and react to changes (light, temperature, touch).
5. **Move** — change position; a plant's shoots grow towards light, an animal walks.
6. **Control their internal conditions** (homeostasis) — keep temperature, water and glucose steady.
7. **Reproduce** — produce offspring.
8. **Grow and develop** — increase in size and cell number; become more complex.

A memory aid: **MRS C GREN** — Movement, Respiration, Sensitivity, Control, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion, Nutrition.

**The eukaryotic groups** (cells with a nucleus):

| Group | Features | Named examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Plants** | multicellular; cells contain **chloroplasts** so they **photosynthesise**; cells have **cellulose cell walls**; store carbohydrate as **starch or sucrose** | flowering plants — a cereal such as **maize**; a herbaceous legume such as **peas or beans** |
| **Animals** | multicellular; **no chloroplasts**, cannot photosynthesise; **no cell walls**; usually have **nervous coordination** and can **move from place to place**; often store carbohydrate as **glycogen** | mammals such as **humans**; insects such as the **housefly** and **mosquito** |
| **Fungi** | cannot photosynthesise; body usually organised into a **mycelium** made of thread-like **hyphae** containing **many nuclei**; some are single-celled; cell walls of **chitin**; feed by **saprotrophic nutrition** — secrete extracellular enzymes onto food and absorb the digested products; may store carbohydrate as glycogen | **Mucor** (typical hyphal structure); **yeast** (single-celled) |
| **Protoctists** | **microscopic single-celled** organisms; some have features like an animal cell (**Amoeba**), others have chloroplasts and are more like plants (**Chlorella**); the pathogenic **Plasmodium** causes **malaria** | Amoeba, Chlorella, Plasmodium |

**Prokaryotes** (no nucleus) — **bacteria**: microscopic single-celled organisms; a **cell wall**, cell membrane, cytoplasm and **plasmids**; **no nucleus** — instead a single **circular chromosome** of DNA lying in the cytoplasm; some can photosynthesise, but most feed off other organisms, living or dead. Examples: **Lactobacillus bulgaricus** (used to make yoghurt from milk); **Pneumococcus** (a pathogen causing pneumonia).

**Viruses** are **not living organisms** — they do not show the characteristics of life, since they cannot reproduce on their own. They are **small particles, smaller than bacteria**, that are **parasitic** and reproduce **only inside living cells**; they infect every type of living organism. Structure: a **protein coat** surrounding **genetic material** (DNA or RNA). Examples: **tobacco mosaic virus** (discolours tobacco leaves by stopping chloroplast formation), **influenza virus** (flu), **HIV** (causes AIDS).

**Pathogens** are organisms that cause disease. They occur in four groups: **fungi**, **bacteria**, **protoctists** and **viruses**. Examples: *Mucor* (moulds), *Pneumococcus* (pneumonia), *Plasmodium* (malaria), HIV and influenza (viruses).

::: method
1. **Characteristics questions**: give all eight, each with one word of explanation if asked ("excretion — removing metabolic waste"). Respiration is releasing energy, not breathing; nutrition is obtaining materials, not just eating.
2. **"Which group?"** Use the deciding features: cell wall + chloroplasts → plant; cell wall of chitin + hyphae → fungus; no cell wall → animal; single-celled with a nucleus → protoctist; no nucleus + plasmids → bacterium; not a cell at all → virus.
3. **Name the spec examples** — maize, peas/beans, human, housefly, mosquito, Mucor, yeast, Amoeba, Chlorella, Plasmodium, Lactobacillus, Pneumococcus, TMV, influenza, HIV. They are worth marks by themselves.
4. **Fungal feeding**: "saprotrophic — secrete enzymes onto dead material and absorb the products" earns two marks; "absorb food" earns none.
5. **Viruses**: always say *why* they are not living — they cannot reproduce independently — and give the protein coat + genetic material structure.
:::

::: example List and define
State three characteristics of living organisms and explain what each means. (3)

Respiration — releasing energy from food inside cells (1). Excretion — removing the waste products of metabolism such as carbon dioxide (1). Sensitivity — detecting and responding to changes in the surroundings (1).
:::

::: example Compare groups
Describe two differences between the cells of a fungus and the cells of a plant. (2)

Fungal cell walls are made of chitin, whereas plant cell walls are made of cellulose (1). Fungal cells have no chloroplasts and cannot photosynthesise, whereas plant cells contain chloroplasts (1).
:::

::: example Explain — fungal nutrition
Explain how a fungus such as *Mucor* obtains its food. (3)

It feeds by saprotrophic nutrition (1): the hyphae secrete extracellular digestive enzymes onto dead organic material (1), which break it down into soluble products that the hyphae then absorb (1).
:::

::: example Explain — viruses
Explain why viruses are not classed as living organisms, and describe their structure. (4)

Viruses show none of the characteristics of life on their own — in particular they cannot reproduce independently (1); they are parasites that reproduce only inside a living host cell, using the cell's machinery (1). Structurally a virus is a particle, much smaller than a bacterium, consisting of a protein coat (1) surrounding a strand of genetic material, either DNA or RNA (1).
:::

::: q
List the eight characteristics of living organisms. (4)
---
Nutrition, respiration, excretion, sensitivity (response), movement, control of internal conditions (homeostasis), reproduction, growth and development.
:::

::: q
Give one named example of a plant, an animal, a fungus and a protoctist. (4)
---
Plant: maize (or pea/bean); animal: human (or housefly, mosquito); fungus: Mucor (or yeast); protoctist: Amoeba (or Chlorella, Plasmodium).
:::

::: q
Describe three features of bacterial cells. (3)
---
No nucleus — a single circular chromosome of DNA in the cytoplasm; plasmids; a cell wall (and cell membrane, cytoplasm).
:::

::: q
State one way in which the carbohydrate storage of plants and animals differs. (1)
---
Plants store starch (or sucrose); animals store glycogen.
:::

::: q
What is a pathogen? Name a pathogen from two different groups. (3)
---
An organism that causes disease (1). Plasmodium (protoctist) (1); Pneumococcus (bacterium) — or HIV (virus), Mucor (fungus) (1).
:::

::: q
Describe the structure of a fungus such as Mucor. (2)
---
A mycelium made of thread-like hyphae (1) which contain many nuclei and have chitin cell walls (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why respiration and breathing are not the same thing. (2)
---
Respiration is the chemical release of energy from glucose inside every cell (1); breathing (ventilation) is the movement of air into and out of the lungs (1).
:::

::: q
Name the disease caused by each: Plasmodium; Pneumococcus; HIV; tobacco mosaic virus. (4)
---
Malaria; pneumonia; AIDS; discoloured (mosaic) tobacco leaves through loss of chloroplasts.
:::

::: q
Compare the cells of an animal with those of a protoctist such as Amoeba. (2)
---
Both have a nucleus, cytoplasm and membrane but no cell wall or chloroplasts (1); an animal is multicellular while Amoeba is a single cell that is the whole organism (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Eight** characteristics, not seven — "control of internal conditions" is the one students forget.
- Respiration ≠ breathing; nutrition ≠ eating. Use the biological meanings.
- Fungi: **chitin walls, hyphae with many nuclei, saprotrophic** — three separate marks.
- Bacteria have **no nucleus** but do have a **cell wall** and **plasmids**; they are not "simple animals".
- Viruses: **not living, not cells** — protein coat + DNA/RNA, reproduce only inside host cells.
:::

::: test
Give three features that plant cells have but animal cells do not. (3)
---
Cell wall made of cellulose (1); chloroplasts (1); large permanent vacuole; also store carbohydrate as starch (1).
:::

::: test
Describe three ways in which fungi differ from plants. (3)
---
Fungi cannot photosynthesise — they feed saprotrophically, secreting enzymes onto food and absorbing the products (1); cell walls made of chitin, not cellulose (1); bodies of thread-like hyphae forming a mycelium; store carbohydrate as glycogen (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why viruses are not considered to be living organisms. (2)
---
They show none of the characteristics of life outside a host cell — no respiration, growth or nutrition (1); they can only reproduce by taking over a host cell's machinery (1).
:::

::: test
Describe the structure of a virus. (2)
---
A protein coat (1) surrounding a strand of genetic material, DNA or RNA (1); no cytoplasm or cell membrane.
:::

::: test
Name the type of organism that causes each disease: influenza; malaria; tuberculosis; athlete's foot. (4)
---
Virus (1); protoctist (Plasmodium) (1); bacterium (1); fungus (1).
:::

::: test
Describe two features of bacteria that are shared with plant cells and two that are not. (4)
---
Shared: cell wall (1), cell membrane and cytoplasm (1). Not shared: no nucleus — circular chromosome free in the cytoplasm, often plasmids (1); no chloroplasts or mitochondria, much smaller (1).
:::

::: test
What is meant by 'sensitivity' as a characteristic of living organisms? Give an example in a plant. (2)
---
The ability to detect and respond to changes in the environment (1); e.g. a shoot growing toward light (1).
:::

::: test
Amoeba is a protoctist. Describe its structure and how it resembles an animal cell. (2)
---
A single cell with a nucleus, cytoplasm and cell membrane, no cell wall (1); it moves and feeds on other organisms, like an animal cell (1).
:::
