## What it is

**Levels of organisation** — from small to large:

**organelle → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism**

- **Organelle** — a structure inside a cell with a specific job (nucleus, mitochondrion, chloroplast).
- **Cell** — the basic unit of life (a red blood cell, a palisade cell).
- **Tissue** — a group of similar cells working together (muscle tissue, xylem).
- **Organ** — several tissues working together for one function (the heart, a leaf).
- **Organ system** — organs working together (the digestive system, the circulatory system).
- **Organism** — the whole living thing.

**Cell structures and their functions:**

| Structure | Function | Animal | Plant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nucleus** | contains the genetic material (DNA) in chromosomes; controls the cell's activities | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Cytoplasm** | jelly-like; where most chemical reactions (metabolism) take place | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Cell membrane** | partially permeable barrier that controls what enters and leaves the cell | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Mitochondria** | site of aerobic respiration, releasing energy | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Ribosomes** | site of protein synthesis | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Cell wall** | made of **cellulose**; rigid; supports the cell and stops it bursting | ✗ | ✓ |
| **Chloroplasts** | contain **chlorophyll**; site of photosynthesis | ✗ | ✓ |
| **Vacuole** | large, permanent, filled with **cell sap**; stores water and dissolved substances; keeps the cell turgid | ✗ (only small, temporary) | ✓ |

**Plant vs animal cells**: both have a nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria and ribosomes. Only plant cells have a **cellulose cell wall**, **chloroplasts** and a **large permanent vacuole**. Plant cells tend to be regular (box-shaped) because of the wall; animal cells are irregular.

**Cell differentiation** — in a multicellular organism, cells become **specialised** for a particular function by developing particular structures: a red blood cell loses its nucleus and fills with haemoglobin; a palisade cell packs in chloroplasts; a sperm cell grows a tail and many mitochondria; a root hair cell grows a long extension. Differentiated cells usually cannot change into other types.

**Stem cells** are undifferentiated cells that can divide repeatedly and **differentiate into many cell types**. **Embryonic stem cells** (from early embryos) can become *any* type of cell; **adult stem cells** (bone marrow, skin) can become only a few types. Uses: replacing damaged tissue — bone marrow transplants for leukaemia, growing new nerve cells for paralysis, insulin-producing cells for diabetes, and research into disease. **The arguments**: *for* — potential to treat currently incurable conditions; cells can be grown to match the patient (no rejection); embryos used are spare ones from fertility treatment that would otherwise be destroyed. *Against* — using embryos destroys a potential human life, which some people find ethically unacceptable; risk that stem cells divide uncontrollably and form tumours; risk of transferring viral infection; treatments are expensive and largely unproven; adult stem cells avoid the ethical issue but are less flexible.

**Magnification** — how many times larger an image is than the real object:

$$\text{magnification} = \frac{\text{image size}}{\text{actual size}}$$

Rearranged: actual size $=$ image size $\div$ magnification. Convert units first: 1 mm $= 1{,}000$ µm.

::: method
1. **Levels of organisation**: give the sequence in order and one example at each level; a tissue is a group of *similar* cells, an organ is *different* tissues together.
2. **Structure ↔ function**: for any organelle, give the job in one phrase (nucleus — controls the cell, contains DNA). Do not say the nucleus "is the brain".
3. **Plant vs animal**: the three plant-only structures — cell wall, chloroplasts, permanent vacuole — and always "cell wall made of **cellulose**".
4. **Differentiation**: name the special structure and link it to the job — "root hair cell: long extension → large surface area for absorbing water".
5. **Stem cell discuss questions**: two benefits, two objections or risks, then a balanced conclusion.
6. **Magnification**: convert to the same units, then divide; show the working and give units for a size.
:::

::: example Levels
Place these in order from smallest to largest, and state which is an organ: heart, mitochondrion, muscle cell, circulatory system, cardiac muscle tissue. (2)

Mitochondrion → muscle cell → cardiac muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system (1). The heart is the organ — several tissues working together to pump blood (1).
:::

::: example Compare
Describe three ways in which a plant cell differs from an animal cell. (3)

A plant cell has a cellulose cell wall outside the membrane; an animal cell has no wall (1). A plant cell contains chloroplasts for photosynthesis; an animal cell has none (1). A plant cell has a large permanent vacuole containing cell sap; an animal cell has at most small temporary vacuoles (1).
:::

::: example Calculate — magnification
A cell is 0.05 mm long. In a drawing it is 40 mm long. Calculate the magnification. (2)

Magnification $= \dfrac{40}{0.05} = 800$ (2). (Or in µm: $40{,}000 \div 50 = 800$.)
:::

::: example Discuss — stem cells
Discuss the arguments for and against using embryonic stem cells to treat disease. (4)

For: embryonic stem cells can differentiate into any cell type, so they could replace damaged nerve, heart or pancreas cells and treat conditions with no current cure (1); the embryos are often spare ones from IVF that would otherwise be discarded (1). Against: obtaining the cells destroys the embryo, which some people regard as taking a human life (1); there are risks that transplanted cells divide uncontrollably and cause tumours or carry infection, and treatments are still experimental (1).
:::

::: q
Define "tissue" and "organ". (2)
---
A tissue is a group of similar cells working together to perform a function (1); an organ is a group of different tissues working together to perform a function (1).
:::

::: q
State the function of the cell membrane, mitochondria and ribosomes. (3)
---
Membrane — controls what enters and leaves the cell; mitochondria — site of aerobic respiration (energy release); ribosomes — site of protein synthesis.
:::

::: q
What is the cell wall made of, and what is its function? (2)
---
Cellulose (1); it supports the cell and stops it bursting when it takes in water (1).
:::

::: q
Explain what is meant by cell differentiation, using an example. (2)
---
A cell becoming specialised for a particular function by developing certain structures (1), e.g. a red blood cell losing its nucleus to make room for haemoglobin (1).
:::

::: q
Give two differences between embryonic and adult stem cells. (2)
---
Embryonic stem cells can become any cell type, adult ones only a few (1); embryonic ones come from embryos, adult ones from tissues such as bone marrow (1).
:::

::: q
A mitochondrion 2 µm long appears 50 mm long in an electron micrograph. Calculate the magnification. (2)
---
50 mm $= 50{,}000$ µm; magnification $= 50{,}000 \div 2 = 25{,}000$.
:::

::: q
A drawing at magnification ×400 shows a cell 60 mm wide. Calculate the actual width in µm. (2)
---
$60 \div 400 = 0.15$ mm $= 150$ µm.
:::

::: q
Which structures are found in both plant and animal cells? (2)
---
Nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, ribosomes.
:::

::: q
Explain one risk of stem cell therapy. (2)
---
Stem cells divide rapidly (1), so if their division is not controlled they may form a tumour (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Cell wall ≠ cell membrane.** Plants have both; the wall is cellulose and outside the membrane.
- A tissue is **similar** cells; an organ is **different** tissues. Examiners check this wording.
- Mitochondria are where **aerobic respiration** happens — not "produce energy" (energy is released, not made).
- Magnification: **same units first**, then image ÷ actual; it has no units.
- Stem cells: give **both** ethical and practical points, and distinguish embryonic from adult.
:::
