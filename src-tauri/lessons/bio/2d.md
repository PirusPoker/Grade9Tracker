## What it is

**Photosynthesis** is the process by which plants make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, using **light energy** absorbed by **chlorophyll** in chloroplasts. Oxygen is released as a by-product.

**Word equation**: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (in the presence of light and chlorophyll)

**Balanced symbol equation**:

$$6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light, chlorophyll}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$$

Light energy is converted into chemical energy stored in glucose. The glucose is used for respiration, converted to **starch** for storage (insoluble, so it does not affect osmosis), to **cellulose** for cell walls, to **lipids** for storage in seeds, and — with nitrate — to **amino acids** and proteins. It is transported as **sucrose** in the phloem.

**Limiting factors** — the rate of photosynthesis is set by whichever factor is in shortest supply:

- **Light intensity** — provides the energy; more light → faster, until another factor limits.
- **Carbon dioxide concentration** — a raw material; more CO₂ → faster, until another factor limits.
- **Temperature** — photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes, so the rate rises with temperature to an optimum and then falls as enzymes denature.
- (Also chlorophyll — a plant short of magnesium photosynthesises slowly.)

**How the leaf is adapted for photosynthesis:**

- **Large surface area** to absorb as much light as possible; **thin**, so light reaches all cells and gases diffuse quickly.
- **Palisade mesophyll** cells near the upper surface, packed with **chloroplasts**, arranged vertically so light passes through few cell walls.
- **Spongy mesophyll** with **air spaces** for gases to diffuse to and from cells.
- **Stomata** (pores) in the lower epidermis, opened and closed by **guard cells**, let CO₂ in and O₂ out.
- **Waxy cuticle** on the upper surface reduces water loss; the **epidermis** is transparent so light passes through.
- **Veins** (xylem brings water; phloem takes away sucrose) and a network that supports the leaf.

**Mineral ions**: **magnesium** is needed to make **chlorophyll** — without it leaves turn yellow (chlorosis) and photosynthesis slows. **Nitrate** provides nitrogen to make **amino acids and proteins** — without it growth is stunted and older leaves yellow. Both are absorbed from the soil by active transport in root hairs.

**Required practicals**: testing a leaf for starch — boil in water to kill it, then in **ethanol** (in a water bath, no flame) to remove chlorophyll, rinse in warm water to soften, add **iodine**: blue-black where starch is present. Used to show that light is needed (cover part of a leaf with foil), chlorophyll is needed (variegated leaf — only green parts go blue-black) and CO₂ is needed (a plant in a bell jar with **soda lime**, which absorbs CO₂, makes no starch). Rate: count bubbles of oxygen from pondweed (*Elodea*) at different distances from a lamp, or collect the gas in a measuring cylinder; keep temperature and CO₂ (add sodium hydrogencarbonate) constant.

::: terms
- **Photosynthesis**: the process by which plants use light energy, absorbed by chlorophyll, to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen.
- **Chlorophyll**: the green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light energy.
- **Limiting factor**: the factor in shortest supply, which limits the rate of a reaction such as photosynthesis — light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration or temperature.
- **Palisade mesophyll**: the layer of tall cells near the top of the leaf containing most chloroplasts.
- **Spongy mesophyll**: the layer of loosely packed cells with air spaces for gas exchange.
- **Stomata**: pores, mostly on the underside of a leaf, that let gases in and out, opened and closed by guard cells.
- **Magnesium ions**: needed to make chlorophyll; deficiency causes yellow leaves.
- **Nitrate ions**: needed to make amino acids and proteins; deficiency causes stunted growth.
- **Destarching**: keeping a plant in the dark for 48 hours so it uses up its stored starch before an experiment.
:::

::: method
1. **Equations**: word equation, then balanced symbol equation with the conditions written over the arrow. Check the balance — 6, 6, 1, 6.
2. **Limiting factor graph questions**: name the factor limiting *on each part* of the curve, and explain the plateau by saying what would raise it.
3. **Leaf adaptations**: pair every feature with its function — "thin → short diffusion distance for CO₂".
4. **Minerals**: magnesium → chlorophyll; nitrate → amino acids/proteins; and the deficiency symptom.
5. **Starch test order**: boiling water, ethanol, rinse, iodine — and *why* each step (kill/soften, remove chlorophyll so the colour change shows, soften, test).
6. **Control experiments**: a destarched plant kept in the dark or without CO₂ is the control that proves the factor is needed.
:::

::: example Equations
Write the word and balanced symbol equations for photosynthesis, and state the two conditions needed. (3)

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (1). $6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$ (1). Light energy and chlorophyll (1).
:::

::: example Limiting factors
Using the graph above, explain why the blue line becomes flat and how the green line shows which factor was limiting. (3)

On the flat part of the blue line, increasing light no longer increases the rate, so light is not the limiting factor — another factor is in short supply (1). When CO₂ concentration is raised (green line) the rate at the same light intensity is higher, showing CO₂ was the limiting factor on the blue plateau (1). At still higher light the green line also levels off, when temperature or CO₂ again limits (1).
:::

::: example Leaf adaptations
Explain three ways in which a leaf is adapted for photosynthesis. (3)

Palisade cells contain many chloroplasts and are near the upper surface, so they absorb the most light (1). The leaf is broad and thin, giving a large surface area to capture light and a short distance for carbon dioxide to diffuse to the cells (1). Stomata in the lower epidermis allow carbon dioxide to diffuse in, and air spaces in the spongy mesophyll let it reach every cell (1).
:::

::: example Practical
Describe how you would show that light is needed for a plant to make starch. (4)

Destarch a plant by leaving it in the dark for 48 hours, then cover part of a leaf with foil and leave the plant in bright light for several hours (1). Remove the leaf, boil it in water, then heat it in ethanol in a water bath to remove the chlorophyll, and rinse in warm water (1). Add iodine solution (1): the uncovered part turns blue-black, showing starch, while the covered part stays orange-brown — only the part that received light made starch (1).
:::

::: example Explain — mineral ions
A plant grown in soil lacking magnesium has yellow leaves; one lacking nitrate is small and weak. Explain these observations. (4)

Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll (1), so without it the leaves have little chlorophyll and look yellow, and less light is absorbed for photosynthesis (1).

Nitrate is needed to make amino acids and so proteins (1), so without it the plant cannot make enough protein for new cells and its growth is stunted (1).
:::

::: q
Describe how plants use the glucose made in photosynthesis. Give three uses. (3)
---
Respiration to release energy; converted to starch for storage; used to make cellulose for cell walls (or lipids, or amino acids with nitrate).
:::

::: q
Explain why plants store carbohydrate as starch rather than glucose. (2)
---
Starch is insoluble (1), so it does not affect the water concentration of the cell and cause osmosis (1).
:::

::: q
State three factors that can limit the rate of photosynthesis. (3)
---
Light intensity; carbon dioxide concentration; temperature.
:::

::: q
Explain why the rate of photosynthesis falls at very high temperatures. (2)
---
Photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes (1), which are denatured above their optimum temperature (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a plant lacking magnesium has yellow leaves and grows slowly. (3)
---
Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll (1); without it the leaves cannot make chlorophyll and turn yellow (1), so less light is absorbed and less glucose is made for growth (1).
:::

::: q
Why does a plant need nitrate ions? (2)
---
To make amino acids (1) and hence proteins for growth (1).
:::

::: q
Why is a leaf heated in ethanol before testing for starch? (2)
---
To remove the chlorophyll (1) so the colour change with iodine can be seen clearly (1).
:::

::: q
Describe how you could measure the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed. (3)
---
Place pondweed in water containing sodium hydrogencarbonate under a lamp (1); count the bubbles of oxygen released per minute, or collect the gas and measure its volume (1); repeat at different distances from the lamp, keeping temperature constant (1).
:::

::: q
A variegated leaf has green and white parts. After testing for starch, only the green parts turn blue-black. What does this show? (2)
---
Only parts containing chlorophyll made starch (1), so chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis (1).
:::

::: q
Explain the function of guard cells. (2)
---
They open and close the stomata (1), controlling the entry of carbon dioxide and the loss of water vapour (1).
:::
