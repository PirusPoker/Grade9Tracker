## What it is

**The male reproductive system**: **testes** (in the scrotum, kept slightly below body temperature) produce **sperm** and the hormone **testosterone**; sperm pass along the **sperm ducts** and mix with fluid from the **prostate gland** and seminal vesicles to form **semen**; the **urethra** carries semen (and, separately, urine) through the **penis**.

**The female reproductive system**: two **ovaries** produce **eggs** (ova) and the hormones **oestrogen** and **progesterone**; the **oviducts** (fallopian tubes) carry the egg to the **uterus**, a muscular organ lined with the **endometrium**, where a fertilised egg implants; the **cervix** is the ring of muscle at the neck of the uterus; the **vagina** receives sperm and is the birth canal.

**Fertilisation** happens in the **oviduct**: a sperm nucleus fuses with the egg nucleus to form a **zygote**, which divides by mitosis into a ball of cells (**embryo**) as it moves to the uterus and **implants** in the endometrium.

**The menstrual cycle** — about 28 days, controlled by four hormones acting in sequence:

1. **Days 1–5 — menstruation**: the thickened uterus lining breaks down and is lost, because progesterone (and oestrogen) fell at the end of the previous cycle.
2. **FSH** (follicle-stimulating hormone, from the **pituitary**) rises: it stimulates an **egg to mature** in a follicle in the ovary, and stimulates the ovary to make **oestrogen**.
3. **Oestrogen** (from the **ovary**) rises: it **rebuilds and thickens the uterus lining**, **inhibits FSH** (so only one egg matures) and, at its peak, **stimulates the release of LH**.
4. **LH** (luteinising hormone, from the **pituitary**) surges around **day 14** and triggers **ovulation** — the release of the mature egg from the ovary. The empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum.
5. **Progesterone** (from the corpus luteum in the **ovary**) rises after ovulation: it **maintains the thick uterus lining** ready for implantation and **inhibits FSH and LH**.
6. If no fertilisation: the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone (and oestrogen) fall, the lining is no longer maintained → **menstruation** and the cycle restarts. If fertilisation and implantation occur, progesterone stays high (later from the placenta), the lining is kept and no more eggs are released during pregnancy.

**Pregnancy**: the embryo implants and develops into a **fetus**. The **placenta** forms where the embryo attaches to the uterus wall. It has a huge surface area of finger-like villi bathed in the mother's blood, with the fetal capillaries very close but **the two bloods never mix** (different blood groups; the mother's higher pressure would damage the fetus). Across it, by diffusion: **oxygen and glucose, amino acids and antibodies** pass from mother to fetus; **carbon dioxide and urea** pass from fetus to mother. The placenta also makes progesterone to maintain the pregnancy, and it acts as a partial barrier — though some harmful substances (alcohol, nicotine, some viruses) cross. The **umbilical cord** connects fetus and placenta. The fetus is surrounded by the **amniotic sac** filled with **amniotic fluid**, which **cushions it against knocks**, supports it and keeps it at a constant temperature.

**Secondary sexual characteristics** at puberty: **testosterone** — sperm production, deeper voice, body and facial hair, muscle growth; **oestrogen** — egg maturation and menstruation begin, breast development, widening hips, body hair.

1. **Structure → function** for each organ, male and female; know that fertilisation is in the **oviduct** and implantation in the **uterus**.
2. **The cycle as four hormones in sequence, with their gland and their job and their timing**: FSH (pituitary, egg matures, early), oestrogen (ovary, lining thickens, rising to day 12), LH (pituitary, ovulation, day 14), progesterone (ovary, maintains lining, second half). Add the inhibition links for top marks.
3. **Menstruation is caused by the fall in progesterone** — say so.
4. **Placenta**: surface area, thin barrier, bloods do not mix, what crosses in each direction, and why the bloods must stay separate.
5. **Amniotic fluid**: cushions and protects — one phrase.
:::

::: example Organs
State the function of the testes, the oviduct and the uterus. (3)

The testes produce sperm and testosterone (1). The oviduct carries the egg from the ovary to the uterus and is where fertilisation occurs (1). The uterus is where the embryo implants and develops, protected by its muscular wall (1).
:::

::: example FSH and LH
Describe the roles of FSH and LH in the menstrual cycle. (4)

FSH is released by the pituitary gland at the start of the cycle (1); it causes an egg to mature in a follicle in the ovary and stimulates the ovary to secrete oestrogen (1). LH, also from the pituitary, is released in a surge around day 14, triggered by the peak of oestrogen (1); it causes ovulation — the release of the mature egg from the ovary (1).
:::

::: example Oestrogen and progesterone
Explain the roles of oestrogen and progesterone in the menstrual cycle. (4)

Oestrogen, secreted by the ovary in the first half of the cycle, causes the lining of the uterus to rebuild and thicken after menstruation (1) and inhibits FSH so no further eggs mature (1). Progesterone, secreted by the corpus luteum after ovulation, maintains the thick lining ready for implantation (1); if no fertilisation occurs its level falls, the lining breaks down and menstruation begins (1).
:::

::: example Placenta
Explain how the placenta is adapted for exchange between mother and fetus, and why the two blood supplies do not mix. (4)

Villi give the placenta a large surface area, and the fetal capillaries lie very close to the mother's blood spaces, giving a short diffusion distance (1). Oxygen, glucose and amino acids diffuse from the mother's blood to the fetus, and carbon dioxide and urea diffuse the other way (1). The bloods are kept separate because the mother's blood pressure is much higher and would damage the fetal vessels (1), and the blood groups may differ, which could cause the mother's immune system to react against the fetus (1).
:::

::: q
Where does fertilisation take place in humans? (1)
---
In the oviduct (fallopian tube).
:::

::: q
Name the hormone that triggers ovulation and the gland that releases it. (2)
---
LH (1); the pituitary gland (1).
:::

::: q
What causes menstruation to begin? (2)
---
The corpus luteum breaks down, so progesterone levels fall (1); the uterus lining is no longer maintained and breaks down (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why only one egg usually matures each cycle. (1)
---
Oestrogen inhibits the release of FSH, so no further follicles are stimulated.
:::

::: q
Name two substances that pass from the mother to the fetus and two that pass the other way. (2)
---
Oxygen and glucose (or amino acids, antibodies) to the fetus (1); carbon dioxide and urea to the mother (1).
:::

::: q
State two functions of the amniotic fluid. (2)
---
Cushions the fetus against knocks (1); supports it and keeps its temperature constant (1).
:::

::: q
Give two secondary sexual characteristics caused by testosterone. (2)
---
Deeper voice; facial and body hair (or muscle development, sperm production).
:::

::: q
Explain why progesterone remains high throughout pregnancy. (2)
---
It maintains the uterus lining so the placenta stays attached (1), and it inhibits FSH and LH so no further eggs are released (1).
:::

::: q
Why are the testes held outside the body in the scrotum? (1)
---
Sperm develop best at a temperature slightly below core body temperature.
:::
