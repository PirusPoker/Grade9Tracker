## What it is

Humans have a **double circulatory system**: blood passes through the heart **twice** on each circuit of the body. The **pulmonary circuit** carries blood from the right side of the heart to the lungs and back to the left side; the **systemic circuit** carries blood from the left side to the body and back to the right. Two circuits keep oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate and let the body circuit run at a higher pressure.

**The heart** — a muscular pump with four chambers: two thin-walled **atria** on top (receive blood) and two thick-walled **ventricles** below (pump blood out). The **septum** divides left from right.

**The route of blood**: body → **vena cava** → **right atrium** → (tricuspid valve) → **right ventricle** → **pulmonary artery** → lungs → **pulmonary vein** → **left atrium** → (bicuspid valve) → **left ventricle** → **aorta** → body. The right side pumps **deoxygenated** blood to the lungs; the left side pumps **oxygenated** blood to the body.

**How it pumps**: the atria contract, pushing blood into the ventricles; then the ventricles contract, forcing blood out through the arteries. **Valves** between atria and ventricles (tricuspid, bicuspid) and at the exits (semilunar valves) prevent blood flowing backwards. The **left ventricle has a much thicker muscular wall** because it must pump blood at high pressure all round the body; the right pumps only to the nearby lungs. The heart muscle has its own blood supply — the **coronary arteries**.

**Heart rate and exercise**: during exercise muscles respire faster and need more oxygen and glucose, and produce more CO₂. Heart rate rises (and each beat pumps more blood) so blood delivers oxygen and removes CO₂ faster. The rise is triggered by the brain detecting the rise in blood CO₂, and by **adrenaline**, released during exercise, excitement or fear, which increases heart rate and breathing rate — preparing the body for "fight or flight". After exercise the rate stays raised while the oxygen debt is repaid.

**Blood vessels:**

| | **Arteries** | **Veins** | **Capillaries** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carry blood | **away from** the heart, under **high pressure** | **towards** the heart, under **low pressure** | between arteries and veins, through tissues |
| Wall | **thick**, muscular and elastic — withstands and smooths the pressure surges | thin | **one cell thick** — short diffusion distance |
| Lumen | narrow | **wide** — less resistance | very narrow — red cells pass in single file |
| Valves | none (except at the heart) | **yes** — prevent backflow at low pressure; skeletal muscles squeeze blood along | none |
| Job | deliver blood to organs | return blood; blood flow helped by muscle contraction | **exchange** of oxygen, glucose, CO₂ and waste between blood and cells; huge total surface area |

**Coronary heart disease (CHD)**: fatty deposits (**cholesterol**) build up in the coronary arteries, narrowing them; less blood reaches the heart muscle, so it receives less oxygen; a clot can block the artery completely — a **heart attack**. **Risk factors**: **smoking** (nicotine, CO), a diet high in **saturated fat and salt**, **high blood pressure**, **stress**, **lack of exercise**, **obesity**, **age**, **genetics**, **excess alcohol**.

::: method
1. **Blood route in order** with the vessels and chambers named — and the words oxygenated/deoxygenated, right/left. The right side goes to the lungs.
2. **Left ventricle wall**: thicker → higher pressure → blood reaches the whole body. Give the reason, not just the fact.
3. **Valves prevent backflow** — say where and when (when the ventricles contract, the atrioventricular valves close).
4. **Exercise**: more respiration → more O₂ needed, more CO₂ made → brain and adrenaline raise heart rate → faster delivery and removal.
5. **Vessels**: relate each feature to the pressure or the job — thick elastic wall for high pressure; valves and wide lumen for low pressure; thin wall for exchange.
6. **CHD**: mechanism (fatty deposits narrow coronary arteries → less oxygen → heart attack) then named risk factors.
:::

::: example Route
Describe the path taken by blood from the vena cava to the aorta. (4)

Blood enters the right atrium from the vena cava and passes through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle (1), which pumps it through the pulmonary artery to the lungs, where it is oxygenated (1). It returns in the pulmonary vein to the left atrium and passes through the bicuspid valve into the left ventricle (1), which pumps it out through the aorta to the body (1).
:::

::: example Structure and function
Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle. (2)

The left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body, so it must generate a high pressure; a thick muscular wall contracts more powerfully (1). The right ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs, at lower pressure, so a thinner wall is enough (1).
:::

::: example Vessels
Explain how the structure of arteries and veins is related to their function. (4)

Arteries carry blood at high pressure from the heart, so they have thick, muscular, elastic walls that withstand the pressure and stretch and recoil to smooth the flow (2). Veins carry blood at low pressure back to the heart, so they have thinner walls and a wide lumen to reduce resistance, and valves that stop blood flowing backwards (2).
:::

::: example Exercise
Explain why heart rate increases during exercise. (3)

Muscles respire faster, so they need more oxygen and glucose and produce more carbon dioxide (1). The brain detects the rise in blood CO₂, and adrenaline is released, both increasing the heart rate (1). Blood is pumped faster, delivering oxygen and removing CO₂ more quickly to meet the muscles' demand (1).
:::

::: q
Explain what is meant by a double circulatory system. (2)
---
Blood passes through the heart twice on each complete circuit of the body (1): once to be pumped to the lungs and once to be pumped to the rest of the body (1).
:::

::: q
Name the vessel that carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart. (1)
---
The pulmonary artery.
:::

::: q
State the function of the valves in the heart. (1)
---
To prevent blood flowing backwards, e.g. from the ventricles into the atria when the ventricles contract.
:::

::: q
Explain why capillary walls are only one cell thick. (2)
---
To give a short diffusion distance (1) so oxygen, glucose and waste can be exchanged quickly between blood and cells (1).
:::

::: q
Explain the role of valves in veins. (2)
---
Blood in veins is at low pressure and could flow backwards (1); valves close if it does, so blood only moves towards the heart, helped by skeletal muscles squeezing the veins (1).
:::

::: q
What is the function of the coronary arteries? (1)
---
To supply the heart muscle itself with oxygenated blood.
:::

::: q
Describe how coronary heart disease develops. (3)
---
Fatty deposits of cholesterol build up in the walls of the coronary arteries (1), narrowing them so less blood and oxygen reach the heart muscle (1); a blood clot can block the narrowed artery, causing part of the heart muscle to die — a heart attack (1).
:::

::: q
Give four risk factors for coronary heart disease. (4)
---
Smoking; a diet high in saturated fat; lack of exercise; high blood pressure (or stress, obesity, age, genetics, alcohol).
:::

::: q
Explain the effect of adrenaline on the heart. (2)
---
It increases heart rate and the force of contraction (1), so more oxygen and glucose reach the muscles in preparation for activity (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Right side → lungs; left side → body.** The heart's right is on *your* left in a diagram.
- Arteries carry blood **away** from the heart — the pulmonary artery carries **deoxygenated** blood, the pulmonary vein **oxygenated**.
- Left ventricle **thicker because higher pressure for the body** — give the reason.
- Vessels: relate every feature to **pressure** or **exchange**.
- Double circulation: blood passes through the heart **twice** per circuit — not "two hearts" or a single loop.
:::
