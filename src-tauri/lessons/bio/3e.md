## What it is

Cells divide in two ways for two different purposes.

**Chromosome numbers**: a **diploid** cell has **two sets** of chromosomes, one from each parent — in humans **46** (23 pairs). A **haploid** cell has **one set** — in humans **23**. Body cells are diploid; gametes (sperm and eggs) are haploid, so that fertilisation restores the diploid number: $23 + 23 = 46$.

**Mitosis** — division of a diploid cell to produce **two genetically identical diploid cells**. Before division the DNA is **copied** (each chromosome becomes two identical copies); the copies are pulled to opposite ends of the cell and the cell splits, so each new cell has a full, identical set of 46. Mitosis is used for:

- **Growth** — making more cells.
- **Repair** — replacing damaged or worn-out cells (skin, blood).
- **Asexual reproduction** — producing offspring that are clones of the parent (runners, tubers, binary fission in bacteria, cloning).

Because the daughter cells are identical, mitosis produces **no variation**.

**Meiosis** — division of a diploid cell in the **reproductive organs** (testes, ovaries, anthers, ovaries of flowers) to produce **four genetically different haploid gametes**. The DNA is copied once, but the cell divides **twice**, so the chromosome number **halves** (46 → 23). The gametes are different from each other because the chromosomes of each pair are shuffled independently into the gametes (and exchange sections), so every gamete carries a different combination of the parents' alleles.

| | **Mitosis** | **Meiosis** |
|---|---|---|
| Where | all over the body (growth, repair) | reproductive organs only |
| Number of divisions | one | two |
| Cells produced | **two** | **four** |
| Chromosome number | **diploid — same as parent** (46) | **haploid — half** (23) |
| Genetically | **identical** to parent and each other | **different** from each other and the parent |
| Purpose | growth, repair, asexual reproduction | producing gametes for sexual reproduction |

**Why meiosis matters**: (1) it halves the chromosome number, so fertilisation restores it rather than doubling it every generation; (2) it produces **genetic variation** — combined with the random fusion of gametes at fertilisation, this is why siblings differ.

::: method
1. **Diploid/haploid with the human numbers** (46/23) — and the reason gametes must be haploid.
2. **Mitosis in three parts**: DNA copied → one division → two identical diploid cells. Then the three uses.
3. **Meiosis in three parts**: DNA copied → two divisions → four different haploid cells; where it happens; why variation results.
4. **Compare** with the table's five rows; "compare" questions need matched pairs ("mitosis produces two cells whereas meiosis produces four").
5. **Which division?** Growth, repair, cloning, asexual → mitosis. Gametes, variation, halving → meiosis.
:::

::: example Mitosis
Describe what happens during mitosis and state why it is important. (4)

The DNA in the nucleus is copied, so each chromosome consists of two identical copies (1). The copies separate and move to opposite ends of the cell, which then divides into two (1). Each new cell has the full diploid set of chromosomes and is genetically identical to the parent cell (1). Mitosis is needed for growth, to repair damaged tissue and for asexual reproduction (1).
:::

::: example Meiosis
Explain how meiosis produces gametes that are haploid and genetically different. (4)

A diploid cell in the reproductive organs copies its DNA, then divides twice (1). Two divisions after one copying halve the chromosome number, so each of the four cells has one set — haploid, 23 in humans (1). The chromosomes of each pair are distributed randomly and independently to the gametes (1), so each gamete receives a different combination of alleles and no two are identical (1).
:::

::: example Compare
Give three differences between mitosis and meiosis. (3)

Mitosis produces two cells, meiosis four (1). Mitosis produces diploid cells with the same chromosome number as the parent; meiosis produces haploid cells with half (1). Mitosis produces genetically identical cells; meiosis produces genetically different cells (1).
:::

::: example Apply
A skin cell of a cat has 38 chromosomes. How many chromosomes are in a cat's sperm cell, and in the cell formed when a sperm fertilises an egg? (2)

Sperm: 19 (haploid, half of 38) (1). Zygote: 38 (19 + 19 — diploid restored) (1).
:::

::: q
State the diploid and haploid chromosome numbers in humans. (2)
---
Diploid 46 (1); haploid 23 (1).
:::

::: q
Name two processes in the body that use mitosis. (2)
---
Growth; repair of damaged tissue (or asexual reproduction).
:::

::: q
Why must gametes be haploid? (2)
---
So that when two gametes fuse at fertilisation the zygote has the normal diploid number (1); if gametes were diploid the chromosome number would double every generation (1).
:::

::: q
Where does meiosis take place in humans? (1)
---
In the testes and ovaries.
:::

::: q
How many cells does one cell produce by mitosis, and by meiosis? (2)
---
Mitosis — two (1); meiosis — four (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why organisms produced by asexual reproduction are genetically identical to the parent. (2)
---
They are produced by mitosis (1), which copies the DNA exactly and gives every cell the same chromosomes as the parent (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why brothers and sisters are not identical. (2)
---
Meiosis produces gametes with different combinations of alleles (1), and which sperm fertilises which egg is random, so each child receives a different mixture (1).
:::

::: q
A cell with 20 chromosomes divides by meiosis. How many chromosomes are in each product? (1)
---
10 — meiosis halves the number.
:::

::: q
Why does mitosis produce no variation but meiosis does? (2)
---
Mitosis copies the chromosomes exactly and shares identical sets to two cells (1); meiosis shuffles the chromosomes of each pair independently into four cells, giving new combinations (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Mitosis: 2 identical diploid cells. Meiosis: 4 different haploid cells.** The numbers 2/4, identical/different, diploid/haploid are the marks.
- Meiosis **halves** the chromosome number and **produces variation**; mitosis does neither.
- Human numbers: **46 and 23** — and be ready to halve any number given.
- Meiosis happens only in the **reproductive organs**.
- Growth, repair, cloning = **mitosis** — never meiosis.
:::
