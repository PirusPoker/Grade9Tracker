## What it is

**Homeostasis** is the **maintenance of a constant internal environment** — keeping conditions inside the body (temperature, water content, blood glucose, CO₂) within narrow limits, whatever happens outside. It matters because **enzymes** work only near their optimum temperature and pH, and cells are damaged by osmotic changes. Homeostasis works by **negative feedback**: a change is detected, and a response reverses it, bringing the level back to normal.

**Control of body temperature** (thermoregulation) — the **skin** is the main effector, coordinated by the **hypothalamus** in the brain, which monitors the temperature of the blood and receives information from temperature receptors in the skin. Normal core temperature is about 37 °C, the optimum for human enzymes.

| Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|
| **Sweating** — sweat glands release sweat onto the skin; as it **evaporates it takes heat from the body** (latent heat) | **No sweating** |
| **Vasodilation** — arterioles near the surface **widen**, so more blood flows through the surface capillaries and more heat is **radiated** away (skin looks red) | **Vasoconstriction** — arterioles **narrow**, less blood reaches the surface, less heat is lost (skin looks pale) |
| Hairs lie flat | **Hairs stand up** (erector muscles contract), trapping a layer of insulating air — effective in furry mammals |
| Less activity | **Shivering** — rapid muscle contractions; respiration in the muscles releases heat |
| | Behaviour — curling up, more clothes; the fat layer beneath the skin insulates |

Blood vessels do not "move" closer to or further from the surface; the **arterioles change diameter**, altering how much blood flows through capillaries near the surface.

**Plant responses — tropisms**: a **tropism** is a growth response to a directional stimulus. Growth **towards** the stimulus is **positive**; **away** is **negative**.

- **Phototropism** — response to **light**. Shoots are **positively phototropic** (grow towards light → more photosynthesis). Roots are negatively phototropic.
- **Geotropism** (gravitropism) — response to **gravity**. Roots are **positively geotropic** (grow downwards → anchorage, water). Shoots are **negatively geotropic** (grow upwards).

**The role of auxin** — a plant hormone (growth substance) made in the **tip** of the shoot, which diffuses down and **makes cells elongate**. In a shoot lit from one side, **auxin accumulates on the shaded side**; the cells on the shaded side **elongate more** than those on the lit side; the shoot therefore **bends towards the light**. (If the tip is removed or covered, no bending occurs, showing the tip detects the light and produces the auxin.) In roots, auxin has the opposite effect — it *inhibits* elongation, so auxin collecting on the lower side of a horizontal root makes the upper side grow faster and the root bends downwards.

Plants also respond to other stimuli (moisture, touch), and hormones control germination, fruit ripening and leaf fall — but the exam focus is phototropism, geotropism and auxin.

::: terms
- **Stimulus**: a change in the environment that an organism detects.
- **Receptor**: a cell or organ that detects a stimulus.
- **Coordinator**: the part of the body that processes information and decides on a response — the central nervous system.
- **Effector**: a muscle or gland that carries out the response.
- **Response**: the action taken as a result of a stimulus.
- **Homeostasis**: the maintenance of a constant internal environment, such as body temperature and blood water content.
- **Tropism**: a growth response of a plant towards or away from a stimulus.
- **Phototropism**: growth in response to light — shoots grow towards light (positive).
- **Geotropism (gravitropism)**: growth in response to gravity — roots grow towards it (positive), shoots away from it (negative).
- **Auxin**: a plant hormone made in the tip that causes cells to elongate.
- **Vasodilation**: the widening of arterioles near the skin surface, so more blood flows near the surface and more heat is lost.
- **Vasoconstriction**: the narrowing of arterioles near the skin surface, so less heat is lost.
:::

::: method
1. **Homeostasis definition**: "constant internal environment"; then *why* — enzymes and osmosis; then **negative feedback**.
2. **Temperature answers name the mechanism and explain the heat transfer**: sweating → evaporation takes heat; vasodilation → more blood near the surface → more radiation. Say "arterioles widen/narrow", never "blood vessels move".
3. **Tropisms**: name the stimulus, the direction (positive/negative), and the advantage to the plant.
4. **Auxin mechanism**: made in the tip → moves to the shaded side → cells there elongate more → bends towards light. All four steps.
5. **Experiments**: tip removed / covered / a barrier inserted — predict and explain the result from where auxin can and cannot go.
:::

::: example Define
Define homeostasis and explain why it is important. (3)

The maintenance of a constant internal environment in the body (1). It is important because enzymes work best at a particular temperature and pH, so the reactions of the body slow or stop if conditions change (1), and cells are damaged if the water content of the blood changes, through osmosis (1).
:::

::: example Cooling
Explain two ways the skin reduces body temperature when a person is too hot. (4)

Sweat glands release sweat onto the skin surface; as the water in the sweat evaporates it takes heat energy from the skin, cooling the body (2). Arterioles near the skin surface widen — vasodilation — so more blood flows through the capillaries near the surface and more heat is transferred to the air by radiation (2).
:::

::: example Phototropism
Explain how a shoot grows towards light from one side. (4)

Auxin is produced in the tip of the shoot (1) and moves to the shaded side, so its concentration is higher there than on the lit side (1). Auxin stimulates cell elongation, so the cells on the shaded side grow longer than those on the lit side (1); the unequal growth makes the shoot bend towards the light, which increases the light available for photosynthesis (1).
:::

::: example Experiment
A shoot tip is covered with a light-proof cap and the shoot is lit from one side. Predict and explain what happens. (2)

The shoot grows straight up and does not bend (1), because the tip detects the light and produces auxin; with the tip covered, light cannot cause auxin to move to the shaded side, so growth is equal on both sides (1).
:::

::: example Explain — geotropism in roots
A seedling is placed on its side. Explain why its root grows downwards. (4)

The root shows positive geotropism — it grows towards gravity (1). Auxin collects on the lower side of the root (1).

In roots, auxin inhibits cell elongation (1), so the cells on the upper side grow faster than those on the lower side and the root curves downwards (1).
:::

::: example Identify — the parts of a response
A person sees a ball coming and catches it. Identify the stimulus, receptor, coordinator, effector and response. (4)

Stimulus: the approaching ball — light reflected from it (1). Receptor: the light-sensitive cells of the retina in the eye (1).

Coordinator: the brain (the central nervous system) (1). Effector: the arm and hand muscles; response: catching the ball (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why homeostasis is described as involving negative feedback, using blood glucose as an example. (3)
---
A change from the set point triggers a response that reverses it (1); when glucose rises, insulin is released and glucose is stored as glycogen, lowering it (1); when it falls, glucagon causes glycogen to be converted back to glucose, raising it (1).
