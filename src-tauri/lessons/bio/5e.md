## What it is

A **clone** is a genetically identical copy of an organism (or cell). Cloning happens naturally in asexual reproduction; humans clone plants and animals deliberately, by two quite different processes.

**Micropropagation (tissue culture) — cloning plants:**

1. A small piece of tissue (an **explant**) is cut from the parent plant — usually from a **shoot tip or meristem**, where cells are dividing and free of viruses.
2. The explant is **sterilised** (to kill microbes) and placed on a sterile **nutrient agar (growth medium)** containing sugar, minerals and **plant hormones (auxins and cytokinins)**.
3. The cells divide by **mitosis** to form a mass of undifferentiated cells (a **callus**).
4. The hormone balance is changed so the callus develops **shoots and roots** — many tiny **plantlets**.
5. The plantlets are transferred to soil or compost and grown on, under aseptic conditions at first, then in a greenhouse.

*Uses*: producing **thousands of identical plants** from one parent with desirable characteristics — high-yield or disease-resistant crops, orchids and ornamental plants, rare or endangered species, GM plants, plants that are hard to grow from seed (bananas are seedless). *Advantages*: rapid, works all year, needs little space, produces disease-free plants, keeps the exact characteristics of the parent. *Disadvantages*: all plants are genetically identical, so one disease could kill them all; needs sterile laboratory conditions and skilled staff; expensive per plant compared with seed.

**Cloning mammals — nuclear transfer (the method that made Dolly the sheep):**

1. A **body (somatic) cell** — e.g. an udder cell — is taken from the **animal to be cloned** (A), and its **diploid nucleus** is removed.
2. An **egg cell** is taken from another animal (B) and its **nucleus is removed** (enucleated).
3. The **nucleus from A is inserted into the empty egg cell**; the cell is given an **electric shock** to make it start dividing by mitosis, forming an embryo.
4. The embryo is **implanted into the uterus of a surrogate mother** (C).
5. The offspring born is a **clone of A** — genetically identical to the animal that supplied the nucleus (not to the egg donor or the surrogate).

*Uses*: copying animals with valuable characteristics (prize bulls, racehorses); producing herds of **transgenic animals**; conserving endangered species; research.

**Cloned transgenic animals** — combining GM with cloning: a gene (e.g. for a human protein such as a blood-clotting factor, or alpha-1-antitrypsin for lung disease) is inserted into the DNA of a cell; that cell's nucleus is used in nuclear transfer to produce an embryo, which is implanted in a surrogate. Every clone carries the gene and, for instance, produces the human protein in its **milk**, from which it is extracted (**"pharming"**). Cloning lets a whole herd of identical transgenic animals be produced from one successful GM cell. Transgenic animals are also used to model human diseases and could provide organs for transplant.

**Advantages of cloning**: exact copies of the best animals or plants; rapid multiplication; herds of transgenic animals producing medicines; saving endangered species; guaranteed characteristics. **Drawbacks**: **reduced genetic variation** — vulnerability to disease and change; **low success rate** — many embryos fail, and cloned animals often have health problems and shorter lives (Dolly had arthritis and died young); expensive and technically difficult; **ethical concerns** — animal welfare, the fear of human cloning, "playing God"; possible unknown long-term effects.

::: terms
- **Cloning**: producing genetically identical organisms.
- **Micropropagation**: growing many identical plants from small pieces of tissue (explants) on sterile agar with nutrients and hormones.
- **Explant**: a small piece of tissue taken from a parent plant to grow into a new plant.
- **Nuclear transfer**: removing the nucleus from an egg cell and replacing it with the nucleus from a body cell of the animal to be cloned.
- **Enucleated egg**: an egg cell whose nucleus has been removed.
- **Surrogate mother**: a female who carries an embryo that is not genetically her own.
:::

::: method
1. **Two processes, kept apart**: micropropagation = plant tissue on agar → callus → plantlets; nuclear transfer = body-cell nucleus into an enucleated egg → shock → embryo → surrogate. Never mix their steps.
2. **Micropropagation steps** include *sterile* conditions, *hormones* in the medium, *mitosis*, and *plantlets*.
3. **Nuclear transfer**: say **which animal the clone is identical to** — the nucleus donor — and why (the nucleus carries the DNA).
4. **Transgenic + cloning**: gene inserted into a cell → that nucleus used for cloning → every clone carries the gene → product in milk.
5. **Discuss questions**: two advantages, two drawbacks (variation, success rate/welfare, ethics), a judgement.
:::

::: example Micropropagation
Describe how micropropagation is used to produce many identical plants. (4)

Small pieces of tissue are cut from the shoot tip of the parent plant and sterilised (1). They are placed on sterile nutrient agar containing sugar, minerals and plant hormones (1). The cells divide by mitosis to form a callus, and changing the hormone balance makes it develop shoots and roots — small plantlets (1). The plantlets are separated and grown on in compost; all are genetically identical to the parent (1).
:::

::: example Nuclear transfer
Describe how a mammal such as a sheep can be cloned. (5)

A body cell is taken from the sheep to be cloned and its diploid nucleus removed (1). An egg cell is taken from another sheep and its nucleus removed (1). The body-cell nucleus is inserted into the empty egg (1), which is stimulated with an electric shock to divide by mitosis into an embryo (1). The embryo is implanted into the uterus of a surrogate ewe, which gives birth to a lamb genetically identical to the sheep that provided the nucleus (1).
:::

::: example Transgenic clones
Explain how cloning is used to produce a herd of transgenic sheep that make a human protein in their milk. (3)

The human gene is inserted into the DNA of a sheep cell (1). The nucleus of this transgenic cell is transferred into an enucleated egg, which develops into an embryo and is implanted into a surrogate (1). Each cloned lamb carries the human gene, so the females produce the protein in their milk, from which it is extracted; cloning many embryos from the same cell gives a whole herd (1).
:::

::: example Discuss
Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of cloning animals. (4)

Advantages: exact copies of animals with the best characteristics — high milk yield, disease resistance — can be produced without waiting for selective breeding; herds of transgenic animals producing medicines can be made from one GM cell; endangered species could be saved (2). Disadvantages: the success rate is very low and clones often suffer health problems and shorter lives; all clones are genetically identical, so a disease could wipe out a herd; the technique is expensive and raises ethical objections about animal welfare and the prospect of human cloning (2).
:::

::: q
Define "clone". (1)
---
A genetically identical copy of an organism (or cell), produced asexually.
:::

::: q
Why is the explant for micropropagation usually taken from a shoot tip? (2)
---
Shoot tip cells are actively dividing (1) and are usually free of viruses, so the plantlets are healthy (1).
:::

::: q
Why must micropropagation be carried out under sterile conditions? (1)
---
The nutrient agar would otherwise be colonised by bacteria and fungi, which would kill the tissue.
:::

::: q
Give two advantages of micropropagation over growing plants from seed. (2)
---
All plants are identical to a parent with desirable characteristics (1); thousands can be produced quickly, all year, and disease-free (1).
:::

::: q
In nuclear transfer, which animal is the clone genetically identical to, and why? (2)
---
The animal that supplied the body-cell nucleus (1), because the nucleus contains the DNA; the egg donor and surrogate contribute no nuclear genes (1).
:::

::: q
Why is the egg cell's nucleus removed before cloning? (1)
---
So the embryo contains only the DNA of the animal being cloned, not the egg donor's.
:::

::: q
What is the role of the electric shock in cloning a mammal? (1)
---
It stimulates the egg cell with the new nucleus to start dividing by mitosis.
:::

::: q
Give one drawback of cloning that applies to both plants and animals. (2)
---
Reduced genetic variation (1): all clones are identical, so a new disease or environmental change could destroy them all (1).
:::

::: q
Explain the difference between micropropagation and nuclear transfer. (2)
---
Micropropagation grows whole plants from small pieces of plant tissue on nutrient agar (1); nuclear transfer places the nucleus of a body cell into an enucleated egg to produce an animal embryo (1).
:::
