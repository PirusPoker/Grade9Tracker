## What it is

Human activity changes ecosystems faster than they can adapt. Four areas are examined: greenhouse gases, air pollution, water pollution and deforestation.

**Greenhouse gases and global warming**: **carbon dioxide**, **methane**, **water vapour**, **nitrous oxide** and **CFCs** are greenhouse gases. They let short-wave radiation from the sun reach the Earth, but absorb the long-wave (infrared) radiation the warm Earth gives off and re-radiate it, trapping heat — the **greenhouse effect**, which keeps the planet warm enough for life. **Human activity is increasing these gases**, enhancing the effect and raising global temperature (**global warming**):

- **Carbon dioxide** — burning **fossil fuels** (power stations, vehicles, industry) and **deforestation** (fewer trees photosynthesise; burning releases stored carbon).
- **Methane** — **cattle** (produced in their digestion), **rice paddies** (bacteria in waterlogged fields), **landfill** sites, natural gas leaks.
- **Nitrous oxide** — fertilisers, vehicle exhausts. **CFCs** — old refrigerants and aerosols (also damage the ozone layer).

**Biological consequences of global warming**: **climate change** — more extreme weather, droughts and floods; **melting ice caps** and **rising sea levels** flooding coastal habitats; changes in the **distribution of species** — organisms move towards the poles or higher altitudes, and species that cannot move (or reach the top of a mountain) become **extinct**; changed **migration and breeding times**, which can separate animals from their food; **spread of pests and diseases** (malaria mosquitoes) into new areas; **coral bleaching** as seas warm; **loss of biodiversity** and crop failures.

**Air pollution**: burning fossil fuels also releases **sulfur dioxide** (from sulfur impurities in coal and oil) and **oxides of nitrogen** (from vehicle engines). These dissolve in water in the air to form **acid rain**, which: **kills trees** (damages leaves, releases toxic aluminium from soil), makes **lakes and rivers acidic** so fish and other aquatic life die, damages buildings, and can be carried far from its source. **Carbon monoxide** from incomplete combustion binds to haemoglobin. Reduced by removing sulfur from fuels, catalytic converters, and using renewable energy.

**Water pollution — eutrophication**: **fertilisers** (nitrates and phosphates) are washed (**leached**) from farmland into rivers and lakes, and **sewage** adds nutrients too. The sequence — and the marks are for the order:

1. Extra **nitrates** enter the water.
2. **Algae grow rapidly** — an **algal bloom** covers the surface.
3. The bloom **blocks light**, so **plants below die** (no photosynthesis); the algae themselves soon die too.
4. **Bacteria (decomposers) multiply** to break down the dead plants and algae.
5. The bacteria **respire aerobically, using up the dissolved oxygen** in the water.
6. **Fish and other animals die** from lack of oxygen.

**Deforestation** — clearing forests for timber, farmland, cattle ranching, roads and mining:

- **Leaching and soil erosion** — without tree roots the soil is washed away and its minerals leached out; rivers silt up; flooding increases.
- **Disturbance of the water cycle** — less transpiration, so less cloud and rainfall; the area can become drier.
- **Disturbance of the carbon cycle** — less photosynthesis to remove CO₂; burning the trees adds CO₂; more global warming.
- **Loss of habitat and biodiversity** — species with nowhere else to live become **extinct**; loss of potential medicines and food.
- Loss of nutrient cycling and of the forest's evaporative cooling.

::: method
1. **Greenhouse gases**: name the gas → the human source → the mechanism (absorbs and re-radiates infrared) → the consequence. Do not confuse the greenhouse effect with ozone depletion or acid rain.
2. **Consequences of warming**: at least three *biological* effects — distribution, extinction, breeding timing, pests, sea level flooding habitats.
3. **Acid rain**: gases named (SO₂, NOₓ) → dissolve → acid rain → effect on trees and aquatic life.
4. **Eutrophication in order** — the six numbered steps; the killer detail is that **bacteria respiring** use up the oxygen, not the algae "using it up" or "poisoning" the fish.
5. **Deforestation: four effects** — erosion/leaching, water cycle, carbon cycle, biodiversity — each explained through the missing trees.
:::

::: example Greenhouse effect
Explain how burning fossil fuels contributes to global warming. (3)

Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide, increasing its concentration in the atmosphere (1). Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas: it absorbs the infrared radiation given off by the Earth's surface and re-radiates it, trapping heat (1). More CO₂ means more heat is trapped, so the average global temperature rises (1).
:::

::: example Consequences
Describe three biological consequences of global warming. (3)

Species move towards the poles or to higher ground as their habitat warms, and those that cannot move become extinct (1). Rising sea levels flood coastal habitats such as salt marshes and coral reefs bleach and die in warmer water (1). Pests and disease-carrying insects such as mosquitoes spread into new regions, and changes in the timing of breeding or migration leave animals out of step with their food supply (1).
:::

::: example Eutrophication
Explain how fertiliser washed into a lake can lead to the death of fish. (5)

Nitrates leached from the fields increase the nutrient concentration of the lake water (1). Algae grow rapidly, forming a bloom on the surface (1). The bloom blocks light, so submerged plants cannot photosynthesise and die, and the algae die too (1). Decomposing bacteria multiply to break down the dead material (1) and respire aerobically, using up the dissolved oxygen, so the fish suffocate (1).
:::

::: example Deforestation
Explain two ways in which cutting down a large area of rainforest affects the environment beyond the forest itself. (4)

Less photosynthesis removes less carbon dioxide from the air, and burning the felled trees releases CO₂, so atmospheric CO₂ rises and global warming increases (2). Without trees transpiring, less water vapour enters the air, so cloud formation and rainfall decrease, making the regional climate drier; the exposed soil is eroded and washed into rivers, causing silting and floods downstream (2).
:::

::: q
Name three greenhouse gases and a human source of each. (3)
---
Carbon dioxide — burning fossil fuels; methane — cattle, rice fields, landfill; nitrous oxide — fertilisers and vehicle exhausts (or CFCs — refrigerants).
:::

::: q
Explain why the greenhouse effect is necessary for life on Earth. (1)
---
Without it, heat would escape to space and the Earth would be far too cold for most life.
:::

::: q
Explain how cattle farming increases greenhouse gases. (2)
---
Cattle produce methane during digestion (1); forests are also cleared for pasture, releasing CO₂ and reducing photosynthesis (1).
:::

::: q
Name the gases that cause acid rain and state where they come from. (2)
---
Sulfur dioxide from burning coal and oil containing sulfur (1); oxides of nitrogen from vehicle engines (1).
:::

::: q
Describe two effects of acid rain on living organisms. (2)
---
Damages tree leaves and releases toxic metals from soil, killing trees (1); acidifies lakes so fish eggs and adults die (1).
:::

::: q
What is meant by leaching? (1)
---
The washing of soluble minerals such as nitrates out of the soil by rainwater into rivers and lakes.
:::

::: q
In eutrophication, why does the oxygen concentration of the water fall? (2)
---
Bacteria multiply to decompose the dead algae and plants (1) and respire aerobically, using up the dissolved oxygen (1).
:::

::: q
Explain how deforestation causes soil erosion. (2)
---
Tree roots no longer bind the soil and the canopy no longer shelters it from rain (1), so soil is washed away and minerals are leached out (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why deforestation reduces biodiversity. (2)
---
The forest is the habitat of many species (1); with it destroyed they lose food and shelter and may become extinct, especially species found nowhere else (1).
:::

::: q
Suggest two ways to reduce the release of carbon dioxide. (2)
---
Use renewable energy instead of fossil fuels; plant trees / stop deforestation (or reduce car use, insulate buildings).
:::

::: watch
- **Eutrophication is a sequence** — fertiliser, algal bloom, light blocked, plants die, **bacteria decompose and respire**, oxygen falls, fish die. Marks are for the order and the bacteria.
- Keep **three problems apart**: greenhouse effect (CO₂, methane → warming), acid rain (SO₂, NOₓ), ozone (CFCs).
- Consequences of warming must be **biological** — distribution, extinction, timing, pests, habitats.
- Deforestation: **carbon cycle, water cycle, soil, biodiversity** — four separate effects.
- Methane comes from **cattle, rice, landfill** — a favourite one-mark question.
:::

::: test
Explain how burning fossil fuels causes global warming. (3)
---
Releases carbon dioxide (1); carbon dioxide absorbs infrared radiated by the Earth and re-emits some back (1); more carbon dioxide traps more heat, raising the average temperature — the enhanced greenhouse effect (1).
:::

::: test
Describe three consequences of global warming. (3)
---
Any three of: sea levels rise as ice melts and water expands, flooding coasts; more extreme weather; changes in the distribution of species; loss of habitats such as coral reefs; changed crop yields (3).
:::

::: test
Describe how fertiliser run-off can lead to the death of fish in a lake. (4)
---
Nitrates leach into the water and cause rapid growth of algae — an algal bloom (1); the algae block light so plants below die (1); bacteria decompose the dead plants and algae, using up oxygen (1); fish and other animals suffocate (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how untreated sewage causes the same problem as fertiliser. (2)
---
Bacteria feed on the organic matter and multiply (1); their respiration uses up the dissolved oxygen (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how deforestation contributes to global warming in two ways. (2)
---
Fewer trees remove less carbon dioxide by photosynthesis (1); burning or decay of the felled trees releases the carbon they stored (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how deforestation can change the local water cycle and soil. (2)
---
Less transpiration means less water vapour and rainfall; roots no longer hold the soil (1); rain washes away topsoil (erosion) and minerals are leached (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why methane is a concern as a greenhouse gas, and name two human sources. (2)
---
It traps far more heat per molecule than carbon dioxide (1); cattle farming and rice paddies (also landfill) (1).
:::

::: test
Explain the difference between global warming and the greenhouse effect. (2)
---
The greenhouse effect is natural: gases keep the Earth warm enough for life (1); global warming is the rise in temperature caused by human activity increasing those gases (1).
:::
