## What it is

Paper 2 is **Speaking**. It is worth **50 marks** and **25%** of the GCSE. At Higher tier the test lasts **10 to 12 minutes**, and marking stops at 12 minutes. Before it you get **15 minutes' supervised preparation** with three cards: the **role-play**, the **reading-aloud text** and the **photo card**. In that time you may write notes on an additional answer sheet, with no limit on how much you write. You can take the notes into the room and use them during the test, and you hand them in at the end. There is no dictionary, and you may not read the text aloud during preparation. Your teacher conducts and records the test, and an AQA examiner marks it.

**Part 1: role-play (10 marks, 1 to 1.5 minutes).** The card sets **five tasks in English** that say exactly what to convey. Each task is marked out of 2: **2** if the message is conveyed without ambiguity, **1** if it is only partly conveyed or is ambiguous, **0** if nothing is conveyed. If a task asks for two details and one is missing or unclear, you get 1. The specification's one fixed rule is that you **must ask a question**, and one task will tell you to. It sets no tense rules, but the wording of a task can demand a time frame. 'Say what you did last weekend' needs a past tense, or the message is ambiguous. Once a task is done, anything extra you say that is wrong is ignored. So there is nothing to gain from saying more, and a short, correct answer is the safest.

**Part 2: reading aloud and questions (15 marks, 3 to 3.5 minutes).** You read a text of at least 50 words. It is chosen to test the sound-spelling rules. The reading earns **5 marks for pronunciation** (AO3). For 5, your pronunciation is *always or nearly always accurate*, with perhaps an occasional minor slip. Then the teacher asks **four compulsory questions** on the text's topic, exactly as they are written in the booklet. The four answers are marked together, out of **10** (AO1). For 9 to 10, you answer *all four clearly*, give *at least two extended answers* and develop *at least one other well*.

**Part 3: photo card (25 marks, 6 to 7 minutes).** The card has two photos from one theme, and it is always a different theme from your reading-aloud text. When the teacher asks you to tell them about the photos, talk for about **a minute and a half** about both. This is worth **5 marks** (AO2). For 5, you convey *a lot of information*, *always clearly*. If you talk about only one photo, you lose a mark. Then comes the **unprepared conversation** (4.5 to 5.5 minutes), which can range over **all three topics of that theme**. It is marked out of **15** for communication (AO1) and **5** for language (AO3). For 13 to 15 on communication, you convey *a lot of information* with *consistent good development and regular extended responses*, always or nearly always clearly. For 5 on language, you use a *wide variety of vocabulary and structures*, with only a few minor errors and few or no major errors *when you attempt complex language*.

**What a grade 9 sounds like.** Every answer gives a detail, an opinion with a reason, and usually a second time frame, without being prompted. The Higher structures from the list turn up naturally: *cuando sea mayor*, *acabo de*, *desde hace*, *ya no*, *lo mejor es*, the conditional and the future.

::: terms
- **Role-play task**: one of five English instructions on the card, each worth 2 marks for an unambiguous message
- **Unambiguous**: the listener can understand only one meaning; the wrong tense or a missing detail makes a message ambiguous
- **Extended response**: an answer that goes well beyond the question: detail, opinion, reason, example and another time frame
- **Developed well**: an answer that adds at least a reason or a detail to the basic reply
- **Major error**: a mistake that gets in the way of meaning, such as the wrong verb or tense ('fui' for 'voy')
- **Minor error**: a mistake that does not affect meaning, such as a wrong gender ('el foto' for 'la foto')
- **en la primera foto**: in the first photo; the standard opener for describing the card
- **se puede ver**: you can see (impersonal se, Foundation grammar); a useful way to list what is in a photo
- **parece que**: it seems that (Higher); use it to go beyond what is visible
- **lo mejor / lo peor**: the best thing / the worst thing (Higher, lo + adjective); an easy complex structure
- **cuando sea mayor**: when I am older (Higher present subjunctive after cuando); the classic future flourish
- **¿Cuál es ...?**: which is / what is ...? (cuál + ser); the easiest safe way to ask a role-play question
- **15 minutes' preparation**: the supervised time before the test, with all three cards and paper for notes
:::

::: method
1. **Preparation, minutes 0 to 4: role-play.** Write a full Spanish answer for each task. Underline any task that names a time (*last weekend*, *next year*) and any task asking for **two** details. Write the question in full, with ¿ and ?. Choose **tú** or **usted** to match the character on the card.
2. **Minutes 4 to 8: reading aloud.** Read the text silently. Mark the stressed syllable of long words, and mark the traps: silent **h**, **ll**, **ñ**, **j** and **ge/gi**, **que/qui**, **rr**, and **c** or **z** before e/i. Guess the four questions: they will be about the text's topic, and often one each on present, past, future and opinion. Note a phrase or two for each.
3. **Minutes 8 to 15: photo card.** Write 4 to 5 notes per photo: who, where, what they are doing (*están* + *-ando/-iendo*), what it seems (*parece que*), your opinion with *porque*. Then list the theme's three topics and one past, one present and one future idea for each.
4. **Role-play in the room.** Answer each task once, clearly and briefly. Do not add risky extras.
5. **Reading aloud.** Read steadily, not fast. Stress falls on the second-to-last syllable of words ending in a vowel, n or s, on the last syllable otherwise, and wherever there is a written accent.
6. **Every answer in the conversations.** Answer, then add a reason (*porque*), a detail or example (*por ejemplo*), a contrast (*sin embargo*) and another time frame (*el año pasado ... / el próximo año ...*). Keep going until the teacher moves on.
7. **If you do not understand a question**, ask in Spanish: *¿Puede repetir, por favor?* It costs nothing.
:::

::: example A role-play at grade 9
Card: *You are talking to your Spanish friend about free time.*

1. **Say what sport you do and how often.** *Juego al baloncesto dos veces a la semana.* This gives two details (the sport and the frequency), so it earns 2.
2. **Give your opinion of watching sport on television and one reason.** *Creo que ver deportes en la tele es aburrido porque prefiero jugar.* Opinion plus reason: 2.
3. **Say what you did last weekend.** *El fin de semana pasado fui al cine con mis amigos.* The task names a past time, so the preterite *fui* makes the message unambiguous. 'Voy al cine' would convey only part of it.
4. **Say what you are going to do next summer.** *El próximo verano voy a aprender a nadar.* Using *ir a* + infinitive is enough; the future tense is not required.
5. **Ask your friend a question about their favourite film.** *¿Cuál es tu película favorita?* It uses *tu*, because the card says 'your friend'. With a hotel receptionist it would be *su*.
:::

::: example Reading aloud and the four questions
Text (53 words): *Para llevar una vida sana, hay que comer bien y dormir ocho horas cada noche. Mi hermano juega al fútbol todos los jueves, pero yo prefiero nadar en la piscina. Ayer mi familia y yo cenamos pescado con verduras, y mañana vamos a correr por el parque que está cerca de la iglesia.*

Traps: *llevar* (ll as the y in 'yes'); *hay* ('eye', silent h); *hermano* (silent h); *juega*, *jueves* (throaty j); *fútbol* (stress on FOOT, because of the accent); *prefiero*; *piscina* (sc then c before i); *verduras* (v sounds like a soft b); *mañana* (ma-NYAH-na); *correr* (rolled rr, tapped final r); *parque* (the u is silent); *cerca*, *iglesia*.

Question 1: *¿Qué haces para llevar una vida sana?*
Model: *Intento comer mucha fruta y verdura, y bebo agua en vez de bebidas con azúcar. Además, voy al gimnasio dos veces a la semana con mi mejor amigo porque me ayuda a relajarme después del instituto.* (developed well)

Question 2: *¿Qué comiste ayer?*
Model: *Para el desayuno comí fruta y bebí un vaso de leche, y en el instituto comí un bocadillo de jamón. Por la noche cenamos pollo con arroz en casa. Fue bastante sano, aunque también bebí un chocolate caliente.* (developed well)

Question 3: *¿Crees que los jóvenes duermen bastante? ¿Por qué?*
Model: *No, creo que muchos jóvenes no duermen bastante porque usan el móvil en la cama hasta muy tarde. Yo antes hacía lo mismo, pero ya no, porque siempre estaba cansado en clase. Ahora dejo el móvil en la cocina a las diez y duermo mucho mejor.* (extended: opinion, reason, past habit with *ya no*, present change)

Question 4: *¿Qué vas a hacer para estar en forma en el futuro?*
Model: *Voy a correr más, y cuando sea mayor, me gustaría correr diez kilómetros sin parar. También tendré que comer menos comida rápida, aunque será difícil porque me encantan las hamburguesas.* (extended: future, subjunctive after *cuando*, conditional, reason)

Two extended answers, two developed well, all clear: this is band 9 to 10.
:::

::: example Describing the photos
Card, theme 3 (Communication and the world around us). Photo 1: a family eating dinner together at home, laughing, with no phones on the table. Photo 2: three teenagers in a living room, each looking at their own phone.

*En la primera foto hay una familia que está cenando en casa. Hay cuatro personas: los padres y dos hijos. Parece que están hablando y riéndose, y nadie usa el móvil. Creo que es una familia feliz porque pasan tiempo juntos.*

*En la segunda foto se puede ver a tres jóvenes en un salón. Cada uno está mirando su propio móvil, así que no hablan entre ellos. Quizás están en las redes sociales o jugando a un videojuego. En mi opinión, esta foto es un poco triste, porque están juntos pero no se comunican. En mi casa pasa lo mismo a veces, pero mis padres no nos dejan usar el móvil durante la cena.*

Why it scores 5: there is a lot of information about both photos, and all of it is clear. It uses the present continuous (*está cenando*, *está mirando*), *parece que*, opinions with reasons, and a link to your own life.
:::

::: example Developing answers in the unprepared conversation
Teacher: *¿Cuántas horas al día usas el móvil?*
*Unas cuatro horas al día, lo que es demasiado. Lo uso sobre todo para hablar con mis amigos y escuchar música. El mes pasado intenté pasar un fin de semana sin móvil. Fue muy difícil, pero dormí mucho mejor.*

Teacher: *¿Cuáles son las ventajas de la tecnología para los jóvenes?*
*Lo mejor es que es muy útil para aprender. Por ejemplo, uso internet para practicar español todos los días. Sin embargo, algunos jóvenes pasan demasiado tiempo en internet y ya no hacen ejercicio.*

Teacher: *¿Prefieres pasar las vacaciones en la ciudad o en el campo?*
*Prefiero el campo porque es más tranquilo y hay menos contaminación. El verano pasado fuimos a un pueblo en las montañas y dimos un paseo cada día. Cuando tenga dieciocho años, quiero ir al norte de España con mis amigos.*

This conversation moves across the theme's topics (technology, then travel), as the unprepared conversation may. Every answer gives information, a reason and another time frame, and it uses *lo que*, *lo mejor*, *ya no* and a subjunctive after *cuando*.
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher speaking test, and when does marking stop?
---
10 to 12 minutes; marking stops at 12 minutes.
:::

::: q
What is the one rule the specification fixes for every role-play?
---
You must ask a question as one of the tasks.
:::

::: q
A role-play task says 'Give two details about your school'. You give one clear detail. What do you score?
---
1 out of 2, because the message is only partly conveyed.
:::

::: q
In the role-play you complete a task correctly, then add an extra sentence with a wrong verb. What happens?
---
Nothing: once the task is accomplished, further incorrect information is ignored. The extra sentence gains nothing either.
:::

::: q
How are the reading-aloud marks split, and how are the four questions marked?
---
5 for pronunciation of the text and 10 for the answers to the four compulsory questions, which are marked as a whole, not question by question.
:::

::: q
What happens if you describe only one photo?
---
You lose one mark from the photo-description mark (unless that mark would only be 1). You must say at least one thing about each photo.
:::

::: q
Which topics can the unprepared conversation cover?
---
Any or all of the three topics in the theme of the photo card.
:::

::: q
Turn this minimal answer into a developed one: ¿Te gusta leer? Sí.
---
Example: Sí, me encanta leer novelas porque me ayudan a relajarme. El año pasado leí diez libros, y cuando sea mayor me gustaría escribir una novela.
:::

::: watch
- **Role-play time frames.** 'Say what you did' needs a past tense; 'say what you will do' needs *ir a* or a future. A present tense makes the message ambiguous.
- **The question task.** Write it out in full during preparation, including the question word (*¿Cuál ...?*, *¿Cuándo ...?*, *¿Dónde ...?*).
- **Two details.** Tick both off in your notes; missing one costs a mark.
- **Reading aloud.** The pronunciation errors examiners hear most: sounding the h, a hard **j** or **g** before e/i, an English **ll**, a missing **ñ**, *que* said as 'kway', and the stress on the wrong syllable.
- **One-photo answers.** Say something about both photos before the minute and a half runs out.
- **Minimal answers.** 'Sí, me gusta' caps you at the lower bands. Give a reason, an example and another time frame every time.
- **Learnt monologues.** The conversation is unprepared. Pre-learnt phrases are fine, but answer the question that was asked.
:::

::: test
Role-play task: 'Ask the receptionist what time breakfast is served.' Write your question in Spanish. (2)
---
¿A qué hora es el desayuno? / ¿A qué hora se sirve el desayuno? (2); 1 if the message is only partly clear, e.g. it asks about breakfast but not the time.
:::

::: test
Role-play task: 'Say what you did last weekend (two details).' Write a Spanish answer that scores 2. (2)
---
Any past-tense sentence with two details, e.g. El fin de semana pasado fui al cine (1) y cené en un restaurante con mi familia (1).
:::

::: test
Give two things you should do with the photo card during the 15 minutes' preparation. (2)
---
Any two: prepare notes on both photos (1); note what people are doing and what it seems, with opinions (1); list the three topics of the theme with past, present and future ideas (1).
:::

::: test
Mark the stressed syllable in each word: (a) hablan (b) ciudad (c) fácil (d) jóvenes. (4)
---
(a) HA-blan (1); (b) ciu-DAD (1); (c) FÁ-cil (1); (d) JÓ-ve-nes (1).
:::

::: test
Write two sentences in Spanish describing a photo of people cycling in a park, using 'hay' and the present continuous. (2)
---
e.g. En la foto hay una familia en un parque (1); están montando en bici / están paseando en bicicleta (1).
:::

::: test
Answer in Spanish with an extended response: ¿Qué hiciste el verano pasado? Include a past tense, an opinion with a reason, and a future plan. (4)
---
Past tense correct (1); opinion (1); reason with porque (1); future plan with ir a / future / conditional (1). Example: El verano pasado fui a la costa con mi familia. Fue genial porque hizo sol todos los días. El próximo verano voy a trabajar en una tienda.
:::

::: test
Explain what the top band requires for the unprepared conversation, for both communication and language. (4)
---
A lot of information conveyed (1); consistent good development with regular extended responses (1); wide variety of vocabulary and structures (1); few or no major errors, even when complex language is attempted (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into Spanish, for use in the conversation: (a) When I am older, I would like to live in Spain. (b) I have just finished my exams. (4)
---
(a) Cuando sea mayor (1), me gustaría vivir en España (1). (b) Acabo de (1) terminar mis exámenes (1).
:::
