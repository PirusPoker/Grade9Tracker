## What it is

Spanish spelling is far more regular than English: once you know how each letter or letter group sounds, you can read any word aloud and write down almost any word you hear. AQA lists the **sound-symbol correspondences** that differ from English, and at Higher adds the **rules for stress and written accents**. This lesson teaches both, and gives you dictation practice: because there is no audio here, each exercise **describes a sound and asks you for the spelling**.

**Where it turns up.**
- **Listening, Section B: dictation (10 marks).** At Higher you hear at least 30 words in short sentences, each played three times (whole, in sections, whole again). Two words are from **outside** the vocabulary list, so you must spell them from the sounds alone. Marks: 5 for communicating the meaning, 5 for accurate transcription and grammar, so agreements and verb endings count too.
- **Speaking, reading aloud.** You read a text of at least 50 words (Higher), and 5 marks go on pronunciation, which means these correspondences and the stress.
- **Writing.** Missing or wrong accents are accuracy errors throughout the paper.

**The sound-symbol list.**
- **Vowels** are short and pure, and every one is pronounced (*clase* = two syllables, *cla-se*): **a** like the 'a' in 'father' but short (*alto*), **e** as in 'get' (*medio*), **i** as in 'see' (*idea*), **o** as in 'hot' (*dos*), **u** as in 'food' (*universidad*).
- **ll** sounds like the 'y' in 'yes' (*llamar, calle*). **ch** as in 'church' (*leche, ocho*). **ñ** as 'ny' in 'canyon' (*mañana, cumpleaños*).
- **c** is hard 'k' before **a, o, u** (*casa, coche, escuchar*); **cu + vowel** is 'kw' (*cuerpo, cuidado*). **ce, ci** and **z** share one sound: 'th' as in 'think' in most of Spain, 's' in Latin America and parts of southern Spain (*cerca, concierto, zona, manzana*). **que, qui** are 'keh, kee' with a silent *u* (*porque, tranquilo*).
- **g** is hard before **a, o, u** (*ganar, largo, preguntar*). **ge, gi** and **j** share a throaty sound, like the 'ch' in Scottish 'loch' (*gente, gimnasio, ojo, naranja*). **gue, gui** are hard 'g' with a silent *u* (*hamburguesa, guitarra*).
- **v** sounds the same as **b** (*verde, verdad*): there is no English 'v' sound.
- **r** between vowels or at the end of a word is a single quick tap, like the 'tt' in a fast American 'butter' (*pero, hablar*). **rr**, **r** at the start of a word and **r** after **n, l, s** are rolled (*cerrar, rojo, sonreír*).
- **h** is always silent (*hospital, hoy*).

**Stress and accents (Higher only).** When reading: stress any vowel with a written accent; otherwise stress the **second-to-last** syllable if the word ends in a **vowel, n or s**, and the **last** syllable if it ends in any other consonant. When writing, the accent goes on the stressed vowel only when a word breaks those rules: stress on the **last** syllable of a word ending in **vowel, n or s** (*café, canción, inglés*); stress on the **second-to-last** syllable of a word ending in **another consonant** (*fácil, árbol*); and **every** word stressed on the **third-to-last** syllable (*música, sábado, teléfono*).

**Grade 9** candidates read aloud with pure vowels, a tapped *r* against a rolled *rr*, and the stress in the right place, and in the dictation they use these rules plus grammar (agreement, verb endings) to spell unfamiliar words correctly.

::: terms
- **sound-symbol correspondence**: the link between a letter or letter group and its sound: *ll* sounds like English 'y'
- **pure vowel**: a short, single vowel sound with no glide; *no* is 'noh', not English 'know'
- **hard c / soft c**: 'k' before *a, o, u* (*casa*); 'th' or 's' before *e, i* (*cine*)
- **hard g / soft g**: 'g' before *a, o, u* (*gato*); throaty 'h' like *j* before *e, i* (*gente*)
- **silent u**: the *u* in *que, qui, gue, gui* is not pronounced; it keeps the *c* or *g* hard: *guitarra*
- **tapped r**: a single flick of the tongue for *r* between vowels or at the end: *pero, comer*
- **rolled r**: a trill for *rr*, initial *r*, and *r* after *n, l, s*: *perro, radio, Enrique*
- **silent h**: *h* is never pronounced: *hora* sounds like 'ora'
- **stressed syllable**: the syllable said most strongly: *CA-sa* (ends in a vowel, so second-to-last), *ha-BLAR*
- **written accent**: the mark on a vowel that breaks the stress rules: *árbol, jardín, sábado*
- **penultimate / antepenultimate**: the second-to-last / third-to-last syllable: *ca-MI-sa*, *MÚ-si-ca*
:::

::: method
**Reading aloud**
1. Split each word into syllables around the vowels: *pe-lí-cu-la*, *ha-blar*.
2. Find the stress: accent → that vowel; no accent and ends in vowel/n/s → second-to-last; ends in another consonant → last.
3. Say every vowel purely; don't swallow final *-e* (*clase* = *CLA-se*).
4. Apply the letter rules: silent *h*; *ll* = 'y'; *j, ge, gi* = throaty; *ce, ci, z* = 'th'/'s'; *qu* = 'k'; *v* = 'b'; tap or roll the *r*.

**Dictation (sound → spelling)**
1. Write what you hear syllable by syllable, choosing the spelling for each sound: 'k' → *c* before *a, o, u*, *qu* before *e, i*; 'th/s' → *z* before *a, o, u*, *c* before *e, i*; throaty 'h' → *j* before *a, o, u*, *g* or *j* before *e, i*; hard 'g' → *g* before *a, o, u*, *gu* before *e, i*.
2. A 'b' sound can be *b* or *v*, and an initial vowel may have a silent *h*: use the vocabulary list to decide (*vivir, hablar, hoy*).
3. Decide whether an accent is needed from where you heard the stress.
4. On the third hearing, check grammar: agreements (*las casas blancas*), verb endings (*hablaron*), and small words (*al, del, que*).
:::

| sound heard | spelling before a, o, u | spelling before e, i | examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 'k' | c | qu | casa, coche, escuchar / porque, tranquilo |
| 'th' (Spain) or 's' | z | c | zona, manzana / cerca, cine |
| hard 'g' | g | gu | gato, largo / hamburguesa, guitarra |
| throaty 'h' | j | g or j | ojo, naranja / gente, gimnasio, jefe |
| 'kw' | cu + vowel | cu + vowel | cuerpo, cuidado |

::: example Reading a sentence aloud
*El jueves quiero ir al gimnasio con mi hermano, pero hoy llueve.*

1. *jueves* → throaty *j*, *ue* as 'weh': **HWEH-bes**. Ends in *s*, so stress on the second-to-last syllable. *v* = 'b'.
2. *quiero* → *qu* = 'k', *u* silent: **KYEH-ro**, with a tapped *r*.
3. *gimnasio* → *gi* is throaty: **heem-NA-syo**.
4. *hermano* → silent *h*: **er-MA-no**.
5. *pero* → tapped *r*: **PE-ro** (not *perro*, 'dog', which is rolled).
6. *hoy llueve* → silent *h*, *ll* = 'y': **oy YWEH-beh**.
:::

::: example Deciding on written accents
Say each word, find the stress, then apply the writing rule.

1. *cancion*, stressed on the last syllable, ends in *n* → rule broken, accent: **canción**. Plural *canciones*: now stressed second-to-last and ends in *s* → no accent.
2. *facil*, stressed on *fa*, ends in *l* → second-to-last with another consonant → accent: **fácil**.
3. *musica*, stressed on *mu*, third-to-last → always accent: **música**.
4. *ciudad*, stressed on the last syllable, ends in *d* → follows the rule, no accent: **ciudad**.
5. *examen*, stressed on *xa*, ends in *n* → follows the rule: **examen**. Plural: stress stays on *xa*, now third-to-last → **exámenes**.
:::

::: example Dictation: spelling words from the sounds
You hear: *Mi abuela vive cerca de la plaza y siempre cocina paella los domingos.*

1. 'bee-beh' → the 'b' sound here is *v*: *vive* (from *vivir*, on the list).
2. 'THER-ka' → 'th' before *e* is spelt *c*: *cerca*.
3. 'PLA-tha' → 'th' before *a* is spelt *z*: *plaza*.
4. 'ko-THEE-na' → *c* before *o*, then 'th' before *i* = *c*: *cocina*.
5. 'pa-EH-ya' → the 'y' sound between vowels here is *ll*: *paella*.
6. Grammar check: *mi abuela* (f), *vive* (3rd person), *los domingos* (plural).
:::

::: example A word from outside the list
You hear an unfamiliar word twice: **'tor-TU-ga'** (tortoise), then **'re-LAM-pa-go'** (lightning).

1. *tortuga*: tapped *r* before a consonant → single *r*; hard 'g' before *a* → *g*; stress on the second-to-last syllable of a word ending in a vowel → no accent. **tortuga**.
2. *relámpago*: *r* at the start is rolled but written with one *r*; hard 'g' before *o* → *g*; the stress falls on the third-to-last syllable → always an accent: **relámpago**.

In the real dictation the two unlisted words are chosen so that these rules lead you to the right spelling.
:::

::: q
How is *ll* pronounced, and how is *ñ* pronounced? Give one listed example of each.
---
*ll* like English 'y' in 'yes' (*calle, llamar*); *ñ* like 'ny' in 'canyon' (*mañana, año*).
:::

::: q
How do you spell the 'k' sound before *e*, and before *a*? Give a listed example of each.
---
Before *e*: *qu* (*porque, querer*); before *a*: *c* (*casa*).
:::

::: q
Dictation: you hear 'GEN-teh' starting with the throaty sound, meaning 'people'. Spell it.
---
*gente* (throaty sound before *e* can be *g* or *j*; the listed word is with *g*).
:::

::: q
Dictation: you hear 'gee-TA-rra' with a hard *g* and a rolled *r*. Spell it.
---
*guitarra*: hard *g* before *i* needs *gu*; a rolled *r* between vowels is *rr*.
:::

::: q
Which letter is always silent in Spanish? Spell the word you hear as 'OY' meaning 'today'.
---
*h*: *hoy*.
:::

::: q
Where is the stress in *hablar, joven, ordenador, lunes*?
---
*ha-BLAR* (ends in *r*: last syllable); *JO-ven* (ends in *n*: second-to-last); *or-de-na-DOR* (ends in *r*); *LU-nes* (ends in *s*).
:::

::: q
Add accents where needed: *arbol, jardin, telefono, mesa, tambien, azul*.
---
*árbol, jardín, teléfono, mesa, también, azul*.
:::

::: q
Why does *pero* differ from *perro* when read aloud?
---
*pero* (but) has a single tapped *r*; *perro* (dog) has *rr*, which is rolled.
:::

::: watch
- **Pronounce every vowel**, including final *-e*: *clase, parque, noche*. English speakers swallow them.
- **h is silent** and **v sounds like b**: don't say an English 'h' in *hotel* or an English 'v' in *vivir*.
- **j, ge, gi** are throaty, not English 'j' or soft 'g': *joven, general*.
- **Tap versus roll**: *pero / perro*, *caro / carro*. A wrong *r* can change the word.
- **Accents break the stress rules**: *canción, fácil, música*; missing them is an accuracy error in writing and in the dictation.
- **Plurals can lose or gain an accent**: *canción → canciones*, *joven → jóvenes*.
- **Dictation marks include grammar**: check agreements and verb endings on the third hearing.
:::

::: test
Dictation: you hear 'ko-MEH-mos' then 'THEE-neh' (Spanish pronunciation). Write both words and give the rule behind each first letter. (3)
---
*comemos* (1); *cine* (1); *c* is hard before *o*, and soft ('th/s') before *i* (1).
:::

::: test
Add written accents where needed and give the rule for each word: (a) *cafe* (b) *util* (c) *pagina* (d) *reloj*. (4)
---
(a) *café*: stressed last syllable ending in a vowel (1); (b) *útil*: stressed second-to-last, ends in *l* (1); (c) *página*: third-to-last, always accented (1); (d) *reloj*: stressed last syllable, ends in *j*, no accent (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how these letters sound: (a) *z* in *zona* (b) *qu* in *tranquilo* (c) *j* in *naranja*. (3)
---
(a) 'th' as in 'think' (or 's' in Latin America) (1); (b) hard 'k', the *u* is silent (1); (c) a throaty 'h', like 'ch' in 'loch' (1).
:::

::: test
Mark the stressed syllable in: (a) *universidad* (b) *instituto* (c) *examen* (d) *hablaron*. (4)
---
(a) *u-ni-ver-si-DAD* (1); (b) *ins-ti-TU-to* (1); (c) *e-XA-men* (1); (d) *ha-BLA-ron* (1).
:::

::: test
Dictation: write the sentence you hear. Sounds: 'mee er-MA-no oy no YEH-ga' ('My brother isn't arriving today'; two words begin with a silent letter). (3)
---
*Mi hermano hoy no llega.* Correct *hermano* with *h* (1); *hoy* (1); *llega* with *ll* (1).
:::

::: test
Write the plural and say what happens to the accent: (a) *la estación* (b) *el joven* (c) *el autobús*. (3)
---
(a) *las estaciones*, accent dropped because the stress is now second-to-last in a word ending in *s* (1); (b) *los jóvenes*, accent added because the stress is now third-to-last (1); (c) *los autobuses*, accent dropped because the stress is now second-to-last in a word ending in *s* (1).
:::

::: test
A student reads aloud *Hoy voy a la playa con mi perro* as 'Hoy VOY a la PLAY-a kon mee PEH-ro' with an English 'h' and 'v' and a single *r*. Identify three pronunciation errors. (3)
---
The *h* in *hoy* should be silent (1); *v* in *voy* should sound like *b* (1); *rr* in *perro* should be rolled, otherwise it sounds like *pero* (1). (Also accept: *playa* should be *PLA-ya* with a pure *a*.)
:::

::: test
Dictation: you hear the unfamiliar word 'thi-GA-rra' (Spain pronunciation, stress on the second syllable, rolled *r*). Give the most likely spelling and explain your choices. (3)
---
*cigarra* (1): 'th' before *i* is spelt *c* (1); rolled *r* between vowels is *rr*, and the stress is second-to-last in a word ending in a vowel, so no accent (1).
:::
