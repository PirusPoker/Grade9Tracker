## What it is

Paper 1 is **Listening**: at Higher tier it lasts about **45 minutes**, is worth **50 marks** and counts for **25%** of the GCSE. The 45 minutes include **five minutes' reading time** at the start and **two minutes' checking time** at the end. The recordings are native speakers talking clearly at no more than a moderate pace, with no background noise. You may make notes at any time, including in the reading time. No dictionary.

**Section A (40 marks)** tests whether you understand spoken Spanish. The questions are **in English** and you answer **in English** or without words (a letter, a tick, a name). You hear **each item twice**, with pauses built in for reading the questions and writing answers. The items are announcements, short conversations, news bulletins, podcasts and descriptions, and they test the overall message, key points, details and opinions. Higher items are built to trap you: a speaker mentions three things and rules two of them out, says what she *used to* do before what she does *now*, or changes her mind half-way through.

**Section B (10 marks)** is a **dictation**. You hear short sentences, at least **30 words** in total at Higher, and write them down in Spanish. Each sentence is played **three times**: first as a whole, then in short sections, then as a whole again. **Two words are from outside the vocabulary list**, so you have to spell them from the sound alone. That is why the specification lists the Spanish sound-spelling rules (3.2.3) and, at Higher only, the stress and accent rules (3.2.3.1).

**How the dictation is marked.** The whole transcript is judged at once on two grids. **Communication of meaning (AO1, 5 marks)**: for 5, the meaning comes across *very clearly throughout*, so a Spanish speaker could read your version and understand every sentence. **Transcription and grammatical accuracy (AO3, 5 marks)**: for 5, words are *always or nearly always* spelt correctly with a *very high level* of grammatical accuracy. That means the right endings and agreements and the right accents. If you score 0 for meaning you also score 0 for accuracy.

**What gets a grade 9.** In Section A, you lose almost no marks to distractors. In Section B, you write words you have never seen with correct spelling *and* correct accents. Spanish spelling is very regular: learn the thirty or so sound-spelling pairs in the specification and the three stress rules and you can write almost anything you hear.

::: terms
- **Distractor**: a detail the speaker mentions but rules out, put there to catch people who write the first thing they hear
- **Not mentioned**: the answer for a statement the recording neither confirms nor contradicts; do not use general knowledge to fill the gap
- **ya no**: no longer (Higher); 'ya no juego' means I used to play but I have stopped
- **(no) tampoco**: not either, neither (Higher); 'yo tampoco' means me neither
- **(no) ni ... ni**: neither ... nor (Higher); 'no bebo ni café ni té'
- **antes / ahora**: before / now; they separate the past habit from the present one in the same sentence
- **al final**: in the end; what follows is usually the real answer after a change of plan
- **sin embargo**: however; the opinion after it often overturns the one before it
- **en cambio**: on the other hand, whereas; signals a contrast between two people or two times
- **Silent h**: Spanish h is never pronounced, so 'hoy' sounds like 'oy' and 'hermano' like 'er-MAH-no'
- **Stressed syllable**: the syllable said most strongly; in Spanish its position decides whether you write an accent
- **Written accent**: the mark on á, é, í, ó, ú; in dictation it counts as spelling, so a missing accent is an error
:::

::: method
1. **Reading time (5 minutes).** Read every question in Section A. Underline the question word (who, when, why, which two) and anything that narrows it, such as *now*, *last year*, *not*, *in the end*. Guess the Spanish you might hear: for 'How often?' expect *a veces*, *dos veces a la semana*, *nunca*.
2. **First hearing.** Listen for the gist and jot notes in the margin in English or Spanish. Do not commit to an answer when you first hear something that fits: wait for *pero*, *sin embargo*, *al final* or *ya no*.
3. **Second hearing.** Check each note against the exact wording of the question. Is it about the right person? The right time? Does the question want one detail or two?
4. **True, false or not mentioned.** 'True' needs the recording to say it. 'False' needs the recording to say the opposite. If it says neither, the answer is 'not mentioned'.
5. **Dictation, first reading.** Listen only, or write the first few words. Count the words if you can.
6. **Second reading, in sections.** Write each section. Spell each unknown word from its sounds using the sound-spelling rules.
7. **Third reading.** Fill the gaps and check the grammar: verb endings, adjective agreement (*las casas blancas*), *al* and *del*.
8. **Checking time.** Go through the dictation word by word for accents. Say each word in your head, find the stressed syllable, and apply the three rules below.
:::

::: example A distractor pair
You hear: *El sábado queríamos ir a la playa, pero hizo demasiado frío, así que al final fuimos al cine. Después comimos en un restaurante italiano.*

Question: Where did they go on Saturday **instead of** their original plan? A beach  B cinema  C shopping centre

1. *Playa* comes first, and it is the distractor. *Queríamos ir* means 'we wanted to go', which is a plan and not what happened.
2. *pero hizo demasiado frío* rules the beach out.
3. *al final fuimos al cine* ('in the end we went to the cinema') gives the answer: **B**.
4. The restaurant was *después* (afterwards), not instead. And nobody mentions a shopping centre.
:::

::: example Negatives and time words
You hear: *Antes jugaba al baloncesto todos los días, pero ya no juego porque no tengo tiempo. Ahora prefiero nadar los fines de semana. Mi hermana, en cambio, no hace ni deporte ni ejercicio.*

Questions and reasoning:
- Which sport does the speaker do **now**? *Antes jugaba al baloncesto* is the past habit and *ya no juego* shows he has stopped. **Swimming** (at weekends) is the answer.
- Why did he stop? *porque no tengo tiempo*: **he has no time**.
- True, false or not mentioned: 'His sister goes swimming with him.' *No hace ni deporte ni ejercicio* means she does neither sport nor exercise, which contradicts the statement: **false**, not 'not mentioned'.
:::

::: example Dictation from the sounds
There is no audio here, so the sound is written in English letters and the stressed syllable is in capitals. In Spain, *c* before e/i and *z* sound like **th**; in Latin America they sound like **s**. You might hear either.

You hear: **mee er-MAH-no peh-KEH-nyo TYEH-neh oon ek-SAH-men deh MOO-see-ka el SAH-ba-do**

1. *mee* is **mi**. *er-MAH-no* is **hermano**, because you know the word, and the h is silent.
2. *peh-KEH-nyo*: 'keh' is spelt **que** and 'nyo' is **ño**, so **pequeño**.
3. *TYEH-neh* is **tiene**. *oon* is **un**.
4. *ek-SAH-men* ends in **n** and is stressed on the second-to-last syllable, which is the normal pattern, so there is **no accent**: **examen**.
5. *MOO-see-ka* is stressed on the third-to-last syllable, so it **always takes an accent**: **música**. 'ka' before a is **ca**.
6. *SAH-ba-do* is also stressed third from last, so **sábado**. Spanish b and v sound the same; you know *sábado* from the list.

Answer: **Mi hermano pequeño tiene un examen de música el sábado.**
:::

::: example Spelling a word that is not on the list
You hear: **kal-theh-TEEN** (or **kal-seh-TEEN**). This is *calcetín* (sock), which is not on the AQA list.

1. 'kal' before a is **cal**.
2. 'theh' (or 'seh') before e: a th or s sound before e is spelt **ce**, giving **calce**.
3. 'TEEN' is **tín**. The stress is on the **last** syllable and the word ends in **n**, so the rule says write an accent: **calcetín**.

Compare **ah-THOOL** (or **ah-SOOL**): stressed on the last syllable but ending in **l**, which is the normal pattern, so no accent: **azul**. The th (or s) sound before u is spelt **z**, because *c* only makes that sound before e and i.
:::

::: q
How many times do you hear each Section A item, and each dictation sentence?
---
Section A items twice; dictation sentences three times (whole, in sections, whole).
:::

::: q
What are the five minutes at the start of the paper for, and what should you do with them?
---
Reading time: read every question, underline the key words (who, when, now, not, in the end) and predict the Spanish you might hear.
:::

::: q
The statement is 'Carlos walks to school'. The recording says only that Carlos lives near his school. True, false or not mentioned?
---
Not mentioned: living nearby does not say how he travels, and nothing contradicts the statement either.
:::

::: q
You hear 'ya no como carne'. What does it tell you?
---
The speaker used to eat meat but no longer does; 'ya no' marks a stopped habit.
:::

::: q
State the three stress rules you use to decide on a written accent in dictation.
---
Words ending in a vowel, n or s are normally stressed on the second-to-last syllable; words ending in any other consonant are normally stressed on the last; write an accent only when the word breaks these patterns (stressed last but ending in vowel/n/s, stressed second-to-last but ending in another consonant), and always when stressed third from last.
:::

::: q
You hear **OY AH-theh SOL** ('today it is sunny'). Spell the first word and explain the trap.
---
hoy: the h is silent, so you must know the spelling from the vocabulary list.
:::

::: q
You hear **gee-TAH-rra** (guitar). How do you spell it and why?
---
guitarra: a hard g before i needs 'gui' (the u is silent), and the rolled sound between vowels is 'rr'.
:::

::: q
Two dictation marks are called AO1 and AO3. What does each reward?
---
AO1 (5): the meaning of the extracts is communicated; AO3 (5): words are transcribed correctly with grammatical accuracy, including spelling, agreements and accents.
:::

::: watch
- **First-heard answers.** The first thing that fits is often the distractor. Wait for *pero*, *sin embargo*, *al final* or *ya no*.
- **Time frame of the question.** 'Now', 'used to' and 'next year' each point to a different sentence of the recording.
- **'Not mentioned' used as a guess.** Choose it only when the recording neither confirms nor contradicts the statement.
- **Two details asked for, one given.** 'Give two reasons' means two, and the marks are separate.
- **Dictation accents.** *música*, *sábado*, *fútbol* and *está* each lose accuracy if the accent is missing.
- **Silent h and b/v.** *hay*, *hoy*, *hacer*, *hermano*; *vamos*, *vivir*, *verde*. Learn these spellings with the vocabulary.
- **Agreement in dictation.** If you hear *las chicas simpáticas*, both words end in -as. Check them against each other.
:::

::: test
You hear: *Normalmente voy al instituto en autobús, pero hoy voy a pie porque hace sol.* How does the speaker travel to school (a) normally and (b) today? (2)
---
(a) by bus (1); (b) on foot / walks (1).
:::

::: test
You hear: *Mi padre antes trabajaba en una fábrica, pero ahora es profesor en un colegio.* True, false or not mentioned? (a) His father works in a factory. (b) His father is a teacher. (c) His father enjoys his job. (3)
---
(a) false (1); (b) true (1); (c) not mentioned (1).
:::

::: test
You hear: *Mi amiga Lucía no come ni carne ni pescado, y yo tampoco.* Give two things Lucía does not eat, and say what the speaker's diet has in common with hers. (3)
---
meat (1); fish (1); the speaker does not eat them either / is the same (1).
:::

::: test
Write the Spanish for this dictation sentence, heard as **NOON-ka BEH-o la teh-leh-bee-SYON por la ma-NYAH-na** (Latin American speaker). (4)
---
Nunca (1); veo (1); la televisión, with the accent on the final ó (1); por la mañana, with ñ (1).
:::

::: test
Add the written accent where needed, and explain one of your decisions: (a) arbol (b) cancion (c) ciudad (d) telefono. (4)
---
(a) árbol: stressed second-to-last but ends in l (1); (b) canción: stressed last and ends in n (1); (c) ciudad: no accent, since it ends in d and is stressed last, the normal pattern (1); (d) teléfono: stressed third from last (1).
:::

::: test
You hear: *El año pasado fui a Francia, pero el próximo verano voy a ir a México con mis primos.* (a) Where will the speaker go next summer? (b) Who with? (2)
---
(a) Mexico (1); (b) cousins (1).
:::

::: test
Spell the words you hear: (a) **LEH-cheh** (milk) (b) **YAH-mo** as in 'me llamo' (c) **KWEHR-po** (body). (3)
---
(a) leche (1); (b) llamo (1); (c) cuerpo (1).
:::

::: test
Explain the two marks awarded for the Higher dictation and what the top band requires for each. (4)
---
Communication of meaning, 5 marks (1): the meaning is conveyed very clearly throughout (1); transcription and grammatical accuracy, 5 marks (1): words always or nearly always spelt correctly with a very high level of grammatical accuracy (1).
:::
