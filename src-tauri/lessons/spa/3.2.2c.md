## What it is

The **present subjunctive** is a second set of present-tense forms that Spanish uses after certain triggers. At GCSE it is **Higher-only** and deliberately narrow: AQA examines it for **five verbs only** (*hacer, ser, ir, venir, tener*), in the **singular persons only** (I, you informal, he/she/you formal), in **three contexts**:

1. **cuando + future meaning**: *Cuando **sea** mayor, seré médico* = When I'm older, I'll be a doctor.
2. **que after a verb of wishing, command, request or emotion**, when the subject changes: *Quiero que **vengas*** = I want you to come.
3. **para que** (so that): *Te llamo para que **tengas** la información* = I'm calling you so that you have the information.

Nothing wider is examinable. You will not be asked for plural subjunctive forms or other verbs, and you can always get round them by restructuring the sentence.

**Where it turns up.** *Cuando sea mayor* and *Cuando tenga dinero* are the classic grade-9 openings for a future-plans bullet point in the 90- and 150-word tasks. *Mis padres quieren que haga ...* is an easy way to add a second person's view. In Reading and Listening you need to recognise *sea, vaya, tenga, venga, haga* as present-tense meanings rather than new words.

**The forms.** All five are on the Higher vocabulary list:

| infinitive | yo | tú | él / ella / usted |
|---|---|---|---|
| hacer | haga | hagas | haga |
| ser | sea | seas | sea |
| ir | vaya | vayas | vaya |
| venir | venga | vengas | venga |
| tener | tenga | tengas | tenga |

Three of them come from the *yo* present (*hago → haga, vengo → venga, tengo → tenga*); *ser* and *ir* are irregular (*sea, vaya*). The *yo* and *él* forms are identical, so context tells you who.

**Grade 9** answers use one or two subjunctives naturally and correctly, and never force one where the subject stays the same: *Quiero ir* (I want to go), but *Quiero que vayas* (I want you to go).

::: terms
- **subjunctive**: a verb form used after certain triggers such as *cuando* (future), *quiero que* and *para que*: *Quiero que vengas*
- **cuando + subjunctive**: 'when' about the future: *Cuando tenga dieciocho años, iré a la universidad*
- **cuando + indicative**: 'when' about a habit or the past, with the normal present: *Cuando hace calor, voy a la playa*
- **verb of wishing**: *querer que, esperar que* + subjunctive: *Espero que sea un buen día*
- **verb of command or request**: *decir que* (tell someone to), *pedir que* (ask someone to): *Mi madre me pide que haga la cena*
- **verb of emotion**: *me alegra que, me gusta que, me molesta que, me preocupa que* + subjunctive: *Me alegra que vengas*
- **change of subject**: the subjunctive is needed only when the second verb has a different subject: *Quiero ir* but *Quiero que vayas*
- **para que**: 'so that', always followed by the subjunctive: *Trabajo para que mi hermana tenga ropa nueva*
- **singular persons only**: AQA examines *haga, hagas, sea, seas, vaya, vayas, venga, vengas, tenga, tengas*
:::

::: method
1. **Spot the trigger**: *cuando* about the future? *que* after wanting, hoping, telling, asking or a feeling? *para que*?
2. **Check the subject.** After *que* and *para*, is the second verb's subject different from the first? If not, use the infinitive: *Quiero ir*, *Estudio para aprobar*.
3. **Check the verb** is one of the five (*hacer, ser, ir, venir, tener*) and the person is singular. If not, rephrase: *Quiero que mis padres vengan* → *Quiero que mi padre venga*, or avoid the subjunctive with an infinitive.
4. **Form it**: *yo* present minus *-o* + *-a / -as / -a* (*haga, venga, tenga*); *sea, vaya* for *ser, ir*.
5. **After *cuando* + subjunctive**, the main verb is usually **future** or *ir a*: *Cuando vaya a Madrid, visitaré el museo*.
:::

::: example cuando with a future meaning
**When I'm older, I will live abroad. When I have money, I'm going to buy a car.**

1. *When I'm older* → future meaning, *ser*, *yo*: **Cuando sea mayor**. Not *cuando soy* (that means 'whenever I am').
2. *I will live abroad* → future: **viviré en el extranjero**.
3. *When I have money* → *tener*, *yo*: **Cuando tenga dinero**.
4. *I'm going to buy a car* → **voy a comprar un coche**.

**Cuando sea mayor, viviré en el extranjero. Cuando tenga dinero, voy a comprar un coche.**

Compare the habit: **Cuando tengo dinero, compro ropa** = Whenever I have money, I buy clothes. No subjunctive, because it is not about the future.
:::

::: example que after wishing, command and request
**My parents want me to do my homework. My mother tells me to be responsible, and I ask my friend to come early.**

1. *want me to do* → wishing, different subject (they want / I do): **Mis padres quieren que haga los deberes**. English 'want me to' has no Spanish equivalent: it must become *que* + subjunctive.
2. *tells me to be responsible* → a command reported with *decir que*: **Mi madre me dice que sea responsable**.
3. *I ask my friend to come early* → request: **Le pido a mi amigo que venga temprano**.

**Mis padres quieren que haga los deberes. Mi madre me dice que sea responsable, y le pido a mi amigo que venga temprano.**

Careful: *Mi madre me dice que es responsable* (indicative) means 'My mother tells me that she is responsible', a statement, not an order.
:::

::: example Emotion and para que
**I'm glad that you are coming to the party. I'll send you the address so that you have it.**

1. *I'm glad that you are coming* → emotion + different subject: **Me alegra que vengas a la fiesta**.
2. *I'll send you the address* → **Te mandaré la dirección**.
3. *so that you have it* → **para que la tengas**.

**Me alegra que vengas a la fiesta. Te mandaré la dirección para que la tengas.**
:::

::: example Same subject: no subjunctive
1. *I want to go to Spain.* → one subject: **Quiero ir a España.**
2. *I want you to go to Spain.* → two subjects: **Quiero que vayas a España.**
3. *I study in order to pass.* → one subject: **Estudio para aprobar.**
4. *My teacher helps me so that I have good marks.* → two subjects: **Mi profesor me ayuda para que tenga buenas notas.**
:::

::: q
Give the subjunctive *tú* form of: *hacer, ser, ir, venir, tener*.
---
*hagas, seas, vayas, vengas, tengas*.
:::

::: q
Fill the gap: *Cuando ___ (yo / ir) a México, visitaré a mis tíos.*
---
*vaya* (future meaning after *cuando*).
:::

::: q
Subjunctive or not? *Cuando ___ (hacer) buen tiempo, siempre salgo a correr.*
---
*hace*: a habit, not the future, so the normal present.
:::

::: q
Spot and correct the error: *Quiero que tú vienes a mi casa.*
---
*Quiero que vengas a mi casa.* (*tú* can be left out.)
:::

::: q
Translate into Spanish: *I hope it is a good film.*
---
*Espero que sea una buena película.*
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *Mi abuela me da dinero para que tenga un buen cumpleaños.*
---
My grandmother gives me money so that I have a good birthday.
:::

::: q
Which sentence is correct? (a) *Quiero que vaya al cine.* (b) *Quiero ir al cine.* Both mean something; explain.
---
Both are correct: (a) means 'I want him / her / you (formal) to go to the cinema' (different subject); (b) means 'I want to go to the cinema' (same subject).
:::

::: q
Rewrite starting *Mi padre quiere que ...*: *Hago la cena.*
---
*Mi padre quiere que haga la cena.*
:::

::: watch
- **Only after the triggers**: *cuando* (future), *que* after wishing/command/request/emotion, *para que*. Everywhere else, use the normal tenses.
- **No subjunctive without a change of subject**: *Quiero ir*, *Estudio para aprobar*.
- **cuando + future meaning never takes the future tense**: *Cuando sea mayor*, not *Cuando seré mayor*.
- **English 'want someone to do'** is *querer que* + subjunctive, never *querer* + person + infinitive.
- **Learn the ten forms exactly**: *vaya* (not *va*), *sea* (not *es*), *venga* (not *viene*).
- **Keep it singular**: the plural subjunctive is not examined; rephrase to a singular subject if you need one.
:::
