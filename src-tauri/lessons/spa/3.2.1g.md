## What it is

This lesson gathers the verb patterns that do not work like English: **modal verbs** followed by an infinitive (*Puedo salir* = I can go out), **gustar-type verbs** where the thing liked is the grammatical subject (*Me gustan los perros* = I like dogs), **reflexive verbs** with *me, te, se, nos, os* (*Me levanto* = I get up) and **impersonal verbs**: *hay, hay que, se puede, se necesita* and *hace* for the weather.

**Where it turns up.** Opinions are the backbone of every Writing task and the conversation, and most of them go through *gustar, encantar, interesar* or *importar*. Daily-routine texts are full of reflexives. Role-plays often ask what you **can**, **must** or **would like** to do, and *hay que* and *se puede* are the quickest way to give advice or describe a town. In the translation into Spanish, *I like / she likes / they like* is tested almost every year.

**The core structures (Foundation).**
- **Modals + infinitive**, all persons: **deber** (must, should), **poder** (can, may), **querer** (want), **tener que** (have to), **saber** (know how to: *Sé nadar* = I can swim). Two fixed phrases: **quisiera** + infinitive (I would like, polite) and **me/te/le gustaría** + infinitive.
- **interesar-type verbs** (*gustar, encantar, interesar, importar, doler, molestar, preocupar*) take an indirect object pronoun, and the verb agrees with **what** is liked: *me gusta el cine*, *me gustan las películas*, *le encanta leer* (an infinitive counts as singular). With a named person, add *a*: *A mi hermano le interesa la historia.*
- **Reflexive verbs**, singular and plural, with *me, te, se, nos, os, se*: *me lavo* (I have a wash), *nos levantamos*. The plural also means **each other**: *nos vemos* (we see each other), *se besan* (they kiss each other). The pronoun follows the usual rules: before the verb, before or after with two verbs, on the end of a command (*¡Lávate!*). Only reflexives with a different meaning from the base verb appear on the list (*llamarse, ponerse, quedarse, llevarse bien*); others (*lavarse*) are the base verb plus *se*.
- **Impersonal verbs**: **hay** (there is/are), **hay que** + infinitive (you have to, one must), **se puede** + infinitive (you can, one can), **se necesita** (you need), **hace** + weather noun (*hace sol, hace frío, hace calor, hace viento, hace buen tiempo*).

**Grade 9** answers vary their opinion verbs (*me encanta, me interesa, me preocupa*), agree *gustar* correctly with plural nouns, and use *hay que* and *se puede* to give advice without needing a subject: *Para estar en forma, hay que comer bien.*

::: terms
- **modal verb**: a verb followed directly by an infinitive: *debo estudiar, puedo salir, quiero viajar*
- **tener que**: 'to have to', always with *que* before the infinitive: *Tengo que trabajar* = I have to work
- **saber + infinitive**: 'to know how to', English 'can' for a skill: *¿Sabes nadar?* = Can you swim?
- **quisiera**: a polite 'I would like', used in shops and role-plays: *Quisiera reservar una mesa*
- **interesar-type verb**: a verb where the thing liked is the subject and the person is an indirect object: *Me interesa el arte*
- **reflexive verb**: a verb with a pronoun referring back to the subject: *me lavo, te levantas, se viste*
- **reciprocal**: a plural reflexive meaning 'each other': *Nos llamamos todos los días* = We phone each other every day
- **hay que**: 'you have to, one must' in general, with no subject: *Hay que reciclar*
- **se puede / se necesita**: 'you can / you need' in general: *Se puede nadar en el lago*, *Se necesita dinero*
- **hace + weather**: *hace sol* (it's sunny), *hace frío* (it's cold), *hace calor* (it's hot), *hace viento* (it's windy)
:::

::: method
1. **Modal?** Conjugate the modal for the person, then add the infinitive unchanged: *mis padres quieren viajar*. For *tener que*, keep *que*.
2. **Opinion with gustar-type verb?** Choose the pronoun for the person (*me, te, le, nos, os, les*), then make the verb **singular** for one thing or an infinitive, **plural** for several things. Put the article on the noun: *me gustan **los** deportes*.
3. **Reflexive?** Pick the pronoun to match the subject (*yo → me, nosotros → nos*), place it before the conjugated verb, or on the end of an infinitive or command.
4. **General advice or description?** *Hay que* + infinitive, *se puede* + infinitive, *se necesita* + noun or infinitive.
5. **Weather?** *Hace* + noun for sun, heat, cold, wind; *llueve* for rain; *hay* for things that are present.
:::

| person | gustar, one thing | gustar, several things | levantarse |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | me gusta | me gustan | me levanto |
| tú | te gusta | te gustan | te levantas |
| él / ella | le gusta | le gustan | se levanta |
| nosotros | nos gusta | nos gustan | nos levantamos |
| vosotros | os gusta | os gustan | os levantáis |
| ellos | les gusta | les gustan | se levantan |

::: example gustar and encantar step by step
**My sister loves animals, but she doesn't like dogs, and I like swimming.**

1. *My sister loves* → *encantar*; the person is *mi hermana*, so **a mi hermana le encanta(n)**.
2. What is loved? *animals*, plural, with the article for a general statement: **le encantan los animales**.
3. *she doesn't like dogs* → *no* before the pronoun: **no le gustan los perros** (plural).
4. *I like swimming* → an infinitive is singular: **me gusta nadar**.

**A mi hermana le encantan los animales, pero no le gustan los perros, y a mí me gusta nadar.**

(The emphatic *a mí* is Higher; *y me gusta nadar* is fine at Foundation.)
:::

::: example A daily routine with reflexives
**We get up at seven. My brother has a wash, but I have to get dressed quickly.**

1. *We get up* → *levantarse*, *nosotros*: **nos levantamos a las siete**.
2. *My brother has a wash* → *lavarse*, *él*: **mi hermano se lava**.
3. *I have to get dressed* → modal + reflexive infinitive; the pronoun matches *yo* even on the infinitive: **tengo que vestirme** (or **me tengo que vestir**).
4. *quickly* → **rápidamente**.

**Nos levantamos a las siete. Mi hermano se lava, pero tengo que vestirme rápidamente.**
:::

::: example Describing a town with impersonal verbs
**In my town there is a big park where you can swim. You have to recycle more, and when it's sunny there are a lot of tourists.**

1. *there is* → **hay un parque grande**.
2. *where you can swim* → general 'you': **donde se puede nadar**.
3. *You have to recycle more* → general obligation: **hay que reciclar más**.
4. *when it's sunny* → **cuando hace sol**.
5. *there are a lot of tourists* → **hay muchos turistas**. *Hay* never changes for plural.

**En mi pueblo hay un parque grande donde se puede nadar. Hay que reciclar más, y cuando hace sol hay muchos turistas.**
:::

::: watch
- **gustar agrees with the thing, not the person**: *me gustan los deportes*, *le gusta el deporte*. *Me gusto* means 'I like myself'.
- **Named person + gustar needs *a* and *le/les***: *A mis amigos les encanta el fútbol*.
- **Article with liked nouns**: *Me gusta **la** música*, not *Me gusta música*.
- **tener que needs *que***: *Tengo que estudiar*.
- **Reflexive pronoun matches the subject**, also on infinitives: *Voy a lavarme*, *Vamos a levantarnos*.
- **hay never goes plural**: *Hay muchos coches*, not *Han muchos coches*.
- **Weather with hacer, not ser/estar**: *Hace calor*, not *Es caliente*.
:::
