## What it is

Adjectives describe nouns (*una casa **grande***) and adverbs describe verbs (*habla **bien***). In Spanish an adjective **agrees** with its noun in gender and number and usually goes **after** it; an adverb never changes. This lesson also covers the two verbs for 'to be' with adjectives (**ser** or **estar**) and **comparisons** (*más ... que, menos ... que, tan ... como, mejor, peor*).

**Where it turns up.** Every description task in Writing and Speaking, every photo card (*En la foto hay una familia; parecen contentos*), and every 'which is better?' question needs these structures. The translation into Spanish nearly always tests an agreement (*my sisters are tall* → *altas*) and a comparison. In Reading and Listening, *más* and *menos* reverse the answer if you miss them, and *estar aburrido* (bored) and *ser aburrido* (boring) mean different things.

**The core structures.**
- **Gender**: *-o → -a* (*alto, alta*). Adjectives in **-e, -z, -ista** and most consonants have one form for both genders (*grande, feliz, optimista, azul*).
- **Number**: *-o/-a → -os/-as*; *-e → -es*; *-z → -ces* (*feliz → felices*); *-ista → -istas*; consonant **+es**, sometimes dropping an accent (*común → comunes*, *internacional → internacionales*).
- **Nationality** in a consonant does take a feminine: *francés, francesa, franceses, francesas* (no accent after the first); *español, española, españoles, españolas*; *alemán, alemana*.
- **Position**: mostly **after** the noun. A small set goes **before** it and shortens before a masculine singular noun: *algún, ningún, primer, tercer, buen, mal*; *segundo* can go either side; *grande* becomes **gran** before any singular noun and then means 'great'. Some change meaning: *mi única amiga* (my only friend) / *una experiencia única* (a unique experience); *una gran ciudad* (a great city) / *una ciudad grande* (a big city).
- **ser vs estar**: *ser* for lasting characteristics, *estar* for states and moods. Some adjectives change meaning: **ser listo** (clever) / **estar listo** (ready); **ser aburrido** (boring) / **estar aburrido** (bored).
- **Comparisons**: *más ... que* (more ... than), *menos ... que* (less ... than), *tan ... como* (as ... as), *más de / menos de* + a number; irregular **mejor** (better) and **peor** (worse). They work with adverbs too: *corre más rápido que yo*.
- **Adverbs of time, manner and place** (*siempre, ahora, bien, mal, aquí, lejos*) usually go right after the verb or at the start of the sentence; *siempre* and *nunca* often go before the verb.

**Grade 9** answers get every agreement right, including with plural and mixed groups (*mis padres son simpáticos*), choose *ser/estar* correctly, and compare two things with a reason: *El campo es más tranquilo que la ciudad, pero es menos divertido.*

::: terms
- **adjective agreement**: the adjective matches its noun in gender and number: *las chicas altas*, *los libros interesantes*
- **invariable in gender**: adjectives in *-e, -z, -ista* or most consonants keep one form for m and f: *un chico feliz, una chica feliz*
- **shortened adjective**: a form used before a masculine singular noun: *buen día, mal tiempo, primer día, tercer piso*
- **gran**: 'great', the short form of *grande* before any singular noun: *una gran idea*, *un gran jugador*
- **único**: before the noun 'only' (*mi única amiga*), after the noun 'unique' (*una experiencia única*)
- **ser + adjective**: a lasting trait: *Mi padre es alto*, *La clase es aburrida* (boring)
- **estar + adjective**: a state or mood at the time: *Estoy cansado*, *Estoy aburrido* (bored), *¿Estás listo?* (ready)
- **comparative**: *más/menos* + adjective + *que*: *Madrid es más grande que Toledo*
- **tan ... como**: 'as ... as': *Mi hermana es tan alta como yo*
- **mejor / peor**: irregular 'better / worse'; plural *mejores / peores*: *Estas películas son mejores*
- **adverb**: a word describing a verb, which never agrees: *Ellas hablan bien*, *Viven lejos*
:::

::: method
1. **Find the noun** the adjective describes and note its gender and number.
2. **Make the adjective agree**: *-o* adjectives have four forms; *-e, -z, -ista* and consonant adjectives have two (singular, plural), except nationalities, which have four.
3. **Place it**: after the noun, unless it is one of the pre-noun set (*buen, mal, primer, tercer, gran, algún, ningún*), which shorten before a masculine singular noun.
4. **'to be' + adjective**: trait → *ser*; state, mood or result → *estar*. Check *listo* and *aburrido*.
5. **Comparing?** *más / menos* + adjective + *que*; *tan* + adjective + *como*; *mejor / peor* instead of *más bueno / más malo*. The adjective still agrees with the first noun.
6. **Adverb?** Leave it unchanged and put it after the verb (or before it for *siempre, nunca, ya*).
:::

::: example Agreement all the way through
**My cousins (f) are very happy and optimistic, but their French teacher is serious.**

1. *My cousins (f)* → **mis primas** (f pl).
2. *are very happy* → a mood, so *estar*: **están muy contentas** (f pl), or as a personality trait, *son muy alegres* (*-e*, one form for m/f).
3. *and optimistic* → *-ista*, plural only: **y optimistas**. Being optimistic is a trait, so the sentence reads best with *son*: **son muy alegres y optimistas**.
4. *their French teacher* → **su profesor de francés** (the language as a noun, no agreement) or **su profesor francés** (a teacher who is French: agreement *francés*).
5. *is serious* → trait: **es serio**.

**Mis primas son muy alegres y optimistas, pero su profesor de francés es serio.**
:::

::: example Position and shortened forms
**It's the first time that I visit a great city. The hotel is on the third floor and the weather is bad.**

1. *the first time* → *primero* before f sg keeps its full feminine form: **la primera vez**.
2. *a great city* → *grande* before a singular noun becomes *gran*, meaning 'great': **una gran ciudad**.
3. *on the third floor* → *tercero* before m sg: **en el tercer piso**.
4. *the weather is bad* → **hace mal tiempo** (*malo* shortens before *tiempo*).

**Es la primera vez que visito una gran ciudad. El hotel está en el tercer piso y hace mal tiempo.**
:::

::: example Comparing two places
**The countryside is quieter than the city, but it is less fun. The buses are worse and there are fewer than ten shops.**

1. *quieter than* → **más tranquilo que** (agrees with *el campo*).
2. *less fun* → **menos divertido**.
3. *The buses are worse* → irregular plural: **los autobuses son peores**.
4. *fewer than ten* → before a number, *de* not *que*: **menos de diez tiendas**.

**El campo es más tranquilo que la ciudad, pero es menos divertido. Los autobuses son peores y hay menos de diez tiendas.**
:::

::: example ser or estar
Choose and explain:

1. *La película **es** aburrida* → the film is boring (a characteristic).
2. *Mi hermano **está** aburrido* → my brother is bored (his state now).
3. *Mi amiga **es** muy lista* → my friend is very clever.
4. *¿**Estás** lista? Salimos ahora.* → Are you ready? We're leaving now.
5. *El café **está** frío* → the coffee is (has gone) cold: a state.
:::

::: watch
- **Agreement with plural and mixed groups**: *mis padres son simpáticos*; a mixed group takes the masculine plural.
- **Nationalities in a consonant still agree**: *una chica española*, *mis amigas francesas* (no accent in the plural).
- **mejor and peor, not *más bueno / más malo*** for 'better / worse'; plural *mejores, peores*.
- **más de + number**: *más de cien personas*.
- **ser vs estar**: *soy aburrido* says you are a boring person; *estoy aburrido* says you are bored.
- **Shortened forms only before a masculine singular noun** (except *gran*): *un buen día* but *una buena idea*, *los buenos amigos*.
- **Adverbs never agree**: *Ellas cantan bien*, not *buenas*.
:::
