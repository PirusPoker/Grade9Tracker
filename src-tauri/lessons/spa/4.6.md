## What it is

Paper 3 is **Reading**: at Higher tier it lasts **1 hour**, is worth **50 marks** and counts for **25%** of the GCSE. There is no dictionary. **Section A (40 marks)** gives you Spanish texts with questions **in English**, and you answer **in English** or without words (a letter, a tick, a name). The texts are the kinds of writing you meet in real life: public notices and adverts, brochure extracts, newspaper and magazine articles, emails and websites, for different audiences and in formal and informal styles. Some items are shared with the Foundation paper, and they are the easy marks at the start. Later texts are longer, use more Higher grammar, and hide the answer behind opinions, time frames and negatives.

The questions test the **overall message, key points, details and opinions**. The types you will meet are multiple choice; 'which person' (match a statement to one of several speakers); true, false or not mentioned; positive, negative or positive and negative; short answers in English; and **inference**, where the answer is implied rather than stated. You must also **work out the meaning of single words that are not on the vocabulary list** from their context. The specification allows this in reading only: a Higher text may gloss up to 2% of its words (with the English beside the text) and may contain up to 2% of unlisted cognates. Reading is also the only paper that uses **word building**: *-ito* (little), *-ísimo* (very), *-mente* (-ly), *-idad* (-ity) and *-able*. So *hermanito* is 'little brother' and *facilísimo* is 'very easy', even though neither is listed.

**Section B (10 marks)** is a **translation into English** of five sentences, at least **50 words** at Higher. The specification defines translation as an **appropriate and sufficient rendering of the meaning**. It does not want a word-for-word gloss, and it does not want a loose summary. The detailed scheme is published with each year's paper. It works element by element: the passage is split into short chunks, and each chunk earns its mark only if **all** of its meaning is there, in English that makes sense. The chunks are usually a subject and verb, a time phrase, or a noun with its adjective. Tense, person, negatives, number (singular or plural) and small words such as *ya no*, *todavía*, *casi*, *cada* and *demasiado* are exactly what decide whether a chunk scores.

**What gets a grade 9.** You drop nothing on the easy early texts. On the long texts you read the whole sentence before answering and check the time frame and any negatives. You infer confidently from evidence rather than guessing. In the translation every tense, person and small word is right, false friends are caught, and the English reads naturally ('I have been living here for two years', not 'I live here since two years').

::: terms
- **Inference**: working out something the text implies but does not state, such as a feeling from a description of behaviour
- **Cognate**: a word that looks and means the same in both languages, like 'hospital' or 'importante'
- **False friend**: a word that looks like an English word but means something else, like 'actual' (current)
- **Gloss**: an English meaning printed beside a text for an unlisted word; Higher texts may gloss up to 2% of words
- **Element**: one chunk of a translation that earns a mark only if all its meaning is conveyed
- **actual**: current, present-day (not 'actual'); 'la situación actual' means the current situation
- **éxito**: success (not 'exit'); 'tener éxito' means to be successful
- **sensible**: sensitive (not 'sensible'); 'es una persona muy sensible'
- **largo**: long (not 'large', which is grande); 'un viaje largo' means a long journey
- **asistir a**: to attend (not 'to assist'); 'asistir a clase' means to go to lessons
- **lectura**: reading (not 'lecture'); 'me encanta la lectura' means I love reading
- **colegio**: school (not 'college'); on the AQA list it means secondary school
- **-ísimo / -ísima**: very (reading only); 'buenísimo' means very good
- **-ito / -ita**: little, or a sign of affection (reading only); 'un perrito' means a little dog
:::

::: method
1. **Read the title, the English question and any gloss first.** They tell you the topic and what to look for. Note the question word and whether it wants one detail or two.
2. **Find the part of the text that matches the question.** Questions usually follow the order of the text.
3. **Read the whole sentence, and the one after it.** Look for *pero*, *sin embargo*, *en cambio*, *ya no*, *antes* and *ahora*, and for a change of time frame.
4. **Match the time frame.** If the question says 'now', the imperfect *jugaba* is the wrong sentence.
5. **For true, false or not mentioned**, find the evidence. Stated means true. Contradicted means false. Neither means not mentioned.
6. **For inference**, find two or three clues and ask what they add up to. Write the answer, not the clues, unless the question asks for evidence.
7. **For an unknown word**, check whether it is a cognate, a known word with *-ito*, *-ísimo*, *-mente* or *-idad*, or guessable from the words around it.
8. **Answer precisely in English.** 'Twice a week', not 'often'. 'His older sister', not 'his sister'. Do not add extra details: a wrong extra can cancel a right answer.
9. **Translation.** Translate sentence by sentence. Underline each verb and decide its tense and person first. Check every small word, then read your English aloud in your head and smooth it until it sounds natural.
:::

::: example Inference from clues
Text: *Mi abuela tiene ochenta años, pero todavía nada en el mar todas las mañanas, incluso en invierno. Dice que el agua fría es la mejor medicina.*

Question: What impression do you get of the grandmother? Give one piece of evidence. (2)

1. The clues are that she is eighty but *todavía* (still) swims, *todas las mañanas* (every morning), and *incluso en invierno* (even in winter).
2. Together they show someone **active, fit and determined** (or brave, or tough). That is the impression.
3. Evidence: she still swims in the sea every morning, even in winter.

'She likes swimming' is too weak for an inference question: it restates the text without saying what it shows about her.
:::

::: example Unknown words
Text: *Mi hermanito tiene cinco años. Ayer vimos una película buenísima y después mi tía nos llevó a una panadería, donde compramos pan recién hecho.*

1. *hermanito*: *hermano* plus *-ito*, so 'little brother'. The age confirms it.
2. *buenísima*: *buena* plus *-ísima*, so 'very good' or 'really good'.
3. *panadería* is not on the list and has no listed ending, but it contains *pan* (bread) and it is a place where they buy bread: a **bakery**. It might be glossed; if it is not, context is your tool.
4. *recién hecho*: *hecho* is the past participle of *hacer*, and the context is bread you buy, so 'freshly made' or 'fresh'. The mark would be for *pan*, not for this phrase, so a sensible guess is enough.
:::

::: example Translation into English, element by element
Passage (66 words):
1. *Mi barrio es bastante tranquilo, pero hay mucho ruido los fines de semana.*
2. *El mes pasado mis amigos y yo recogimos la basura de la playa.*
3. *Antes tirábamos todo a la basura, pero ahora reciclamos el plástico y el papel.*
4. *Lo peor es que mucha gente todavía usa el coche para ir al centro.*
5. *Si todos hacemos un pequeño esfuerzo, la ciudad será mucho más limpia.*

The ten elements, with accepted English:
1. My neighbourhood / area is quite / fairly quiet | but there is a lot of noise / it is very noisy at (the) weekends.
2. Last month my friends and I | picked up / collected (the) rubbish / litter from / on the beach.
3. Before / previously we used to throw / threw everything in the bin / away | but now we recycle plastic and paper.
4. The worst thing is that | a lot of / many people still use the car / drive to go (in)to town / the (town) centre.
5. If we all / everyone make(s) a small / little effort | the town / city will be much cleaner.

Accepted and rejected answers:
- 'My neighbourhood is tranquil' is accepted, but 'My neighbour is quiet' is not (*barrio*, not *vecino*).
- 'Last month my friends and I collect': the tense is wrong, so the element is lost.
- 'Before we threw everything' is accepted for the imperfect. Missing out *antes* loses the contrast and the mark.
- 'The worst is that many people use the car': *todavía* (still) is missing, so the element is lost.
- 'The city is much cleaner': the future *será* is missed.
:::

::: example True, false or not mentioned
Text: *En mi instituto está prohibido usar el móvil en clase. A mí me parece bien, aunque muchos de mis compañeros no están de acuerdo. El director dice que las notas han mejorado este año.*

- 'Phones are banned in lessons.' **True**: *prohibido usar el móvil en clase*. (*prohibido* is a cognate of 'prohibited'.)
- 'The writer disagrees with the rule.' **False**: *me parece bien* means she thinks it is fine.
- 'All the writer's classmates agree with the rule.' **False**: *muchos ... no están de acuerdo* says many disagree.
- 'Pupils now read more books.' **Not mentioned**: only better marks (*notas*) are mentioned.
:::

::: q
How long is the Higher reading paper, and how are its 50 marks split?
---
1 hour; Section A 40 marks of questions in English, Section B a 10-mark translation into English of at least 50 words.
:::

::: q
Which word-building endings can appear in reading texts on words that are not listed?
---
-ito/-ita (little), -ísimo/-ísima (very), -mente (-ly), -idad (-ity) and -able (-able).
:::

::: q
Translate: 'La situación actual es grave.'
---
The current situation is serious. ('actual' is a false friend.)
:::

::: q
Translate: 'Mi hermana tuvo mucho éxito en su examen.'
---
My sister was very successful in / did very well in her exam. ('éxito' means success, not exit.)
:::

::: q
Three false friends you may meet off the AQA list are embarazada, carpeta and librería. What do they mean?
---
Pregnant (not embarrassed), folder (not carpet), bookshop (not library, which is biblioteca). They are not on the AQA list, so they could only appear with a gloss, but they are classic howlers.
:::

::: q
What does 'ya no voy al gimnasio' mean, and why does 'ya no' matter in a translation?
---
I no longer go / I don't go to the gym any more; leaving out 'ya no' or writing 'I don't go yet' changes the meaning and loses the element.
:::

::: q
A question asks 'How does Pablo feel about his new school now?' The text says 'Al principio lo odiaba, pero ahora me encanta.' Answer it.
---
He loves it (now). The hatred was at the beginning, which is the distractor.
:::

::: q
Why should you avoid adding extra details to a short answer in English?
---
A wrong extra detail can contradict a correct answer and cancel the mark.
:::

::: watch
- **Time frames.** *jugaba* (used to play), *jugué* (played), *juego* (play), *voy a jugar* (am going to play) and *jugaría* (would play) each give a different answer.
- **Negatives and small words.** *ya no*, *todavía*, *nunca*, *tampoco*, *casi*, *solo*, *cada* and *demasiado* are where translation elements are lost.
- **Person.** *vamos* is 'we go', *van* is 'they go'. Check the verb ending before you write a subject.
- **False friends.** *actual*, *éxito*, *sensible*, *largo*, *asistir*, *lectura*, *colegio*; off the list, *embarazada*, *carpeta*, *librería*.
- **Not mentioned.** Do not import common sense: if the text does not say it, it is not mentioned.
- **Unnatural English.** 'I have 15 years' for *tengo 15 años* will not be credited as natural English; write 'I am 15'.
- **Leaving gaps.** Every blank is a certain zero. A sensible inference from context often scores.
:::

::: test
Translate into English: 'Llevo dos años viviendo en Madrid y me encanta.' (2)
---
I have been living in Madrid for two years (1); and I love it (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: 'Cuando era pequeño, iba a la playa con mis abuelos cada verano.' (3)
---
When I was little / young (1); I used to go / went to the beach with my grandparents (1); every summer (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: 'Mis padres acaban de comprar un coche eléctrico, pero todavía no lo usan.' (3)
---
My parents have just bought (1); an electric car (1); but they do not use it yet / still do not use it (1).
:::

::: test
Read: 'Rosa trabaja en un hotel de lunes a viernes. Antes trabajaba los fines de semana, pero ya no, porque prefiere pasar tiempo con su hijo.' (a) When does Rosa work now? (b) Why did she stop working at weekends? (2)
---
(a) Monday to Friday / weekdays (1); (b) she prefers to spend time with her son (1).
:::

::: test
Read: 'Javier no come carne ni pescado. Su hermana tampoco.' True, false or not mentioned? (a) Javier eats fish. (b) Javier's sister is a vegetarian. (c) Javier likes vegetables. (3)
---
(a) false (1); (b) true, since she eats neither meat nor fish either (1); (c) not mentioned (1).
:::

::: test
Work out the meaning of largísimo and lentamente and say how you did it: 'El viaje fue largísimo' and 'Habla lentamente'. (4)
---
very long (1): largo + -ísimo means very (1); slowly (1): lento + -mente means -ly (1).
:::

::: test
Read: 'Mi amigo Luis siempre llega tarde, nunca devuelve el dinero que le presto y habla mal de todos.' What impression do you get of Luis? Give two pieces of evidence. (3)
---
He is unreliable / not a good friend / selfish (1); any two of: he is always late (1); he never gives back money he borrows (1); he speaks badly of everyone (1).
:::

::: test
Translate into English: 'Lo mejor de mi instituto es que los profesores son muy sensibles.' (2)
---
The best thing about my school is that (1); the teachers are very sensitive / understanding (1). 'Sensible' as the English word scores 0 for the second element.
:::
