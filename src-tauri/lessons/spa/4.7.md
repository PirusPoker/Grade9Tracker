## What it is

Paper 4 is **Writing**: at Higher tier it lasts **1 hour 15 minutes**, is worth **50 marks** and counts for **25%** of the GCSE. All instructions and questions are in English and you write in Spanish, without a dictionary. There are three questions. Correct language from beyond the AQA list gets equal credit, but the list is what you should rely on.

**Question 1: translation into Spanish (10 marks).** You translate five English sentences, at least **50 words** in all. The mark is made of two grids. **Grid one (5 marks)** splits the passage into **15 elements**. Each element gets a tick if its meaning is communicated, even with minor inaccuracies. The ticks convert to a mark: 13 to 15 ticks is 5, 10 to 12 is 4, 7 to 9 is 3, 4 to 6 is 2, 1 to 3 is 1. **Grid two (5 marks)** judges your vocabulary and grammar across all five sentences as a whole. For 5, you show *very good knowledge of the vocabulary*, with *few if any wrong or missing items*, and your grammar is *highly accurate*, with *only minor errors*. A zero on grid one means a zero on grid two.

**Question 2: the 90-word task (15 marks).** You choose one of two tasks (2.1 or 2.2). Each has **three compulsory bullet points**, and you write about **90 words**. The question is shared with the Foundation paper. **Content (AO2, 10 marks)**: for 9 to 10, you cover *all three bullets*, communication is clear, and ideas are *regularly developed* with *a lot of relevant information*. **Language (AO3, 5 marks)**: for 5, you use a *good variety of vocabulary*, make *regular attempts at complex language*, and refer to *all three time frames* (past, present and future), mainly successfully. Errors are mainly minor. The bullets need not be covered equally.

**Question 3: the 150-word task (25 marks).** You choose one of two tasks (3.1 or 3.2). Each has **two compulsory bullet points**, and you write about **150 words**. **Content (AO2, 15 marks)**: for 13 to 15, you convey *a lot of information*, with *regular successful development of ideas, accounts and description*, and communication is clear with very few or no lapses. If you cover only one bullet, you can get **at most 12** for content. **Range of language (5 marks)**: for 5, you use a *very good variety of vocabulary and structures*, and *complex language is regularly attempted and often successful*. **Accuracy (5 marks)**: for 5, the writing is *usually accurate*, perhaps with the odd major error in a complex structure, and *verbs and tense formations are secure*. All of the writing is judged for language, even any part that wanders off the bullets.

**Word counts are approximate.** Examiners mark everything you write, and the descriptors matter more than the count. Writing 250 words for Question 3 simply spends time you need for checking. A sensible split of the 75 minutes: about **20** for Question 1, **20** for Question 2, **30** for Question 3, and **5** for checking verb endings, agreements and accents.

**What gets a grade 9.** Every element of the translation is exactly right. Every bullet is developed with opinions, reasons and detail across three time frames. And the Higher structures are used accurately rather than scattered at random: *cuando sea mayor*, *para que tenga*, *quieren que sea*, *acabo de*, *desde hace*, *llevo ... -ando*, *ya no*, *ni ... ni*, *lo mejor/lo peor*, *el que*, *lo que*, the imperfect for 'used to', the future and the conditional.

::: terms
- **Element**: one of the 15 chunks of the Question 1 translation; each earns a tick if its meaning comes across
- **Grid one / grid two**: in Question 1, grid one converts the ticks into a mark out of 5; grid two marks vocabulary and grammar out of 5
- **Compulsory bullet**: a point you must write something about; Question 2 has three, Question 3 has two
- **Development**: adding reasons, examples, details, consequences or comparisons to a basic point
- **Time frames**: past, present and future; Question 2 needs all three for the top language mark
- **Complex language**: subordinate clauses, pronouns, Higher tenses and structures, varied linking words
- **Major error**: a mistake that obscures meaning, such as the wrong tense or person of a verb
- **Minor error**: a mistake that leaves meaning clear, such as a missing accent or a wrong gender
- **cuando + subjunctive**: Higher only; 'cuando sea mayor' (when I am older), 'cuando tenga dinero' (when I have money)
- **querer que + subjunctive**: Higher only; 'mis padres quieren que sea médico' (want me to be a doctor)
- **para que + subjunctive**: Higher only; 'trabajo para que mi hermano tenga un móvil' (so that he has)
- **desde hace**: for (a time), with the present tense (Higher); 'vivo aquí desde hace tres años' means I have been living here for three years
- **acabar de + infinitive**: to have just done (Higher); 'acabo de comer' means I have just eaten
- **lo + adjective**: the ... thing (Higher); 'lo mejor', 'lo peor', 'lo bueno', 'lo malo'
:::

::: method
1. **Question 1.** Read all five sentences. Mark every verb's **tense and person**, every **time phrase**, and anything English writes differently: 'I am 15' (*tengo 15 años*), 'I like' (*me gusta*), 'I have been ... for' (*desde hace*), 'I have just' (*acabo de*), a personal *a* before a person object.
2. **Translate element by element.** Never leave a gap: a simpler word that conveys the meaning still gets the tick. Then check agreements and accents.
3. **Questions 2 and 3: choose the task.** Pick the one whose bullets you can develop with vocabulary you know. It does not have to be the one that looks more interesting.
4. **Plan in two minutes.** Under each bullet, note: the main point, an opinion with a reason, an example, and another time frame. In Question 2, mark where the past, the present and the future will go.
5. **Write each bullet as its own paragraph.** Start with a direct answer, then develop it: *porque*, *por ejemplo*, *sin embargo*, *además*, *así que*.
6. **Place two or three Higher structures deliberately.** Choose ones you are certain of, and use each only once. A subjunctive you get wrong is worse than a conditional you get right.
7. **Check in three passes.** First the verbs (tense, person, stem changes, irregular preterites). Then agreement (gender and number of adjectives and articles). Then accents (*fútbol*, *también*, *más*, *está*, *fácil*, and the preterite endings *-é*, *-ó*).
:::

::: example Translation into Spanish, element by element
1. My best friend is called Ana | and she is | very funny.
*Mi mejor amiga se llama Ana y es muy divertida.* The friend is female, so *amiga* and *divertida* agree. *Es* is right, because being funny is a trait (*ser*).

2. Yesterday we went | to the market | and bought some fruit.
*Ayer fuimos al mercado y compramos fruta.* The preterite is needed for a finished action on a known day. *a + el* becomes *al*. 'Some' is usually left out in Spanish.

3. When I was younger, | I used to watch television | every morning.
*Cuando era más joven, veía la televisión todas las mañanas.* 'Used to' means the imperfect. *Era* is the listed imperfect of *ser*. 'Every morning' is *todas las mañanas* (feminine plural).

4. Next year | my brother will study | at a university in Spain.
*El año próximo mi hermano estudiará en una universidad de España.* The inflectional future needs the accent on *-á*. *El próximo año* is equally good.

5. My parents want me to be a doctor, | but I have been playing the guitar | for five years.
*Mis padres quieren que sea médico, pero toco la guitarra desde hace cinco años.* 'Want me to be' is *querer que* + the subjunctive *sea* (Higher). 'Have been playing ... for' is the present tense + *desde hace*. Writing *he tocado* loses accuracy but may still get the element's tick.

Fifteen elements, all conveyed: grid one 5. With the grammar highly accurate, grid two 5.
:::

::: example A 90-word answer (Question 2)
Task: *You are writing to your Spanish friend about your school. Write approximately 90 words in Spanish. You must write something about each bullet point.* Mention: your favourite subject; a school trip you went on; your plans for next year.

*¡Hola, Pablo! Mi asignatura favorita es el dibujo porque me encanta pintar y la profesora es muy paciente. Sin embargo, no me gustan nada las matemáticas, ya que son difíciles.*

*El mes pasado fuimos de excursión a Londres con el instituto. Visitamos un museo de arte y comimos en un parque enorme. ¡Lo pasamos genial, aunque llovió un poco!*

*El año próximo voy a estudiar arte e historia en otro instituto. Además, me gustaría trabajar los sábados en un café para ganar dinero. ¿Y tú? ¿Qué vas a hacer?*

(About 90 words.) All three bullets are developed. Present, past and future are all correct: *es*, *fuimos*, *voy a estudiar*, *me gustaría*. It varies its linking words (*porque*, *sin embargo*, *ya que*, *aunque*, *además*) and gets *e* before *historia* right. That is content 10 and language 5.
:::

::: example A 150-word answer (Question 3)
Task: *You are writing an article about celebrations for a Spanish website. Write approximately 150 words in Spanish. You must write something about both bullet points.* Mention: a celebration you enjoyed recently; how you would like to celebrate a special occasion in the future.

*Hace dos semanas celebré mi cumpleaños con mi familia en un restaurante mexicano. Lo mejor fue que mis primos vinieron desde muy lejos para verme, algo que no esperaba. Comimos muchísimo, bailamos toda la noche y mi abuela me regaló un libro precioso. Fue uno de los mejores días de mi vida, aunque al día siguiente estaba muy cansada.*

*Antes celebraba mis cumpleaños en casa con una fiesta pequeña, pero ya no, porque ahora prefiero salir con mis amigos. Este año, por ejemplo, fuimos al cine por la tarde.*

*En el futuro, me gustaría celebrar mis dieciocho años de una manera diferente. Quiero ir a Valencia durante las Fallas, porque he visto muchas fotos y parece una fiesta increíble. Si tengo bastante dinero, alquilaremos un piso y veremos los fuegos artificiales. Mis padres dicen que es mejor esperar, pero voy a ahorrar desde ahora para que mi sueño sea realidad.*

(About 150 words.) Both bullets are developed as accounts, with a past narrative and a detailed future plan. The range includes the preterite, the imperfect, *ya no*, the present perfect, *lo mejor*, a superlative, a *si* clause with the future, and a subjunctive after *para que*. Verbs are secure, so both language grids would get 5 and content 15.
:::

::: example Planning Question 3 in two minutes
Task 3.2: *You are writing a blog about the environment where you live.* Mention: what you and your family do to protect the environment; how your area could be improved in the future.

Plan:
- **Bullet 1.** Present: *reciclamos plástico y papel; voy en bici al instituto*. Past: *el mes pasado recogimos basura en la playa*. Opinion: *lo peor es que mucha gente todavía usa el coche*. Higher: *ya no usamos bolsas de plástico*.
- **Bullet 2.** Future: *habrá más parques; tendremos autobuses eléctricos*. Conditional: *me gustaría que ...* (avoid: the verb after it would be outside the listed subjunctive), so use *sería mejor tener más zonas verdes* instead. Subjunctive: *cuando sea mayor, trabajaré para proteger el medio ambiente*.
- **Link words** to vary: *además, sin embargo, por eso, por un lado ... por otro lado*.

Notice the edit: a planned structure you are not sure of is swapped for one you are sure of.
:::

::: q
Why is 'Me gustaría que mi hermano pueda venir' a risky choice at GCSE?
---
The listed subjunctive covers only singular forms of hacer, ser, ir, venir and tener after cuando, que and para que; a clause after 'me gustaría que' needs a different form (the imperfect subjunctive), so the sentence is wrong as written. Stick to structures on the list.
:::

::: watch
- **Tense and person in Question 1.** 'We went' is *fuimos*, not *fui* or *fueron*. One wrong ending can cost the element.
- **English structures copied into Spanish.** *Soy 15* for 'I am 15', *yo gusto* for 'I like', *he estado jugando por* for 'have been playing for'.
- **Missing personal a.** *Visito a mis abuelos*, *veo a mi amigo*.
- **ser and estar.** A trait takes *ser* (*es simpática*); a state or location takes *estar* (*está cansada*, *está en Madrid*).
- **Agreement.** *las ciudades limpias*, *muchas personas*, *todos los días*, *todas las semanas*.
- **Accents on preterites and futures.** *comí*, *visitó*, *estudiaré*, *será*. Without them the verb is a different person or tense.
- **Skipping a bullet.** It caps your content mark. Cover every bullet, even briefly.
- **Unsafe structures.** A subjunctive outside the listed verbs and triggers, or an invented idiom, loses accuracy for no gain.
:::

::: test
Write a sentence in Spanish about your free time that uses 'ya no' and one about the future that uses 'cuando' + subjunctive. (4)
---
Correct use of ya no with a present verb (1) and sensible meaning (1), e.g. Ya no juego al fútbol porque prefiero nadar; cuando + sea / tenga / vaya / haga / venga (1) with a correct future or conditional main clause (1), e.g. Cuando tenga dinero, viajaré a México.
:::

::: test
A student writes 70 excellent words on the first two bullets of Question 2 and nothing on the third. Explain what this does to their mark. (2)
---
Content drops to the band where at least two bullets are covered, at most 5 to 6 out of 10 (1); the language mark can still be awarded on what is written, but the missing time frame may also limit it (1).
:::

::: test
Write two developed sentences in Spanish for the bullet 'what you did last summer', using a preterite, an imperfect and an opinion. (4)
---
Correct preterite (1); correct imperfect (1); opinion (1); reason or further detail (1). Example: El verano pasado fui a la costa con mis amigos. Hacía mucho calor, así que nadábamos todos los días. Fue genial porque me encanta el mar.
:::
