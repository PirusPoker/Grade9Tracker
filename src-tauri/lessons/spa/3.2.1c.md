## What it is

The present tense is the tense you use most, in every paper. It says what you **do** (*Juego al fútbol* = I play football) and what you **are doing** right now (*¿Qué haces? — Estudio* = What are you doing? — I'm studying). One Spanish form covers both English forms, so do not go looking for an extra verb every time you see '-ing'.

**Where it turns up.** Reading and Listening texts are built on it; the verb ending is how you tell *juego* (I play) from *juegan* (they play), and the questions often hinge on who does what. In the translation into Spanish, at least one sentence will need a present-tense verb in a person other than *yo*, usually *nosotros* or *ellos*. In Speaking, the photo card and conversation start in the present: *En la foto hay ...*, *Normalmente juego ...*

**The core structures.**
- **Regular verbs**: remove *-ar, -er, -ir* and add the endings for all six persons.
- **Five clusters of stem-changers**, each following an 'anchor' verb: **encontrar** (o → ue: *encuentro*), **pensar** (e → ie: *pienso*), **pedir** (e → i: *pido*), **conocer** (c → zc in the *yo* form only: *conozco*) and **poner** (adds -g in the *yo* form only: *pongo*). The first three change in four persons and **stay regular in *nosotros* and *vosotros***.
- **Irregular verbs** listed in full: **ser, estar, ir, tener, hacer**, plus listed forms such as *doy* (dar), *veo* (ver) and *sé* (saber).
- **tener + noun** where English uses 'be + adjective': *tengo frío* (I'm cold), *tengo calor, tengo hambre, tengo sed, tengo miedo, tengo quince años*.

At Foundation, small spelling changes that keep the sound (*coger → cojo*) and dropping the *u* in *sigo* (seguir) are not individually credited, but they are still correct Spanish and you should write them properly. At Higher, the *g → j* change is credit-bearing (see the Higher verbs lesson).

**Grade 9** answers get the *nosotros* and *ellos* forms right under pressure (*podemos* not *puedemos*; *prefieren* not *preferen*), use *tener* expressions instead of translating 'be' word for word, and switch persons confidently when describing other people.

::: terms
- **infinitive**: the dictionary form ending in *-ar, -er* or *-ir*: *hablar* (to speak), *comer* (to eat), *vivir* (to live)
- **stem**: the infinitive minus its ending, to which endings are added: *habl-*, *com-*, *viv-*
- **stem-changing verb**: a verb whose stressed stem vowel changes in four persons: *poder → puedo, puedes, puede, pueden*
- **anchor verb**: the model verb for a cluster: *encontrar* (ue), *pensar* (ie), *pedir* (i), *conocer* (zc), *poner* (-go)
- **boot verb**: another name for o→ue, e→ie and e→i verbs, because the changed persons form a boot shape in the table
- **-go verb**: a verb with *-go* in the *yo* form only: *pongo, salgo, traigo, hago, tengo, vengo*
- **irregular verb**: a verb that does not follow the patterns: *soy, estoy, voy, tengo, hago*
- **tener expression**: *tener* + noun for English 'be' + adjective: *tengo sed* = I'm thirsty, *tiene miedo* = he's scared
- **ser**: 'to be' for what something is (identity, nationality, job, character): *Soy inglés, es simpática*
- **estar**: 'to be' for where something is and how it is at the moment: *Estoy en casa, está cansado*
:::

::: method
1. **Who is doing it?** Pick the person: *yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros, vosotros, ellos/ellas/ustedes*.
2. **Is the verb irregular?** *ser, estar, ir, tener, hacer* have their own forms; learn them as a list.
3. **Is it in a cluster?** If it patterns like *encontrar, pensar* or *pedir*, change the stem vowel for *yo, tú, él, ellos*, but **not** for *nosotros, vosotros*. If it patterns like *conocer* or *poner*, only the *yo* form is special.
4. **Take off the -ar/-er/-ir** and add the ending for your person.
5. **English 'be + adjective'** for cold, hot, hungry, thirsty, scared, age? Use *tener*.
6. **Check the ending matches the subject**, especially after a noun subject: *Mis padres trabajan*, *La gente piensa*.

Common members of each cluster: like *encontrar*: *poder, volver, dormir, costar, recordar, morir, llover* (and *jugar*, u → ue: *juego*). Like *pensar*: *querer, preferir, empezar, entender, cerrar, perder, despertar, sentir*. Like *pedir*: *servir, repetir, seguir (sigo), vestir*. Like *conocer*: *parecer (parezco), conducir (conduzco), producir*. Like *poner*: *salir (salgo), traer (traigo)*; *hacer (hago)*, *tener (tengo)* and *venir (vengo)* also have -go.

::: example Forming a stem-changer in every person
**poder** (to be able) belongs to the *encontrar* cluster.

1. Stem *pod-*; the stressed *o* becomes *ue* in four persons.
2. *yo* **puedo**, *tú* **puedes**, *él* **puede**, *ellos* **pueden**: the stress falls on the stem, so it changes.
3. *nosotros* **podemos**, *vosotros* **podéis**: the stress falls on the ending, so the stem stays *pod-*.

Test yourself with **preferir** (*pensar* cluster): *prefiero, prefieres, prefiere, preferimos, preferís, prefieren*. And **servir** (*pedir* cluster): *sirvo, sirves, sirve, servimos, servís, sirven*.
:::

::: example Translation: choosing the verb forms
**We always sleep a lot, but my parents start work early and they are often tired.**

1. *We sleep* → *dormir* is in the *encontrar* cluster, but *nosotros* keeps the stem: **dormimos**.
2. *a lot* → **mucho**; *always* → **siempre**, before the verb: *Siempre dormimos mucho*.
3. *my parents start* → *empezar* (*pensar* cluster), *ellos* form changes: **empiezan**. Work → *a trabajar* after *empezar* + infinitive: *empiezan a trabajar*.
4. *early* → **temprano**.
5. *they are tired* → a state at the moment, so *estar*: **están cansados** (plural agreement).

**Siempre dormimos mucho, pero mis padres empiezan a trabajar temprano y muchas veces están cansados.**
:::

::: example tener where English says 'be'
**I am fifteen, my brother is always hungry and we are cold in winter.**

1. *I am fifteen* → age uses *tener*: **tengo quince años**. Never *soy quince*.
2. *is always hungry* → **siempre tiene hambre**. *Hambre* is a noun, so no agreement with the person.
3. *we are cold* → **tenemos frío**. *Estamos fríos* would mean our bodies are cold to the touch.
4. *in winter* → **en invierno**.

**Tengo quince años, mi hermano siempre tiene hambre y en invierno tenemos frío.**
:::

---
*Estoy en casa*: location is always *estar*.

::: watch
- **Nosotros and vosotros keep the stem**: *podemos, queremos, pedimos, dormimos*. Changing them is the most common verb error in the translation.
- **-zco and -go only in the *yo* form**: *conozco* but *conoces*; *salgo* but *sales*.
- **Match the ending to a noun subject**: *La gente piensa* (singular), *Mis amigos piensan* (plural).
- **Age, hunger, thirst, fear, heat and cold use *tener***: *tengo hambre*, not *soy hambriento* or *estoy hambre*.
- **Don't translate every '-ing' with *estar***: *Mañana juego al fútbol* (I'm playing football tomorrow) is just the present.
- **Irregulars cost the most when wrong**: *voy, soy, estoy, tengo, hago* must be automatic.
:::
