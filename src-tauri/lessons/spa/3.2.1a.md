## What it is

Every Spanish noun is **masculine or feminine**, and every word that goes with it (the article, *este*, *mi*, *todo*, the adjective) has to **agree** in gender and number. This is the grammar you use in every single sentence, so it is where careless marks leak away: *la problema*, *mi amigos*, *todo los días*. In the 10-mark translation into Spanish on the Writing paper, each of those is an accuracy error; in Listening and Reading, the article and the ending are often the only clue that tells you whether it was *la hermana* or *los hermanos*.

**Gender and feminine forms.** Most nouns in **-o** are masculine and most in **-a** are feminine. For people: **-o → -a** (*el vecino, la vecina*); **-or adds -a** (*el profesor, la profesora*); **-ante, -ente and -ista do not change** (*el estudiante, la estudiante; el artista, la artista*) — only the article shows the gender. Job titles are in flux, and AQA accepts both *la jefe* and *la jefa*.

**Plurals.** Vowel **+s** (*libro → libros*). Consonant **+es** (*ciudad → ciudades*). **-z → -ces** (*vez → veces*, *actriz → actrices*). **-ión → -iones, dropping the accent** (*canción → canciones*, *habitación → habitaciones*); the same happens with *inglés → ingleses*. A few nouns gain an accent instead, to keep the stress where it was: *joven → jóvenes*, *examen → exámenes*.

**Articles and determiners.** *el, la, los, las* (the) and *un, una, unos, unas* (a, some); *de + el = del*, *a + el = al*. Spanish uses *the* where English does not: in **general statements** (*La educación es importante*, *Me gusta el fútbol*) and with days (*el lunes* = on Monday). It **drops** *a* after *ser* with a job (*Mi madre es médica*) and never says *un otro* (*otro libro* = another book). The **determiners** on the list are *este/ese* (this, that), *cada* (never changes), *mismo, otro, todo*, *alguno/ninguno* (shortened to *algún/ningún* before a masculine singular noun) and the possessives *mi, tu, su, nuestro, vuestro*. Two more patterns: an **infinitive works as a noun** (*Nadar es sano* = Swimming is healthy), and a **masculine adjective of nationality** can be a noun (*el francés* = French, the language; *los españoles* = Spanish people; *la inglesa* = the English woman).

**Grade 9** here is not about rare words: it is about **zero agreement errors** under pressure. Top answers use *todos los días, cada semana, la misma asignatura, otra vez* and *nuestros profesores* naturally and correctly, and translate *swimming is ...* with an infinitive, not an *-ando* form.

::: terms
- **gender**: every noun is masculine or feminine; *el libro* (m), *la casa* (f), and articles and adjectives must match it
- **agreement**: changing a determiner or adjective to match its noun's gender and number, as in *estas ciudades pequeñas*
- **definite article**: the word for 'the': *el, la, los, las*, used in Spanish for general statements, as in *la salud es importante*
- **indefinite article**: 'a' or 'some': *un, una, unos, unas*; left out after *ser* + a job: *es profesora*
- **contraction**: *de + el* becomes *del* and *a + el* becomes *al*: *voy al parque*, *la casa del vecino*
- **demonstrative adjective**: 'this, these' (*este, esta, estos, estas*) or 'that, those' (*ese, esa, esos, esas*)
- **possessive adjective**: *mi, tu, su, nuestro, vuestro*; it agrees with the thing owned, not the owner: *mis libros*, *nuestra casa*
- **indefinite adjective**: *cada, mismo, otro, todo, alguno, ninguno*: *cada día*, *la misma clase*, *todas las semanas*
- **algún / ningún**: the short forms of *alguno / ninguno* before a masculine singular noun: *algún día*, *ningún problema*
- **infinitive as a noun**: the infinitive translates English '-ing' used as a subject: *Leer es importante* = Reading is important
- **nominalised adjective**: an adjective used as a noun: *el español* (Spanish, the language), *los ingleses* (English people)
:::

::: method
1. **Find the noun first** and decide its gender: *-o* usually m, *-a* usually f; *-ción, -dad* are f; learn the exceptions (*el día, el problema, el idioma, el programa, el tema, el clima, la mano, la foto*).
2. **Decide singular or plural**, and form the plural: vowel +s; consonant +es; *-z → -ces*; *-ión → -iones* (no accent).
3. **Choose the article.** English 'the' → *el/la/los/las*. General statement with no 'the' in English (*Sport is healthy*, *I like music*)? Spanish still needs the article.
4. **Add any determiner**, making it agree: *este → esta/estos/estas*, *todo → toda/todos/todas*, *mi → mis*. *Cada* never changes. *Alguno/ninguno* shrink to *algún/ningún* before a masculine singular noun.
5. **Check the traps**: no *un otro*, no article after *ser* + job, *de el → del*, *a el → al*.
6. **Read the phrase back** and check every word matches: *todas nuestras amigas*.
:::

::: example Building plurals and feminines
Make each noun plural, then give the feminine where it exists.

| Singular | Rule | Plural | Feminine |
|---|---|---|---|
| el vecino | vowel +s; -o → -a | los vecinos | la vecina |
| el profesor | consonant +es; -or +a | los profesores | la profesora |
| el estudiante | vowel +s; -ante does not change | los estudiantes | la estudiante |
| el artista | vowel +s; -ista does not change | los artistas | la artista |
| la canción | -ión → -iones, accent goes | las canciones | — |
| la actriz | -z → -ces | las actrices | (el actor) |
| el joven | consonant +es, accent added to keep the stress | los jóvenes | la joven |

Notice *actriz* is itself the feminine of *actor*: the vocabulary list gives such pairs as separate words, like *hijo / hija*.
:::

::: example Translation: agreement all the way through
**Every day my sisters read the same books in the library.**

1. *Every day* → *todos los días* (masculine plural: *día* is masculine despite the -a). *Cada día* is also correct.
2. *my sisters* → plural owner-thing, so *mis hermanas*. The possessive agrees with *hermanas*, not with me.
3. *read* → they, present: *leen*.
4. *the same books* → *los mismos libros*: *mismo* goes before the noun and agrees (m pl).
5. *in the library* → *en la biblioteca*.

**Todos los días mis hermanas leen los mismos libros en la biblioteca.**
:::

::: example Translation: where Spanish uses the article and English does not
**Swimming is healthy. I like sport, but education is more important.**

1. *Swimming* as a subject → the infinitive: *Nadar es sano* (never *Nadando es sano*).
2. *I like sport* → a general statement, so *el deporte*: *Me gusta el deporte*.
3. *education* → *la educación*, again general: *la educación es más importante*.

**Nadar es sano. Me gusta el deporte, pero la educación es más importante.**
:::

::: example Determiners with algún, ningún, cada and otro
**Some day I want to visit another country, but I have no problem with my town.**

1. *Some day* → *día* is m sg, so *alguno* shortens: *algún día*.
2. *another country* → *otro país*; no *un* before *otro*.
3. *no problem* → *problema* is m sg, so *ningún problema*, with *no* before the verb: *no tengo ningún problema*.
4. *my town* → *mi pueblo* or *mi ciudad*.

**Algún día quiero visitar otro país, pero no tengo ningún problema con mi pueblo.**
:::

::: q
Give the plural of: *la canción, la vez, la ciudad, el examen*.
---
*las canciones* (accent dropped), *las veces* (-z → -ces), *las ciudades*, *los exámenes* (accent added to keep the stress).
:::

Fill the gaps with the right form of *este*: ___ semana, ___ libros, ___ casas, ___ programa.
---
*esta semana, estos libros, estas casas, este programa* (*programa* is masculine).

::: q
Translate into Spanish: *Each student has another idea.*
---
*Cada estudiante tiene otra idea.* (*cada* never changes; no *una* before *otra*.)
:::

::: q
Spot and correct the error: *Mi hermano es un profesor y trabaja en la mismo instituto.*
---
Two errors: drop *un* after *ser* + job (*es profesor*), and *mismo* must agree with *instituto* (m): *el mismo instituto*.
:::

::: q
Translate into English: *Todos los sábados vamos al mercado con nuestros vecinos.*
---
Every Saturday we go to the market with our neighbours.
:::

::: q
Why is it *el agua* but *el agua fría*?
---
*Agua* is feminine, but a feminine singular noun starting with a stressed *a-* takes *el* to avoid two *a* sounds together; any adjective still agrees in the feminine: *fría*.
:::

::: q
Translate into Spanish: *Reading is fun.*
---
*Leer es divertido.* The infinitive is the Spanish for the English '-ing' subject.
:::

::: q
Complete with *alguno* or *ninguno* in the right form: *¿Tienes ___ libro de español? No, no tengo ___.*
---
*¿Tienes algún libro de español? No, no tengo ninguno.* (*algún* before the m sg noun; *ninguno* in full when it stands alone.)
:::

::: watch
- **Possessives agree with the thing owned**: *mis padres*, *nuestra profesora*; *su* can mean his, her, their or your (formal), so check the English carefully.
- **General statements need the article**: *Me gusta la música*, *Los jóvenes usan el móvil*. Leaving it out is the commonest article error in translation.
- **No *un otro*** and no article after *ser* + job: *es enfermera*.
- **Masculine nouns in -a** (*el día, el problema, el idioma, el programa, el tema, el clima*) and feminines in -o (*la mano, la foto*) catch people every year.
- **-ión plurals lose the accent**: *canciones*, *estaciones*. Writing *canciónes* is an accuracy error.
- **Todo takes the article after it**: *todo el día, toda la semana, todos los días*.
:::
