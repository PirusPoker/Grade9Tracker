## What it is

Pronouns are the small words that stand in for a noun: *it, him, me, this, who*. Spanish handles them differently from English in three ways that cost marks. It **drops the subject pronoun** (*Juego* = I play, because the ending already says 'I'). It puts object pronouns **before** the verb (*Lo compro* = I buy it). And it has separate words for 'it' as a direct object (*lo, la*) and 'to him/her' (*le*). This lesson also covers the two things that live alongside pronouns: **negatives** (*nada, nadie, nunca, ninguno*) and **questions** (*¿quién?, ¿cuál?, ¿cuánto?*).

**Where it turns up.** Object pronouns and negatives appear in almost every Reading and Listening text, and a missed *no* or *nunca* flips the answer. In the translation into Spanish, AQA regularly tests *it/them* and *never/nobody*. In Speaking, the role-play asks you to **ask a question**, so you need question words with their accents.

**The core structures (Foundation).**
- Subject pronouns (*yo, tú, él, ella, usted, nosotros/as, vosotros/as, ellos, ellas, ustedes*) are left out unless you need **contrast or emphasis**: *Yo prefiero el cine, pero ella prefiere el teatro.*
- Direct object **me, te, lo, la, los, las** and indirect object **me, te, le, les** go **before a conjugated verb**, **either before the first verb or on the end of the infinitive** in a two-verb structure, and **on the end of a positive command**: *lo miro — lo puedo mirar / puedo mirarlo — ¡Míralo!*; *te doy — te quiero dar / quiero darte — ¡Dame!*
- Singular reflexive **me, te, se** follow the same rules: *se lava — se debe lavar / debe lavarse — ¡Lávate!* (Plural reflexives are in the lesson on reflexive verbs; *nos* and *os* as objects are Higher.)
- **que** joins a clause about the subject: *el chico que vive aquí*. **esto** and **eso** mean 'this' and 'that' when there is no noun: *¿Qué es esto?*
- **alguno / ninguno** stand alone as pronouns: *¿Tienes alguno? No tengo ninguno.* **algo / nada**, **alguien / nadie**.
- Negatives: **no** goes before the verb (and before any object pronoun); *nada, nunca, nadie, ninguno* go after the verb with *no* in front, or before the verb without *no*: *No como carne nunca = Nunca como carne.*
- Questions: rising intonation on a statement (*¿Vas al cine?*), or a question word then **verb then subject** (*¿Dónde está Daniel?*, *¿Qué hace la chica?*). Question words carry an accent.

**Grade 9** answers use pronouns to avoid repeating nouns (*Compré una camiseta, pero no la llevo nunca*), place them correctly with two verbs, and use negatives with complete accuracy. AQA only tests **one pronoun at a time**: you do not need *me lo*.

::: terms
- **subject pronoun**: the person doing the action (*yo, tú, él ...*); normally left out because the verb ending shows it: *Hablo* = I speak
- **direct object pronoun**: 'me, you, it, him, her, them' receiving the action: *me, te, lo, la, los, las*, as in *La veo* = I see her
- **indirect object pronoun**: 'to/for me, you, him, her, them': *me, te, le, les*, as in *Le doy el libro* = I give him the book
- **reflexive pronoun**: 'myself, yourself, himself': *me, te, se*, as in *Me lavo* = I wash (myself)
- **usted / ustedes**: the formal 'you'; they take the él and ellos verb endings: *¿Usted habla inglés?*
- **relative pronoun que**: 'who, which, that' joining two clauses: *la chica que canta* = the girl who sings
- **neuter pronoun**: *esto* (this) and *eso* (that) for an idea or unknown thing, never with a noun: *Eso es verdad*
- **interrogative**: a question word with an accent: *¿quién? ¿cuál? ¿cuánto? ¿dónde? ¿cuándo? ¿cómo? ¿qué? ¿por qué?*
- **double negative**: Spanish keeps *no* before the verb with *nada, nadie, nunca, ninguno* after it: *No veo a nadie*
- **alguien / nadie**: someone / no one; *algo / nada*: something / nothing; *alguno / ninguno*: some one / none of a group
:::

::: method
1. **Drop the subject pronoun** unless you are contrasting two people or stressing one: *Juego al fútbol*, but *Yo juego y tú miras*.
2. **Replace the noun** with the right pronoun. Is it the thing/person receiving the action (direct: *lo, la, los, las*) or the person something is given/said/sent **to** (indirect: *le, les*)?
3. **Count the verbs.** One conjugated verb: pronoun **before** it (*lo compro*). Conjugated verb + infinitive: before the first verb **or** stuck on the infinitive (*lo voy a comprar / voy a comprarlo*). Positive command: stuck on the end, with an accent if needed (*¡Cómpralo!*).
4. **Add the negative**: *no* goes before the pronoun and verb (*No lo compro*); *nada, nunca, nadie, ninguno* go after, or before the verb without *no*.
5. **For a question**, put the question word first, then the verb, then any subject: *¿Cuándo llega tu hermano?* Check the accent on the question word and the opening *¿*.
:::

::: example Placing an object pronoun, step by step
Start from **Compro el libro** (I buy the book). *El libro* is masculine singular and receives the action, so it becomes **lo**.

1. One verb: **Lo compro.** = I buy it.
2. Two verbs: **Lo quiero comprar.** or **Quiero comprarlo.** = I want to buy it. Both are correct.
3. Going to: **Lo voy a comprar.** or **Voy a comprarlo.**
4. Command: **¡Cómpralo!** = Buy it! The stress stays on *com-*, so it now needs an accent.
5. Negative: **No lo compro.** = I don't buy it. *No* goes in front of the pronoun, never between the pronoun and the verb.

Same pattern with a feminine noun: *Veo la película → La veo → La quiero ver / Quiero verla → ¡Mírala!*
:::

::: example Indirect objects: to whom?
**My grandmother gives me money, and I send her photos.**

1. *gives me* → the money is the direct object; *me* is who it goes **to**, so indirect: *me da dinero*.
2. *I send her photos* → the photos are what is sent; *her* is the person they go to, so **le**: *le mando fotos*. Not *la*: *la* would mean she herself is being sent.
3. The subject 'I' is clear from *mando*, so no *yo* (unless you want contrast: *y yo le mando fotos* is fine for emphasis).

**Mi abuela me da dinero y le mando fotos.**

With two verbs: *Quiero mandarle una foto* or *Le quiero mandar una foto*. As a command: *¡Mándale una foto!*
:::

::: example Negatives and questions in a role-play
Task: ask your friend if anyone is coming, say you never go to the cinema, and ask which film they prefer.

1. *Is anyone coming?* → *alguien* (someone) in a question: **¿Viene alguien?** Verb before the subject.
2. *No, nobody is coming.* → **No, no viene nadie.** or **No, nadie viene.** Both are correct; the first keeps *no* before the verb because *nadie* follows it.
3. *I never go to the cinema.* → **No voy nunca al cine.** or **Nunca voy al cine.**
4. *Which film do you prefer?* → choosing from a set, so **¿Cuál?**, but before a noun Spanish uses **qué**: **¿Qué película prefieres?** On its own: **¿Cuál prefieres?**
5. *How many tickets?* → *cuánto* agrees: **¿Cuántos billetes?** (*billete* is m pl).
:::

::: example Que, esto and eso
**The boy who lives next door says that this is not true.**

1. *who lives* → subject relative clause: *el chico que vive al lado*. Never *quien* here and never leave *que* out, although English can drop 'who/that'.
2. *says that* → *dice que*. 'That' joining two clauses is always *que* in Spanish, even when English leaves it out (*He says it's true* = *Dice que es verdad*).
3. *this* with no noun → neuter **esto**: *esto no es verdad*.

**El chico que vive al lado dice que esto no es verdad.**
:::

---

---
*¡Llama a tu madre!* → *¡Llámala!* (accent needed once *la* is added.)

::: q
Why is *Yo tengo un perro. Yo vivo en Madrid. Yo tengo quince años.* poor Spanish?
---
The subject pronoun is repeated for no reason; the endings already show 'I'. Use *yo* only for contrast or emphasis.
:::

::: watch
- **No before the pronoun**: *No lo sé*, *No te veo*. *Lo no sé* is always wrong.
- **Keep *no* with a negative after the verb**: *No hago nada*, *No veo a nadie*. Leaving out *no* is the most common negative error.
- **lo/la vs le**: *it, him, her* receiving the action is *lo/la*; *to him, to her* is *le*. Check who receives what.
- **Accents**: question words (*¿dónde?, ¿cuándo?, ¿qué?*) have them; *que* joining clauses does not. A command with a pronoun on the end often needs one: *¡Cómpralo!, ¡Lávate!* (but *¡Dame!* does not).
- **Don't drop *que***: English 'the film I saw' needs *la película que vi*; *He says he is tired* needs *Dice que está cansado*.
- **¿Cuál? vs ¿Qué?**: *¿Cuál es ...?* for 'what/which is...' choices; *¿Qué + noun?* for 'which film?'.
:::
