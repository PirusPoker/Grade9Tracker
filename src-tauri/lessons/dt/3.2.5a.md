## What it is

A commercial product works because its materials have the **right properties in the right places**. This topic asks two things. First, *why* are these materials used in these products: how do the physical and working properties influence **use** and affect **performance**? Second, how can a material's properties be **modified** when the natural material is not quite good enough? The spec names two commercial products per category, plus one modification per category (two for systems). Learn them all, because Section B lets you choose.

Remember the property words from the core topic:
- **physical** properties: absorbency, density, fusibility, electrical and thermal conductivity;
- **working (mechanical)** properties: strength, hardness, toughness, malleability, ductility and elasticity.

A top answer always links **property → why it matters in this product → the effect on performance**.

### Properties in the named commercial products

| Category | Product | Typical material | Key properties and why they matter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Papers and boards** | **flyers and leaflets** | coated (gloss or silk) paper, roughly 100–170 gsm | light and thin, so cheap to print and post in bulk; a smooth coated surface holds ink sharply for bright photos; folds cleanly; low absorbency stops ink spreading |
| | **card-based food packaging** | folding boxboard, solid white board, duplex or foil-lined board | stiff enough to protect and stack; creases and folds without cracking; white printable face for branding; food-safe; coatings or foil resist grease and moisture and keep heat in |
| **Timbers** | **traditional children's toys** | beech (or maple, birch plywood) | hard and tough, so it survives being dropped and chewed; close, even grain gives a smooth surface with **no splinters**; heavy enough to feel solid; takes non-toxic finishes |
| | **flat-pack furniture** | MDF or chipboard with veneer or melamine laminate; pine | manufactured boards come in large, **stable** sheets that don't warp; consistent, so CNC machining and KD fittings line up; cheap; the laminate gives a hard-wearing, wipe-clean surface |
| **Metals** | **cooking utensils** | stainless steel; aluminium; copper-based pans; cast iron | stainless steel is **corrosion resistant**, hygienic and tough; aluminium and copper are **good thermal conductors** for even heating; handles use poor conductors such as wood, thermosets or silicone |
| | **hand tools** | high carbon (tool) steel; high speed steel; chrome vanadium steel | can be **hardened and tempered**, so blades and bits stay sharp yet tough enough not to snap; HSS drill bits keep their hardness when hot; handles are tough polymers or ash, which absorb shock |
| **Polymers** | **polymer seating** | polypropylene (PP); ABS; polycarbonate | PP is **tough** and slightly flexible, so a moulded shell absorbs knocks and flexes comfortably; light and stackable; coloured right through; cheap to injection mould; UV-stabilised grades for outdoors |
| | **electrical fittings** (plugs, sockets, switches) | urea-formaldehyde (UF), a thermosetting polymer; PVC for cable insulation | **electrical insulator**; a thermoset stays **hard and rigid** and **does not soften** when it gets hot, so it is safe near warm contacts; it doesn't readily burn; PVC is flexible for cable sheaths |
| **Textiles** | **sportswear** | polyester, polyamide, elastane blends; microfibres | **wicks** sweat away from skin and **dries quickly**; lightweight; elastane gives **stretch and recovery** for movement; strong and abrasion resistant |
| | **furnishings** (upholstery, curtains) | wool, cotton, polyester and blends | **durable** and abrasion resistant for daily use; stain resistant or easy to clean; good drape and colourfast for curtains; must meet **fire safety** rules, so flame retardant |
| **Electronic and mechanical systems** | **motor vehicles** | pressed steel, aluminium, polymers, rubber, glass, electronic modules | steel body panels are **strong** and can be pressed into shape; aluminium parts are **light** (better fuel economy) and conduct heat away from the engine; tough polymer bumpers absorb impact; copper wiring conducts; sensors and microcontrollers control engine and safety systems |
| | **domestic appliances** (e.g. washing machine, kettle) | stainless steel, enamelled or coated steel, PP, copper wiring, control PCBs | stainless drum resists corrosion from water and detergent; PP tub and kettle bodies are tough, light, insulating and water resistant; motors and heating elements convert energy; control circuits give programmes and safety cut-outs |

### Modifying properties for specific purposes

| Category | Modification | How it is done | Why it improves performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Papers and boards** | **additives to prevent moisture transfer** | **sizing** agents (e.g. starch) added to the pulp or surface reduce absorbency; wax or polymer (polyethylene) **coatings or laminates** make a barrier; foil lining | stops ink bleeding on printed paper; lets board hold liquids or frozen food without going soggy, as in paper cups, milk cartons and freezer boxes |
| **Timbers** | **seasoning** to reduce moisture content | air or kiln drying (see 3.2.4) | seasoned timber is **stable** (it won't shrink, warp or split in use), stronger and stiffer, resists rot and fungi, and glues and finishes adhere better |
| **Metals** | **annealing** to soften and improve malleability | heat the metal to a set temperature, hold it, then cool (steel slowly; copper can be quenched or air cooled) | hammering, bending and spinning **work harden** a metal until it cracks; annealing reforms the grain structure and relieves stress, so the metal is soft and **malleable** again and can keep being shaped, e.g. beating a copper bowl or deep drawing |
| **Polymers** | **stabilisers to resist UV degradation** | UV absorbers or stabilisers (and pigments such as carbon black) are mixed into the polymer before moulding | **ultraviolet** light breaks polymer chains, so outdoor polymers fade, go **brittle** and crack; stabilisers give garden furniture, uPVC window frames and outdoor toys a long life |
| **Textiles** | **flame retardants** to reduce combustion and fire hazards | chemical finishes applied to the fabric, or fibres made inherently flame retardant (e.g. modacrylic, aramids) | fabric is slower to ignite and flames spread more slowly, giving people time to escape; needed for upholstery, curtains in public buildings, children's nightwear and protective clothing |
| **Electronic and mechanical systems** | **photosensitive PCB board** | copper-clad board coated with light-sensitive **resist**; exposed to **UV** through a mask of the track layout, **developed** to remove the unwanted resist, then **etched** to remove the exposed copper | produces accurate, fine, repeatable tracks, so small complex circuits can be made reliably |
| | **anodising aluminium** | the aluminium part is made the **anode** in an acid electrolyte; a current thickens the natural **oxide** layer, which can be **dyed** and then sealed | the thick oxide layer is much **harder** and more **corrosion resistant** than bare aluminium, and gives a durable coloured finish, e.g. torches, bike parts, carabiners, laptop cases |

**The science of anodising.** Aluminium reacts with oxygen to form a thin aluminium oxide layer, a type of **oxidation**. Anodising deliberately thickens this layer by electrolysis. The oxide is part of the metal's surface rather than a coat on top, so it cannot peel off like paint.

::: terms
- **Commercial product**: a product manufactured in quantity for sale, whose materials are chosen for performance and cost.
- **Physical property**: a property of the material itself, such as density, absorbency, fusibility or conductivity.
- **Working property**: how a material behaves when a force acts on it or it is shaped, e.g. strength, hardness, toughness, malleability.
- **Sizing**: an additive such as starch that reduces the absorbency of paper so ink and moisture do not soak in.
- **Seasoning**: drying timber to a suitable moisture content so it is stable, stronger and resists rot.
- **Work hardening**: the hardening and embrittlement of a metal caused by hammering, bending or rolling it cold.
- **Annealing**: heating a metal and cooling it to relieve work hardening, making it softer and more malleable.
- **UV stabiliser**: an additive that protects a polymer from ultraviolet light, which would make it fade and go brittle.
- **Flame retardant**: a chemical treatment or fibre that makes a textile slower to ignite and slows the spread of flame.
- **Photosensitive PCB board**: copper-clad board coated with a light-sensitive resist, used to transfer a track layout by UV exposure.
- **Anodising**: an electrolytic process that thickens the oxide layer on aluminium, making it harder, corrosion resistant and dyeable.
- **Wicking**: the drawing of moisture along fibres away from the skin to the fabric surface, where it evaporates.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the specific material**: beech, UF, PP, stainless steel, polyester/elastane, foil-lined board. Not "wood" or "plastic".
2. **Name the property precisely**, using the property vocabulary: tough, hard, corrosion resistant, thermal insulator, low absorbency.
3. **Link it to the product's use**: "beech is tough, so a toy survives being dropped".
4. **State the effect on performance** for the user: safety, durability, comfort, cost.
5. For **modification** questions, give the technique's name, **how it is done** in one or two steps, the **problem it solves**, and the benefit.
6. For "choose one product" tables, answer **all parts about the same product**, and make each part specific to it.
:::

::: example Explain: property to product
Explain why urea-formaldehyde is used for electrical plugs. (3)

Urea-formaldehyde is a good electrical insulator (1), so the user cannot get a shock when handling the plug. It is a thermosetting polymer, so it does not soften when the contacts warm up (1). It is hard and rigid, so the pins are held firmly in position and the casing resists cracking when the plug is knocked or trodden on (1).
:::

::: example Explain: annealing
Explain why a silversmith anneals copper while beating it into a bowl. (3)

Hammering the copper makes it work harden, so it becomes harder and more brittle (1). If it is beaten further it will crack (1). Annealing, heating it and letting it cool, softens it and restores its malleability, so it can be shaped further without splitting (1).
:::

::: example Choose one product: modification
Choose one product: a polypropylene garden chair, a beech rocking horse, an anodised aluminium torch, a flame-retardant sofa fabric, a PCB for a timer, a waxed paper coffee cup. (a) Name the specific material. (b) Name one modification used. (c) Explain why the material needs modifying. (1 + 1 + 2) (4)

Chosen product: the polypropylene garden chair.
(a) Polypropylene (PP) (1).
(b) UV stabilisers added to the polymer (1).
(c) The chair is left outdoors in sunlight, and ultraviolet light breaks down the polymer chains (1), so without stabilisers it would fade, go brittle and crack, becoming unsafe to sit on and needing replacing (1).
:::

::: example Explain: flat-pack material
Explain why chipboard with a melamine laminate is used for flat-pack furniture. (4)

Chipboard is made in large, flat, stable sheets that do not warp (1), so panels can be CNC cut accurately and KD fittings line up every time (1). It is made from waste wood chips, so it is much cheaper than solid timber, keeping the retail price low (1). The melamine laminate gives a hard, wipe-clean, moisture-resistant surface in any colour or wood-grain print, so no finishing is needed in the factory (1).
:::

::: example Explain: extended
Explain how the properties of materials are matched to two commercial products in one material category of your choice. (6)

Category: textiles. Sportswear such as a running top is made from polyester with some elastane. Polyester fibres absorb little water and **wick** sweat along the fibre to the surface, where it evaporates, so the runner stays dry and cool, and the fabric dries quickly after washing. Elastane gives stretch and recovery, so the top moves with the body without sagging, while polyester's strength and abrasion resistance let it survive repeated washing and wear. Furnishing fabric for a sofa is chosen for different properties. A tightly woven wool or polyester blend is durable and abrasion resistant, so it survives years of people sitting and rubbing on it, and a stain-resistant finish makes spills easy to clean. The fabric must also meet fire safety rules, so it is treated with a **flame retardant** that makes it slower to ignite, giving people time to escape in a fire. In both products the chosen properties directly improve comfort, lifespan and safety. (Level 3: 5–6 marks)
:::

::: q
Give two properties that make beech suitable for a child's toy. (2)
---
Any two (1 each): hard; tough; close/fine grain; doesn't splinter; smooth finish; takes non-toxic finish; durable.
:::

::: q
Explain why aluminium is used for saucepans. (2)
---
It is a good conductor of heat, so food heats quickly and evenly (1); it is light and resists corrosion, so the pan is easy to lift and does not rust (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why paper cups are coated with a thin layer of polyethylene. (2)
---
Paper is absorbent, so it would go soggy and leak (1); the polymer coating is a barrier that stops moisture transfer, so the cup holds hot drinks (1).
:::

::: q
Describe how an aluminium part is anodised. (3)
---
The cleaned aluminium part is made the anode in an acid electrolyte bath (1); an electric current is passed, which thickens the oxide layer on the surface (1); the layer can be dyed a colour and then sealed (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why flame retardants are applied to curtains in a cinema. (2)
---
They make the fabric slower to ignite and slow the spread of flame (1), giving large numbers of people time to escape if there is a fire (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why high speed steel is used for drill bits. (2)
---
It is very hard, so the cutting edges stay sharp (1), and it keeps its hardness at the high temperatures produced by friction when drilling (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Annealing softens.** It improves malleability; it does not harden. Hardening and tempering is a different process.
- Say *what the modification solves*: UV → brittleness and fading; moisture → sogginess or warping; fire → ignition and spread.
- **Anodising is for aluminium** and thickens an oxide layer by electrolysis; it is not a paint or a plating.
- Link property **→ product → performance**. "Stainless steel is strong" earns little; "it resists corrosion, so a utensil stays hygienic after washing" earns both marks.
- In "choose one" questions, keep all your answers about **one product**, and use the specific material name.
- Thermosets are chosen for electrical fittings because they **don't soften when hot**, not just because they are "plastic".
:::

::: test
Explain why polypropylene is used for stackable classroom chairs. (3)
---
It is tough and slightly flexible, so the shell absorbs knocks and does not crack when chairs are dropped or stacked (1); it is lightweight, so the chairs are easy to lift and stack (1); it is cheap to injection mould in one piece and is coloured throughout, so no finishing is needed (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why seasoned timber performs better than unseasoned timber in a piece of furniture. (3)
---
Seasoned timber has had its moisture content reduced (1), so it will not shrink, warp or split as it dries further in a warm room (1); it is also stronger and less likely to rot, and glues and finishes stick to it better (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why polyester and elastane are used in a cycling top. (4)
---
Polyester absorbs little moisture and wicks sweat away from the skin (1), so it dries quickly and keeps the cyclist comfortable (1). Elastane stretches and recovers (1), so the top fits closely for aerodynamics and moves with the body without sagging (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how photosensitive PCB board is used to make a printed circuit board. (4)
---
Copper-clad board is coated with light-sensitive resist (1); a mask/transparency of the track layout is placed on it and it is exposed to UV light (1); the board is put in developer, which removes the resist that is not needed (1); it is then etched to dissolve the exposed copper, leaving the protected copper tracks (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why steel hand tools such as chisels are hardened and tempered. (2)
---
Hardening makes the cutting edge very hard so it stays sharp (1); tempering reduces the brittleness so the tool is tough and does not chip or snap when struck (1).
:::

::: test
Explain one property of steel and one property of polymers that make them suitable for parts of a motor vehicle. (4)
---
Steel is strong and malleable/ductile (1), so body panels can be pressed into complex shapes and protect occupants in a crash (1). Polymers such as PP are tough and lightweight (1), so bumpers absorb minor impacts without cracking and reduce the car's weight, improving fuel economy (1). Accept other correct examples, e.g. aluminium for light engine parts, copper for wiring.
:::

::: test
The products in Table 1 use materials that are modified for specific purposes: waxed paper milk carton · oak garden bench · copper bowl beaten by hand · uPVC window frame · children's nightdress · aluminium bike stem. Choose one product. (a) Name the specific main material. (b) Name one modification technique used on it. (c) Explain why the material needs modifying. (1 + 1 + 2) (4)

(a) and (b), 1 each; (c), 2 for a reason and its consequence. Milk carton: board; wax or polymer coating (moisture barrier); board absorbs liquid and would go soggy and leak. ::: test
Explain why domestic appliances such as washing machines use a stainless steel drum. (2)
---
It resists corrosion from constant water and detergent (1), so the drum stays smooth and hygienic and does not stain clothes or weaken over many years (1).
:::

::: test
Explain why a thermosetting polymer, rather than a thermoforming polymer, is used for the handle of a saucepan. (2)
---
A thermoset has cross-links, so it does not soften or melt when it gets hot from the pan (1); it is also a poor conductor of heat, so the handle stays cool enough to hold safely (1).
:::
