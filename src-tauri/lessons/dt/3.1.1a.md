## What it is

New and emerging technologies change **what** gets made, **how** it is made, **where** it is made and **who** does the work. This topic has three parts in the spec: the impact on **industry** (the workplace, buildings and tools), **enterprise** (new ways to launch and sell an innovation) and **production techniques and systems** (automation, CAD, CAM, FMS, JIT and lean manufacturing). In the exam it appears as 1-mark multiple choice in Section A, 2–4 mark "give" and "explain" questions, and it feeds Section C questions about making a product commercially.

### Industry: the workplace, buildings and tools

**The design and organisation of the workplace.** **Automation** means using machines and control systems to carry out a process with little or no human input. **Robotics** is the use of programmable robot arms and vehicles to handle, weld, paint, assemble, pick and pack. A modern factory is laid out around the machines: production is arranged in **cells**, with robots doing the repetitive, heavy, dangerous or highly precise jobs, and fewer people on the line. The people who remain are more often **technicians, programmers and maintenance engineers** than manual assemblers. **Collaborative robots (cobots)** are smaller robots with sensors that let them work safely beside people, taking the strain out of lifting or repetitive tasks.

| Impact of automation and robotics | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Output | runs 24 hours a day without breaks; faster cycle times | a breakdown can stop a whole line |
| Quality | every product made to the same accuracy; fewer human errors | a programming error is repeated on every item until noticed |
| Workers | removes dull, dirty and dangerous jobs; new skilled roles | unskilled jobs lost; retraining needed; some workers deskilled |
| Costs | lower labour cost per unit once running | very high set-up and maintenance cost |
| Safety | people kept away from fumes, heat, heavy loads | robots need guarding, sensors and safe systems of work |

**Buildings and the place of work.** Factories no longer need to be near a large pool of manual labour, so they are built where land is cheap and transport links are good. Buildings are designed for machines and automated guided vehicles: large, open floors, clean controlled environments, and in the most automated plants very little lighting or heating in production areas because few people are there. Offices have changed too: fast broadband, cloud storage and video calls allow **remote and home working**, **hot-desking** and teams spread across countries, so companies may need less office space. Design work can be shared globally, with a CAD file worked on in one country and manufactured in another.

**Tools and equipment.** Hand tools have been joined by **CNC** (computer numerically controlled) machines such as laser cutters, routers, lathes and milling machines, by **3D printers**, and by cordless power tools running on rechargeable lithium-ion batteries. Digital measuring tools (digital callipers, laser measures) and scanners speed up checking. The impact: greater accuracy and speed, but a need for new skills, higher equipment costs and regular software updates.

### Enterprise: launching a business innovation

Enterprise means turning a new idea into a business. The spec names four routes.

| Route | What it is | Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crowdfunding** | raising money online from a large number of people, each giving a small amount, usually in return for a reward, an early product or a small share | no bank loan needed; tests whether people want the product before it is made; builds a customer base and publicity | the idea is public and may be copied; if the target is not reached the money may not be released; backers expect the promises to be kept on time |
| **Virtual marketing** | promoting products online: social media, influencers, targeted adverts, email, search results | cheap compared with TV or print; can target exactly the right users; results can be measured | easy to ignore; bad reviews spread fast; needs constant new content |
| **Virtual retail** | selling through a website or app rather than a physical shop | open 24/7 worldwide; no shop rent; customers can compare and review | customers cannot touch or try the product; returns are costly; delivery costs; cyber-security risks |
| **Co-operative** | a business owned and run by its members (its workers or its customers), who share decisions and profits | members are motivated because they share the rewards; democratic and fair; profits stay with members | decisions can be slow; harder to raise large sums of money; disagreements between members |
| **Fair trade** | a certification scheme that guarantees producers in developing countries a fair minimum price plus an extra premium to spend on their community, with safe working conditions | better lives for farmers and workers; ethical image attracts customers; more secure supply | products cost the consumer more; the certification process costs producers money |

Virtual marketing also uses **augmented reality** (seeing a sofa in your room through a phone camera) and online configurators that let a customer design their own version before it is made.

### Production techniques and systems

| System | What it is | Main benefits | Main drawbacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Automation** | machines and control systems carry out processes with little human input | high output, consistent quality, lower labour cost per unit | high set-up cost; job losses; breakdowns stop production |
| **CAD** (computer aided design) | software used to draw, model, test and present designs in 2D and 3D | quick to edit and copy; very accurate; realistic renders for clients; virtual testing (e.g. stress simulation); files shared instantly worldwide; links directly to CAM | software and hardware cost; training needed; files can be corrupted, hacked or incompatible |
| **CAM** (computer aided manufacture) | computer-controlled machines make parts from a CAD file: laser cutters, CNC routers, lathes and mills, 3D printers, vinyl cutters, embroidery machines | accurate and identical every time; fast; complex shapes; runs unattended; less waste through nesting | expensive machines; maintenance and skilled programmers needed; fewer manual jobs |
| **FMS** (flexible manufacturing system) | a cell of CNC machines, robots and automated transport, all controlled by a central computer, that can be switched quickly to make a different product | fast changeover between products; suits batch production and customised products; responds quickly to changing demand | very expensive to set up; complex to program and maintain |
| **JIT** (just in time) | materials and components are ordered and delivered exactly when they are needed for production, not stored in advance | less money tied up in stock; less storage space; no waste from damaged or out-of-date stock | depends on reliable suppliers and transport; one late delivery stops production; no bulk-buying discounts; more deliveries |
| **Lean manufacturing** | a whole approach to production that aims to remove every kind of waste: materials, time, movement, waiting, over-production, excess stock and defects | lower costs; less material waste; faster production; higher quality through continuous improvement | needs commitment from all staff; little spare capacity if something goes wrong |

**How they fit together.** CAD produces the design; the same file drives CAM; several CAM machines plus robots and a central computer form an FMS; JIT keeps the stock arriving as the FMS needs it; lean manufacturing is the overall philosophy that JIT supports. A strong answer shows these links.

**Contemporary and future use.** Today these systems are standard in car, electronics and furniture manufacture. Emerging directions include factories where machines share data over the internet and adjust themselves (sometimes called **Industry 4.0**), artificial intelligence spotting defects from camera images, **digital twins** (a live virtual model of a machine or factory used to test changes), 3D printing of finished end-use parts, and **mass customisation**, where each customer's product is slightly different but still made at mass-production speed.

### Maths link: putting numbers on JIT and automation

Stock costs money to hold: storage space, insurance, damage and money tied up. If a firm holds £40,000 of stock and holding costs are 25% of the stock value per year, cutting average stock to £6,000 with JIT saves

$$0.25 \times (40\,000 - 6\,000) = 0.25 \times 34\,000 = 8\,500$$

so £8,500 a year. The stock has fallen by $34\,000 \div 40\,000 \times 100 = 85\%$.

A **payback period** tells a firm how long an automated machine takes to pay for itself: payback $=$ cost $\div$ yearly saving. A robot cell costing £90,000 that saves £24,000 of labour a year pays back in $90\,000 \div 24\,000 = 3.75$ years.

::: terms
- **Automation**: using machines and control systems to carry out a process with little or no human input.
- **Robotics**: the use of programmable robots to handle, assemble, weld, paint or move products and materials.
- **CAD**: computer aided design; software used to draw, model, test and present designs in 2D and 3D.
- **CAM**: computer aided manufacture; computer-controlled machines, such as laser cutters and CNC routers, making parts from a CAD file.
- **CNC**: computer numerical control; a machine tool whose movements are controlled by coded instructions from a computer.
- **FMS**: flexible manufacturing system; a computer-controlled cell of CNC machines, robots and transport that can switch quickly between products.
- **Just in time (JIT)**: ordering materials and components so they arrive exactly when they are needed, so little or no stock is held.
- **Lean manufacturing**: an approach to production that aims to remove all forms of waste, including time, materials, stock and defects.
- **Crowdfunding**: raising money for a new product online from many people, each giving a small amount, often in return for a reward.
- **Virtual marketing and retail**: promoting and selling products online through websites, apps and social media rather than in physical shops.
- **Co-operative**: a business owned and run by its members, who share the decisions and the profits.
- **Fair trade**: a certification scheme guaranteeing producers in developing countries a fair price, a community premium and safe working conditions.
:::

::: method
1. **Identify the technology or system** in the question and define it in one clause, using the right term (CAM, JIT, FMS, not "robots" or "computers" in general).
2. **For "give" questions**, write short separate points: one mark each, no explanation needed.
3. **For "explain" questions**, make each point a chain: the feature, then "so" or "which means", then the consequence for the manufacturer, worker or customer. That is two marks per point.
4. **Say who benefits.** An advantage to the manufacturer (lower cost, higher output) is not the same as one to the worker (safer job) or the customer (cheaper, consistent product). Match the question.
5. **For impact questions, balance positives and negatives**: output, quality, cost, jobs, skills, safety.
6. **For calculations**, write the formula, substitute, and give the unit and precision asked (nearest pound, one decimal place).
:::

::: example Give: advantages of CAD
Give two advantages of using CAD rather than drawing by hand. (2)

Designs can be edited and copied quickly without redrawing (1). Files can be sent instantly to clients or manufacturers anywhere in the world (1). (Also: very accurate dimensions; realistic renders; virtual testing; direct link to CAM.)
:::

::: example Explain: how JIT reduces costs
Explain how just in time production reduces a manufacturer's costs. (3)

Materials arrive only when they are needed, so the firm holds very little stock (1). This means less warehouse space has to be rented, heated and insured (1), and less money is tied up in stock or lost when stock is damaged or goes out of date (1).
:::

::: example Explain: advantages of CAM
Explain two advantages to a manufacturer of using CAM to make a batch of acrylic key rings. (4)

Every key ring is cut from the same file with the same accuracy (1), so all are identical and very few are rejected at quality control (1). The laser cutter can run for long periods without breaks (1), so the batch is finished faster and the labour cost per key ring is lower (1).
:::

::: example Calculate: saving from JIT
A company holds £60,000 of stock. Holding stock costs 20% of its value per year. After changing to JIT, it holds £9,000 of stock on average. Calculate the yearly saving in holding costs and the percentage reduction in stock. (4)

Fall in stock $= 60\,000 - 9\,000 = 51\,000$ (1). Saving $= 0.20 \times 51\,000 = 10\,200$, so £10,200 a year (1). Percentage reduction $= 51\,000 \div 60\,000 \times 100$ (1) $= 85\%$ (1).
:::

::: example Extended: automation and robotics
Explain the impact of automation and robotics on the workplace and on workers. (6)

Robots can work 24 hours a day at a constant speed, so output rises and the cost of labour per product falls (1). Because they repeat the same movements exactly, quality is consistent and fewer products are rejected (1). Dangerous jobs such as spray painting and welding are taken away from people, so workers are exposed to fewer fumes and injuries (1). However, many unskilled assembly jobs are lost, which can cause unemployment in areas that relied on the factory (1). The jobs that remain need new skills such as programming and maintaining robots, so workers must be retrained, and some feel deskilled or less involved (1). The workplace itself changes: it is laid out around robot cells with safety guarding and space for automated vehicles, and fewer people work on the factory floor (1).
Level 3 (5–6): a balanced, detailed explanation covering the workplace and workers. Level 2 (3–4): some explained points, possibly one-sided. Level 1 (1–2): simple listed points.
:::

::: q
Describe what is meant by crowdfunding. (2)
---
Raising the money needed to develop or launch a product (1) from a large number of people online, each giving a small amount, often in return for a reward or early product (1).
:::

::: q
Explain one benefit to a farmer of selling crops through fair trade. (2)
---
They are guaranteed a fair minimum price that covers the cost of production (1), so their income is more secure even when world prices fall (1). (Or: the premium pays for community projects such as schools or clean water; safe working conditions.)
:::

::: q
Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of a business being run as a co-operative. (4)
---
Advantage: members share the profits and have a say in decisions (1), so they are motivated to work hard and stay loyal (1). Disadvantage: every member has a vote (1), so decisions can be slow, or it can be hard to raise large amounts of money for expansion (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a flexible manufacturing system suits a company making customised bicycles. (3)
---
An FMS is a computer-controlled cell of CNC machines and robots (1) that can be reprogrammed and switched quickly from one design to another (1), so each customer's frame size or specification can be made without long, costly changeovers (1).
:::

::: q
Explain the link between lean manufacturing and just in time. (3)
---
Lean manufacturing aims to remove all forms of waste from production (1). Holding excess stock is one form of waste (1), and JIT removes it by delivering materials only when they are needed, so JIT is one of the tools used to make production lean (1).
:::

::: q
A laser cutter costs £18,000. It saves the company £7,500 a year in labour. Calculate the payback period in years, to one decimal place. (2)
---
Payback $= 18\,000 \div 7\,500$ (1) $= 2.4$ years (1).
:::

::: q
Explain two ways new technology has changed buildings and the place of work. (4)
---
Fast broadband and video calls allow staff to work from home (1), so companies need smaller offices and may use hot-desking (1). Automated factories are laid out around machines rather than people (1), so they have open floors for robots and automated vehicles and can be sited where land is cheap and transport links are good rather than near a large workforce (1).
:::

::: watch
- **JIT is about when stock arrives**, not about producing quickly. "JIT means products are made fast" scores nothing.
- **CAD designs, CAM makes.** Swapping them is a classic lost mark in multiple choice.
- An **FMS** is a computer-controlled cell that can switch between products; do not describe it as "flexible working hours".
- "Explain" needs a consequence after each point. "CAM is accurate" is one mark at most; "CAM is accurate, so every part is identical and waste is reduced" is two.
- Say **who** benefits: manufacturer, worker, consumer or society, as the question asks.
- Fair trade is about **fair pay and conditions for producers**, not about cheap prices for shoppers.
:::

::: test
A factory holds £25,000 of stock. Holding costs are 30% of the stock value each year. Using JIT, average stock falls to £4,000. Calculate the yearly saving in holding costs. (3)
---
Fall in stock $= 25\,000 - 4\,000 = 21\,000$ (1). Saving $= 0.30 \times 21\,000$ (1) $= 6\,300$, so £6,300 a year (1). Alternative method: $0.30 \times 25\,000 - 0.30 \times 4\,000 = 7\,500 - 1\,200 = 6\,300$ also gains full marks.
:::

::: test
Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to an inventor of using crowdfunding to launch a new product. (4)
---
Advantage: the inventor does not need a bank loan or to give control to a big investor (1), and if many people back the project it proves there is demand before money is spent on production (1). Disadvantage: the idea is made public before it is on sale (1), so a larger company could copy it and bring it to market first; or if the target is not reached the inventor gets no money (1).
:::

::: test
Explain how CAD and CAM work together to speed up the development of a new product. (4)
---
The product is modelled in CAD and can be changed quickly and tested virtually (1), so fewer physical prototypes are needed (1). The same CAD file is sent straight to CAM machines such as a 3D printer or laser cutter (1), so prototypes and then production parts are made accurately without redrawing or manual marking out, cutting development time (1).
:::
