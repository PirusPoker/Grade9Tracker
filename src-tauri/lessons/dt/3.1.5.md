## What it is

Mechanical devices take an input movement and force and change them into the output a product needs: a different **type** of movement, a bigger or smaller **force**, a different **speed**, or a different **direction**. The spec covers the four types of movement, **levers** (first, second and third order), **linkages** (bell cranks and push/pull), and **rotary systems** (cams and followers, simple gear trains, pulleys and belts). This is one of the most mathematical topics: expect multiple choice on lever orders and how far a linkage moves, and 2–6 mark calculations of moments, gear ratios and output speeds. Always show your working.

### Four types of movement

| Movement | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Linear** | in a straight line, in one direction | a conveyor belt carrying a box, a paper trimmer blade, a train on a straight track |
| **Rotary** | turning round in a circle about an axis | a wheel, a drill bit, a fan, a gear |
| **Reciprocating** | backwards and forwards (or up and down) in a straight line | a sewing machine needle, an engine piston, a jigsaw blade, a pump |
| **Oscillating** | swinging backwards and forwards in an arc about a pivot | a pendulum, a child's swing, a windscreen wiper, a metronome |

Mechanisms convert one into another: a cam turns **rotary into reciprocating**; a rack and pinion turns **rotary into linear**; a crank and slider converts **reciprocating into rotary** (as in an engine).

### Levers

A **lever** is a rigid bar that pivots about a **fulcrum**. An **effort** (input force) applied at one point moves a **load** (output force) at another. The order depends on what is in the middle.

| Order | In the middle | Examples | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First order** | the **fulcrum** is between effort and load | see-saw, crowbar, scissors, pliers, claw hammer pulling a nail | can magnify force or distance, depending on where the fulcrum is; the load moves in the opposite direction to the effort |
| **Second order** | the **load** is between fulcrum and effort | wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener, oar | always magnifies force: the effort is further from the fulcrum than the load, so a small effort moves a large load |
| **Third order** | the **effort** is between fulcrum and load | tweezers, tongs, fishing rod, the human forearm, a broom | effort is bigger than the load, but the load moves further and faster; gives precise control |

Memory aid: **F-L-E** for 1-2-3: in first order the **F**ulcrum is in the middle, in second order the **L**oad, in third order the **E**ffort.

**Moments.** A moment is the turning effect of a force: moment $=$ force $\times$ perpendicular distance from the fulcrum. A lever balances when the moments on each side are equal:

$$\text{effort} \times \text{effort distance} = \text{load} \times \text{load distance}$$

**Mechanical advantage (MA)** tells you how much the lever multiplies force:

$$\text{MA} = \frac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}}$$

MA greater than 1 means the lever magnifies force (typical of second order); less than 1 means it magnifies distance (third order).

**Distance moved.** Because the lever is rigid, each point moves in proportion to its distance from the fulcrum. A point twice as far out moves twice as far. What you gain in force you lose in distance.

### Linkages

A **linkage** is a set of rigid parts (links) joined by pivots that transfers movement and force, often changing its direction. **Fixed pivots** are attached to the product's frame; **moving pivots** join links together.

- **Push/pull linkage**: transfers a push or pull from one place to another. In a **reverse motion linkage**, a link turns about a central fixed pivot, so the output moves in the **opposite direction** to the input. If the fixed pivot is exactly in the middle, the output moves the **same distance**; if it is off-centre, the distances are in the ratio of the arm lengths. In a **parallel motion linkage**, two links are joined so the output moves in the **same direction** as the input (e.g. extending toolbox trays, a folding pushchair frame).
- **Bell crank**: an L-shaped link pivoting at the corner, which changes the direction of motion **through 90°**. It is used in bicycle brakes (the cable pull becomes a squeeze on the rim) and in door-bell and signal mechanisms. If its two arms are different lengths, it also changes the force and distance in the ratio of the arm lengths, like a lever.

### Rotary systems

**Cams and followers.** A **cam** is a shaped piece fixed to a rotating shaft. A **follower** rests on its edge and is pushed up and down as the cam turns, converting **rotary** motion into **reciprocating** motion. Gravity or a spring keeps the follower on the cam.

| Cam shape | Follower movement |
|---|---|
| **Circular (eccentric)** | a circle turning off-centre; smooth, continuous rise and fall |
| **Pear** | the follower stays still (**dwell**) for about half a turn, then rises and falls |
| **Heart** | rises and falls at a steady rate: uniform motion |
| **Snail (drop)** | gradual rise, then a sudden drop; works in one direction of rotation only |

Followers can be **knife-edge** (accurate, but wears quickly), **flat** (hard-wearing, can take more force) or **roller** (low friction, suits high speeds). The **stroke** (lift) of the follower is the difference between the cam's largest and smallest radius. Cam positions are described in **degrees** of rotation: a pear cam might dwell from 0° to 180°, rise from 180° to 270° and fall from 270° to 360°.

**Simple gear trains.** A **gear** is a toothed wheel. The gear turned by the input is the **driver**; the one it turns is the **driven** gear. Meshing gears turn in **opposite directions**. An **idler gear** placed between them makes the driven gear turn in the **same direction** as the driver without changing the ratio.

$$\text{gear ratio} = \frac{\text{teeth on driven gear}}{\text{teeth on driver gear}} \qquad \text{output speed} = \frac{\text{input speed}}{\text{gear ratio}}$$

A small driver turning a large driven gear **reduces speed** but **increases turning force (torque)**: useful for a winch or a toy car climbing a slope. A large driver turning a small gear **increases speed** and reduces torque. In a **compound gear train**, two gears are fixed on the same shaft and turn together; the overall ratio is found by **multiplying** the ratios of each pair. Speeds are often given in **rpm** (revolutions per minute).

**Pulleys and belts.** Two pulleys are joined by a belt. Both turn in the **same direction** (a crossed belt reverses the direction).

$$\text{velocity ratio} = \frac{\text{diameter of driven pulley}}{\text{diameter of driver pulley}} \qquad \text{output speed} = \frac{\text{input speed}}{\text{velocity ratio}}$$

Belts are quiet, cheap and can transfer motion over a distance. A plain vee-belt can **slip** if the load is too great, which protects the motor (a useful safety feature, e.g. on a pillar drill) but means the speed is not exact. **Toothed belts** do not slip, so they are used where timing matters. A simple lifting pulley changes the direction of a force, and a system with a moving pulley gives a mechanical advantage (ideally 2 for one moving pulley), so a smaller effort lifts the load.

### Worked maths summary

- **Moments**: a wheelbarrow carries a 600 N load 0.4 m from the wheel; the handles are 1.2 m from the wheel. Effort $= 600 \times 0.4 \div 1.2 = 200$ N. MA $= 600 \div 200 = 3$.
- **Lever distance**: point A is 80 mm from the pivot and point B 20 mm on the same side. A moves 40 mm, so B moves $40 \times 20 \div 80 = 10$ mm in the same direction.
- **Gears**: a 15-tooth driver at 300 rpm turns a 45-tooth gear. Ratio $= 45 \div 15 = 3$, written 3:1. Output $= 300 \div 3 = 100$ rpm.
- **Angles**: with that 3:1 ratio, a 90° turn of the driver turns the driven gear $90 \div 3 = 30°$.
- **Pulleys**: a 50 mm motor pulley at 1500 rpm drives a 150 mm pulley. Ratio $= 150 \div 50 = 3$. Output $= 1500 \div 3 = 500$ rpm.

::: terms
- **Linear motion**: movement in a straight line in one direction.
- **Rotary motion**: movement round in a circle about an axis, like a wheel.
- **Reciprocating motion**: movement backwards and forwards in a straight line, like a piston.
- **Oscillating motion**: swinging backwards and forwards in an arc about a pivot, like a pendulum.
- **Fulcrum**: the fixed pivot point about which a lever turns.
- **Moment**: the turning effect of a force, found by multiplying the force by its distance from the pivot.
- **Mechanical advantage**: the load divided by the effort; how many times a mechanism multiplies the input force.
- **Bell crank**: an L-shaped linkage pivoted at its corner that changes the direction of motion through 90 degrees.
- **Cam and follower**: a shaped rotating cam that pushes a follower up and down, turning rotary into reciprocating motion.
- **Dwell**: the part of a cam's rotation during which the follower does not move.
- **Gear ratio**: the number of teeth on the driven gear divided by the number of teeth on the driver gear.
- **Idler gear**: a gear placed between the driver and driven gears so they turn in the same direction.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the movement or mechanism first**: input motion, output motion, and what the device does to force, speed or direction.
2. **Lever order**: find what is in the middle (fulcrum, load or effort): F-L-E gives first, second, third.
3. **Moments**: write $\text{effort} \times d_E = \text{load} \times d_L$, substitute with units (convert mm and m so both distances match), then rearrange.
4. **Gear and pulley ratio**: always **driven ÷ driver**. Output speed $=$ input speed $\div$ ratio. For compound trains, multiply each stage.
5. **Direction**: meshing gears reverse; an idler restores the direction; belts keep the same direction unless crossed.
6. **Show every step** and give the unit (N, mm, rpm) and the precision asked. A correct method earns marks even if the final answer slips.
:::

::: example Identify: lever order
A pair of tweezers is used to pick up a splinter. State the order of lever and explain your answer. (2)

Third order lever (1): the effort from the fingers is applied between the fulcrum (the joined end) and the load (the splinter at the tips) (1).
:::

::: example Calculate: effort using moments
A crowbar is 900 mm long. The fulcrum is 100 mm from the end touching a 1200 N load. Calculate the effort needed at the other end, and the mechanical advantage. (4)

Effort distance $= 900 - 100 = 800$ mm (1). $\text{effort} \times 800 = 1200 \times 100$ (1). Effort $= 120\,000 \div 800 = 150$ N (1). MA $= 1200 \div 150 = 8$ (1).
:::

::: example Calculate: simple gear train
A motor turns a 12-tooth driver gear at 1800 rpm. It meshes with a 48-tooth driven gear. Calculate the gear ratio and the output speed, and state the direction of the driven gear. (4)

Gear ratio $= 48 \div 12 = 4$, i.e. 4:1 (1)(1). Output speed $= 1800 \div 4 = 450$ rpm (1). The driven gear turns in the opposite direction to the driver (1).
:::

::: example Explain: cam
Explain how a cam and follower converts rotary motion into reciprocating motion. (3)

The cam is fixed to a shaft that rotates (1). Its shaped edge pushes the follower, which rests on it, as the distance from the shaft to the edge changes (1). The follower is guided so it can only move up and down, so it rises and falls in a straight line, with a spring or gravity keeping it in contact with the cam (1).
:::

::: example Calculate: compound gear train
In a compound gear train, gear A (10 teeth) drives gear B (40 teeth). Gear C (15 teeth) is fixed to the same shaft as B and drives gear D (45 teeth). Gear A turns at 1200 rpm. Calculate the speed of gear D. (4)

Ratio A to B $= 40 \div 10 = 4$ (1). Ratio C to D $= 45 \div 15 = 3$ (1). Overall ratio $= 4 \times 3 = 12$ (1). Speed of D $= 1200 \div 12 = 100$ rpm (1).
:::

::: q
Name the type of motion produced by a sewing machine needle. (1)
---
Reciprocating.
:::

::: q
Give one example each of a first order and a second order lever. (2)
---
First order: see-saw, scissors, crowbar, pliers (1). Second order: wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener (1).
:::

::: q
Explain the function of a bell crank in a bicycle brake. (2)
---
The bell crank changes the direction of the cable's pull through 90° (1), so pulling the brake lever makes the brake blocks squeeze inwards onto the wheel rim (1).
:::

::: q
A driver pulley of diameter 40 mm turns at 900 rpm. It drives a pulley of diameter 120 mm. Calculate the velocity ratio and the output speed. (3)
---
Velocity ratio $= 120 \div 40 = 3$ (1). Output speed $= 900 \div 3$ (1) $= 300$ rpm (1).
:::

::: q
A pear-shaped cam has a largest radius of 42 mm and a smallest radius of 18 mm. Calculate the stroke of the follower. (1)
---
$42 - 18 = 24$ mm.
:::

::: q
Explain why a vee-belt drive is used on a pillar drill. (3)
---
The belt can slip on the pulleys if the drill bit jams (1), protecting the motor and the user from sudden forces (1); moving the belt between pulleys of different sizes changes the speed of the chuck to suit the material (1).
:::

::: q
A second order lever holds a 90 N load 50 mm from the fulcrum. The effort is applied 300 mm from the fulcrum. Calculate the effort. (3)
---
$\text{effort} \times 300 = 90 \times 50$ (1). $= 4500$ (1). Effort $= 4500 \div 300 = 15$ N (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Gear ratio is driven ÷ driver.** Upside down, you will get a faster speed when it should be slower. Sense-check: a bigger driven gear turns more slowly.
- **Reciprocating** is a straight line back and forth; **oscillating** is an arc. Do not mix them up.
- In moments, **both distances must be in the same units** and measured from the fulcrum, not from the end of the bar.
- **Meshing gears reverse direction**; belts do not (unless crossed). Direction is often a separate mark.
- For a reverse motion linkage with a central pivot, the output moves the **same distance in the opposite direction**.
- Show working on every calculation: the answer alone can lose marks, and method marks rescue a slip.
:::

::: test
Explain why a snail (drop) cam is used where the follower must fall suddenly. (2)
---
Its radius increases gradually as it turns, so the follower rises slowly (1), then the radius drops suddenly at the step, so the follower falls sharply, e.g. for a hammering action in a toy (1).

---
First order: the fulcrum is between the effort and the load (1), e.g. a see-saw or crowbar (1). Third order: the effort is between the fulcrum and the load (1), e.g. tweezers, tongs or a fishing rod (1).
:::

::: test
A bottle opener is a second order lever. The cap resists with a force of 60 N at 20 mm from the fulcrum. The hand pushes at 120 mm from the fulcrum. Calculate the effort needed and the mechanical advantage. (3)
---
$\text{effort} \times 120 = 60 \times 20$ (1). Effort $= 1200 \div 120 = 10$ N (1). MA $= 60 \div 10 = 6$ (1).
:::

::: test
A motor spins at 3000 rpm. It drives a 10-tooth gear which meshes with a 50-tooth gear. Calculate the gear ratio and the output speed. (3)
---
Gear ratio $= 50 \div 10 = 5$, i.e. 5:1 (1). Output speed $= 3000 \div 5$ (1) $= 600$ rpm (1).
:::

::: test
A 16-tooth driver gear is turned through 180°. It drives a 48-tooth gear. Calculate the angle the driven gear turns through. (2)
---
Ratio $= 48 \div 16 = 3$ (1). Angle $= 180 \div 3 = 60°$ (1).
:::

::: test
A motor pulley of diameter 60 mm turns at 1440 rpm. A belt connects it to a pulley of diameter 180 mm on a fan. Calculate the fan speed and state its direction compared with the motor. (3)
---
Ratio $= 180 \div 60 = 3$ (1). Fan speed $= 1440 \div 3 = 480$ rpm (1). Same direction as the motor (open belt) (1).
:::

::: test
Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of using a belt and pulley system rather than gears to drive a machine. (4)
---
Advantage: a belt can transfer motion between shafts some distance apart, runs quietly and cheaply (1), and it can slip if the machine jams, protecting the motor and user (1). Disadvantage: a plain belt can slip under heavy loads or stretch with wear (1), so the output speed is not exact and the belt needs adjusting or replacing; gears give a positive drive with no slip (1).
:::

::: test
A toy car's motor turns at 4800 rpm. It drives a compound gear train: an 8-tooth gear drives a 40-tooth gear; a 10-tooth gear on the same shaft as the 40-tooth gear drives a 30-tooth gear on the wheel axle. The wheels have a circumference of 150 mm. (a) Calculate the overall gear ratio. (b) Calculate the speed of the wheel axle in rpm. (c) Calculate how far the car travels in one minute, in metres. (6)
---
(a) First stage $= 40 \div 8 = 5$ (1). Second stage $= 30 \div 10 = 3$ (1). Overall $= 5 \times 3 = 15$, i.e. 15:1 (1).
(b) $4800 \div 15 = 320$ rpm (1).
(c) Distance $= 320 \times 150 = 48\,000$ mm (1) $= 48$ m (1).
Follow-through: credit correct method in (b) and (c) using an incorrect ratio from (a).
:::
