## What it is

To make a high-quality prototype you must **select** the right **specialist tools and equipment** for the material and the task, and use them **safely** to protect yourself and others. The spec groups equipment into three families: **hand tools**, **machinery**, and **digital design and manufacture**. Exam questions ask you to name a tool for a job (1 mark), justify one piece of equipment over another (3–4 marks), list specific safety precautions for a machine (often 5 marks), or describe making a part safely and accurately (6 marks).

**Selecting equipment: the questions to ask**

- **Material**: will the tool cut, shape or join this material cleanly? (A tenon saw for timber, a hacksaw for metal and plastic, a rotary cutter for fabric.)
- **Task**: straight or curved cut, hole, joint, shaped form, finish?
- **Accuracy and quality**: how close a tolerance and how good a finish is needed?
- **Quantity**: one-off hand work, or repeated parts that suit a machine, jig or CAM?
- **Time and cost**: is the machine available, and is setting it up worth it for the number of parts?
- **Safety and skill**: can it be used safely by the maker, with the right guards and training?

**Hand tools by material area**

| Material | Cutting | Shaping and forming | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Papers and boards** | craft knife or scalpel with a **safety rule** on a cutting mat; scissors; rotary trimmer/guillotine | scoring and creasing tools; bone folder | compass cutter, circle cutter, hole punch |
| **Timbers** | **tenon saw** (straight cuts, joints); **coping saw** (curves); panel saw | **chisels** (joints), **smoothing plane**, rasps, surforms, abrasive paper | marking gauge, try square, hand drill, G-cramps, sash cramps |
| **Metals** | **hacksaw** and junior hacksaw; tin snips for thin sheet | **files** (flat, half-round, needle), draw filing; ball-pein hammer for forming and riveting | scriber, centre punch, engineer's square, taps and dies for threads |
| **Polymers** | hacksaw, coping saw; scoring tool to snap acrylic sheet | files, wet-and-dry paper, polishing | line bender (strip heater) for bending |
| **Textiles** | **fabric shears**, pinking shears (reduce fraying), rotary cutter | hand sewing needles, seam ripper | pins, tape measure, tailor's chalk, iron |
| **Electronic and mechanical systems** | wire cutters and **wire strippers** | pliers | **soldering iron** with stand, solder, desoldering pump; multimeter to test |

**Machinery**

| Machine | Used for | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Pillar drill** | accurate vertical holes in timber, metal, polymer | depth stop; work held in a machine vice or clamped |
| **Scroll saw** | intricate curved cuts in thin timber and polymer | fine reciprocating blade; blade guard and hold-down foot |
| **Bandsaw** | fast straight and gentle curved cuts in timber | continuous blade; adjustable guard just above the work |
| **Disc and belt sanders** | shaping and smoothing edges | dust extraction; use the down-moving side of the disc |
| **Centre lathe** | turning metal (and polymer) cylinders, facing, drilling on the axis | chuck guard; remove chuck key |
| **Wood lathe** | turning bowls and spindles | tool rest close to the work |
| **Sewing machine / overlocker** | seams, hems; overlocker trims and neatens edges in one go | finger guard; adjustable stitch length |
| **Vacuum former, strip heater** | forming thermoplastic sheet | hot surfaces; heat-resistant gloves for handling sheet |
| **Brazing hearth, welding** | joining metals with heat | heat-resistant gloves, apron, visor or tinted goggles |

**Digital design and manufacture (CAD/CAM)**

| Equipment | What it does | Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Laser cutter** | cuts and engraves sheet (card, timber, MDF, acrylic, fabric) along a CAD path | very accurate and repeatable; intricate shapes; clean sealed edges on acrylic; can nest parts to save material | limited to sheet thickness; scorched edges on timber; fumes need extraction; some materials unsafe (e.g. PVC gives off toxic chlorine gas) |
| **3D printer** | builds a part layer by layer from polymer filament (e.g. PLA) | complex hollow shapes; no tooling; quick prototypes | slow for large parts; visible layers; limited strength and size |
| **CNC router / miller** | cuts and carves timber, MDF, foam, aluminium from a CAD file | accurate 2D and 3D shapes; repeatable | setting up takes time; noisy; dust |
| **Vinyl cutter** | cuts self-adhesive vinyl and card | decals, stencils, signs, packaging graphics | thin materials only |
| **CNC embroidery machine** | stitches a digitised design onto fabric | detailed, repeatable logos and patterns | set-up of hooping and threads |
| **PCB mill / CAD circuit software** | designs and makes circuit boards | accurate tracks; simulation before making | cost and set-up |

**Why use CAM rather than hand tools?** Precision, **repeatability** (every part identical), speed for batches, complex shapes that are hard by hand, less skill needed at the cutting stage, and the design can be edited and re-cut. Hand tools remain better for one-off simple jobs, adjustments and where no machine is available.

**Reading machine settings (maths link)** Laser cutters have **speed** and **power** settings. Thicker or denser material needs more power and/or lower speed; engraving uses low power and high speed. You may be given a table of settings and asked to pick one or explain the pattern.

**Working safely**

General rules in the workshop:

- **Risk assessment** before using any machine or process: identify hazards, who could be harmed, and the control measures.
- **Training and permission**: only use a machine you have been shown how to use.
- **PPE** (personal protective equipment): safety goggles or visor, apron, heat-resistant gloves for hot work, dust mask where needed. **Never wear gloves on rotating machinery** (drill, lathe), as they can be caught.
- Tie back long hair, tuck in ties and loose clothing, remove jewellery.
- **Guards** in place and adjusted close to the work.
- **Secure the work**: machine vice, G-cramp, bench hook, vice; never hold small work by hand on a drill.
- Know the **emergency stop** and keep the area clear and tidy.
- **Extraction and ventilation** for dust (sanders, CNC) and fumes (laser cutter, soldering, adhesives, spray finishes).
- **COSHH** (Control of Substances Hazardous to Health): check data sheets for adhesives, solvents and finishes; use in ventilated areas.
- Keep hands behind the cutting edge; carry tools pointing down; use a safety rule with a craft knife.
- Never leave a laser cutter unattended (fire risk); keep a soldering iron in its stand.

Precautions in an answer must be **specific to the machine**. "Be careful" earns nothing. For a pillar drill: goggles; clamp the work in a machine vice; lower the chuck guard; remove the chuck key before switching on; tie back hair and no gloves; set the correct speed for the material; use the depth stop; stop the machine before clearing swarf with a brush.

::: terms
- **Hand tool**: a tool powered and controlled by the user's hand, such as a saw, chisel, file or craft knife.
- **Machinery**: powered equipment such as a pillar drill, lathe, bandsaw or sewing machine.
- **CAD**: computer-aided design, creating and editing designs on a computer.
- **CAM**: computer-aided manufacture, using a computer-controlled machine to make parts from a CAD file.
- **CNC**: computer numerical control, where a machine's movements are controlled by coded coordinates.
- **Laser cutter**: a CAM machine that uses a focused beam to cut or engrave sheet materials.
- **PPE**: personal protective equipment such as goggles, aprons, gloves and masks.
- **Risk assessment**: identifying hazards, who could be harmed and the control measures needed before an activity.
- **Guard**: a physical barrier that stops the user touching moving or cutting parts of a machine.
- **COSHH**: regulations on the safe use and storage of substances that are hazardous to health.
- **Extraction**: removing dust or fumes from the work area with a fan and ducting.
- **Repeatability**: the ability of a process to produce identical parts every time.
:::

::: method
1. **Name the exact tool or machine**: "coping saw", not "a saw"; "laser cutter", not "a machine".
2. **Match it to material and task**, and say why it gives the required quality or accuracy.
3. **For "why use X rather than Y"**, compare on accuracy, repeatability, speed, complexity and finish, and give a drawback.
4. **For safety precautions**, make each one specific: the hazard **and** the action for that machine.
5. **For 6-mark "describe how to make safely and accurately"**, give an ordered sequence: mark out, secure, cut or shape, check, finish, with a safety point at each machine stage.
:::

::: example Safety precautions
Give five safety precautions a student should follow when using a pillar drill. (5)

Wear safety goggles to protect the eyes from swarf (1). Hold the work securely in a machine vice or clamp it to the table, never by hand (1). Lower the chuck guard before drilling (1). Remove the chuck key before switching the drill on (1). Tie back long hair, tuck in loose clothing and don't wear gloves, so nothing can be caught by the rotating chuck (1).
:::

::: example Laser cutter vs coping saw
Explain why a laser cutter might be used rather than a coping saw to cut a batch of 20 acrylic keyring shapes. (4)

The laser follows the CAD path exactly, so every keyring is identical and accurate (1). It cuts intricate curves and small details that would be difficult to achieve by hand with a coping saw (1). The laser melts and seals the edge of acrylic, giving a polished edge that needs no filing, saving finishing time (1). It is much quicker for a batch, and the parts can be nested to reduce waste, although the machine needs extraction and set-up time (1).
:::

::: example Naming tools
Name a suitable hand tool for each task: (a) cutting a curve in 6 mm plywood; (b) cutting 1 mm aluminium sheet; (c) removing insulation from a wire. (3)

(a) Coping saw (1). (b) Tin snips (1). (c) Wire strippers (1).
:::

::: example Reading settings
A laser cutter's settings table gives: 3 mm acrylic cut at speed 20, power 70; 6 mm acrylic cut at speed 8, power 90; engraving acrylic at speed 300, power 20. Explain the pattern in these settings. (3)

Thicker material needs more power, 70 up to 90, to cut all the way through (1). It also needs a slower speed, 20 down to 8, so the beam stays on each point longer and cuts deeper (1). Engraving only marks the surface, so it uses low power and a high speed (1).
:::

::: q
Name the tool used to cut straight lines in thin card safely with a craft knife. (1)
---
A safety rule (steel safety rule) on a cutting mat (1).
:::

::: q
Give two advantages of 3D printing a prototype part. (2)
---
Any two: complex/hollow shapes possible; no tooling needed; quick to make and modify from CAD; little waste; accurate.
:::

::: q
Explain why gloves should not be worn when using a centre lathe. (2)
---
The glove could be caught by the rotating chuck or work (1), dragging the hand into the machine and causing serious injury (1).
:::

::: q
Explain why a laser cutter must have fume extraction. (2)
---
Cutting burns or vaporises the material, releasing smoke and harmful fumes (1); extraction removes them so they aren't breathed in and don't damage the lens (1).
:::

::: q
Give three safety precautions when soldering. (3)
---
Any three: keep the iron in its stand; use fume extraction/ventilation; wear goggles; hold components with pliers or a helping-hands clamp; don't touch the tip or joint until cool; lead-free solder; wash hands after.
:::

::: q
Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of an overlocker compared with a standard sewing machine for finishing seams. (4)
---
Advantage: it stitches, trims and neatens the raw edge in one pass (1), so seams are quicker and less likely to fray (1). Disadvantage: it is more complex to thread and set up (1), and it cannot sew straight top-stitching or buttonholes, so a standard machine is still needed (1).
:::

::: watch
- **Specific tool names only.** "Saw" and "machine" score nothing when a type is needed.
- **Specific precautions only.** Each must name the hazard or action for that machine.
- **No gloves on rotating machines**; heat-resistant gloves are for hot work only.
- Laser cutters: **extraction, never unattended, no PVC**.
- When justifying CAM, include **repeatability** and **accuracy**, and give a limitation for balance.
- Don't give the same precaution twice in different words: "wear goggles" and "protect your eyes" is one mark.
:::

::: test
A laser cutter cuts 3 mm MDF at 15 mm per second. A part has a cutting path 1.2 m long. Calculate the cutting time in seconds and minutes. (3)
---
$1.2$ m $= 1200$ mm (1). $1200 \div 15 = 80$ s (1). $80 \div 60 = 1.33$ minutes (1 min 20 s) (1).
:::

::: test
Explain two reasons why dust extraction is needed on a disc sander. (4)
---
Fine dust, especially from MDF, is harmful to breathe (1), so extraction protects the user's lungs (1). Dust on the floor and in the air is a slip and fire risk and makes it hard to see the work (1), so extraction keeps the area safe and clean (1).
:::

::: test
Describe how to make a batch of ten identical acrylic phone stands safely and accurately. (6)
---
5–6: a detailed, logical sequence with appropriate equipment and specific safety at each stage. 3–4: sound sequence with some equipment and safety. 1–2: basic steps. Indicative: design in 2D CAD and nest parts on the sheet; laser cut with extraction on, lid closed, correct speed/power, never unattended; or mark out on protective film and cut with a coping saw in a vice; file and wet-and-dry edges if needed; heat the bend line on a strip heater, wearing heat-resistant gloves; bend over a former or jig to the same angle each time; check each stand against a template or angle gauge; remove protective film; quality check.
:::
