## What it is

Food safety starts long before cooking: in the shop, in the car home, and in the fridge, freezer and cupboard. This topic covers the **temperatures** that control bacteria, the correct use of **fridges and freezers**, **ambient** storage, **date marks** and **covering food**. All temperatures and advice follow **Food Standards Agency (FSA)** guidance.

### The key temperatures

| Temperature | What it means | Effect on bacteria |
|---|---|---|
| **−18 °C** | Freezer | Bacteria are **dormant** — they stop multiplying but are **not killed** |
| **0 °C to below 5 °C** | Fridge (chilling) | Growth of most bacteria is **slowed** (listeria can still grow slowly) |
| **5 °C to 63 °C** | The **danger zone** | Bacteria multiply **rapidly**, fastest at about 37 °C |
| **Above 63 °C** | Hot holding | Bacteria cannot multiply; hot food must be **kept above 63 °C** |
| **75 °C** | Cooking and reheating (core temperature) | **Most** bacteria are killed |

**Reheated** food must reach **75 °C** in the centre (steaming hot all the way through), and food should be reheated **only once**. Food should spend as **little time as possible** in the danger zone: cooked food should be cooled quickly (ideally within about **90 minutes**) and then refrigerated.

### Buying food safely

- **Check date marks**: don't buy food past its use-by date, and choose dates that leave time to use it.
- **Check packaging**: no torn packets, broken seals, or **dented, bulging, rusty or leaking cans** (a bulging can may mean bacteria are producing gas inside).
- **Check temperature**: chilled food should feel cold; frozen food should be frozen solid, with no sign of thawing and refreezing (ice crystals, clumps).
- **Buy chilled and frozen food last** and pack it in a **cool bag**; go straight home and put it away **first**.
- **Keep raw meat and fish separate** from ready-to-eat food, in a separate bag.
- Buy from **clean** shops that keep raw and cooked food apart; a **food hygiene rating** sticker shows how well a business met hygiene standards at its last inspection.

### Ambient storage

**Ambient** means room temperature. Foods with little water or that are sealed and heat-treated keep safely in a **cupboard**: flour, rice, pasta, sugar, cereals, cans, unopened jars, bottles, bread, and vegetables such as potatoes and onions.
- Store in a **cool, dry, dark** place, **off the floor**, away from heat (cookers, sunlight) and cleaning products.
- Once opened, transfer dry foods to **sealed containers** to keep out moisture and **pests** (weevils, mice).
- Use **stock rotation** — **first in, first out**: put new shopping behind older packets.
- Read the label: many jars and sauces say "**once opened, keep refrigerated** and use within X days".
- Never store food in an **opened can** — transfer it to a covered container in the fridge.

### Correct use of a domestic fridge

- Keep the fridge between **0 °C and below 5 °C**, and **check** with a fridge thermometer.
- **Don't overload** it: cold air must circulate to keep every item cold.
- **Don't put hot food in**: it raises the temperature of everything else. Cool it quickly first (spread it out in a shallow dish, divide into portions), then refrigerate within about 90 minutes.
- **Keep the door closed** as much as possible.
- **Clean regularly** and wipe up spills at once.

**Where to put things:**

| Place | What goes there | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Top and middle shelves** | **Ready-to-eat** foods: dairy products, cooked meats, leftovers, desserts | They are above raw food, so nothing can drip onto them |
| **Bottom shelf** | **Raw meat, poultry and fish**, in **sealed containers** or on a plate, covered | If juices leak, they cannot drip onto food that will not be cooked |
| **Salad drawer** | Fruit, vegetables and salad | Higher humidity keeps them crisp; separates soil from other foods |
| **Door** | Milk, drinks, sauces, butter | The warmest part of the fridge, so not for high-risk food like raw meat |

**Covering food** in the fridge (lids, containers, cling film, foil) prevents **cross-contamination**, stops food **drying out**, and stops strong **smells** spreading. Leftovers should be covered, labelled, and eaten **within two days**.

### Correct use of a freezer

- Keep it at **−18 °C** or below.
- Freezing makes bacteria **dormant** but **doesn't kill them**: when food thaws, they become active again.
- **Wrap** food well in freezer bags or containers, squeezing out air, to prevent **freezer burn** (dry, discoloured patches caused by moisture loss).
- **Label and date** everything, and use it within the recommended time.
- **Freeze food before its use-by date** — for example, freeze fresh meat on the day of purchase if it won't be used in time.
- **Defrost in the fridge**, covered, on a plate on the bottom shelf, so the food stays out of the danger zone. Poultry and large joints must be **fully defrosted** before cooking, or the centre may not reach 75 °C.
- **Don't refreeze raw food** that has thawed — unless it is **cooked** first. Cooked food made from frozen raw ingredients can then be frozen.
- Food frozen straight after cooling keeps its quality best; freeze in **portions** so only what is needed is defrosted.

### Date marks

| Date mark | Found on | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Use by** | **Perishable, high-risk** foods: fresh meat and fish, milk and cream, soft cheese, ready meals, bagged salad, cooked sliced meats | A **safety** date. Don't eat, cook or freeze the food after this date, even if it looks and smells fine. Follow storage instructions, e.g. "keep refrigerated" |
| **Best before** | **Longer-life** foods: dried pasta and rice, cereals, canned and frozen food, biscuits, crisps | A **quality** date. After it the food is usually still **safe** but may have lost flavour, colour or texture |
| **Display until / sell by** | Shop labels | For **staff** stock control only; not a guide for consumers |

A use-by date only applies while the pack is **unopened** and **stored correctly**. Once opened, follow the instruction on the pack (for example, "once opened, use within 2 days").

**Understanding date marks cuts waste**: throwing away food only because its best-before date has passed wastes money and food, while eating food past its use-by date risks food poisoning.

::: terms
- **Danger zone**: the temperature range of 5 °C to 63 °C, in which bacteria multiply rapidly.
- **Chilling**: storing food between 0 °C and below 5 °C to slow the growth of bacteria.
- **Freezing**: storing food at −18 °C so bacteria become dormant; they are not killed.
- **Core temperature**: the temperature at the centre of food, which must reach 75 °C when cooking or reheating.
- **Hot holding**: keeping cooked food hot above 63 °C so bacteria cannot multiply before it is served.
- **Ambient storage**: storing foods such as cans, dried goods and unopened jars at room temperature in a cool, dry place.
- **Use-by date**: a safety date on perishable, high-risk foods, after which they should not be eaten.
- **Best-before date**: a quality date on longer-life foods, after which they are usually safe but may be past their best.
- **Stock rotation**: using older food first by placing new purchases behind existing stock — first in, first out.
- **Freezer burn**: dry, discoloured patches on frozen food caused by moisture loss when it is not wrapped properly.
- **Defrosting**: thawing frozen food safely, preferably covered in the fridge so it stays out of the danger zone.
:::

::: method
1. **Quote the exact temperature** the question needs: −18 °C, 0 to below 5 °C, 5–63 °C, 63 °C, 75 °C.
2. **Say what the temperature does to bacteria**: dormant, slowed, multiply rapidly, killed.
3. **For storage rules, give the rule and the reason**: "raw meat on the bottom shelf, so juices cannot drip onto ready-to-eat food".
4. **For date marks**, link the mark to the type of food and to **safety** (use by) or **quality** (best before).
5. **Use the sequence of the food's journey** in longer answers: buying → transporting → storing → using leftovers.
6. **For 12-mark questions**, analyse the risks at each stage, then evaluate the controls — how effective, how practical, and how they also cut waste.
:::

::: example Identify — use-by dates
A student is buying ingredients for a chicken and bean sprout stir fry: raw chicken breast, dried egg noodles, soy sauce, fresh bean sprouts and a tin of water chestnuts. Identify the two ingredients that would carry a use-by date. (2)

Raw chicken breast (1). Fresh bean sprouts (1).
:::

::: example Explain — date marks
Explain the difference between a use-by date and a best-before date. (2)

A use-by date is about safety: it is on perishable, high-risk foods, which should not be eaten after the date even if they look fine (1). A best-before date is about quality: food is usually still safe to eat after it, but may have lost some flavour or texture (1).
:::

::: example Give — fridge rules
Give four rules for using a domestic fridge safely. (4)

Keep it at 0 °C to below 5 °C and check with a thermometer (1). Store raw meat and fish covered on the bottom shelf (1). Don't put hot food in; cool it first (1). Don't overload it, so cold air can circulate (1).
:::

::: example Explain — freezing
Explain why food that has been frozen must be handled as carefully as fresh food once it is defrosted. (3)

Freezing at −18 °C does not kill bacteria; it only makes them dormant (1). When the food thaws, the bacteria become active and begin to multiply again (1). The food must therefore be kept chilled, cooked thoroughly to 75 °C and not refrozen raw, just like fresh food (1).
:::

::: example Explain — shopping
Explain how to keep chilled and frozen food safe when shopping. (4)

Put chilled and frozen food in the trolley last, so it spends the least time out of the fridge or freezer (1). Pack it in a cool bag with ice packs, to keep it below 5 °C or frozen on the way home (1). Pack raw meat separately from ready-to-eat food so juices cannot contaminate it (1). Go straight home and put chilled and frozen food away first (1).
:::

---

- Raw meat goes on the **bottom** shelf **because** juices could drip — always give the reason.
- "Display until" is for **shop staff**, not consumers.
- Ambient storage needs **cool, dry, off the floor, sealed, first in first out** — not just "in a cupboard".
