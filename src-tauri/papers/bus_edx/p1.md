**Section A**

**1.** (a) Which **one** of the following is a feature of a private limited company?

A Its shares are bought and sold on the stock exchange
B Its owners have limited liability
C Its owners have unlimited liability
D It is owned and run by the government

Select one answer. (1)

(b) Which **one** of the following is a short-term source of finance?

A Share capital
B Venture capital
C Trade credit
D Crowdfunding

Select one answer. (1)

(c) Explain **one** way a small business could use social media to increase its sales. (3)

(d) Explain **one** possible disadvantage to a small business of a rise in interest rates. (3)

(8)
---
(a) B (1). A describes a public limited company, C a sole trader or partnership, D a public-sector organisation.

(b) C (1). The other three are long-term sources.

(c) 1 mark for a way, plus up to 2 marks for two linked developments. For example: it can post photos and videos of new products to its followers free of charge (1); followers share the posts, so the business reaches potential customers who did not know about it (1); more of these people visit or order, so sales rise without the cost of paid advertising (1). Or: it can reply to messages and comments quickly (1), which builds a relationship with customers (1), so they return and recommend the business, giving repeat sales (1). An answer that lists several ways without explaining any scores 1 mark at most.

(d) 1 mark for a disadvantage, plus up to 2 for linked developments. For example: interest on existing loans or an overdraft rises (1), which increases the business's costs (1), so its profit falls or it has less cash to pay suppliers (1). Or: customers with mortgages and credit-card debt have less disposable income (1), so they cut back on non-essential spending (1), which reduces the small business's sales and revenue (1). Do not credit "it is harder to borrow" unless it is developed into a cost or a cash consequence.

**2.** (a) Which **two** of the following are methods of **primary** market research?

A Focus group
B Government report
C Observation
D Market report
E Internet search

Select two answers. (2)

(b) Which **two** of the following are **non-financial** objectives for a new business?

A Survival
B Independence
C Market share
D Personal satisfaction
E Profit

Select two answers. (2)

(c) A small bakery has the following monthly figures.

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Loaves sold | 2,400 |
| Selling price | £3.50 per loaf |
| Variable cost | £1.20 per loaf |
| Fixed costs | £2,600 per month |

Using the information in the table, calculate the bakery's monthly profit. You are advised to show your workings. (2)

(d) Explain **one** way a business could use branding to add value to its products. (3)

(e) Explain **one** benefit to customers of increased competition in a market. (3)

(12)
---
(a) A and C (1 mark each). B, D and E are secondary sources.

(b) B and D (1 mark each). Survival, market share and profit are financial objectives.

(c) Revenue = 2,400 × £3.50 = £8,400. Total costs = £2,600 + (2,400 × £1.20) = £2,600 + £2,880 = £5,480 (1). Profit = £8,400 − £5,480 = **£2,920** (1). Award 2 marks for the correct answer without working.

(d) 1 mark for a way, plus up to 2 marks for explaining how it adds value. For example: a strong brand gives the product a recognised name and image that customers trust (1); customers see it as better than unbranded rivals (1); so the business can charge a higher price than the cost of making it, increasing the value added per item (1). The final mark must show added value as a financial idea (a higher price relative to costs); "more customers" on its own does not earn it.

(e) 1 mark for a benefit, plus up to 2 for linked developments. For example: lower prices (1), because businesses cut prices to win customers from rivals (1), so customers pay less and have more income left to spend (1). Or: better quality or service (1), as businesses try to stand out from competitors (1), so customers get better value for money (1). Or: more choice, so customers are more likely to find a product that matches their needs.

**3.** (a) Which **one** of the following is the best definition of a **stakeholder**?

A A person or group that owns shares in a business
B A person or group with an interest in, or affected by, a business's activities
C A customer who buys from a business regularly
D A manager responsible for making business decisions

Select one answer. (1)

(b) A sole trader borrowed £8,000 from a bank. She will repay a total of £9,200.

Calculate the interest on the loan as a percentage. You are advised to show your workings. (2)

(c) Explain **one** reason why a small business needs enough cash to pay its suppliers. (3)

(d) Explain **one** benefit to an entrepreneur of writing a business plan before starting up. (3)

(e) Discuss how the internet could influence the location decision of a small business. (6)

(15)
---
(a) B (1). A defines a shareholder, who is only one type of stakeholder.

(b) Interest = £9,200 − £8,000 = £1,200 (1). £1,200 ÷ £8,000 × 100 = **15%** (1). Award 2 marks for the correct answer without working.

(c) 1 mark for a reason, plus up to 2 for linked developments. For example: if suppliers are not paid on time they may stop delivering stock or materials (1); the business then cannot make or sell its products (1), so it loses sales and could fail (become insolvent) even if it is profitable (1). Or: paying on time builds trust (1), so suppliers offer trade credit in future (1), which helps the business manage its cash flow (1).

(d) 1 mark for a benefit, plus up to 2 for linked developments. For example: it helps the entrepreneur obtain finance (1), because a bank can see the forecast revenue, costs and cash flow (1), so it is more confident the loan will be repaid and is more likely to lend (1). Or: it reduces risk (1), because writing it forces the owner to research the market and the competition (1), so problems such as a cash shortfall can be planned for before money is spent (1).

(e) Indicative content (AO1b understanding, AO3a analysis):
- With e-commerce the business does not need to be near its customers, because they order online from anywhere, so it can choose a cheaper site such as a unit on an industrial estate.
- Lower rent reduces fixed costs, so the business breaks even at a lower output, which reduces the risk for a new business.
- It can sell to customers across the UK rather than only locally, so its potential market is much larger.
- Location becomes more about access to delivery and courier networks and good broadband than about footfall.
- But some businesses still need fixed premises: a hairdresser or café delivers its service face to face, so proximity to the market still decides its location.
- Some businesses combine both: a shop in a high-footfall street plus a website, so the shop acts as a showroom and the internet extends its reach.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): accurate understanding throughout with business terms used well; two developed lines of reasoning with logical, linked chains.
- Level 2 (3–4): mostly accurate understanding; some chains of reasoning, though links may be incomplete.
- Level 1 (1–2): some knowledge of location or the internet; isolated points with little development.

Credit only points about the internet's effect on location; general location factors unconnected to the internet are not rewarded.

**Section B**

**Extract A: Kiln Corner**

Grace Okafor spent eight years as a primary school teacher before opening Kiln Corner, a paint-your-own-pottery studio and café in Shrewsbury, in 2024. Customers choose a plain clay piece (a mug, a plate or a figure), paint it at the studio, and collect it a week later once it has been glazed and fired in the kiln. Grace runs children's parties at weekends and adult evening sessions with music and cake on weekdays.

Grace is a sole trader. She left her teaching job and its regular salary, and put £10,000 of her savings into the business, alongside a £15,000 bank loan, to fit out the studio and buy the kiln. Kiln Corner has 3,800 followers on Instagram, where Grace posts photos of customers' finished pieces. It has an average customer rating of 4.8 out of 5 online.

In 2026, a large craft studio offering pottery wheel classes, candle making and paint-your-own-pottery opened on the other side of the town, with lower prices for children's parties.

**4.** (a) Outline **one** way Kiln Corner could respond to the opening of the new craft studio. (2)

(b) Analyse the impact on Grace of the financial risk she took when starting Kiln Corner. (6)

(8)
---
(a) 2 marks for a linked point in context; 1 mark if the point is not developed or not applied to Kiln Corner. For example: Grace could use her Instagram page to show customers' finished pieces and her 4.8 rating (1), reminding families that Kiln Corner offers a personal service, so they choose it over the larger, cheaper studio (1). Or: she could add something the rival does not offer, such as cake with the adult evening sessions, so customers see better value and are less tempted by lower party prices (1+1). "Lower prices" with no development or context scores 1.

(b) Indicative content (AO2 application, AO3a analysis):
- Grace gave up a teacher's regular salary, so she has lost the security of a guaranteed income; if Kiln Corner has a quiet month she may earn nothing.
- She put £10,000 of her savings in. As a sole trader she has unlimited liability, so if the studio fails she could lose those savings and also be personally responsible for repaying the £15,000 bank loan, putting her home and possessions at risk.
- The loan must be repaid with interest every month, even when sales fall, for example after the rival studio opened with cheaper parties, which increases the pressure on her cash flow.
- On the other hand, taking the risk gives Grace the reward of independence and control over how she runs the studio, and all the profit is hers; strong reviews (4.8 out of 5) suggest the risk may be paying off.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): the case is used throughout (for example savings, loan, teaching salary, sole trader); developed chains of reasoning with clear, logical links.
- Level 2 (3–4): sound use of the case with some gaps; some chains of reasoning, though not fully developed.
- Level 1 (1–2): little or no use of the case; simple points with weak links.

An answer with no application to Kiln Corner cannot score above 3 marks.

**5.** Kiln Corner's adult evening sessions have the following costs and prices.

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Price per adult place | £28 |
| Variable cost per place (clay piece, paint, glaze, firing, cake) | £10 |
| Fixed costs of the evening sessions | £4,500 per month |
| Places sold in March 2026 | 330 |

(a) Using the information in the table, calculate the number of adult places Kiln Corner must sell each month to break even. You are advised to show your workings. (2)

(b) Using the information in the table and your answer to (a), calculate Kiln Corner's margin of safety in March 2026. (2)

(c) Analyse the impact on Kiln Corner of a fall in consumer incomes. (6)

(10)
---
(a) Break even = fixed costs ÷ (selling price − variable cost per unit) = £4,500 ÷ (£28 − £10) (1) = £4,500 ÷ £18 = **250 places** (1). Award 2 marks for the correct answer without working.

(b) Margin of safety = actual sales − break-even sales = 330 − 250 (1) = **80 places** (1). Allow own figure from (a). Do not accept an answer in pounds unless clearly converted (80 × £28 = £2,240 is acceptable if labelled as revenue).

(c) Indicative content (AO2 application, AO3a analysis):
- Painting pottery and parties are non-essential, so when incomes fall families cut back on them first; fewer adult places and parties are booked.
- If adult places fall below 250 a month, the evening sessions stop covering their £4,500 fixed costs and make a loss; March's margin of safety of 80 places gives some protection, but not much.
- Lower bookings reduce cash inflows, while the £15,000 loan repayments continue, so Kiln Corner could face a cash-flow problem.
- Customers may switch to the new, cheaper craft studio, which makes the fall in Kiln Corner's sales larger.
- But Grace could respond with cheaper options, for example smaller pieces at a lower price, to keep customers; and parents may still treat children to a birthday party even when money is tight.

Levels:
- Level 3 (5–6): the case is used throughout (for example the break-even point, the loan, the rival studio); developed, logical chains of reasoning.
- Level 2 (3–4): sound use of the case with some gaps; some developed reasoning.
- Level 1 (1–2): generic points about incomes with weak links to Kiln Corner.

An answer with no application to Kiln Corner cannot score above 3 marks.

**6.** Kiln Corner is open Tuesday to Saturday. Grace wants to increase revenue and is considering two options.

Option 1: open on Sundays and Mondays as well, employing a part-time member of staff to help run the extra days.

Option 2: launch an online shop selling home pottery kits (a plain piece, paints and brushes) by post. Customers would post their painted pieces back to Kiln Corner for glazing and firing.

(a) State **one** cash outflow for Kiln Corner. (1)

(b) Outline **one** benefit to Kiln Corner of accepting contactless card payments. (2)

(c) Justify which **one** of these two options Grace should choose. (9)

(12)
---
(a) Any one, for example: loan repayments to the bank; rent for the studio; payments for clay pieces, paint or glaze; electricity for the kiln; buying cake ingredients (1).

(b) 2 marks for a linked point in context; 1 mark without development or context. For example: customers at parties and evening sessions can pay quickly with a card or phone (1), so queues at the end of a busy children's party are shorter and customers are less likely to leave without buying a cake or an extra piece (1). Or: less cash is kept in the studio, so there is less risk of theft and less time spent banking money (1+1).

(c) Indicative content (AO2 application, AO3a analysis, AO3b evaluation):

Option 1, opening on Sundays and Mondays:
- Two more trading days use the studio and kiln that Grace already pays for, so the fixed costs are spread over more sessions and profit per session rises.
- Sunday is a popular day for children's parties, so demand may be strong.
- But wages for the part-time employee add costs; if Monday sessions are quiet, revenue may not cover them.
- Grace must meet employment law (at least the minimum wage, health and safety), and she gives up her rest days or has to trust a new member of staff.

Option 2, the online shop:
- E-commerce lets Kiln Corner sell to customers anywhere in the UK, not just Shrewsbury, increasing its potential market far beyond what one studio can hold.
- Grace already has 3,800 Instagram followers to promote the kits to at little cost.
- But the kit loses the experience (music, cake, the studio atmosphere), which is how Kiln Corner adds value; customers may see a kit as worth less.
- Posting fragile painted pieces back for firing adds postage costs and the risk of breakages and complaints, which could damage the 4.8 rating.

A conclusion might choose Option 1, because it builds on what customers already value (the experience) and on existing demand, provided there is enough Sunday demand to cover the employee's wages; or Option 2, because it is not limited by the size of the studio, provided the postage and breakage problems can be solved. The conclusion should add a deciding factor rather than repeat earlier points. Either option can score full marks.

Levels:
- Level 3 (7–9): the case is used throughout, including the conclusion; developed chains of reasoning; a well-supported judgement on the chosen option that weighs its benefits against its drawbacks.
- Level 2 (4–6): sound use of the case with some inconsistencies; some developed reasoning; a judgement with some support.
- Level 1 (1–3): limited use of the case; simple points; a judgement that is asserted or missing.

A one-sided answer (only the benefits of the chosen option) cannot reach Level 3, and listing the benefits of both options with no comparison is not evaluation.

**Section C**

**Extract B: FitFix**

Tomasz Nowak and Ellie Shaw set up FitFix in Bristol in 2024 as a partnership. FitFix is a mobile bike repair service: a technician drives a fully equipped van to the customer's home or workplace and services or repairs the bike on the spot. Tomasz had noticed that many commuters bought electric bikes during and after the pandemic, but bike shops often had waiting lists of two weeks or more for repairs. FitFix charges £55 for a standard service and £35 an hour for repairs, plus the cost of parts.

Customers book through FitFix's website, which shows the free slots each day. The business now has more customers than one van can serve, and it wants to buy a second van and employ a second technician.

Before starting, Tomasz and Ellie surveyed 200 local cyclists.

| What matters most when choosing a repair service? | Number of cyclists |
|---|---|
| Price | 46 |
| Speed and convenience | 78 |
| Quality of the repair | 52 |
| Choice of services | 24 |

**7.** (a) State **one** example of a variable cost for FitFix. (1)

(b) Using the information in the table, identify the customer need that was most important to the cyclists surveyed. (1)

(c) Outline **one** reason why the idea for FitFix came about. (2)

(d) FitFix needs £18,000 to buy and equip a second van. It is considering two options.

Option 1: a bank loan repaid over four years.

Option 2: crowdfunding, offering backers rewards such as a free annual service.

Justify which **one** of these two options FitFix should choose. (9)

(13)
---
(a) Any one, for example: spare parts such as tyres, chains or brake pads; fuel for the van (1). Do not accept fixed costs such as insurance, the website or the van's loan repayments.

(b) Speed and convenience (1).

(c) 2 marks for a linked point in context; 1 mark without development or context. For example: Tomasz saw that many commuters had bought electric bikes (1), but bike shops had waiting lists of two weeks or more, so there was a gap for a fast repair service (1). Or: changes in what consumers want: busy commuters want convenience, so a service that comes to their home or workplace meets that need (1+1).

(d) Indicative content (AO2 application, AO3a analysis, AO3b evaluation):

Option 1, a bank loan:
- Tomasz and Ellie keep full ownership and control and all of the extra profit the second van earns.
- The money is available quickly once the loan is approved, so FitFix can meet the extra demand it already has before customers go elsewhere.
- But the loan must be repaid with interest every month for four years, whether or not the second van is busy, which adds to costs and puts pressure on cash flow.
- As partners they have unlimited liability, so if FitFix cannot repay, their personal assets are at risk.

Option 2, crowdfunding:
- There is no interest and no monthly repayment, so cash flow is protected while the second technician builds up customers.
- A campaign is also promotion: backers become loyal customers and tell others, which suits a business with a local Bristol market.
- But the rewards have a cost: every free annual service given to a backer is £55 of work done with no revenue, which reduces future profit.
- The campaign may not reach £18,000, and it takes time to run, delaying the second van while demand is already high.

A conclusion should decide, for example a loan, because the survey shows strong demand for speed and convenience (78 of 200) and FitFix already has more customers than one van can serve, so the repayments should be covered, provided the extra bookings are steady. Or crowdfunding, if the partners want to avoid more personal risk, provided enough backers can be found. A good conclusion adds a deciding factor rather than repeating earlier points.

Levels:
- Level 3 (7–9): the case is used throughout, including the conclusion; developed chains of reasoning; a well-supported judgement that weighs the chosen option's benefits against its drawbacks.
- Level 2 (4–6): sound use of the case with some inconsistencies; some developed reasoning; a judgement with some support.
- Level 1 (1–3): limited use of the case; simple points; a judgement that is asserted or missing.

**7.** (e) FitFix (Extract B) is growing. Tomasz and Ellie are considering changing FitFix from a partnership into a private limited company (Ltd) before they buy the second van and employ their first member of staff.

Evaluate whether FitFix should become a private limited company. You should use the information provided as well as your knowledge of business. (12)
---
Indicative content (AO1b understanding, AO2 application, AO3a analysis, AO3b evaluation):

For becoming a private limited company:
- Limited liability: the company is a separate legal body, so if FitFix fails, Tomasz and Ellie lose only the money they invested, not their homes or savings. This matters more now that they plan to borrow £18,000 and take on an employee's wages.
- Lenders and suppliers may see a limited company as more established, which could make the loan for the second van easier to obtain.
- They can raise money by selling shares to friends, family or an investor, without having to go public, and they keep control by choosing who the shareholders are.
- The business continues if one partner leaves, which reassures customers and staff.

Against:
- Setting up and running a company costs time and money: registration, accounts filed with Companies House each year, possibly an accountant's fees, which a two-person business may struggle to afford.
- Its accounts are publicly available, so rivals can see FitFix's financial information.
- Lenders often ask the directors of a small new company for a personal guarantee, so the protection of limited liability may be weaker in practice than it appears.
- Selling shares to raise money means sharing future profits and some control.

A conclusion might recommend becoming a private limited company now, because FitFix is about to take on a loan and its first employee, so the risk to Tomasz's and Ellie's personal assets is rising; it depends on whether the extra costs of running a company are small compared with the extra profit the second van brings, and on whether the bank still asks for personal guarantees. The conclusion should be justified with evidence from the case.

Levels:
- Level 3 (9–12): accurate understanding throughout; detailed use of the case; developed, logical chains of reasoning; a valid, well-reasoned conclusion that weighs both sides and identifies what the decision depends on.
- Level 2 (5–8): mostly accurate understanding; sound use of the case with some gaps; some chains of reasoning; a conclusion based on some evaluation.
- Level 1 (1–4): some knowledge with limited terminology; limited use of the case; simple points; a conclusion that is asserted or generic.
