intro: AQA GCSE style. The questions follow one business, Brightside Bakes Ltd, a private limited company making cakes for cafés and online customers from a unit in Leeds, with 12 staff. State means a fact; explain means point plus effect on the business; analyse means a chain of effects; recommend means a decision with justification. The paper ramps from grade 4–5 to grade 8–9.

::: q 4-5 3.1.2 3.1.4
**1.** Brightside Bakes Ltd was started by two friends, Amara and Josh, who each invested £15,000.

(a) State two features of a private limited company. (2)

(b) Explain one benefit to Amara and Josh of limited liability. (2)

(c) Identify two stakeholders of Brightside Bakes other than its owners. (2)

(6)
---
(a) Owned by shareholders; shares cannot be sold to the public (1); limited liability; a separate legal identity (1).

(b) If the business fails they lose only their £15,000 investment (1); their homes and savings are protected (1).

(c) Any two of: employees, customers (cafés), suppliers, the bank, the local community, the government (2).
:::

::: q 4-5 3.1.6
**2.** Last month Brightside Bakes sold 8000 cakes at £3.50 each. Its fixed costs were £9,000 and variable costs were £1.75 per cake.

(a) Calculate total revenue. (1)

(b) Calculate total costs. (2)

(c) Calculate the profit. (1)

(d) State one fixed cost a bakery would have. (1)

(5)
---
(a) $8000 \times 3.50 = £28,000$ (1).

(b) Variable $8000 \times 1.75 = £14,000$ (1); total $£23,000$ (1).

(c) $£5,000$ (1).

(d) Rent, insurance, salaries, loan repayments (1).
:::

::: q 4-5 3.4.2 3.4.4
**3.** Brightside Bakes wants to recruit a new baker.

(a) State two methods of external recruitment it could use. (2)

(b) Explain one reason it might use on-the-job training for the new baker. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Job advert online or in the local press; recruitment agency; job centre; social media (2).

(b) The baker learns Brightside's own recipes and equipment while producing (1); it is cheaper than sending them on a course (1).

::: q 6-7 3.6.2
**4.** Brightside Bakes sells to cafés on 30 days' credit but pays its flour supplier on delivery. Its cash-flow forecast for next month shows an opening balance of £2,000, inflows of £21,000 and outflows of £24,500.

(a) Calculate the net cash flow and the closing balance. (2)

(b) Explain why a business can be profitable and still run short of cash. (2)

(c) Explain two ways Brightside could improve its cash flow. (4)

(8)
---
(a) Net cash flow $−£3,500$ (1); closing balance $−£1,500$ (1).

(b) Sales on credit count as revenue before the cash arrives (1); meanwhile suppliers and wages must be paid immediately (1).

(c) Ask cafés to pay within 14 days or offer a prompt-payment discount (2); negotiate credit from the flour supplier, reduce stock, or arrange an overdraft for the shortfall (2).
:::

::: q 6-7 3.1.5 3.2.1
**5.** Brightside Bakes is deciding whether to open a small shop in Leeds city centre or to sell only online.

(a) Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of the city-centre location. (4)

(b) Explain how e-commerce could help Brightside reach more customers. (2)

(6)
---
(a) Advantage: high footfall brings passing customers and raises brand awareness (2); disadvantage: high rent and rates increase fixed costs (2).

(b) Customers anywhere can order 24 hours a day (1); the website costs less than a shop and social media can promote it cheaply (1).

::: q 6-7 3.6.3
**6.** Brightside Bakes has invested £40,000 in a new oven that is expected to increase profits by £12,000 a year for 5 years.

(a) Calculate the average rate of return. (3)

(b) Explain one limitation of using ARR to make this decision. (2)

(5)
---
(a) Total profit $£60,000$; average $£12,000$ a year (1); $12\,000 \div 40\,000 \times 100$ (1) $= 30\%$ (1).

(b) It ignores the timing of the returns and the risk that forecasts are wrong (1); the oven may need repairs or demand may change (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 3.5.2 3.5.4
**7.** Market research shows Brightside's customers value freshness and fast delivery over low price.

(a) Explain how Brightside could use segmentation in its marketing. (3)

(b) Explain one way the research findings should influence the marketing mix. (2)

(5)
---
(a) Divide customers into groups, e.g. cafés and individual online buyers, or by occasion such as birthdays (1); design products and promotion for each group (1); target spending on the groups most likely to buy (1).

(b) Place/product: offer next-day or same-day delivery and promote freshness (1); price can stay premium rather than competing on cost (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 3.4.1
**8.** Brightside Bakes has grown to 25 staff. Amara wants to introduce a tall organisational structure with supervisors; Josh prefers to keep the flat structure and delegate more.

Analyse the effects on the business of Josh's approach. (6)
---
Delegation frees the owners to focus on growth and strategy (1); staff given responsibility feel trusted, raising motivation and productivity (1); decisions are made quickly by those closest to the work (1); but with 25 staff the span of control is wide, so mistakes may go unnoticed (1); inexperienced staff may need training before they can be trusted with decisions (1); quality could suffer if control is lost — the outcome depends on staff skills and how well delegation is managed (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 3.6.2 3.1.7 3.2.6
**9.** A national supermarket chain has offered to stock Brightside's cakes, but wants a 30% lower price and 60 days' credit.

Recommend whether Brightside Bakes should accept the offer. Justify your answer. (9)
---
For: a huge increase in sales volume and brand awareness (1); economies of scale as bulk buying and longer production runs cut unit costs (1); a stable long-term contract (1). Against: a 30% price cut leaves a thin margin — with variable costs of £1.75, a £2.45 price gives only 70p per cake (1); 60 days' credit worsens the cash-flow problem already identified (1); dependence on one customer that can dictate terms (1); capacity — 12 staff and one unit may not cope, so investment and borrowing are needed (1). Judgement: accept only if the margin covers the extra costs and finance for the cash gap is arranged, or negotiate better terms; otherwise the risk to cash flow could sink a business that is profitable on paper (2).
:::

::: q 8-9 3.2.3 3.2.4
**10.** Brightside's flour supplier has raised prices by 20% after poor harvests, and the pound has fallen against the euro, where Brightside buys its packaging.

Analyse how these changes in the external environment might affect Brightside's profit. (6)
---
Variable costs rise, so the profit per cake falls (1); the weaker pound makes imported packaging more expensive in pounds, raising costs further (1); if Brightside raises prices, sales may fall — though its customers value quality over price, so demand may be inelastic (1); if it absorbs the costs, profit margins shrink (1); it could seek cheaper suppliers, buy in bulk, or reduce packaging (1); overall profit is likely to fall in the short term, with the size depending on how much cost it can pass on (1).
:::
