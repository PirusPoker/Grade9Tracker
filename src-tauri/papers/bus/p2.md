intro: AQA GCSE style. The questions follow Fernwood Bikes Ltd, a Bristol company with 40 staff that builds bicycles to order and is launching a standard model, run by its founder Priya. State means a fact; explain means point plus effect on the business; analyse means a chain of effects; recommend means a decision with justification. The paper ramps from grade 4–5 to grade 8–9.

::: q 4-5 3.1.1 3.1.3
**1.** Priya started Fernwood Bikes in her garage eight years ago.

(a) State two characteristics of a successful entrepreneur. (2)

(b) Explain why a business's objectives change as it grows. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Any two: takes calculated risks; determined and hard-working; creative; good at making decisions; organised; willing to learn from mistakes (2).

(b) At the start the aim is survival — enough sales to cover costs (1); once established, the owner can aim for profit, growth or market share, and new shareholders bring their own objectives (1).
:::

::: q 4-5 3.5.3
**2.** Before launching its standard model, Fernwood carried out market research.

(a) State one method of primary research and one method of secondary research it could use. (2)

(b) Explain one benefit of running a focus group with cyclists. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Primary: survey, interview, focus group, observation, test marketing (1); secondary: industry reports, government statistics, competitors' websites, sales records (1).

(b) Detailed opinions on the design, price and features in the customers' own words (1); so the model can be changed before money is spent on production (1).
:::

::: q 4-5 3.3.1
**3.** Fernwood has always used job production. The standard model will be made by flow production.

(a) Define job production. (1)

(b) Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to Fernwood of using flow production for the standard model. (4)

(5)
---
(a) Making one product at a time to the customer's specification (1).

(b) Advantage: large numbers can be made quickly with lower unit costs, as tasks are specialised and machinery is used (2); disadvantage: high set-up cost for the production line and the workers may be bored by repetitive tasks, or a breakdown stops everything (2).
:::

::: q 4-5 3.6.1
**4.** Fernwood needs £120,000 for a frame-welding machine.

(a) State two sources of finance it could use. (2)

(b) Explain one drawback of using a bank loan. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Any two: bank loan; retained profit; hire purchase or leasing; share capital; trade credit; overdraft (2).

(b) Interest must be paid, increasing costs (1); repayments are due whether or not sales are good, and the bank may want security such as the premises (1).
:::

::: q 4-5 3.2.5
**5.** Fernwood is recruiting ten workers for its new production line.

(a) Name one law that affects how Fernwood recruits, and one law that protects its customers. (2)

(b) Explain one consequence for Fernwood of breaking health and safety law. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Equality Act (no discrimination in recruitment); National Minimum Wage Act (1); Consumer Rights Act (goods must be of satisfactory quality) (1).

(b) Fines or being closed down by inspectors, and compensation claims from injured workers (1); bad publicity that damages sales and makes recruitment harder (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 3.6.4
**6.** Last year Fernwood's revenue was £900,000, its cost of sales £540,000 and its other expenses £240,000. The year before, its gross profit margin was 35%.

(a) Calculate gross profit. (1)

(b) Calculate the gross profit margin. (2)

(c) Calculate net profit and the net profit margin. (2)

(d) State what the change in gross profit margin suggests. (1)

(6)
---
(a) $900\,000 - 540\,000 = £360\,000$ (1).

(b) $360\,000 \div 900\,000 \times 100$ (1) $= 40\%$ (1).

(c) Net profit $£120\,000$ (1); margin $13.3\%$ (1).

(d) Fernwood is buying materials more cheaply or charging more per bike — each £1 of sales now earns more gross profit (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 3.3.2 3.3.3
**7.** Fernwood plans to hold parts for the standard model using just-in-time (JIT) stock control.

(a) Explain one advantage and one risk of JIT for Fernwood. (4)

(b) Explain the difference between quality control and quality assurance. (2)

(6)
---
(a) Advantage: little money tied up in stock and less storage space, so cash flow improves (2); risk: if a supplier is late with frames or tyres the production line stops and orders are missed (2).

(b) Quality control inspects finished bikes and removes faulty ones (1); quality assurance builds checks into every stage so faults are prevented, with every worker responsible (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 3.4.3
**8.** Priya is considering how to pay and motivate the production-line workers.

(a) Explain how paying a piece rate might affect the workers and the business. (2)

(b) Explain how job enrichment might affect them. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Workers are paid per bike, so output rises as they work faster (1); but quality may fall as they rush, and pay is insecure when demand is low (1).

(b) Giving workers more responsibility, e.g. checking their own quality or rotating tasks (1); makes the work more interesting, raising motivation and reducing staff turnover (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 3.5.1 3.3.4
**9.** Fernwood offers a free service and safety check six months after each sale.

Explain two ways this after-sales service benefits the business. (4)
---
Customers are more satisfied and more likely to buy again or recommend Fernwood, so repeat sales rise (2); it gives Fernwood information about faults and what customers want, and a chance to sell accessories, building customer loyalty in a competitive market (2).
:::

::: q 8-9 3.2.2
**10.** A supplier in another country offers Fernwood frames 30% cheaper. Its factories are known for poor working conditions and high pollution.

Analyse the effects on Fernwood of switching to this supplier. (6)
---
Costs fall, so profit margins rise or prices can be cut to compete (1); this could fund expansion or the new model (1); but Fernwood sells to environmentally aware cyclists, who may boycott it if the supplier's record becomes known (1); bad publicity on social media could damage the brand it has built over eight years (1); staff may be demotivated by working for a business that ignores ethics (1); the decision depends on whether the cost saving outweighs the risk to reputation — it may be better to pay more and market the ethical sourcing (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 3.6.1 3.1.7
**11.** To expand, Fernwood needs £500,000. Priya could sell 40% of the shares to a venture capitalist or take a ten-year bank loan.

Recommend which source of finance Fernwood should use. Justify your answer. (9)
---
Venture capital: no repayments or interest, so cash flow is protected while the new line starts up (1); the investor brings expertise and contacts (1); but Priya gives up 40% of ownership and control, shares future profits, and the investor may push for rapid growth or a sale (2). Loan: Priya keeps full control and all the profit (1); interest is a known cost and the loan ends after ten years (1); but repayments are fixed whatever happens to sales, adding risk in a downturn, and the bank will want security (2). Judgement, e.g. a loan if the forecast cash flow comfortably covers repayments and Priya values control; venture capital if sales are uncertain or she needs expertise to grow — either accepted with reasons tied to the case (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 3.2.6
**12.** Explain how uncertainty about future demand for bicycles should affect Priya's decision to expand. (4)
---
If demand falls, the extra capacity and fixed costs of the expansion could not be covered, leading to losses (1); so Priya should research the market, consider a smaller or staged expansion, and keep a cash reserve (1); she could use flexible options such as leasing rather than buying equipment (1); the higher the uncertainty, the more cautious the plan and the more important it is to have finance that does not require fixed repayments (1).
:::
