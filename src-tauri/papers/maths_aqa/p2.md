intro: AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) Higher tier, Paper 2/3 style: 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, answer every question and show your working. A calculator is allowed, but method marks still need the working written down. Questions 1 to 4 are multiple choice (circle one answer). The questions climb from grade 4–5 to grade 8–9, so the last third is where the top grades are decided. In the mark scheme M marks are for a correct method, A marks for an accurate answer that depends on the method, and B marks stand alone; oe means "or equivalent" and ft means "follow through" from an earlier slip.

::: q 6-7 A7
**15.** $f(x) = 2x + 5$ and $g(x) = x^2 - 3$

(a) Find $f^{-1}(x)$. (2)

(b) Solve $gf(x) = 6$. (3)

(5)
---
(a) M1 $y = 2x + 5$ rearranged to $x = \dfrac{y - 5}{2}$, or the inverse operations in the right order. A1 $f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x - 5}{2}$.

(b) M1 $gf(x) = (2x + 5)^2 - 3$.

M1 $(2x + 5)^2 = 9$, so $2x + 5 = \pm 3$ (or the expanded quadratic $4x^2 + 20x + 16 = 0$).

A1 $x = -1$ and $x = -4$.

**16.** (a) Show that the equation $x^3 - 5x - 3 = 0$ has a solution between $x = 2$ and $x = 3$. (2)

(b) Show that $x^3 - 5x - 3 = 0$ can be rearranged to give $x = \sqrt[3]{5x + 3}$. (1)

(c) Starting with $x_0 = 2.5$, use the iteration formula $x_{n+1} = \sqrt[3]{5x_n + 3}$ to work out $x_1$, $x_2$ and $x_3$. Give each value to 4 decimal places. (2)

(5)
---
(a) M1 substitutes both values: $2^3 - 10 - 3 = -5$ and $3^3 - 15 - 3 = 9$.

A1 there is a change of sign (and the function is continuous), so there is a solution between 2 and 3.

(b) B1 $x^3 = 5x + 3$, then take the cube root of both sides.

(c) M1 $x_1 = \sqrt[3]{15.5} = 2.4933$.

A1 $x_2 = 2.4915$ and $x_3 = 2.4910$.

**17.** A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 18 m/s in 12 seconds. It travels at 18 m/s for 20 seconds, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 8 seconds.

(a) Work out the acceleration of the car in the first 12 seconds. (1)

(b) Work out the total distance travelled by the car. (2)

(3)
---
(a) B1 $18 \div 12 = 1.5\text{ m/s}^2$ (the gradient of the velocity–time graph).

(b) M1 area under the velocity–time graph, e.g. $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 18 + 20 \times 18 + \dfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 18$, or the trapezium $\dfrac{1}{2}(40 + 20) \times 18$.

A1 540 m.

**19.** A rectangle has length $(2x + 3)$ cm and width $(x - 1)$ cm. Its area is $40\text{ cm}^2$.

(a) Show that $2x^2 + x - 43 = 0$ (2)

(b) Work out the value of $x$. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. (2)

(4)
---
(a) M1 $(2x + 3)(x - 1) = 40$. A1 $2x^2 - 2x + 3x - 3 = 40$, so $2x^2 + x - 43 = 0$, with no errors.

(b) M1 $x = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 - 4 \times 2 \times (-43)}}{2 \times 2} = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{345}}{4}$.

A1 $x = 4.39$, rejecting the negative solution ($-4.89$) because the width $x - 1$ must be positive.
:::

**20.** Solve the simultaneous equations

$x^2 + y^2 = 25$

$y = x + 1$

You must show your working. (5)
---
M1 substitutes: $x^2 + (x + 1)^2 = 25$.

M1 expands and rearranges: $2x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0$, i.e. $x^2 + x - 12 = 0$.

M1 solves: $(x + 4)(x - 3) = 0$.

A1 $x = 3$ and $x = -4$.

A1 $y = 4$ and $y = -3$, paired correctly: $(3, 4)$ and $(-4, -3)$.

**22.** A bag contains $n$ counters. Exactly 4 of the counters are red. Two counters are taken at random from the bag without replacement.

The probability that both counters are red is $\dfrac{1}{11}$.

(a) Show that $n^2 - n - 132 = 0$ (2)

(b) Work out the value of $n$. (2)

(4)
---
(a) M1 $\dfrac{4}{n} \times \dfrac{3}{n - 1} = \dfrac{1}{11}$. A1 $n(n - 1) = 132$, so $n^2 - n - 132 = 0$, with no errors.

(b) M1 $(n - 12)(n + 11) = 0$. A1 $n = 12$ (as $n$ must be positive).
:::

| Mass, $m$ grams | Frequency density |
|---|---|
| $0 < m \le 10$ | 1.5 |
| $10 < m \le 25$ | 2.4 |
| $25 < m \le 30$ | 6.0 |

(b) Estimate the median mass. (2)

(4)

**24.** The value of a machine, £$V$, after $t$ years is given by $V = A \times k^t$, where $A$ and $k$ are constants.

After 2 years the machine is worth £12,800. After 5 years it is worth £6553.60.

(a) Work out the values of $A$ and $k$. (3)

(b) Explain what the value of $k$ tells you about how the value of the machine changes. (1)

(4)
---
(a) M1 divides the equations: $k^3 = \dfrac{6553.60}{12\,800} = 0.512$.

A1 $k = 0.8$.

A1 $A = \dfrac{12\,800}{0.8^2} = 20\,000$.

(b) B1ft the value falls by 20% each year (it is multiplied by 0.8 every year).
