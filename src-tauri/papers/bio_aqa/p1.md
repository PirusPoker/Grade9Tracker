**04** A student investigated osmosis in potato tissue.

She cut five potato cylinders, blotted them dry and weighed them. She put each cylinder in a different concentration of sugar solution. After 24 hours she removed each cylinder, blotted it dry and weighed it again.

|---|---|---|---|

**04.2** Use Table 2 to estimate the concentration of the solution inside the potato cells. (1)

**05.1** Which blood vessel carries blood from the heart to the lungs?

- **A** aorta
- **B** pulmonary artery
- **C** pulmonary vein
- **D** vena cava

(1)

**05.2** In coronary heart disease, layers of fatty material build up inside the coronary arteries.

Explain why this can damage the heart muscle. (2)

**05.3** Stents and statins are two treatments for coronary heart disease.

Evaluate the use of stents compared with statins. (4)

(7)
---
**05.1** B (1).

**05.2** The coronary arteries narrow, so less blood flows to the heart muscle (1); the muscle receives less oxygen (and glucose) for (aerobic) respiration, so cells may be damaged or die (1).

**05.3** Levels of response.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): a detailed evaluation that weighs advantages and disadvantages of **both** treatments and comes to a reasoned judgement.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple statements about one or both treatments, with little comparison.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- a stent is a tube placed in the coronary artery to keep it open, so blood flow to the heart muscle is restored straight away
- a stent is usually a one-off procedure, so the patient does not have to remember to take medicine
- inserting a stent needs surgery, with risks such as infection, bleeding or a blood clot / heart attack during the procedure
- the artery may narrow again, or a clot may form in the stent
- statins reduce the level of (LDL) cholesterol in the blood, which slows the rate at which fatty material is deposited
- statins need no surgery and are cheap
- statins must be taken every day for life, take time to work, and do not unblock an artery that is already narrow
- statins can have side effects, such as muscle pain or liver problems
- judgement: for example, a stent suits a patient whose artery is already badly narrowed; statins suit a patient at earlier risk, or one too unwell for surgery

**06** A student investigated the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed.

She put the pondweed in a test tube of sodium hydrogencarbonate solution and placed a lamp at different distances from it. She collected the gas given off in a gas syringe for 4 minutes at each distance.

**06.1** Light intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the lamp.

At 10 cm from the lamp the light intensity was 160 arbitrary units.

Calculate the light intensity at 40 cm from the lamp. (2)

**06.2** With the lamp 20 cm away, 0.60 cm³ of gas was collected in 4 minutes.

**06.4** Above a certain light intensity, the rate of photosynthesis stopped increasing.

Explain why. (2)

**06.3** To absorb heat from the lamp so that the temperature of the pondweed stays constant / temperature is not a variable (1). Allow "to act as a heat shield".

**07** A student used a potometer to measure the rate of water uptake by a leafy shoot.

An air bubble in the capillary tube moved 45 mm in 10 minutes. The radius of the capillary tube is 0.5 mm.

**07.1** Calculate the rate of water uptake in mm³ per minute.

Use the equation: volume = π × r² × length

**07.2** The student then placed a fan next to the shoot.

Explain how this would change the rate of water uptake. (2)

**07.3** On a hot, dry day many plants close their stomata.

Explain how guard cells close the stomata, and why this helps the plant. (2)

(7)

**07.1** volume = π × 0.5² × 45 (1)

= 35.3 mm³ (1)

::: q 8-9 4.4.2
**09** A runner ran hard for 10 minutes. Table 3 shows the concentration of lactic acid in her blood.

**Table 3**

| Time | Lactic acid concentration (mmol/dm³) |
|---|---|
| at rest, before the run | 1.0 |
| at the end of the run | 8.0 |
| 10 minutes after the run | 4.5 |
| 30 minutes after the run | 1.5 |

**09.1** Explain why the runner's muscles respired anaerobically during the run. (2)

**09.2** The runner's breathing rate stayed high for some time after the run.

Use Table 3 and your knowledge of oxygen debt to explain why. (4)

(6)
---
**09.1** Even with a raised heart rate and breathing rate, not enough oxygen could be supplied to the muscles (1); anaerobic respiration transfers energy without oxygen, so the muscles could keep contracting (1).

**09.2** Any four (4):

- anaerobic respiration in the muscles produced lactic acid, which built up to 8.0 mmol/dm³ by the end of the run
- an oxygen debt formed: the extra oxygen needed to react with the lactic acid and remove it from the cells
- blood flowing through the muscles carries the lactic acid to the liver
- in the liver the lactic acid is converted back into glucose, which needs oxygen
- the lactic acid was still 4.5 mmol/dm³ after 10 minutes, so the debt had not yet been repaid; breathing (and heart rate) stay high until it falls back towards the resting 1.0 mmol/dm³

At least one point must use data from Table 3 for full marks.

::: q 8-9 4.3.2 4.3.1b
**10.1** Describe how monoclonal antibodies are produced. (4)

**10.2** New drugs are tested in clinical trials. Many trials are double-blind and use a placebo.

Explain what a double-blind trial is, and why it is used. (2)

(6)
---
**10.1** Any four (4):

- a mouse is injected with an antigen, so its lymphocytes make the specific antibody
- these lymphocytes are combined / fused with a tumour cell
- this makes a hybridoma cell
- the hybridoma cell can both divide (rapidly) and make the antibody
- a single hybridoma cell is cloned to produce many identical cells, all making the same antibody
- a large amount of the antibody is collected and purified

**10.2** Neither the volunteers / patients nor the doctors know who is given the drug and who is given the placebo (1); this prevents bias in the results, for example a doctor treating or assessing the groups differently (1).

::: q 8-9 4.2.2b 4.1.3
**12** Muscle cells need oxygen for aerobic respiration.

Explain how the lungs, the blood and the blood vessels are adapted to deliver oxygen efficiently from the air to muscle cells. (6)
---
Levels of response.

Level 3 (5–6 marks): a detailed, coherent explanation that links adaptations of the lungs, blood **and** blood vessels to how they increase the rate of diffusion or transport of oxygen, following the oxygen from the air to the muscle.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): adaptations from at least two of lungs, blood and vessels are given, with some linked to their function.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple statements of adaptations, with little or no explanation.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- the lungs contain millions of alveoli, giving a very large surface area for diffusion
- alveolar walls and capillary walls are one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance
- ventilation brings in air with a high oxygen concentration, and a rich blood supply carries oxygen away, keeping a steep concentration gradient
- red blood cells contain haemoglobin, which binds oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin
- red blood cells have no nucleus (more room for haemoglobin) and a biconcave shape (large surface area)
- the heart is a double pump: the left ventricle has a thick muscular wall to pump oxygenated blood at high pressure round the body
- arteries have thick, muscular, elastic walls to withstand the high pressure
- capillaries form networks close to every muscle cell; their thin walls give a short diffusion distance
- muscle cells use oxygen in respiration, so oxygen keeps diffusing in down its concentration gradient
