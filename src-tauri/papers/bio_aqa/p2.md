Level 2 (3–4 marks): a detailed evaluation that weighs advantages and disadvantages of **both** treatments and comes to a reasoned judgement.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple statements about one or both treatments, with little comparison.

**10** A population of mice lived on a large island. Rising sea levels split the island into two smaller islands. One island is covered in dark forest; the other is mostly pale sand dunes.

After many thousands of years, mice from the two islands can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring.

Explain how the two populations became two different species. (6)
---
Levels of response.

Level 3 (5–6 marks): a detailed, logically ordered explanation of speciation that links isolation, variation, different selection pressures, natural selection over many generations and the final inability to interbreed.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): an explanation of natural selection in at least one population, with some reference to isolation or to the populations becoming unable to interbreed.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple, unlinked statements about isolation, variation or survival.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- the populations are geographically isolated by the sea, so they cannot interbreed and alleles are not exchanged between them
- each population has genetic variation, and new alleles arise by random mutation
- the environments differ, so the selection pressures differ (e.g. predators, food, camouflage against dark forest floor or pale sand)
- on the dune island, paler mice are better camouflaged, so they are more likely to survive and breed; on the forest island darker mice are favoured
- the survivors pass on their advantageous alleles to their offspring
- over many generations the frequency of these alleles increases — natural selection
- the populations change in different ways and their genes become so different that they can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring
- they are now separate species (speciation)

**11** A student investigated the effect of temperature on the decay of milk.

She mixed milk, sodium carbonate solution and the enzyme lipase at 30 °C. She measured the pH with a pH probe. The pH fell from 8.5 to 7.0 in 6 minutes.

**11.1** Explain why the pH fell. (2)

Explain why using peat as compost increases the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. (2)

(7)
---
**11.1** Lipase breaks down the fat (lipid) in the milk into fatty acids and glycerol (1); the fatty acids lower the pH (1).

**11.2** (8.5 − 7.0) ÷ 6 = 0.25 pH units per minute (1).

**11.3** The pH falls more slowly / does not fall (1); the high temperature denatures the lipase, so its active site changes shape and fat no longer fits (1).

**11.4** In the garden the peat is exposed to oxygen, so microorganisms decay it (allow: it may be burned) (1); their respiration releases the carbon stored in the peat as carbon dioxide (1).
