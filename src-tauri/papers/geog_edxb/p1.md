**1 (e)** Figure 5 describes a plate boundary. Two oceanic plates are moving away from each other. Magma rises through the gap to form new crust and a chain of underwater volcanoes.

Identify the type of plate boundary described in Figure 5. (1)
A. Conservative
B. Convergent
C. Divergent
D. Collision

**(f)** Explain one reason why tectonic plates move. (2)

**(g)** Explain two reasons why volcanoes at convergent plate boundaries are usually more explosive than volcanoes at hotspots. (4)

**(h)** Explain one way that countries can prepare for tropical cyclones, such as using satellite technology. (3)

(10)
---
(e) C (1).

(f) 1 mark for a reason, 1 for development. E.g. heat from radioactive decay in the core creates convection currents in the mantle (1), which drag the plates above them (1). Accept slab pull: the denser edge of an oceanic plate sinks into the mantle (1) and pulls the rest of the plate with it (1).

(g) 2 × (1 mark for a reason + 1 for development). E.g.
- At convergent boundaries the subducting plate melts to form andesitic magma, which is viscous (1), so it traps gas until pressure builds and is released violently (1).
- Hotspot magma is basaltic, runny and low in silica (1), so gas escapes easily and lava flows out gently to build shield volcanoes (1).
- Subducted ocean crust carries sea water into the mantle (1), adding dissolved gas and steam to the magma that makes eruptions more explosive (1).

(h) 1 mark for a way, up to 2 for development. E.g. satellites track the storm's path and strength (1), so forecasters can predict where it will reach land several days in advance (1) and issue warnings that give time for evacuation, saving lives (1). Accept evacuation plans, storm-surge barriers or shelters, each developed with how it reduces impact.

**2 (c)** Explain two reasons why physical geography can contribute to global inequality. (4)

**(d)** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of using large infrastructure projects funded by inter-governmental organisations (IGOs) to increase development. (4)

**(e)** Explain one difference between Rostow's modernisation theory and Frank's dependency theory. (2)

(10)
---
(c) 2 × (1 mark for a reason + 1 for development). E.g.
- Landlocked countries have no direct sea access (1), so imports and exports cost more, which discourages trade and investment (1).
- Hot, dry climates bring drought and crop failure (1), keeping many people in subsistence farming and poverty (1).
- Tropical climates have diseases such as malaria (1), which reduce the number of people able to work and the money available for development (1).
- Mountainous topography makes roads and services expensive to build (1), isolating rural communities (1).

(d) 1 + 1 for an advantage, 1 + 1 for a disadvantage. E.g. advantage: a large dam can provide electricity for industry and irrigation water for farms (1), helping the national economy grow quickly (1). Disadvantage: loans may have to be repaid with interest (1), adding to national debt; or large projects can displace thousands of people and benefit cities more than the rural poor (1).

(e) 1 for a difference, 1 for development. E.g. Rostow suggests every country can develop by passing through five stages towards high mass consumption (1), whereas Frank argues that rich core countries keep poorer periphery countries dependent and underdeveloped through trade and investment (1).

AO2 (4 marks) / AO3 (4 marks). Mark in levels.

Indicative content (Nepal 2015 shown; credit any valid example):
- AO2: Nepal is a low-income country with poorly enforced building codes, so many unreinforced brick and stone buildings collapsed in the magnitude 7.8 earthquake of April 2015, killing about 9,000 people.
