**1.** Lena buys a car for £14,500. The value of the car depreciates by 12% each year.

Work out the value of the car after 3 years. Give your answer to the nearest pound. (3)
---
Multiplier $1 - 0.12 = 0.88$ (M1); $14\,500 \times 0.88^3$ (M1); $£9881$ (A1) (from $9881.344$). (Taking off $3 \times 12\% = 36\%$ gives £9280: simple interest, 0 marks.)

::: q 4-5 G16-18
**3.** A sector of a circle has radius 9 cm and angle $140^\circ$.

Work out the perimeter of the sector. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. (3)
---
Arc length $= \dfrac{140}{360} \times 2 \times \pi \times 9$ (M1) $= 21.99$ … cm; perimeter $= 21.99 + 9 + 9$ (M1); $40.0$ cm (A1) (accept $39.99$). (The arc alone, $22.0$ cm, gets M1 only.)
:::

::: q 4-5 A23-25
**4.** Here are the first four terms of an arithmetic sequence.

5, 11, 17, 23

(a) Find an expression, in terms of $n$, for the $n$th term. (2)

(b) Is 301 a term of the sequence? Give a reason for your answer. (1)

(3)
---
(a) $6n$ (M1, for $6n + k$ with $k \neq -1$); $6n - 1$ (A1).

(b) No, because $6n - 1 = 301$ gives $n = 50.33$ …, which is not a whole number (C1). (Or: the 50th term is 299 and the 51st term is 305.)

(b) $n$ is an integer with $-3 \le 2n + 1 < 7$. Write down all the possible values of $n$. (2)

(4)
---
(a) $3x > 18$ (M1); $x > 6$ (A1). (An answer of $x = 6$ loses the A mark.)

(b) $-4 \le 2n < 6$, so $-2 \le n < 3$ (M1); $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2$ (A1: all five, no extras).

(a) Work out the angle the ramp makes with the ground. Give your answer correct to 1 decimal place. (2)

(b) Work out the horizontal distance covered by the ramp. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. (2)

(4)

| Number | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probability | 0.35 | 0.2 | $2x$ | $x$ |

(b) The spinner is spun 200 times. Work out an estimate for the number of times it lands on 4. (1)

(3)

**8.** The table shows information about the time, $t$ minutes, that 40 students took to travel to school.

| Time ($t$ minutes) | $0 < t \le 10$ | $10 < t \le 20$ | $20 < t \le 30$ | $30 < t \le 50$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 14 | 11 | 7 |

(a) Work out an estimate for the mean time. (3)

(b) Explain why your answer to (a) is only an estimate. (1)

(4)
---
(a) Midpoints $5, 15, 25, 40$ (M1); $40 + 210 + 275 + 280 = 805$ (M1); $805 \div 40 = 20.1$ minutes (A1) (accept $20.125$).

(b) The exact times are not known: the midpoint of each class is used for every student in it (C1).

**9.** An alloy is made by mixing copper and zinc in the ratio $7 : 3$ by mass.

The density of copper is 8.96 g/cm$^3$. The density of zinc is 7.13 g/cm$^3$.

A block of the alloy has a mass of 500 g. Work out the density of the alloy. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. (4)
---
Copper $= 350$ g and zinc $= 150$ g (P1); volumes $350 \div 8.96 = 39.06$ … cm$^3$ and $150 \div 7.13 = 21.04$ … cm$^3$ (P1); total volume $60.10$ … cm$^3$, and $500 \div 60.10$ … (P1); $8.32$ g/cm$^3$ (A1). (Averaging the two densities, e.g. $0.7 \times 8.96 + 0.3 \times 7.13 = 8.41$, is wrong: densities do not combine by mass.)
:::

**10.** A metal cube has sides of length 4.2 cm, correct to the nearest millimetre. The mass of the cube is 620 g, correct to the nearest 10 g.

Work out the upper bound for the density of the metal. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. (3)
---
Bounds $625$ g and $4.15$ cm used (B1); upper bound $= \dfrac{\text{upper mass}}{(\text{lower length})^3} = \dfrac{625}{4.15^3} = \dfrac{625}{71.47}$ … (M1); $8.74$ g/cm$^3$ (A1) (from $8.7445$ …).

::: q 6-7 S1
**11.** Ecologists want to estimate the number of fish in a lake. They catch 30 fish, mark them and put them back. A week later they catch 40 fish, and 6 of these are marked.

(a) Work out an estimate for the number of fish in the lake. (2)

(b) State one assumption you have made. (1)

(3)
---
(a) $\dfrac{6}{40} = \dfrac{30}{N}$, or $30 \times 40 \div 6$ (M1); $200$ fish (A1).

(b) One of: the marked fish mix evenly with the others; no fish are born, die, arrive or leave between the two samples; the marks do not come off; marked fish are no easier or harder to catch (C1).

**12.** In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 8$ cm, $AC = 11$ cm and angle $BAC = 64^\circ$.

(a) Work out the length of $BC$. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. (3)

(b) Work out the area of triangle $ABC$. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. (2)

(5)

::: q 6-7 A20
**13.** (a) Show that the equation $x^3 + 4x - 7 = 0$ has a solution between $x = 1$ and $x = 2$. (2)

(b) Show that the equation $x^3 + 4x - 7 = 0$ can be rearranged to give $x = \dfrac{7}{x^2 + 4}$. (1)

(c) Starting with $x_0 = 1$, use the iteration formula $x_{n+1} = \dfrac{7}{x_n^2 + 4}$ three times to find an estimate for a solution of $x^3 + 4x - 7 = 0$. Give your answer correct to 4 decimal places. (3)

(6)
---
(a) Substitute both values: $1 + 4 - 7 = -2$ and $8 + 8 - 7 = 9$ (M1); there is a change of sign between $-2$ and $9$ (and the function is continuous), so there is a solution between 1 and 2 (A1).

(b) $x^3 + 4x = 7$, so $x(x^2 + 4) = 7$, giving $x = \dfrac{7}{x^2 + 4}$ (C1: the factorising step must be seen).

(c) $x_1 = \dfrac{7}{1 + 4} = 1.4$ (M1); $x_2 = \dfrac{7}{1.96 + 4} = 1.17449$ … (M1); $x_3 = 1.3013$ (A1) (from $1.30125$ …).
:::

::: q 6-7 G19
**14.** Two containers are mathematically similar. The surface area of the smaller container is 150 cm$^2$ and the surface area of the larger container is 384 cm$^2$. The volume of the smaller container is 250 cm$^3$.

Work out the volume of the larger container. (3)
---
Area scale factor $\dfrac{384}{150} = 2.56$ (P1); length scale factor $\sqrt{2.56} = 1.6$ and volume scale factor $1.6^3 = 4.096$ (P1); $250 \times 4.096 = 1024$ cm$^3$ (A1). (Using $250 \times 2.56 = 640$ uses the area factor on a volume: P1 only.)
:::

After how many whole years will the investment first be worth more than £6000? You must show your working. (3)

(b) Write down the coordinates of the turning point of the curve $y = -f(x)$. (1)

**17.** Solve the inequality $x^2 - 2x - 15 < 0$. (3)
---
$(x - 5)(x + 3)$ (M1); critical values $x = 5$ and $x = -3$ (A1); the curve is below the $x$-axis between its roots, so $-3 < x < 5$ (A1). (Writing $x < 5$, $x < -3$ or $x > -3$ only gets the first two marks.)

::: q 8-9 A7
**18.** $f(x) = \dfrac{x + 5}{2}$ and $g(x) = x^2 - 4$.

(a) Find $gf(3)$. (1)

(b) Find $f^{-1}(x)$. (2)

(c) Solve $fg(x) = 3.5$. Give your answers correct to 3 significant figures. (2)

(5)
---
(a) $f(3) = 4$, so $gf(3) = 4^2 - 4 = 12$ (B1).

(b) $y = \dfrac{x + 5}{2}$, so $x = 2y - 5$ (M1); $f^{-1}(x) = 2x - 5$ (A1).

(c) $fg(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4 + 5}{2} = \dfrac{x^2 + 1}{2}$, so $x^2 + 1 = 7$ (M1); $x = \pm\sqrt{6} = \pm 2.45$ (A1, both values needed).
:::

::: q 8-9 A11-12 R16
**19.** The graph of $y = ab^x$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants and $b > 0$, passes through the points $(1, 12)$ and $(3, 108)$.

Find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$. (3)
---
$ab = 12$ and $ab^3 = 108$ (M1); divide: $b^2 = 9$, so $b = 3$ as $b > 0$ (A1); $a = 12 \div 3 = 4$ (A1).

::: q 8-9 P9 P6-8
**20.** At a school, 60% of the students walk to school. The rest come by bus.

The probability that a student who walks is late is 0.1. The probability that a student who comes by bus is late is 0.25.

A student is chosen at random. Given that the student was late, work out the probability that the student walked to school. (4)
---
A tree diagram or equivalent: $0.6 \times 0.1 = 0.06$ (M1); $P(\text{late}) = 0.06 + 0.4 \times 0.25 = 0.16$ (M1); $\dfrac{0.06}{0.16}$ (M1); $0.375$ or $\dfrac{3}{8}$ (A1). (The answer $0.06$ is P(walks and late), not the conditional probability: M1 only.)
:::

::: q 8-9 A23-25
**21.** Here are the first five terms of a quadratic sequence.

2, 9, 22, 41, 66

Find an expression, in terms of $n$, for the $n$th term. (3)
---
First differences $7, 13, 19, 25$, second difference $6$, so the sequence starts $3n^2$ (M1); subtract $3n^2$: $2 - 3 = -1$, $9 - 12 = -3$, $22 - 27 = -5$, a linear sequence $-2n + 1$ (M1); $3n^2 - 2n + 1$ (A1).

**22.** $ABCDEFGH$ is a cuboid. The base $ABCD$ is a rectangle with $AB = 8$ cm and $BC = 6$ cm, and the edges $AE$, $BF$, $CG$ and $DH$ are vertical with length 5 cm.

Work out the size of the angle between the diagonal $AG$ and the base $ABCD$. Give your answer correct to 1 decimal place. (3)

**23.** A car starts moving at time $t = 0$. The table gives its speed, $v$ m/s, at some times during the first 8 seconds. (The speed follows a smooth curve that gets steeper as $t$ increases.)

| $t$ (seconds) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $v$ (m/s) | 1 | 3 | 9 | 19 | 33 |

(a) Use 4 strips of equal width to work out an estimate for the distance travelled in the first 8 seconds. (3)

(b) Is your answer to (a) an underestimate or an overestimate of the actual distance? Give a reason for your answer. (1)

(4)
---
(a) Distance is the area under the speed–time graph, using 4 trapezia of width 2 (M1); $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times (1 + 3) + \dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times (3 + 9) + \dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times (9 + 19) + \dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times (19 + 33)$ $= 4 + 12 + 28 + 52$ (M1); $96$ m (A1).

(b) Overestimate, because the curve bends upwards (gets steeper), so the straight tops of the trapezia lie above the curve (C1).
