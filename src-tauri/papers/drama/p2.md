**Section A: Theatre roles and terminology**

**01.** Who is responsible for the running of the theatre building, including the box office, front-of-house staff and the safety of the audience?

A The stage manager
B The theatre manager
C The director

Write the letter of the correct answer. (1)
---
B, the theatre manager (1). The stage manager runs the stage and the performance; the theatre manager runs the building and everything the audience experiences front of house.

**02.** A stage juts out into the auditorium so that the audience sits on three sides of it, with a back wall behind the acting area. What type of staging is this?

A Thrust
B Proscenium arch
C Theatre in the round

Write the letter of the correct answer. (1)
---
A, thrust (1). In the round has the audience on all sides; a proscenium arch stage is viewed from the front through a frame.

C, upstage centre (1). Upstage is the area furthest from the audience.

**Section B: Blood Brothers**

The extract for Question 06 is the scene in **Act One** set in Mrs Lyons's house, from the point where Mrs Johnstone is at work there and tells Mrs Lyons that she is expecting twins, to the moment the two women swear on the Bible. Find it in your copy of the play. Answer 06.1, 06.2 and 06.3, then either 06.4 or 06.5.

**06.1** You are designing a costume for Mrs Lyons to wear in a performance of this extract.

The costume must reflect the context of *Blood Brothers*, set in a Liverpudlian community in the early 1960s. Describe your design ideas for the costume. (4)
---
Mark as a single judgement, 1 mark per level. 4: an excellent, precise design entirely right for the brief, showing secure knowledge of the play and of costume (fabric, colour, cut, condition and accessories specified and justified by period and class). 3: a good design with a number of precise details and clear links to context. 2: a reasonable design with a few precise details and some relevance to the period. 1: a limited design with minimal detail (for example "posh clothes").

Indicative content: a fitted, knee-length shift or tailored dress of the early sixties in a good-quality fabric such as wool crepe or a fine tweed, in a restrained, expensive colour - camel, pale grey or powder blue - that contrasts with Mrs Johnstone's worn, faded housecoat; a neat cardigan or short matching jacket; pearls or a small brooch and a wedding and engagement ring; stockings and low court shoes; hair set in a smooth, lacquered bouffant or neat bob; understated make-up with a pale lipstick; the costume is immaculate and slightly stiff, showing her middle-class wealth and her need for control, and the lack of warmth in the colours can hint at her loneliness while her husband is away on business. Credit a design that also plans for her later decline (a looser, crumpled version of the same look).
:::

**06.2** You are performing the role of Mrs Johnstone.

Describe how you would use your vocal and physical skills to perform the moment in the extract when Mrs Johnstone tells Mrs Lyons that she is expecting twins, and explain the effects you want to create. (8)
---
Levels. 7–8: exact description of both vocal and physical skills with precise detail throughout; the intended effects are entirely right for Mrs Johnstone and for the moment, and are fully explained. 5–6: clear description with a number of precise details; effects appropriate and clearly explained. 3–4: reasonably clear description with a few details; effects have some relevance. 1–2: limited description with effects unexplained. A response covering only voice or only movement cannot go above the 3–4 band.

Indicative content: vocal - a broad Liverpool accent throughout; the news delivered in a rush, with a nervous half-laugh, rising pitch and quickened pace to show panic about money; a sudden drop in volume and a pause before the word "twins" as though she can hardly believe it; a flat, resigned tone when she lists what she cannot afford. Physical - she is mid-task (polishing, folding, holding a duster), and her hands stop when the subject comes up; a hand pressed to her stomach or the small of her back, shoulders sagging to show exhaustion; she avoids Mrs Lyons's eyes at first because she is embarrassed to raise it with her employer, then looks up at her for sympathy. Effects - the audience should warm to her and laugh with her, but also feel the weight of her poverty, which makes Mrs Lyons's offer seem tempting and makes the later tragedy land harder.
:::

**06.3** You are performing the role of Mrs Lyons.

Focus on the part of the extract that runs from Mrs Lyons first suggesting that Mrs Johnstone gives one of the babies to her, to the swearing on the Bible.

Explain how you might use the performance space and interact with the actor playing Mrs Johnstone to show the audience how Mrs Lyons pressures her into agreeing. (12)
---
Levels. 10–12: an exact, well-developed explanation, entirely right for the brief, with precise detail throughout (positions, moves, proxemics and reactions tied to specific moments). 7–9: a clear, developed explanation with a number of precise details. 4–6: a reasonably clear explanation with a few details and some relevance. 1–3: limited, generalised explanation with minimal detail. Answers that ignore the space or never refer to Mrs Johnstone cap at 4–6.

Indicative content: use of space - Mrs Lyons starts at a distance, perhaps downstage by the window, and gradually closes the gap as she builds the argument, until she is close enough to take Mrs Johnstone's hands; circling her, or blocking her route to the door, so Mrs Johnstone has nowhere to go; using the furniture of her own home to show ownership - sitting Mrs Johnstone down on the sofa and standing over her (higher level = power); interaction - repeated touch on the arm or shoulder that starts as comfort and becomes controlling; sustained eye contact while Mrs Johnstone looks away; mirroring Mrs Johnstone's worries back at her with a softer, persuasive tone, then a sudden, urgent shift when she feels the chance slipping; fetching the Bible and placing it between them on the table, pressing Mrs Johnstone's hand onto it and holding it there; a held pause and stillness after the oath, with Mrs Lyons stepping back, satisfied, leaving Mrs Johnstone isolated centre stage. Effects - the audience sees that the deal is not equal: class and money give Mrs Lyons the power, and her desperation already has a dangerous edge.
:::

**06.5** You are a designer working on one aspect of design for this extract (lighting, sound, set, costume or puppets).

Describe how you would use your design skills to create effects which support the action.

Explain why your ideas are appropriate for:
- this extract
- your chosen design skill in the play as a whole.

(20)
---
Levels (either option). 16–20: excellent, exact and well-developed ideas entirely right for the extract, with an extensive range of skills precisely targeted at the character or action and excellent knowledge of the whole play; precise detail throughout. 11–15: clear, secure ideas with a wide range of skills, a good degree of appropriateness and good knowledge of the whole play. 6–10: reasonably clear ideas with a fair range of skills, some relevance and some knowledge of the whole play. 1–5: limited ideas with a narrow range, little detail and little sense of the whole play. An answer that never moves beyond the extract cannot go above 11–15; one that retells the plot without skills stays in 1–5.

Indicative content, 06.4 Mrs Johnstone: a working-class Liverpool mother, young but worn down (the Narrator and the opening song tell us she looks older than her years); voice - broad Scouse accent, warm, quick and funny with a natural singing quality, but tightening and dropping in pitch when she is frightened; physical - a busy, practical energy, always moving and cleaning in someone else's house, then stillness and a protective hand on her stomach as she is drawn into the deal; hesitation shown through backing away and looking to the door; the reluctant hand on the Bible, trembling; in the play as a whole - she ages across the play, from this young mother to the woman in the final scene, so the voice deepens and the body slows, yet the warmth and humour remain; her superstition, planted here with the oath, makes her fear later credible; her songs are her moments of hope and must be played for real feeling rather than as show numbers; her final despair over both sons needs the full emotional range the extract only hints at.

Indicative content, 06.5 design: set - a composite set with the Lyons house on one side, all pale, polished and uncluttered (a smart sofa, a bureau, a vase of fresh flowers), sharply contrasting with the cramped Johnstone house; a truck or flown flat to move between the two; lighting - clean, cool white daylight for the Lyons house, becoming a tighter, warmer pool on the two women as the secret is made, then a cold special on the Bible at the oath and a shift to a colder state for the Narrator's warning; sound - the ticking of a clock to build tension, silence at the oath, then the sinister repeated musical motif that returns whenever fate is invoked; costume - the contrast of Mrs Lyons's expensive clothes with Mrs Johnstone's overall and headscarf. Whole play - the design must move quickly between locations and across twenty-odd years, keep the Narrator as a constant, threatening presence, and build the contrast of the two classes that the ending depends on.

**Section C: Live theatre production**

State the title of the live or digital theatre production you saw. Answer **one** of Questions 14, 15 or 16 with reference to that production. You must write about a different play from the one you answered on in Section B.

**14.** Describe how one actor used their vocal and physical skills to show how their character changed during the production. Analyse and evaluate how successful the actor was in showing this change to the audience. You could refer to vocal skills (for example accent, pitch and pace), physical skills (for example posture, gesture and movement), and two or more moments in the production.

**15.** Describe how sound was used to create tension. Analyse and evaluate how successful the sound design was in creating tension for the audience. You could refer to music, sound effects and silence, volume, direction and amplification, and a scene or section and/or the production as a whole.

**16.** Describe how costume, including hair and make-up where relevant, was used to communicate two or more characters. Analyse and evaluate how successfully the costume design communicated the characters to the audience. You could refer to materials, style, cut and fit, colour, texture and condition, and a scene or section and/or the production as a whole.

(32)
---
Marked in two parts and added together.

AO3 knowledge and understanding (12). 10–12: an excellent range of skills or design elements, precisely described with exact, detailed examples. 7–9: a good range, clearly described with a number of precise details. 4–6: a fair range with a few details, some of it generalised. 1–3: a narrow range, vague description.

AO4 analysis and evaluation (20). 16–20: critical and insightful; explains exactly how each choice communicated meaning and makes convincing, justified judgements about how well it worked, fully supported by specific moments. 11–15: developed and clear analysis and judgements, supported by a number of examples. 6–10: some analysis and judgement but often slips into description; points sound but not always explored. 1–5: mostly description or personal opinion without reasons.

Indicative content, 14: name the actor or character and the change (for example confident to broken, innocent to hardened, cold to warm); compare specific vocal and physical choices at an early and a late moment - pitch, pace, volume, accent, posture, gait, gesture, eye contact, use of space; explain how each communicated the change and judge how convincing and moving it was, including any moment that was less successful. Indicative content, 15: specific cues - underscoring, a rising drone, a heartbeat or clock, sudden loud effects, directional or surround sound, live versus recorded, reverb, and the use of silence; tie each to a moment and the tension it built or released; judge effectiveness. Indicative content, 16: at least two characters described precisely - garment, fabric, colour palette, fit, condition, accessories, hair and make-up - and what each told the audience about status, personality, period, relationships or change; judge how clearly and how quickly the audience read the characters. Top-band answers evaluate throughout rather than adding a judgement at the end, and use the correct terminology.
