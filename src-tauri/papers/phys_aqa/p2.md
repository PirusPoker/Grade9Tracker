**06** A student investigated how the acceleration of a trolley depends on the resultant force acting on it.

She had a trolley, a bench pulley, string, a hanger with ten 0.10 N slotted masses, a metre rule, and two light gates connected to a data logger.

Describe a method she could use. Your answer should explain how she finds the acceleration and how she makes sure the investigation is a fair test. (6)
---
Levels of response.

Level 3 (5–6 marks): a coherent, logically ordered method that could be followed to produce valid results. It includes how the force is changed, how the acceleration is found from measurements, and how the total mass being accelerated is kept constant.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): a method with the main steps, but some detail is missing (for example how acceleration is calculated, or how mass is kept constant). It could produce results, but they may not all be valid.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple, relevant steps that are not linked into a workable method.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- attach the string to the trolley, pass it over the pulley clamped at the end of the bench, and hang the mass hanger on the other end
- the weight of the hanger and masses is the accelerating (resultant) force
- place two light gates a measured distance apart along the bench; the trolley carries a card (interrupt card) of measured length
- release the trolley from rest at the same starting point each time
- the data logger records the speed at each gate (card length ÷ time to pass the gate) and the time taken between the gates
- acceleration = (speed at second gate − speed at first gate) ÷ time between the gates
- alternatively, use one light gate with a double interrupt card, or measure the distance travelled from rest and use v² − u² = 2 a s
- increase the force by moving 0.10 N masses from the trolley to the hanger, one at a time
- keeping all the unused masses on the trolley keeps the total mass of the system (trolley + hanger + masses) constant — the control variable
- tilt the bench slightly (friction-compensated runway) so the trolley just rolls at a steady speed with no hanger, so friction is cancelled out
- repeat each force (for example three times) and calculate a mean acceleration
- plot a graph of acceleration against force; a straight line through the origin shows that acceleration is directly proportional to resultant force

**07.3** A simple electric motor has a rectangular coil of wire between the poles of a magnet. The coil is connected to a d.c. supply through a split-ring commutator.

Explain how the motor keeps rotating in the same direction. (4)

(7)
---
**07.1** length = 5.0 cm = 0.050 m; F = B I l = 0.20 × 3.0 × 0.050 (1)

= 0.030 N (1). Allow 3.0 × 10⁻² N. An answer of 0.030 N with no working scores 2 marks. Using 5.0 (giving 3.0 N) scores 1 mark.

**07.2** B (1). (Fleming's left-hand rule: first finger along the field, from left to right; second finger along the current, into the page; the thumb points downwards.)

**07.3** Levels of response.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): a clear, linked explanation that covers the forces on both sides of the coil producing a turning effect **and** the role of the split-ring commutator in reversing the current every half turn.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): relevant points, such as "there is a force on the wire" or "the commutator reverses the current", that are not linked into an explanation.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- the current-carrying sides of the coil are in a magnetic field, so a force acts on each side (the motor effect)
- the current flows in opposite directions in the two sides of the coil, so the forces act in opposite directions (one up, one down)
- the two forces produce a moment / turning effect, so the coil rotates about its axis
- after half a turn the sides of the coil have swapped places; without a commutator the forces would now turn the coil back
- the split-ring commutator reverses the direction of the current in the coil every half turn
- so the force on the side of the coil nearest each pole is always in the same direction, and the coil keeps turning the same way
- the coil's momentum carries it past the vertical position, where the forces have no turning effect
