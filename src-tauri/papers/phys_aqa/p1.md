**01** A child slides from the top of a playground slide to the bottom. The child has a mass of 32 kg. The top of the slide is 2.5 m higher than the bottom.

gravitational field strength = 9.8 N/kg

**01.1** Calculate the gravitational potential energy lost by the child between the top and the bottom of the slide. (2)

**01.2** The child starts from rest and reaches the bottom of the slide at a speed of 5.0 m/s.

Calculate the kinetic energy of the child at the bottom of the slide. (2)

**01.3** Calculate the efficiency of the slide at transferring the child's gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy. Use your answers to 01.1 and 01.2. (2)

(6)
---
**01.1** gravitational potential energy = mass × gravitational field strength × height, Ep = m g h (1)

32 × 9.8 × 2.5 = 784 J (1). An answer of 784 J (allow 780 J) with no working scores 2 marks.

**01.2** kinetic energy = ½ × mass × speed², Ek = ½ m v² = 0.5 × 32 × 5.0² (1)

= 400 J (1). An answer of 400 J with no working scores 2 marks. 0.5 × 32 × 5.0 = 80 J (speed not squared) scores 0.

**01.3** efficiency = useful output energy transfer ÷ total input energy transfer = 400 ÷ 784 (1)

= 0.51 or 51% (1). Allow error carried forward from 01.1 and 01.2. Do not accept an answer greater than 1 (or 100%).

**03.1** In the alpha particle scattering experiment, alpha particles were fired at a very thin sheet of gold foil. A very small number of alpha particles bounced back towards the source.

What did this show?

- **A** Atoms are mostly empty space.
- **B** Electrons orbit the nucleus at fixed distances.
- **C** The positive charge and most of the mass of an atom are concentrated in a tiny nucleus.
- **D** The nucleus contains neutrons.

(1)

**03.2** Carbon-14 is an isotope of carbon. A carbon-14 nucleus (mass number 14, atomic number 6) decays by emitting a beta particle, ⁰₋₁e, and becomes a nitrogen nucleus.

¹⁴₆C → N + ⁰₋₁e

The nitrogen nucleus has mass number **a** and atomic number **b**.

Give the values of **a** and **b**. (2)

**03.3** A sample of a different radioactive isotope has an activity of 640 Bq. After 24 hours its activity is 80 Bq.

Calculate the half-life of the isotope. (2)

(5)
---
**03.1** C (1). A is shown by most alpha particles passing straight through, not by the few that bounced back.

**03.2** a = 14 (1)

b = 7 (1). In beta decay a neutron turns into a proton (and an electron), so the mass number does not change and the atomic number increases by 1.

**03.3** 640 → 320 → 160 → 80, so 24 hours is 3 half-lives (1)

half-life = 24 ÷ 3 = 8 hours (1). An answer of 8 hours with no working scores 2 marks. 24 ÷ 8 = 3 hours (dividing by the ratio of activities) scores 0.

**05** A student did an experiment to find the specific heat capacity of aluminium.

She used a 1.00 kg aluminium block with two holes drilled in it: one for an electric heater and one for a thermometer. The heater had a power of 48 W. She switched the heater on for 4.0 minutes. The temperature of the block rose from 19.5 °C to 30.5 °C.

**05.1** Calculate the energy transferred by the heater in 4.0 minutes. (2)

**05.2** Calculate the specific heat capacity of aluminium given by the student's results.

Use the equation: change in thermal energy = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change

Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (3)

**05.3** The accepted value for the specific heat capacity of aluminium is 900 J/kg °C.

Explain why the student's value is higher than the accepted value, and give **one** change to the method that would bring her value closer to it. (2)

(7)
---
**05.1** energy transferred = power × time, E = P t = 48 × 240 (1). The time must be converted to seconds.

= 11 520 J (1). Allow 11 500 J or 1.15 × 10⁴ J. 48 × 4.0 = 192 J scores 1 mark.

**05.2** temperature change = 30.5 − 19.5 = 11.0 °C (1)

c = ΔE ÷ (m × Δθ) = 11 520 ÷ (1.00 × 11.0) (1)

= 1050 J/kg °C (1). Allow error carried forward from 05.1 (for example 192 ÷ 11.0 = 17.5 J/kg °C scores 2 marks). 1047 J/kg °C (not to 3 significant figures) scores 2 marks.

**06** An electric kettle is plugged into the UK mains supply. The mains supply is a 230 V alternating current supply.

**06.1** The kettle has an on/off switch. The kettle's cable is damaged so that the live wire is exposed.

Explain why touching the exposed live wire can give a person an electric shock even when the kettle's switch is off. (2)

**06.2** The power of the kettle is 2.3 kW.

Calculate the current in the kettle when it is switched on. (2)

**06.3** Electricity reaches homes through the National Grid. Step-up transformers increase the potential difference to very high values before the electricity is transmitted through the cables. Step-down transformers then decrease it.

Explain why the National Grid uses step-up and step-down transformers. (3)

(7)
---
**06.1** The live wire is still at 230 V / at a high potential while the kettle is plugged in, even when the switch is off (1); the person's body is at 0 V / earth potential, so there is a large potential difference across the body and a current flows through it (1). Do not accept "the electricity is stored in the wire".

**06.2** power = potential difference × current, P = V I, so I = 2300 ÷ 230 (1)

= 10 A (1). An answer of 10 A with no working scores 2 marks. 2.3 ÷ 230 = 0.01 A (kW not converted) scores 1 mark.

**06.3** For the same power transmitted, increasing the potential difference decreases the current (P = V I) (1); a smaller current causes less heating of the transmission cables, so less energy is dissipated / wasted and the system is efficient (1); step-down transformers reduce the potential difference to a much lower (safer) value, 230 V, for use in homes (1).

**Table 1**

|---|---|---|

| Carbon dioxide released per year (tonnes) | 4000 | almost none |

| Expected lifetime (years) | 20 | 25 |

Evaluate the two options for generating the island's electricity.

Use Table 1 and your own knowledge. (6)
---
Levels of response.

Level 3 (5–6 marks): a detailed evaluation that weighs advantages and disadvantages of **both** options, uses data from Table 1 (including a comparison of the costs over time) and comes to a reasoned judgement.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): advantages and disadvantages of both options are given, with some use of the data; the judgement may be missing or not supported.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple statements about one or both options, with little or no use of the data.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- the wind turbines cost £4.5 million more to build
- running costs are £1.0 million per year for diesel but £0.2 million per year for wind, a saving of £0.8 million per year, so the extra building cost is paid back after about 5.6 years
- over 20 years: diesel costs 1.5 + 20 × 1.0 = £21.5 million; wind costs 6.0 + 20 × 0.2 = £10.0 million, and the turbines last 5 years longer
- diesel is a fossil fuel: non-renewable, so it will run out, and its price may rise

**08** A student rubs a polythene rod with a woollen cloth. The rod becomes negatively charged. She hangs the rod from a nylon thread so that it can turn freely.

She then rubs a second polythene rod with the cloth and brings it close to one end of the hanging rod, without touching it. That end of the hanging rod moves away.

Explain why the rods become charged and why the hanging rod moves. Use ideas about electrons and electric fields in your answer. (4)
---
Levels of response.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): a clear, linked explanation of how rubbing transfers electrons to charge the rod **and** of how the electric field of the second rod causes a force of repulsion.

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple statements, for example "electrons move" or "like charges repel", with little explanation or linking.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- rubbing (friction) transfers electrons from the cloth to the polythene rod
- the rod gains electrons, so it becomes negatively charged
- the cloth loses electrons and is left with an equal positive charge
- polythene is an insulator, so the charge stays on the rod
- both rods have the same type of charge (negative)
- the second rod creates an electric field around itself
- the hanging rod is in this field, so it experiences a force
- like charges repel, so the end of the hanging rod is pushed away; this is a non-contact force
- the field is strongest close to the charged rod, so the force increases as the rods get closer

Do not accept answers in which positive charges / protons move.

**09** A student connects a 20 Ω resistor and a thermistor in parallel with a 6.0 V battery. An ammeter measures the total current from the battery.

At room temperature the resistance of the thermistor is 30 Ω.

**09.1** Calculate the reading on the ammeter at room temperature. (3)

**09.2** The student warms the thermistor with her hand.

Describe and explain what happens to the reading on the ammeter. (2)

(5)
---
**09.1** In a parallel circuit the potential difference across each branch is 6.0 V.

current in the resistor = 6.0 ÷ 20 = 0.30 A (1)

current in the thermistor = 6.0 ÷ 30 = 0.20 A (1)

total current = 0.30 + 0.20 = 0.50 A (1)

Alternative method: 1 ÷ R = 1 ÷ 20 + 1 ÷ 30, so R = 12 Ω (1); I = 6.0 ÷ 12 (1) = 0.50 A (1). An answer of 0.50 A with no working scores 3 marks. Adding the resistances (50 Ω, 0.12 A) scores 0.

**09.2** The ammeter reading increases; "increases" with no explanation scores 0.

The resistance of the thermistor decreases as its temperature increases, so the current through the thermistor increases (the potential difference across it is still 6.0 V) (1); the current through the 20 Ω resistor is unchanged, and the total current is the sum of the branch currents, so the total current increases (1).

Use the particle model to explain why the pressure of the air in the barrel increases. (2)

**10.2** Calculate how far the piston moves into the barrel before the valve opens. Assume the temperature of the air stays constant.

The freezer removes energy from the water at an average rate of 25 W.

specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg °C

specific heat capacity of ice = 2100 J/kg °C

specific latent heat of fusion of water = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg

Use the equations:

::: q 8-9 4.4.4
**13** Some nuclear reactors use plutonium-239 as their fuel. In the Sun, energy is released by a different nuclear process.

Explain how a plutonium-239 reactor releases energy at a steady rate, and compare the process in the reactor with the process that releases energy in the Sun. (6)
---
Levels of response.

Level 3 (5–6 marks): a detailed, coherent explanation of fission and of how the chain reaction is controlled to give a steady rate of energy release, **and** a clear comparison with fusion in the Sun giving at least two differences.

Level 2 (3–4 marks): fission and the chain reaction are described, with either how it is controlled or a comparison with fusion; some steps may be missing or confused (for example "control rods slow the neutrons down").

Level 1 (1–2 marks): simple statements, such as "the atom splits" or "the Sun joins atoms", with little explanation.

0 marks: no relevant content.

Indicative content:

- a plutonium-239 nucleus absorbs a neutron and becomes even more unstable
- the nucleus splits (nuclear fission) into two smaller nuclei of roughly equal size
- two or three neutrons and gamma rays are emitted, and energy is released; all the fission products have kinetic energy
- the released neutrons can be absorbed by other plutonium-239 nuclei, causing more fissions: a chain reaction
- control rods absorb neutrons, so that on average only one neutron from each fission goes on to cause another fission
- this keeps the number of fissions per second constant, so energy is released at a steady rate; an uncontrolled chain reaction would release the energy in an explosion, as in a nuclear weapon
- a moderator slows the neutrons so that they are more likely to be absorbed
- the kinetic energy of the fission products heats the reactor core; the thermal energy boils water to make steam, which turns turbines and generators
- in the Sun, energy is released by nuclear fusion: two light nuclei (isotopes of hydrogen) join to form a heavier nucleus (helium)
- fission splits a large, heavy nucleus; fusion joins small, light nuclei
- in fusion some of the mass is converted into the energy of radiation
- fission is started by absorbing a neutron; fusion needs extremely high temperatures and pressures, because the positively charged nuclei repel each other and must be forced very close together
