(a) Work out the area of the sector. (2)

(b) Work out the perimeter of the sector. (2)

(4)

**4.** A box is a cuboid measuring 0.6 m by 0.4 m by 0.25 m. It has a weight of 360 newtons.

$\text{pressure} = \dfrac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$

(a) The box rests on the floor on its 0.6 m by 0.4 m face. Work out the pressure on the floor, in N/m$^2$. (2)

(b) The box is turned so that it rests on its smallest face. Work out the percentage increase in the pressure on the floor. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Area $0.6 \times 0.4 = 0.24$ m$^2$ (M1); $360 \div 0.24 = 1500$ N/m$^2$ (A1).

(b) Smallest face $0.4 \times 0.25 = 0.1$ m$^2$, so pressure $3600$ N/m$^2$ (M1); $\dfrac{3600 - 1500}{1500} \times 100 = 140\%$ (A1) FT their (a). (Answer 240% is the new pressure as a percentage of the old: M1 A0.)

(a) Work out the distance $PR$. (2)

(4)

**7.** 50 students each solved a puzzle. The table shows the times, $t$ seconds, they took.

| Time | $0 < t \le 20$ | $20 < t \le 40$ | $40 < t \le 60$ | $60 < t \le 80$ | $80 < t \le 100$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 11 | 17 | 13 | 5 |

(a) Calculate an estimate of the mean time. (3)

(b) Explain why your answer to (a) is only an estimate. (1)

(4)
---
(a) Midpoints $10, 30, 50, 70, 90$ (M1); $\sum fx = 40 + 330 + 850 + 910 + 450 = 2580$ (M1); $2580 \div 50 = 51.6$ seconds (A1). (Using the upper class limits gives 61.6: M0 M1 A0.)

(b) The exact times are not known, so each time is assumed to be at the midpoint of its class (B1).

| Number | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**9.** For an object moving with constant acceleration $a$, with initial velocity $u$ and final velocity $v$, after time $t$ and displacement $s$:

$s = ut + \dfrac{1}{2}at^2 \qquad v^2 = u^2 + 2as$

(a) Work out $s$ when $u = 4.5$, $t = 6$ and $a = 2.5$. (2)

(b) Rearrange $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ to make $a$ the subject. (2)

(c) A car speeds up from 12 m/s to 30 m/s over a distance of 210 m. Work out its acceleration. (1)

(5)
---
(a) $4.5 \times 6 + \dfrac{1}{2} \times 2.5 \times 6^2$ (M1); $s = 72$ (A1).

(b) $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$ (M1); $a = \dfrac{v^2 - u^2}{2s}$ (A1).

(c) $\dfrac{30^2 - 12^2}{2 \times 210} = \dfrac{756}{420} = 1.8$ m/s$^2$ (B1).

**10.** Ali and Ben share some money in the ratio $3 : 5$. Ben then gives Ali £24. The ratio of Ali's money to Ben's money is now $5 : 7$.

Work out the total amount of money they share. (4)
---
Ali has $3k$ and Ben has $5k$ (M1); $\dfrac{3k + 24}{5k - 24} = \dfrac{5}{7}$, so $7(3k + 24) = 5(5k - 24)$ (M1); $21k + 168 = 25k - 120$, $k = 72$ (A1); total $8 \times 72 = 576$, so £576 (A1). (Check: £240 and £336 are in the ratio $5 : 7$.)

**11.** Here are the first five terms of a quadratic sequence.

4, 11, 22, 37, 56

Find an expression for the $n$th term of the sequence. (3)
---
First differences 7, 11, 15, 19 and second difference 4, so $2n^2$ (M1); subtract $2n^2$: $4 - 2, 11 - 8, 22 - 18, \ldots = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$, giving $n + 1$ (M1); $2n^2 + n + 1$ (A1).

**15.** (a) Show that the equation $x^3 - 5x - 3 = 0$ has a solution between $x = 2$ and $x = 3$. (2)

(b) Show that $x^3 - 5x - 3 = 0$ can be rearranged to give $x = \sqrt[3]{5x + 3}$. (1)

(c) Starting with $x_0 = 2.5$, use the iteration $x_{n + 1} = \sqrt[3]{5x_n + 3}$ to find $x_1$, $x_2$ and $x_3$, giving each to 4 decimal places. Hence write down the solution to 2 decimal places. (3)

(6)
---
(a) $x = 2$: $8 - 10 - 3 = -5$ and $x = 3$: $27 - 15 - 3 = 9$ (M1); there is a change of sign, and the function is continuous, so there is a solution between 2 and 3 (A1).

(b) $x^3 = 5x + 3$, so $x = \sqrt[3]{5x + 3}$ (B1).

(c) $x_1 = 2.4933$ (M1); $x_2 = 2.4915$, $x_3 = 2.4910$ (A1); solution $2.49$ (A1). (For full rigour, $f(2.485) = -0.080 < 0$ and $f(2.495) = 0.056 > 0$ confirm 2.49, but that is not required here.)

Work out the volume of the toy. (4)

**18.** $VABCD$ is a pyramid with a square base $ABCD$ of side 10 cm. The vertex $V$ is directly above the centre $M$ of the base. Each sloping edge, such as $VA$, is 13 cm long.

(a) Work out the height $VM$ of the pyramid. (2)

(b) Work out the angle between the edge $VA$ and the base $ABCD$. (2)

(4)
---
(a) $AM = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{10^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{50} = 7.071\ldots$ (M1); $VM = \sqrt{13^2 - 50} = \sqrt{119} = 10.9$ cm (A1).

(b) The angle is $VAM$: $\cos VAM = \dfrac{\sqrt{50}}{13}$, or $\tan VAM = \dfrac{10.9}{7.07}$ (M1); $57.0^\circ$ (A1). (Using $AM = 5$, half the side instead of half the diagonal, is M0 in (a) and gives $67.4^\circ$: M1 A0 in (b) on follow-through.)
:::

::: q 8-9 A18 A21-22
**19.** A rectangle has length $(2x + 3)$ cm and width $(x - 1)$ cm. Its area is 40 cm$^2$.

(a) Show that $2x^2 + x - 43 = 0$. (2)

(b) Work out the perimeter of the rectangle. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (3)

(5)
---
(a) $(2x + 3)(x - 1) = 40$ (M1); $2x^2 - 2x + 3x - 3 = 40$, so $2x^2 + x - 43 = 0$ (A1).

(b) $x = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 - 4 \times 2 \times (-43)}}{2 \times 2} = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{345}}{4}$ (M1); $x = 4.3935\ldots$, rejecting $-4.89$ because the width $x - 1$ must be positive (A1); perimeter $2(3x + 2) = 6x + 4 = 30.4$ cm (A1).
:::

::: q 8-9 A7
**20.** $f(x) = 3x - 1$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{2}{x + 1}$, $x \ne -1$.

(a) Find $gf(1)$. (2)

(b) Find $f^{-1}(x)$. (2)

(c) Solve $fg(x) = f^{-1}(x)$. (4)

(8)
---
(a) $f(1) = 2$ (M1); $g(2) = \dfrac{2}{3}$ (A1).

(b) $y = 3x - 1 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{y + 1}{3}$ (M1); $f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x + 1}{3}$ (A1).

(c) $fg(x) = \dfrac{6}{x + 1} - 1$ (B1); $\dfrac{6}{x + 1} - 1 = \dfrac{x + 1}{3}$, so $18 - 3(x + 1) = (x + 1)^2$ (M1); $x^2 + 5x - 14 = 0$ (A1); $x = 2$ or $x = -7$ (A1). (Both values check: each side equals 1 when $x = 2$ and $-2$ when $x = -7$.)
:::

**21.** The number of bacteria, $N$, in a culture $t$ hours after it is set up is modelled by $N = ab^t$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants.

When $t = 2$, $N = 360$. When $t = 5$, $N = 1215$.

(a) Find the values of $a$ and $b$. (3)

(b) Using the model, find the number of whole hours after the start when the number of bacteria first exceeds 10 000. (2)

(5)
---
(a) $ab^2 = 360$ and $ab^5 = 1215$ (M1); divide: $b^3 = 3.375$, $b = 1.5$ (A1); $a = 360 \div 1.5^2 = 160$ (A1).

(b) Trials, e.g. $160 \times 1.5^{10} = 9226$ and $160 \times 1.5^{11} = 13\,840$ (M1) FT their $a$ and $b$; 11 hours (A1).

**22.** A car starts from rest. The table gives its velocity, $v$ m/s, at times $t$ seconds. The velocity–time graph is a smooth curve through these points, and its gradient decreases as $t$ increases.

| $t$ | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $v$ | 0 | 3.2 | 5.6 | 7.2 | 8.0 |

(a) Use 4 strips of equal width to estimate the distance travelled in the first 8 seconds. (3)

(b) Is your answer to (a) an underestimate or an overestimate? Give a reason. (1)

(4)
---
(a) Area under the graph using trapezia of width 2 (M1); $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times \big[0 + 2(3.2 + 5.6 + 7.2) + 8.0\big]$ (M1); $40$ m (A1). (Separate trapezia $3.2 + 8.8 + 12.8 + 15.2 = 40$ are equally good.)

(b) Underestimate: the curve bends downwards (its gradient decreases), so the straight tops of the trapezia lie below the curve (B1).

**23.** The probability that it rains on Monday is 0.3.

If it rains on Monday, the probability that it rains on Tuesday is 0.6. If it does not rain on Monday, the probability that it rains on Tuesday is 0.25.

Given that it rains on Tuesday, work out the probability that it rained on Monday. (4)
