::: q 4-5 1a
**1.** A bottle of perfume is opened at one side of a room. A few minutes later it can be smelt at the other side.

(a) Explain this in terms of particles. (2)

(b) Explain why the smell spreads faster on a warm day. (1)

(3)
---
(a) Perfume particles evaporate and move randomly (1); they spread out and mix with the air particles — diffusion — until they reach the other side (1).

(b) The particles have more kinetic energy and move faster (1).

::: q 6-7 1j 1k
**6.** (a) Explain why graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not. (2)

(b) Explain why metals conduct electricity and can be bent into shape. (2)

(c) Explain why silicon dioxide has a very high melting point. (2)

(6)
---
(a) In graphite each carbon atom bonds to only three others, leaving one delocalised electron per atom free to move and carry charge (1); in diamond every carbon forms four bonds, so there are no free electrons (1).

(b) Metals consist of a lattice of positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons that can move and carry charge (1); the layers of ions can slide over each other without the bonding breaking, so metals are malleable (1).

(c) It is a giant covalent structure in which every atom is joined to its neighbours by strong covalent bonds (1); a great deal of energy is needed to break these many bonds (1).
:::

**10.** At 60 °C, 40 g of a salt dissolves completely in 80 g of water to give a saturated solution.

(a) Calculate the solubility of the salt at 60 °C in g per 100 g of water. (2)

(b) The solubility at 20 °C is 30 g per 100 g of water. Calculate the mass of salt that crystallises when the solution is cooled to 20 °C. (2)

(4)
---
(a) $\dfrac{40}{80} \times 100$ (1) $= 50$ g per 100 g water (1).

(b) At 20 °C, 80 g of water holds $0.8 \times 30 = 24$ g (1); $40 - 24 = 16$ g crystallises (1).

::: q 8-9 1f 2g
**11.** 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution is exactly neutralised by 22.5 cm³ of hydrochloric acid of concentration 0.100 mol/dm³.

(a) Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid used. (1)

(b) Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide solution in mol/dm³. (2)

(c) Convert your answer to g/dm³. (1)

(d) Describe how you would obtain pure, dry crystals of sodium chloride from the neutralised solution. (2)

(6)
---
(a) $0.100 \times \dfrac{22.5}{1000} = 0.00225$ mol (1).

(b) NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O, so moles NaOH $= 0.00225$ (1); concentration $= \dfrac{0.00225}{0.0250} = 0.0900$ mol/dm³ (1).

(c) $M_r$(NaOH) $= 40$; $0.0900 \times 40 = 3.60$ g/dm³ (1).

(d) Repeat the titration without indicator using the same volumes to get an uncontaminated solution (1); heat gently to evaporate about half the water, leave to cool so crystals form, filter and dry them between filter papers or in a warm oven (1).

**12.** Ethanol can be made from ethene or by fermentation.

(a) State the conditions and write the equation for making ethanol from ethene. (2)

(b) State the conditions for fermentation and write the equation. (2)

(c) Explain the difference between addition and condensation polymerisation. (2)

(d) Explain why polymers such as poly(ethene) cause disposal problems. (1)

(7)
---
(a) Steam, a phosphoric acid catalyst, about 300 °C and 60–70 atm (1); C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH (1).

::: q 8-9 2f
**13.** Solutions of ethanoic acid and hydrochloric acid have the same concentration. Explain why the hydrochloric acid has the lower pH. (2)
---
Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid, fully dissociated into ions in water, while ethanoic acid is weak and only partly dissociated (1); so the hydrochloric acid has a much higher concentration of H⁺ ions, and pH falls as H⁺ concentration rises (1).
