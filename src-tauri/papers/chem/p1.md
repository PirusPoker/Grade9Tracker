(4)

::: q 6-7
**5.** Magnesium burns in oxygen: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. 12 g of magnesium burns completely.

(a) Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide formed. (3)

(b) Calculate the volume of oxygen used, at room temperature and pressure. (2)

(5)
---
(a) $12 \div 24 = 0.5$ mol Mg (1); $0.5$ mol MgO (1); $0.5 \times 40 = 20$ g (1).

(b) $0.25$ mol O₂ (1); $0.25 \times 24 = 6$ dm³ (1).

::: q 6-7
**6.** (a) Explain why sodium chloride has a high melting point and conducts electricity when molten but not when solid. (3)

(b) Explain why carbon dioxide is a gas at room temperature even though its covalent bonds are strong. (2)

(5)
---
(a) A giant lattice of oppositely charged ions held by strong electrostatic forces needing much energy to break (1); in the solid the ions are fixed and cannot move (1); when molten the ions are free to move and carry charge (1).

(b) It is simple molecular: the strong bonds are inside the molecules and are not broken on boiling (1); only weak intermolecular forces between molecules must be overcome, which needs little energy (1).
:::

::: q 6-7
**7.** Marble chips react with hydrochloric acid, producing carbon dioxide.

(a) Explain, using collision theory, why the reaction is faster at a higher temperature. (3)

(b) A student measures the volume of gas every 10 s at 20 °C and again at 40 °C, with all other conditions the same. Describe how the two graphs of volume against time differ. (2)

(5)
---
(a) Particles move faster and collide more often (1); more collisions have energy above the activation energy (1); so a greater proportion of collisions are successful (1).

(b) The 40 °C curve is steeper at the start (1); both level off at the same final volume, because the same amount of reactant is used (1).
:::

::: q 6-7
**8.** A compound contains 5.6 g of iron and 2.4 g of oxygen.

(a) Find its empirical formula. (3)

(b) Calculate its relative formula mass. (1)

(4)
---
(a) Fe: $5.6 \div 56 = 0.1$; O: $2.4 \div 16 = 0.15$ (1); ratio $2 : 3$ (1); Fe₂O₃ (1).

(b) $2 \times 56 + 3 \times 16 = 160$ (1).

::: q 8-9
**9.** Concentrated sodium chloride solution is electrolysed with inert electrodes.

(a) Name the product at each electrode and write a half-equation for each. (4)

(b) Explain why sodium is not produced at the cathode. (2)

(6)
---
(a) Cathode: hydrogen, 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ (2); anode: chlorine, 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (2).

(b) Sodium is more reactive than hydrogen (1); the less reactive element's ions are discharged, so hydrogen ions from the water are reduced instead (1).
:::

::: q 8-9
**10.** Hydrogen reacts with chlorine: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. Bond energies: H–H 436, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432 kJ/mol.

(a) Calculate the enthalpy change of the reaction. (3)

(b) State whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic, giving the reason from your calculation. (1)

(c) In a separate experiment, a reaction heats 50 g of water by 8 °C. Calculate the energy released. (c = 4.2 J/g °C) (2)

(6)
---
(a) Bonds broken $436 + 243 = 679$ kJ (1); bonds made $2 \times 432 = 864$ kJ (1); $\Delta H = 679 - 864 = -185$ kJ/mol (1).

(b) Exothermic — more energy is released making bonds than is needed to break them, so ΔH is negative (1).

(c) $Q = 50 \times 4.2 \times 8$ (1) $= 1680$ J (1).
:::

::: q 8-9
**11.** Sulfur dioxide is converted to sulfur trioxide: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH negative.

(a) Explain the effect on the equilibrium yield of SO₃ of increasing the pressure. (2)

(b) Explain the effect of increasing the temperature. (2)

(c) State the effect of adding a catalyst on the position of equilibrium. (1)

(5)
---
(a) 3 moles of gas on the left, 2 on the right (1); higher pressure shifts equilibrium to the side with fewer gas molecules — more SO₃ (1).

(b) The forward reaction is exothermic (1); higher temperature favours the backward, endothermic direction, so less SO₃ (1).

(c) No effect on the position — equilibrium is reached faster (1).
:::
