intro: Eduqas GCSE Music C660QS Component 3 style: eight 12-mark questions, two on each area of study, with the set works in Q1 (Badinerie) and Q7 (Africa). The real exam is played from a recording, each extract heard three or four times; this mock drills the knowledge behind it, so every extract is described in words and short passages of music are written as note names and rhythm values. Q2 contains the dictation and Q6 the 10-mark extended answer, which you should mark honestly against the bands. The questions ramp from grade 4-5 to grade 8-9.

(b) Motif X opens the movement in the flute part. Describe two features of motif X. (2)

(c) Later in Section A the cellos take motif X and repeat it at a different pitch, moving the music first to A major and then to F sharp minor. Name the device used when a motif is repeated at a higher or lower pitch in the same part. (1)

(e) The symbol *tr* is printed above notes in bars 8, 10 and 15. Name this ornament. (1)

(h) Name the form of the whole movement. (1)

(12)

**2. Area of study 1 - unprepared extract.** A short minuet for solo piano, in G major and 3/4 time. Its plan is: A section (8 bars) played twice, then B section (8 bars) played twice. The left hand plays chords broken into the pattern lowest note, highest note, middle note, highest note (for example G, D, B, D). The first phrase starts on the last beat of the bar before bar 1. The A section ends with chord V followed by chord I in D major, and the B section begins in D major before returning to G major.

(a) Name the form of this minuet. (1)

(b) Name the type of left-hand accompaniment described. (1)

(c) Name the term for a phrase that begins before the first strong beat. (1)

(d) **Dictation.** Bar 5 of the melody contains six notes, all taken from the G major scale. The first note is D. Each following note moves as follows: up one step, up one step, up one step, down a third (skipping one note of the scale), up one step. Write the five missing pitches in order, using letter names. (5)

(e) Give the relationship of D major to G major. (1)

(f) Name the cadence at the end of the A section. (1)

(g) Name the period in which this minuet was most probably composed, and give one feature from the description that supports your answer. (2)

(12)
---
(a) Binary form / AB / AABB (1).

(b) Alberti bass (1).

(c) Anacrusis / upbeat (1).

(d) E (1), F sharp (1), G (1), E (1), F sharp (1). One mark for each correct pitch in the correct position. The fourth note is a third below G, which is E; F (natural) is wrong in G major, so "F" without a sharp scores 0 for that note.

(e) Dominant (1).

(f) Perfect cadence (1).

(g) Classical (1). Feature (1), any one: Alberti bass; balanced, regular phrases; clear melody and accompaniment (homophonic) texture; piano as the keyboard instrument; a minuet in binary form with a move to the dominant.

**3. Area of study 2 - unprepared extract.** A song from a stage musical. Verse 1 is sung by a solo female voice accompanied by piano. In Verse 2 a male character joins her, singing a different melody with different words at the same time. In the final chorus the whole company sings the same melody together, the men an octave below the women. The song begins in C minor and the chorus moves to E flat major. On her highest notes the soloist uses a powerful, chest-voice sound. Before the final chord the tempo gradually slows down.

(a) Name the texture of Verse 2. (1)

(b) Name the texture of the final chorus. (1)

(c) Name the type of musical theatre number in which two characters sing together. (1)

(d) Name the vocal technique the soloist uses on her highest notes. (1)

(e) Explain the difference between syllabic and melismatic word-setting. (2)

(f) Give the relationship of E flat major to C minor. (1)

(g) Give the Italian term for gradually getting slower. (1)

(h) Describe how a composer of musical theatre can use texture to show two characters disagreeing, and then agreeing, in the same number. (4)

(12)
---
(a) Polyphonic / contrapuntal (accept countermelody, two independent melodies) (1).

(b) Unison / in octaves (1). Do not accept homophonic.

(c) Duet (1).

(d) Belt / belting (1).

(e) Syllabic: one note for each syllable of the words (1). Melismatic: one syllable sung to several notes (1).

(f) Relative major (1).

(g) Rallentando / ritardando (1).

(h) Up to 4, one per valid point. Disagreeing: separate melodies at the same time, so polyphonic or contrapuntal (1); different rhythms or styles for each character (1); overlapping or interrupting entries, or call and response with contrasting answers (1); lines crossing or clashing (dissonance) (1). Agreeing: the characters move into the same rhythm, homophonic or homorhythmic (1); they sing in harmony, for example in parallel thirds or sixths (1); they finish in unison or octaves (1); the accompaniment thickens and the chorus may join to confirm it (1). Maximum 3 if only one half of the question is addressed.

**5. Area of study 3 - unprepared extract.** Music from a fantasy film about a young hero. The hero's theme is first played by a solo French horn in D major at a moderate tempo, legato and mezzo forte. It opens with a rising leap from D up to G and then moves mainly by step. When the hero is captured, the theme returns in D minor, much more slowly, played quietly by a low clarinet over a sustained, clashing chord. In the final battle the theme is played fortissimo by the whole brass section, in D major again, over a fast repeated string pattern.

(a) Give the term for a short musical idea that represents a character. (1)

(b) Name the interval from D up to G. (1)

(c) Explain why a rising interval like this is often used in a hero's theme. (1)

(d) Give the term for melody that moves mainly by step. (1)

(e) Name the technique of changing a theme's character while keeping it recognisable. (1)

(f) Explain how four of the changes made to the theme when the hero is captured affect the mood. (4)

(g) Give two ways the final-battle version differs from the opening horn version, and explain the effect. (2)

(h) Give the term for film music that plays underneath the dialogue and action. (1)

(12)
---
(a) Leitmotif (1).

(b) Perfect 4th (1).

(c) It sounds strong, bold or open, like a bugle call or fanfare, so it suggests heroism and confidence (1).

(d) Conjunct / stepwise (1).

(e) Thematic transformation (1).

(f) Up to 4, one per change linked to mood: minor key makes it sad or defeated (1); slower tempo makes it heavy, weary or hopeless (1); quiet dynamic makes it fragile or weak (1); low clarinet's dark, hollow timbre replaces the bright horn, so the hero seems diminished (1); sustained dissonant chord creates tension and unease (1). A change named with no effect scores 0.

(g) One mark for two valid differences with effect: fortissimo and full brass instead of a solo horn, plus a fast string ostinato underneath (accept thicker texture) (1); this makes the theme triumphant and exciting, showing the hero's victory (1).

(h) Underscore / underscoring (1).

**6. Area of study 3 - unprepared extract.** Music for a film scene in which a lone climber is caught in a storm near a mountain summit, then reaches the top as the storm clears. Here is what you would hear, described in everyday words:

- The music starts very quietly. High violins hold one note, moving the bow very rapidly back and forth; a drum rumbles softly underneath.
- Cellos and double basses repeat a short three-note pattern again and again, each note only a semitone away from the next.
- A solo French horn plays a slow melody in a minor key. Each time the melody repeats, it starts a little higher.
- The chords clash, using many notes from outside the key.
- The music gradually gets louder, the repeated low pattern gets faster, and more instruments join in one after another.
- Suddenly there is one very loud, accented chord from the brass with a cymbal crash, followed by complete silence.
- Then a solo flute plays the horn melody softly and slowly, now in a major key, as the climber reaches the summit.

(a) Name the type of ensemble that would play this music, with strings, woodwind, brass and percussion. (1)

(b) Name the string technique of moving the bow very rapidly back and forth on one note. (1)

(c) Explain how the music creates the danger of the storm and then the relief of reaching the summit. Use musical vocabulary. In your answer refer to the musical elements and how they are used to create the mood of each part of the scene. (10)

(12)
---
(a) Orchestra / symphony orchestra (1).

(b) Tremolo (1).

(c) Mark with the bands; the indicative content is not exhaustive. Credit a term only when it is linked to the scene.

Indicative content: very quiet (pianissimo) opening creates stillness before the storm; sustained high tremolo suggests wind and nervous tension; drum roll suggests distant thunder; low ostinato builds unease and suggests the relentless storm; semitone or chromatic movement sounds menacing; minor tonality for danger and fear; the horn melody rising in sequence raises tension like the climb; dissonant and chromatic harmony for danger; crescendo and accelerando build panic; layering or thickening texture as more instruments join makes the storm overwhelm the climber; sforzando or fortissimo tutti chord with cymbal crash as the storm's climax or a fall; the silence (general pause) creates shock and suspense; contrast to pianissimo; thematic transformation of the horn theme into the major key on a solo flute shows relief and triumph; slower tempo and thin texture for calm; high flute timbre suggests clear air and peace.

- 9-10: a perceptive answer that explains many features in detail, each linked closely to the action and both halves of the scene, with evaluative judgements about why the choices work. Specialist vocabulary is used accurately throughout.
- 7-8: a secure answer that explains a good range of features, generally linked to the action, with some judgement of their effect. Vocabulary is mostly accurate.
- 5-6: an inconsistent answer that identifies several features, with some links to the scene. Some accurate vocabulary.
- 3-4: a limited answer that names a few features with little explanation of effect. Limited accurate vocabulary.
- 1-2: a very limited answer, mostly everyday description with little or no link to the scene.
- 0: nothing of value.

An answer that covers only the storm, or only the summit, cannot go above 6.
