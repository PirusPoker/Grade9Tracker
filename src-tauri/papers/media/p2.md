**4. Music: industries and audiences** (continued)

(b) Briefly explain one way either Rio (Duran Duran, 1982) or Waterfalls (TLC, 1995) reflects the time in which it was made. (3)

(3)
---
Up to 3 marks for one developed point: identify the feature (1), link it to its context (1), explain the effect or meaning (1).

Rio: shot on film in the Caribbean on a yacht, with a large budget, reflecting how the new music channel MTV (from 1981) made videos essential marketing, so glamour and exotic spectacle sold the band to a global audience; the band's designer suits and luxury lifestyle reflect the aspirational, consumer-driven 1980s; the women shown mainly as objects of the camera's gaze reflect less-challenged gender attitudes of the time.

Waterfalls: its narrative addresses drug dealing, gun violence and HIV/AIDS, social issues affecting young people, particularly in African American communities, in the mid-1990s, so it reflects a period of public-health campaigning about safe sex; the then-new CGI water figures reflect developing video technology and big-budget videos made for MTV; three women taking a moral, advisory role reflects 1990s R&B and hip hop groups building confident female identities.

Answer on the contemporary set episode you have studied: Trigger Point (Series 2, Episode 1), Man Like Mobeen (Series 1, Episode 2) or Modern Family (Season 8, Episode 2). Refer to specific scenes.

(a) Explore how mise-en-scène (setting, costume, props and performance) creates meaning in the set episode. (10)

(10)
---
Level-marked, 10 marks (AO2):
- 9–10: excellent, detailed analysis of how mise-en-scène creates meaning; thorough use of precise examples from the episode; highly appropriate terminology.
- 7–8: good, reasonably detailed analysis; secure examples; appropriate terminology.
- 5–6: satisfactory analysis, less developed; generally appropriate examples.
- 3–4: basic analysis, mostly description.

Indicative content.

Trigger Point: urban London settings, the operations base and dangerous locations such as the power station create realism and threat; bomb-disposal suits, protective kit, robots, screens and police uniforms are genre iconography that signals expertise and danger; Lana's kit and calm, controlled performance connote authority and competence in a male-dominated role; the room of pressure plates where she rescues Thom builds tension through performance (his fear against her composure); explosions and bodies reinforce the high stakes.

Man Like Mobeen: real locations in Small Heath, Birmingham (the family home, the local shop, the street outside the mosque) create verisimilitude and a strong regional and community identity; Mobeen's kurta with a baseball cap and designer coat connotes a character between cultures; Aqsa's school uniform establishes her age and his responsibility; the older men's traditional dress signals age and faith; the sofa in the front yard as a meeting point gives a familiar sitcom convention a fresh twist; performance (open, warm body language; exaggerated reactions) drives the comedy.

Modern Family: affluent Los Angeles homes signal the families' comfortable lifestyles; the sofa for the interview scenes is iconic of the programme and its mockumentary form; Manny's Mao-style suit creates comedy from his sudden political pose; Alex's dressing gown signals her illness and suffering; Phil's physical comedy (counting sweets, falling while trying to escape) and Claire's and Phil's switch from work suits to casual clothes mark their changing roles; Lily's bedroom mural drives a comic storyline.

(b) Explain how crime dramas or sitcoms reflect the social and cultural contexts in which they were made. Refer to The Sweeney or Friends to support your points. (8)

(8)
---
Level-marked, 8 marks (AO1 contexts):
- 7–8: excellent, detailed and accurate knowledge of how the genre reflects its contexts; precise, well-chosen reference to the 1970s or 1990s programme; confident terminology.
- 5–6: good, accurate knowledge with appropriate reference.
- 3–4: satisfactory, generally accurate; some reference.
- 1–2: basic; little reference to the programme.

Indicative content.

The Sweeney (1975): made for ITV by a commercial producer and filmed on location on film, which reflects a push for gritty realism in 1970s police drama; the Flying Squad detectives are white, working-class men whose rule-bending, violence and heavy drinking reflect contemporary debate about police corruption and a tougher approach to crime; women appear mainly as girlfriends, wives or victims, reflecting male-dominated workplaces and attitudes before equal-opportunities laws had much effect; run-down London locations, cars and chases reflect a period of economic decline and an interest in action.

Friends (1994): made in the USA for NBC and bought by Channel 4 for British audiences, reflecting the global trade in American sitcoms; six young, white, middle-class friends in New York reflect 1990s ideas of youth, city living and friendship as a new "family"; the pilot's storylines (Rachel leaving her wedding to become independent, Ross's former wife now with a female partner) reflect changing attitudes to gender and sexuality, but the jokes often rely on the characters' discomfort; the almost total absence of ethnic diversity is typical of mainstream American sitcoms of the time; a studio audience and laughter track reflect the multi-camera conventions of the era.

**1. Television: media language** (continued)

(b) How typical of the genre is the narrative of the set episode you have studied? (10)

(10)
---
Level-marked, 10 marks (AO2, analyse and make judgements):
- 9–10: excellent analysis with a clear, sustained judgement about how far the narrative is typical; detailed examples; relevant narrative and genre theory (such as Propp, equilibrium, repetition and variation) used to support the argument.
- 7–8: good analysis and a clear judgement; appropriate examples and theory.
- 5–6: satisfactory; some judgement, less developed.
- 3–4: basic; mostly retells the plot.
- 1–2: minimal.

Indicative content.

Trigger Point, largely typical of a crime thriller: enigmas (who are the bombers and what do they want?), ticking-clock suspense while devices are defused, and a cliffhanger ending that brings viewers back for the next episode; Propp's roles fit clearly (Lana the hero, Danny the helper, the unseen bombers the villains, a senior officer who interferes as a false hero, Thom as the victim she rescues); less typical: the focus on a bomb-disposal unit rather than detectives, and a female hero in a traditionally male role, showing genre variation.

Man Like Mobeen, typical of a sitcom: a circular narrative in which an ordinary day (equilibrium) is disrupted when Aqsa is suspended and accuses Mobeen of failing to parent her; his attempt to find a wife ("maybe she needs a mum") creates the comedy of situation, and it ends with a return to the original situation when the characters decide they are happy as they are; stock character types (the comic friends, the meddling uncles); less typical: real locations, a strong sense of British Muslim community and a pop and grime soundtrack.

Modern Family, a mix of typical and untypical: three storylines, one per family, each with a problem, complications and a resolution within about 22 minutes; comedy from characters failing to solve their problems (Jay trying to prove he is not prejudiced to his new neighbour); less typical: the mockumentary form with talking-head interviews that give the audience privileged knowledge, non-linear flashbacks, single-camera filming and no laughter track.

Top answers reach a judgement (for example, "familiar structure, varied form") rather than listing conventions.

**3. Music: representation**

Use the two set music videos you have studied: Good as Hell (Lizzo) OR The Man (Taylor Swift), AND Superheroes (Stormzy) OR Intentions (Justin Bieber).

You will be assessed on the quality of your written response, including the ability to construct and develop a sustained line of reasoning which is coherent, relevant, substantiated and logically structured.

How far do music videos construct positive representations of their artists?

In your response, you must:
- explore how the artists are represented in the two music videos you have studied
- refer to relevant media contexts
- make judgements and draw conclusions. (20)

(20)
---
Level-marked, 20 marks (AO2, analyse, judge and conclude):
- 17–20: excellent, detailed analysis of both videos; precise use of media language to show how the artists are constructed; contexts used to explain the representations; a sustained, well-supported judgement about how far they are positive.
- 13–16: good analysis of both; clear judgements; appropriate contexts and terminology.
- 9–12: satisfactory; some analysis, uneven between videos; judgements less developed.
- 5–8: basic; mainly description of the videos.
- 1–4: minimal.

Indicative content (credit accurate detail on whichever pair was studied).

Lizzo, Good as Hell: Lizzo is constructed as confident, joyful and in control, a role model of body positivity who leads and supports students at a historically black university marching band (Southern University) as three young women overcome setbacks; the palette grows richer, ending in blue and gold homecoming costumes, and her gold outfit marks her star persona while matching costumes signal unity; direct address and dance celebrate self-love; context: the body-positivity movement and the under-representation of plus-size black women in mainstream pop; a self-representation that challenges narrow beauty ideals, though a feminist reading might question the focus on hair, nails and dance-troupe costumes.

Taylor Swift, The Man: Swift performs as a male alter ego (with prosthetics and a deep voice) to expose double standards: behaviour praised in men is criticised in women; the satire presents her as intelligent and in control of her own image (she directed the video); context: feminist debates, the #MeToo period and her own disputes over ownership of her master recordings.

Stormzy, Superheroes: an animated video in which black children imagine futures as scientists, doctors, athletes and leaders; Stormzy appears as a mentor figure; bright animation connotes hope; context: the 2020 Black Lives Matter protests, the under-representation of black professionals, and Stormzy's scholarship and education initiatives, so the artist is represented through his values as a positive influence.

Justin Bieber, Intentions: the video shows real women helped by a charity supporting women and families facing hardship, with pastel colours and a warm tone; Bieber is represented as caring and generous, a change from his troubled earlier image; context: celebrity charity and image rehabilitation; some will read this as promotional as much as altruistic.

Judgement: each video is a constructed, promotional self-representation, so "positive" images serve marketing and brand identity; better answers weigh how far the representations are genuine social messages, and for whom they are positive.
