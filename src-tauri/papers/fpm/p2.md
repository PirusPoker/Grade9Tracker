**2.** A sector of a circle of radius 6 cm has an angle of 1.2 radians.

(a) Find the length of the arc. (1)

(b) Find the area of the sector. (2)

(3)
---
(a) $r\theta = 6 \times 1.2 = 7.2$ cm (1).

(b) $\dfrac{1}{2} r^2 \theta = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 36 \times 1.2$ (1) $= 21.6$ cm$^2$ (1).

::: q 4-5 8
**3.** $A$ is the point $(-2, 3)$ and $B$ is the point $(4, -1)$.

(a) Find the coordinates of the midpoint of $AB$. (1)

(b) Find the exact length of $AB$, giving your answer as a simplified surd. (2)

(c) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ in the form $ax + by + c = 0$. (2)

(5)
---
(a) $(1, 1)$ (1).

(b) $\sqrt{6^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{52}$ (1) $= 2\sqrt{13}$ (1).

(c) Gradient of $AB$ is $-\dfrac{2}{3}$, so the bisector has gradient $\dfrac{3}{2}$ (1); $y - 1 = \dfrac{3}{2}(x - 1)$, so $3x - 2y - 1 = 0$ (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 10c
**9.** (a) Prove that $\dfrac{\sin\theta}{1 + \cos\theta} + \dfrac{1 + \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \equiv \dfrac{2}{\sin\theta}$. (3)

(b) Solve $\tan^2 x = 3$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ$. (2)

(5)
---
(a) Common denominator: $\dfrac{\sin^2\theta + (1 + \cos\theta)^2}{\sin\theta(1 + \cos\theta)}$ (1); numerator $= \sin^2\theta + 1 + 2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta = 2 + 2\cos\theta$ (1); $= \dfrac{2(1 + \cos\theta)}{\sin\theta(1 + \cos\theta)} = \dfrac{2}{\sin\theta}$ (1).

(b) $\tan x = \pm\sqrt{3}$ (1); $x = 60^\circ$ or $120^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 9d
**11.** (a) The radius of a circle increases at a constant rate of 0.5 cm/s. Find the rate of increase of the area when the radius is 10 cm. (3)

(b) The radius of a sphere increases at 0.2 cm/s. Find the rate of increase of the volume when the radius is 3 cm, leaving your answer in terms of $\pi$. (2)

(5)
---
(a) $A = \pi r^2$, $\dfrac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi r$ (1); $\dfrac{dA}{dt} = \dfrac{dA}{dr}\times\dfrac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi \times 10 \times 0.5$ (1) $= 10\pi$ cm$^2$/s (1).

(b) $\dfrac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 = 36\pi$ (1); $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = 36\pi \times 0.2 = 7.2\pi$ cm$^3$/s (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 3c
**12.** A workshop makes $x$ tables and $y$ chairs a day. Each table needs 1 hour of cutting and 1 hour of finishing; each chair needs 1 hour of cutting and 3 hours of finishing. There are 8 hours of cutting and 18 hours of finishing available each day. The profit is £2 per table and £3 per chair.

(a) Write down the constraints as inequalities. (2)

(b) Find the number of tables and chairs that gives the greatest profit, and state that profit. (3)

(5)
---
(a) $x + y \le 8$; $x + 3y \le 18$ (1); $x \ge 0$, $y \ge 0$ (1).

(b) Vertices of the feasible region: $(0, 0)$, $(8, 0)$, $(0, 6)$ and $(3, 5)$ where the lines meet (1); $P = 2x + 3y$ gives $0, 16, 18, 21$ (1); 3 tables and 5 chairs, profit £21 (1).
:::

**13.** A pyramid has a square base $ABCD$ of side 8 cm and its vertex $V$ is 10 cm vertically above the centre of the base.

(a) Find the length of the slant edge $VA$. (2)

(b) Find the angle between $VA$ and the base. (2)

(c) Find the angle between the face $VBC$ and the base. (2)

(6)
---
(a) Half-diagonal $= \sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}$; $VA^2 = 32 + 100 = 132$ (1); $VA = 11.5$ cm (1).

(b) $\tan\theta = \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{32}}$ (1); $\theta = 60.5^\circ$ (1).

(c) The perpendicular from $V$ to $BC$ meets it at its midpoint, 4 cm from the centre: $\tan\phi = \dfrac{10}{4}$ (1); $\phi = 68.2^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 9a 9b
**14.** The curve $y = x^2\text{e}^{-x}$ has two stationary points.

Find their coordinates and determine the nature of each. (5)
---
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x\text{e}^{-x} - x^2\text{e}^{-x} = x(2 - x)\text{e}^{-x}$ (2); zero at $x = 0$ and $x = 2$ (1); $(0, 0)$ is a minimum — the gradient changes from negative to positive (1); $(2, 4\text{e}^{-2})$ is a maximum — the gradient changes from positive to negative (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 1a 1b
**15.** (a) Solve $4^x - 5 \times 2^x + 4 = 0$. (4)

(b) Solve $3^{2x + 1} = 20$, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. (1)

(5)
---
(a) Let $u = 2^x$, so $u^2 - 5u + 4 = 0$ (1); $(u - 1)(u - 4) = 0$ (1); $2^x = 1$ gives $x = 0$ (1); $2^x = 4$ gives $x = 2$ (1).

(b) $2x + 1 = \dfrac{\log 20}{\log 3} = 2.727$, so $x = 0.863$ (1).
:::
