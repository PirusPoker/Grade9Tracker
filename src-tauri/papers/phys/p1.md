intro: Structured questions in the Edexcel International GCSE style. Show every calculation — formula, substitution, answer with unit. Take g = 10 N/kg, speed of sound 340 m/s and speed of light 3 × 10⁸ m/s. The questions ramp from grade 4–5 to grade 8–9.

::: q 4-5
**1.** A cyclist rides 1.8 km in 4 minutes.

(a) Calculate her average speed in m/s. (2)

(b) At this speed, how far does she travel in 15 s? (1)

(3)
---
(a) $1800 \div 240$ (1) $= 7.5$ m/s (1).

(b) $7.5 \times 15 = 112.5$ m (1).
:::

::: q 4-5
**2.** A 3 Ω resistor and a 5 Ω resistor are connected in series across a 12 V battery.

(a) Calculate the current in the circuit. (2)

(b) Calculate the voltage across the 5 Ω resistor. (1)

(3)
---
(a) Total resistance $8\ \Omega$ (1); $I = 12 \div 8 = 1.5$ A (1).

(b) $V = 1.5 \times 5 = 7.5$ V (1).
:::

::: q 4-5
**3.** A sound wave in air has a frequency of 170 Hz.

(a) Calculate its wavelength. (2)

(b) State the unit of frequency and name one wave in the list that is transverse: sound, light. (2)

(4)
---
(a) $\lambda = v \div f = 340 \div 170$ (1) $= 2$ m (1).

(b) Hertz (1); light is transverse — sound is longitudinal (1).
:::

::: q 4-5
**4.** A 2 kg ball is dropped from a height of 5 m. Ignore air resistance.

(a) Calculate the gravitational potential energy it loses. (2)

(b) Calculate its speed just before it hits the ground. (2)

(4)
---
(a) $GPE = mgh = 2 \times 10 \times 5$ (1) $= 100$ J (1).

(b) $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times v^2 = 100$ (1); $v = 10$ m/s (1).
:::

::: q 4-5
**5.** A block of mass 250 g measures 8 cm by 5 cm by 2.5 cm.

(a) Calculate its density in g/cm³. (3)

(b) State, with a reason, whether it floats in water (density 1 g/cm³). (1)

(4)
---
(a) Volume $= 8 \times 5 \times 2.5 = 100$ cm$^3$ (1); $\rho = m \div V = 250 \div 100$ (1) $= 2.5$ g/cm$^3$ (1).

(b) Sinks — its density is greater than water's (1).
:::

::: q 6-7
**6.** An 800 kg car moving at 15 m/s collides with a stationary 1200 kg van. They move off together.

(a) Calculate their velocity immediately after the collision. (3)

(b) The collision lasts 0.2 s. Calculate the average force on the car during the collision. (2)

(5)
---
(a) Momentum before $= 800 \times 15 = 12\,000$ kg m/s (1); after $= 2000v$ (1); $v = 6$ m/s (1).

(b) Change in momentum of the car $= 800 \times (15 - 6) = 7200$ kg m/s (1); $F = 7200 \div 0.2 = 36\,000$ N (1).
:::

::: q 6-7
**7.** A kettle is rated 2.3 kW and works from the 230 V mains. It runs for 3 minutes.

(a) Calculate the current it draws. (2)

(b) Calculate the energy transferred. (2)

(c) Calculate the charge that flows. (2)

(6)
---
(a) $I = P \div V = 2300 \div 230$ (1) $= 10$ A (1).

(b) $E = Pt = 2300 \times 180$ (1) $= 414\,000$ J (1).

(c) $Q = It = 10 \times 180$ (1) $= 1800$ C (1).
:::

::: q 6-7
**8.** A ray of light enters a glass block of refractive index 1.5 at an angle of incidence of 42°.

(a) Calculate the angle of refraction. (3)

(b) Calculate the critical angle of the glass. (2)

(5)
---
(a) $\sin r = \dfrac{\sin 42^\circ}{1.5}$ (1) $= 0.446$ (1); $r = 26.5^\circ$ (1).

(b) $\sin c = \dfrac{1}{1.5}$ (1); $c = 41.8^\circ$ (1).
:::

::: q 6-7
**9.** A sealed container holds gas at 150 kPa and 27 °C. It is heated to 127 °C at constant volume.

(a) Calculate the new pressure. (3)

(b) Explain, in terms of particles, why the pressure changes. (2)

(5)
---
(a) Temperatures $300$ K and $400$ K (1); $\dfrac{p_2}{400} = \dfrac{150}{300}$ (1); $p_2 = 200$ kPa (1).

(b) Particles move faster with more kinetic energy (1); they hit the walls harder and more often, increasing the force per unit area (1).
:::

::: q 8-9
**10.** The activity of a radioactive source falls from 3200 Bq to 200 Bq in 24 days.

(a) Calculate the half-life of the source. (2)

(b) Calculate the activity 30 days after the start. (1)

(c) A second sample of the same isotope has an activity of 1000 Bq. How long will it take to fall to 125 Bq? (2)

(5)
---
(a) $3200 \to 1600 \to 800 \to 400 \to 200$: four half-lives (1); $24 \div 4 = 6$ days (1).

(b) 30 days is five half-lives: $100$ Bq (1).

(c) $1000 \to 500 \to 250 \to 125$: three half-lives (1); $18$ days (1).
:::

::: q 8-9
**11.** A generator delivers 20 kW along a cable of total resistance 0.5 Ω.

(a) The power is transmitted at 400 V. Calculate the current and the power lost as heat in the cable. (4)

(b) A transformer steps the voltage up to 4000 V before transmission. Calculate the new power loss. (2)

(c) Explain why the National Grid transmits at very high voltage. (1)

(7)
---
(a) $I = P \div V = 20\,000 \div 400 = 50$ A (2); $P_{loss} = I^2R = 50^2 \times 0.5 = 1250$ W (2).

(b) $I = 5$ A (1); $P_{loss} = 5^2 \times 0.5 = 12.5$ W (1).

(c) For the same power a higher voltage means a smaller current, and the heat loss ($I^2R$) falls with the square of the current (1).
:::

::: q 8-9
**12.** A satellite orbits the Earth in a circle of radius $7.0 \times 10^6$ m with a period of 5800 s.

(a) Calculate its orbital speed. (3)

(b) A geostationary satellite has a period of 24 hours. Explain why it must orbit at a much greater radius. (2)

(5)
---
(a) $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T} = \dfrac{2\pi \times 7.0 \times 10^6}{5800}$ (2) $= 7580$ m/s (1).

(b) A longer period means a slower orbit (1); gravity is weaker further out, so a slower speed is exactly what a larger orbit needs to stay circular (1).
:::

::: q 8-9
**13.** A uranium-235 nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits: $^{235}_{92}\text{U} + ^{1}_{0}\text{n} \rightarrow ^{141}_{56}\text{Ba} + ^{A}_{Z}\text{Kr} + 3\,^{1}_{0}\text{n}$.

(a) Find $A$ and $Z$. (2)

(b) Explain how the reaction is kept at a steady rate inside a reactor. (2)

(4)
---
(a) $236 = 141 + A + 3$, so $A = 92$ (1); $92 = 56 + Z$, so $Z = 36$ (1).

(b) Each fission releases 2–3 neutrons that can cause further fissions (1); control rods of boron or cadmium absorb neutrons so that on average exactly one neutron per fission goes on to cause another (1).
:::
