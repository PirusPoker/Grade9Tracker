**1.** A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 8 s, travels at 20 m/s for 12 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 5 s.

(a) Calculate the acceleration during the first 8 s. (2)

(b) Calculate the total distance travelled, using the area under a velocity–time graph. (3)

(5)
---
(a) $a = \dfrac{20 - 0}{8}$ (1) $= 2.5$ m/s$^2$ (1).

(b) $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 20 = 80$ m; $12 \times 20 = 240$ m; $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 20 = 50$ m (2); total $370$ m (1).
:::

::: q 4-5 1d 1c
**2.** A car of mass 1200 kg has a driving force of 3000 N. The total resistive force is 1800 N.

(a) Calculate the resultant force on the car. (1)

(b) Calculate the acceleration of the car. (2)

(c) Calculate the weight of the car. (1)

(4)
---
(a) $3000 - 1800 = 1200$ N forwards (1).

(b) $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{1200}{1200}$ (1) $= 1$ m/s$^2$ (1).

(c) $W = mg = 1200 \times 10 = 12\,000$ N (1).

::: q 4-5 1f
**3.** A spring extends by 4 cm when a force of 6 N is applied.

(a) Calculate the spring constant in N/m. (2)

(b) Calculate the extension when a force of 9 N is applied, assuming the spring obeys Hooke's law. (1)

(c) State what is meant by the elastic limit. (1)

(4)
---
(a) $k = \dfrac{F}{x} = \dfrac{6}{0.04}$ (1) $= 150$ N/m (1).

(b) $x = \dfrac{9}{150} = 0.06$ m $= 6$ cm (1).

(c) The point beyond which the spring no longer returns to its original length when the force is removed (1).

::: q 4-5 4b 4a

(a) Calculate the efficiency of the motor. (2)

(b) State what happens to the rest of the energy. (1)

(c) Describe how a Sankey diagram would show this. (1)

(4)
---
(a) $\dfrac{350}{500}$ (1) $= 0.7$ or $70\%$ (1).

(b) Transferred to the surroundings as heat (and sound), mainly by friction (1).

(c) One arrow in of width 500, splitting into a useful arrow of width 350 straight on and a wasted arrow of width 150 turning away (1).

::: q 6-7 1h
**6.** A child of mass 40 kg sits 1.5 m from the pivot of a seesaw.

(a) Calculate the moment of the child's weight about the pivot. (2)

(b) An adult exerts a force of 600 N on the other side. How far from the pivot must the adult sit to balance the seesaw? (1)

(c) The seesaw plank is not uniform. Explain why this affects the answer. (1)

(4)
---
(a) Weight $400$ N; moment $= 400 \times 1.5$ (1) $= 600$ Nm (1).

(b) $600 = 600 \times d$, $d = 1.0$ m (1).

(c) The plank's own weight acts at its centre of gravity, which is not at the pivot, so it adds a moment on one side that must be included (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 5b
**7.** A 1.5 kW heater warms 2 kg of water from 20 °C to 70 °C. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/kg °C.

(a) Calculate the energy needed. (2)

(b) Calculate the minimum time this takes. (2)

(c) Explain why the real time is longer. (1)

(5)
---
(a) $E = mc\Delta T = 2 \times 4200 \times 50$ (1) $= 420\,000$ J (1).

(b) $t = \dfrac{E}{P} = \dfrac{420\,000}{1500}$ (1) $= 280$ s (1).

(c) Some energy is transferred to the container and the surroundings, not the water (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 2c
**8.** (a) Sketch or describe the current–voltage graph of a filament lamp and explain its shape. (3)

(b) Describe the current–voltage graph of a diode. (1)

(4)
---
(a) A curve that flattens as voltage increases, in both directions (1); as current increases the filament gets hotter (1); its resistance rises, so the current increases less for each extra volt (1).

(b) Almost no current in the reverse direction; in the forward direction current is zero until about 0.7 V, then rises steeply (1).

::: q 8-9 1e 1d
**11.** A car of mass 1500 kg travels at 20 m/s. The driver's reaction time is 0.6 s and the brakes provide a force of 6000 N.

(a) Calculate the thinking distance. (1)

(b) Calculate the braking distance. (3)

(c) Calculate the total stopping distance. (1)

(d) Explain the effect of a wet road on the stopping distance. (1)

(6)
---
(a) $20 \times 0.6 = 12$ m (1).

(b) Deceleration $\dfrac{6000}{1500} = 4$ m/s$^2$ (1); $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ gives $0 = 400 - 8s$ (1); $s = 50$ m (1).

(c) $62$ m (1).

(d) Less friction between tyres and road reduces the braking force, so the braking distance increases (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 4e
**12.** A country is deciding whether to build wind farms or a gas-fired power station.

Evaluate the two options. (4)
---
Wind: renewable, no fuel cost, no carbon dioxide in use (1); but output depends on the weather, so back-up is needed, and turbines take up land and can be unpopular (1). Gas: reliable, can be switched on quickly to meet demand, high power from one site (1); but burns a non-renewable fuel and releases carbon dioxide; a judgement, e.g. wind for the bulk with gas for back-up (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 8c 8b
**13.** (a) Explain how red shift in the light from distant galaxies supports the Big Bang theory. (3)

(b) Describe the life cycle of a star much more massive than the Sun after it leaves the main sequence. (3)

(6)
---
(a) Light from distant galaxies has its spectral lines shifted to longer wavelengths (1); this shows the galaxies are moving away from us, and the further away they are the faster they recede (1); so the universe is expanding from a single point, which is what the Big Bang predicts (1).

(b) It expands into a red supergiant (1); its core collapses and it explodes as a supernova (1); the remnant becomes a neutron star, or a black hole if massive enough (1).
:::
