::: q 4-5 2h
**3.** (a) Describe how air is drawn into the lungs when you breathe in. (3)

(b) State one effect of smoking on the gas exchange system. (1)

(4)
---
(a) The external intercostal muscles contract, raising the ribcage up and out (1); the diaphragm contracts and flattens (1); the volume of the thorax increases, so the pressure inside falls below atmospheric pressure and air moves in (1).

(b) Describe the shape of a pyramid of biomass for this chain. (1)

(c) Explain why only a small fraction of the energy in the grass reaches the foxes. (2)

(5)
---

(b) A wide bar for grass at the bottom, narrower for rabbits, narrowest for foxes (1).

::: q 6-7 2n
**6.** A person touches a hot pan and pulls their hand away before they feel the pain.

(a) Describe the pathway of the reflex, naming the neurones in order. (3)

(b) Explain why a reflex is faster than a conscious response. (1)

(c) State what happens to the pupil of the eye in bright light and why. (1)

(5)
---
(a) Receptor in the skin detects the stimulus and a sensory neurone carries the impulse to the spinal cord (1); a relay neurone passes it across a synapse (1); a motor neurone carries the impulse to the effector, the arm muscle, which contracts (1).

(b) The impulse does not have to travel to the brain and be processed — it goes through the spinal cord only (1).

(c) It constricts (circular muscles of the iris contract) so less light enters and the retina is protected (1).
:::

(b) Describe how the body responds when blood glucose concentration rises after a meal. (2)

(4)

**9.** (a) Compare mitosis and meiosis in terms of the number of divisions, the number of cells produced and the chromosome number of the cells. (3)

(4)

(a) Mitosis: one division, two cells, same chromosome number as the parent (diploid); genetically identical (1). Meiosis: two divisions, four cells (1), half the chromosome number (haploid), genetically different — used to make gametes (1).

(b) A section of DNA that codes for one protein (or for one characteristic) (1).

(b) Explain how deforestation increases the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. (2)

(c) Describe the sequence of events by which fertiliser washed into a lake can kill the fish. (3)

(7)

::: q 8-9 2j
**11.** (a) Explain how a vaccination makes a person immune to a disease. (4)

(b) Explain why antibiotics are not used to treat influenza. (1)

(5)
---
(a) The vaccine contains a dead or weakened form of the pathogen, or its antigens (1); lymphocytes recognise the antigens and produce specific antibodies (1); memory cells are made and remain in the blood (1); if the real pathogen enters, the memory cells produce antibodies much faster and in greater numbers, so the pathogen is destroyed before symptoms develop (1).

(b) Influenza is caused by a virus, and antibiotics only kill bacteria — viruses reproduce inside the host's own cells (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 5b 5c
**12.** (a) Penicillin is produced in an industrial fermenter. State three conditions that are controlled in the fermenter and explain why each is needed. (3)

(b) Describe how a farmer could use selective breeding to produce cows that give more milk. (3)

(6)
---
(a) Any three of: temperature (kept at the optimum for the fungus's enzymes, with a water jacket to remove heat from respiration); pH (monitored and adjusted so enzymes work); oxygen (sterile air bubbled in for aerobic respiration); nutrients (sugars added as food); stirring (keeps the fungus and nutrients evenly mixed); aseptic conditions (steam-sterilised so no competing microbes) (3).

(b) Choose the cows with the highest milk yield and breed them with bulls whose mothers or daughters gave high yields (1); select the offspring with the highest yields and breed from those (1); repeat over many generations so the desired alleles become more common in the herd (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 5e
**14.** Explain why a grower might use micropropagation rather than seeds to produce orchids. (2)
---
Every plant produced is genetically identical to the parent, so a plant with desirable features is reproduced exactly (1); many plants can be produced quickly from a small amount of tissue, all year round and free from disease (1).
