::: q 4-5 2a
**1.** (a) Name the part of a cell where (i) photosynthesis takes place, (ii) proteins are made, (iii) aerobic respiration takes place. (3)

(b) Name one structure found in plant cells but not in animal cells. (1)

(4)
---
(a) (i) chloroplast (1); (ii) ribosome (1); (iii) mitochondrion (1).

(b) Cell wall, chloroplast or large permanent vacuole (1).

::: q 4-5 2b
**2.** (a) What is an enzyme? (1)

(b) Explain why an enzyme stops working at 60 °C. (2)

(c) Describe the test for starch. (1)

(4)
---
(a) A biological catalyst — a protein that speeds up a reaction without being used up (1).

(b) The high temperature breaks the bonds holding its shape, so the active site changes shape (1); the substrate no longer fits — the enzyme is denatured (1).

(c) Add iodine solution; it turns blue-black (1).
:::

::: q 4-5 2e
**3.** (a) Name the enzyme that digests protein in the stomach, and the product of protein digestion. (2)

(b) Describe two functions of bile. (2)

(4)
---
(a) Pepsin (protease) (1); amino acids (1).

(b) Neutralises stomach acid so small-intestine enzymes can work (1); emulsifies fats into droplets, increasing the surface area for lipase (1).
:::

::: q 4-5 2k
**4.** (a) List, in order, the chambers and vessels that blood passes through from the vena cava to the aorta. (3)

(b) Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle. (1)

(4)
---
(a) Right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery (1) → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium (1) → left ventricle → aorta (1).

(b) It pumps blood around the whole body, so needs to produce a higher pressure (1).

::: q 6-7 2c
**5.** Potato cylinders were weighed, left in salt solutions of different concentrations for an hour, and reweighed. In distilled water a cylinder gained 6% in mass.

(a) Define osmosis. (2)

(b) Explain why the cylinder in distilled water gained mass. (2)

(c) Explain why percentage change in mass is used rather than the change in grams. (1)

(5)
---
(a) Net movement of water from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one (1) across a partially permeable membrane (1).

(b) The potato cells contain a more concentrated solution than pure water (1); water moved into the cells by osmosis (1).

(c) The cylinders had different starting masses, so percentages make the results comparable (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 2d
**6.** A student measured the rate of photosynthesis of pondweed at increasing light intensities. The rate rose steadily, then levelled off.

(a) Explain the shape of the graph. (3)

(b) Write the balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis. (2)

(5)
---
(a) At low intensity light is the limiting factor, so more light gives more photosynthesis (1); the graph levels off when light is no longer limiting (1); another factor — carbon dioxide concentration or temperature — now limits the rate (1).

(b) 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (2; one mark if unbalanced).
:::

::: q 6-7 3d
**7.** In mice, brown fur (B) is dominant to white fur (b). Two brown mice that are both heterozygous are bred together.

(a) Draw a Punnett square for the cross. (2)

(b) Give the expected ratio of brown to white offspring. (1)

(c) What is the probability that a given offspring is white? (1)

(d) Explain what is meant by codominance. (1)

(5)
---
(a) Gametes B, b × B, b (1); offspring BB, Bb, Bb, bb (1).

(b) 3 brown : 1 white (1).

(c) $\dfrac{1}{4}$ or 25% (1).

(d) Both alleles are expressed in the phenotype of a heterozygote, e.g. blood group AB (1).
:::

::: q 6-7 2m
**8.** (a) Describe three ways the body responds when its temperature rises above normal. (3)

(b) Explain why temperature control is an example of negative feedback. (2)

(5)
---
(a) Sweating — evaporation takes heat from the skin (1); vasodilation — more blood flows near the surface, losing heat by radiation (1); hairs lie flat; less shivering/activity (1).

(b) A change from the set point is detected (by the hypothalamus) (1); the response reverses the change, bringing the temperature back toward normal (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 2l
**9.** (a) Describe how urine is produced in the nephron, naming the two main processes. (4)

(b) A person exercises hard on a hot day without drinking. Explain how the kidneys respond. (3)

(7)
---
(a) Ultrafiltration: blood at high pressure in the glomerulus forces small molecules — water, glucose, salts, urea — into the Bowman's capsule (1); proteins and cells are too large to pass (1); selective reabsorption: all the glucose, and needed water and salts, are reabsorbed into the blood along the tubule (1); the remaining water, salts and urea form urine (1).

(b) Sweating makes the blood more concentrated; the hypothalamus detects this (1); the pituitary releases more ADH (1); the collecting ducts become more permeable, more water is reabsorbed, and a small volume of concentrated urine is produced (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 3f
**10.** (a) Explain how a population of bacteria can become resistant to an antibiotic. (4)

(b) Explain why mutation is essential to this process. (1)

(5)
---
(a) A random mutation gives a few bacteria resistance (1); the antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria (1); the resistant ones survive and reproduce, passing on the resistance allele (1); over generations the proportion of resistant bacteria increases — natural selection (1).

(b) Mutation provides the variation on which selection acts; without it there would be no resistant individuals to select (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 4a
**11.** A student placed ten 0.25 m² quadrats at random in a field of area 2000 m² and counted 40 clover plants in total.

(a) Estimate the number of clover plants in the field. (3)

(b) Explain why the quadrats were placed at random. (1)

(c) Fertiliser from the field was washed into a nearby pond, and fish died. Explain the sequence of events. (4)

(8)
---
(a) Area sampled $= 2.5$ m$^2$ (1); density $= 40 \div 2.5 = 16$ per m$^2$ (1); $16 \times 2000 = 32\,000$ plants (1).

(b) To avoid bias — so the sample represents the whole field (1).

(c) Nitrates cause rapid algal growth, an algal bloom (1); the algae block light so plants below die (1); bacteria decompose the dead material and multiply, using up dissolved oxygen in respiration (1); fish suffocate — eutrophication (1).
:::

::: q 8-9 5d
**12.** Describe how bacteria are genetically modified to produce human insulin. (4)
---
The insulin gene is cut from human DNA using a restriction enzyme (1); a plasmid is cut open with the same enzyme, leaving matching sticky ends (1); the gene is joined into the plasmid with DNA ligase (1); the recombinant plasmid is taken up by bacteria, which are grown in a fermenter and produce insulin (1).
:::
